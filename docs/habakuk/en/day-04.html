Yet I Will Rejoice · Day 4 / 6

The Righteous Will Live by Faith

Scripture Habakkuk 2:2–5

2Then the LORD answered me and said:

Write down the visionand make it plain on the tablets,so that whoever reads it may run.3For the vision is still for the appointed time;it pants toward the end, and it will not deceive.If it lingers, wait for it,for it will surely come; it will not delay.4Look, he is puffed up – his soul is not upright within him;but the righteous will live by his faithfulness.5And moreover, wine is treacherous;an arrogant man does not stay at home.He opens his throat wide like the grave;he is like death and is never sated.He gathers to himself all the nationsand assembles to himself all the peoples.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

While Habakkuk stands at his guard post (2:1), God's voice breaks through the silence once more. This time the LORD does not give a spoken answer for the prophet alone; he gives a lasting revelation that is to be written plainly on tablets for every generation that would come after. Here we stand on the theological summit of the whole book of Habakkuk. Verse 4b ("but the righteous will live by his faithfulness") is one of the most influential sentences in the whole Bible – the cornerstone on which the apostle Paul built his gospel in Romans and Galatians, and the spark that lit the Protestant Reformation.

In the ancient Near East, important royal decrees were set out in large, clear script on clay tablets or stone stelae and erected in public places. The phrase "so that whoever reads it may run" (v. 2) is doubly rich: the letters must be so large and clear that they can be read at a glance, and the messenger (herald) who has read them can at once run off to spread the good news of God's final victory throughout the land.

In verse 5 God uses a sharp image: "wine is treacherous". Daniel 5, with Belshazzar's feast, and later Greek historians tell of a feast on the night Babylon fell. Just as wine deceives you – it makes you feel invincible while in fact it makes you stumble – so Babylon's drunkenness with power and its greed, with a throat as insatiable as Sheol (the grave), sealed its own downfall. Notice the contrast too: the Hebrew word for "treacherous" (boged, 2:5; "the treacherous", 1:13) means acting treacherously, breaking faith – and so it stands over against the faithfulness of the righteous (2:4).

Insights from the Hebrew

  • Appointed time (mo'ed – מוֹעֵד): In verse 3 God says the vision is for the mo'ed – a specific, fixed time on God's calendar. History does not drift aimlessly; God has already set the exact hour at which evil will be stopped.
  • It pants toward the end (weyafeach laqqets – וְיָפֵחַ לַקֵּץ): Literally, "it pants toward the end". Like an athlete racing for the finishing line, breathing hard, God's promise moves unstoppably forward – even when to the human eye it seems to stand still.
  • Puffed up ('uppelah – עֻפְּלָה): In verse 4 the proud one (probably the Babylonian) is described as 'uppelah – puffed up, swollen. It is the picture of a swelling, of a false self-importance that refuses to bow before God.
  • Faithfulness / faith ('emunah – אֱמוּנָה): The Hebrew word in verse 4 is 'emunah, from the root 'aman (from which our word "Amen" comes). It means steadfastness, reliability, persevering loyalty and unshakeable trust. In Exodus 17:12 we read that Moses' hands remained 'emunah (firm and steady) during the battle. That is why our translation reads "by his faithfulness": it is a trust that holds on and stays faithful. The same root stands in 1:5, where God says: "that you would not believe if it were told" (lo ta'aminu – לֹא תַאֲמִינוּ, from he'emin, "believe"). In Hebrew, believing and being faithful belong to one family of words. The ancient Greek translation (the Septuagint) reads: "the righteous will live by my faithfulness" – God's faithfulness. Paul quotes the verse in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11 as "The righteous will live by faith", and Hebrews 10:38 reads: "but my righteous one will live by faith". The New Testament therefore reads 'emunah as faith – and the word holds that meaning too, because faithfulness in Habakkuk is trust that holds on: a faith that stays faithful. True faith in Habakkuk is not a quick mental assent; it is a lifelong, reliable clinging to God's promises in the middle of the fiery trial.

Core Theological Truths

  1. God's promises are delayed, but never cancelled: Verse 3 strikes a wonderful balance: "If it lingers, wait for it, for it will surely come". From our human point of view God's promises often seem delayed ("if it lingers"), but from God's point of view they are never late ("it will not delay"). The waiting period is not empty time; it is the school in which faith is formed.
  2. Two ways of living in a broken world: Verse 4 divides the whole of humanity into two camps. There is the person with the "puffed-up soul" who leans on his own pride, power and visible success – and he goes under. And there is the "righteous" one who knows he cannot save himself, and therefore lives by 'emunah (trusting faith in God).
  3. The threefold power of Habakkuk 2:4 in the New Testament: This single verse is so rich that the New Testament quotes it three times, each time with a different emphasis: - Romans 1:17 stresses "the righteous" – how does a person become righteous before God? Only through faith in Christ. - Galatians 3:11 stresses "will live" – how do we receive and keep spiritual life? Not by works of the law, but by faith. - Hebrews 10:38 stresses "by faith" – how does a believer survive and persevere when persecution and hardship go on and on? By steadfast, waiting trust ('emunah).
  4. Pride and greed are addictive and self-destructive: Just as death never says, "Now I have swallowed enough people" (v. 5), so earthly power, money and recognition can never satisfy the human soul. They promise freedom, but deliver only a deeper emptiness.

Living It Out

We live in a culture of instant gratification. If a web page takes more than three seconds to load, we get frustrated. So when God places us in a season of "waiting" – when the healing, the breakthrough, the restoring of justice or the answer to prayer drags on for months or years – we are tempted to think that God's promise has "deceived" us (v. 3).

Habakkuk 2:2–5 calls us back to the basic rhythm of the kingdom: "If it lingers, wait for it." But notice how we are to wait. We do not wait passively with folded hands; we live by faith while we wait. To live by 'emunah means that today – before your circumstances have changed by a single millimetre – you choose to act as if God's Word is true. While the world around you "puffs up" its soul with self-promotion, anxious control and an insatiable chase after more, the righteous rest in the knowledge that the Judge of the universe has already set the appointed time (mo'ed) for all injustice.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • In which area of your life are you feeling the tension of verse 3 right now: "If it lingers, wait for it"? How has this time of waiting affected your relationship with the Lord so far – has it driven you closer to him or left you discouraged?
  • God commands Habakkuk to write down the vision and make it plain on the tablets, so that it will not be forgotten in the dark days to come. Which specific promises from God's Word, or lessons from your past, do you need to "write down" today to carry you through your present storm?
  • Looking honestly at the contrast in verse 4: where in your daily decisions are you operating with a "puffed-up soul" (trust in your own control, pride and visible results) instead of simple, steadfast 'emunah (trust in God's character)?

Prayer

Faithful Father, God of the appointed time, I bow before you today with a heart that often grows weary of waiting. You know the questions I carry and the things I have been praying for so long. Thank you for the unshakeable promise in your Word that your vision will not deceive and that you are never too late.

Lord, heal my soul of every form of pride and self-sufficiency. Keep me from the treacherous wine of this world that makes me think more possessions, more recognition or more human control will ever satisfy me. Write your truth deep on the tablets of my heart today: that the righteous will live by faith.

Give me that Hebrew 'emunah – a steadfast, reliable and persevering trust in Jesus Christ, my Righteousness and my Life. I choose to wait for you, even when it lingers, because I know it will surely come. Amen.
