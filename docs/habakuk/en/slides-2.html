Yet I Will Rejoice · Session 2

Write Down the Vision

Habakkuk 2:1–20

To get started

What is the hardest thing for you to wait for in everyday life – a slow queue, a loading screen, a parcel, a reply to a message? What do you usually do while you wait?

Where we are in the story

Last time Habakkuk asked how a holy God can use the cruel Babylonians to punish Judah (1:12–17). Now he climbs his guard post to wait for God's answer (2:1). The LORD gives him a vision to be written plainly on tablets: it will surely come at the appointed time, and the righteous will live by his faithfulness (2:2–5). Then the peoples oppressed by Babylon take up a taunt-song of five “Woes” that lay bare the empire's greed, pride, bloodshed, cruelty and idolatry (2:6–20).

A look at the Hebrew

אֱמוּנָה

'emunah · “faithfulness”

2:4 reads “but the righteous will live by his faithfulness”. 'Emunah, from the root 'aman (behind our “Amen”), means steadfastness, reliability, persevering loyalty and unshakeable trust; in Exodus 17:12 Moses' hands stayed 'emunah – steady. The same root stands in 1:5 (“you would not believe”): in Hebrew, believing and being faithful are one family of words. Paul (Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11) and Hebrews 10:38 (“but my righteous one will live by faith”) read the word as faith – a lifelong, reliable clinging to God's promises.

A look at the Hebrew

כָּבוֹד

kavod · “glory”

The root of kavod means “to be heavy, weighty”; its opposite, qalon, “disgrace” (2:16), comes from “to be light”. Babylon is “sated with disgrace instead of glory”: its power will vanish like a light mist, while the true weight and majesty of God will fill the earth “as the waters cover the sea” (2:14).

A look at the Hebrew

הַס

has · “silence”

The chapter closes with the sound-word has! – like our “hush!” (2:20). The idols are mute; the living God in his holy temple brings the whole noisy, proud world to silence. Chapter 1 asked why God keeps silent (1:13); chapter 2 ends with Habakkuk himself falling silent in worship.

The big idea

While we wait at the guard post, God's promise is sure: the proud will not stand, but the righteous will live by his faithfulness – trusting God until the appointed time, when the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD.

Discussion · 1/7

“I will stand at my guard post and take my place on the rampart; I will watch to see what he will say to me.” What does your own “guard post” look like? Do you make room to be still and listen – or do you do all the talking?

Habakkuk 2:1

Discussion · 2/7

Habakkuk also waits to hear “what I shall answer concerning my reproof” – he is ready to be corrected. Why is it hard to let God correct our perspective when we are the ones who feel wronged?

Habakkuk 2:1

Discussion · 3/7

God tells Habakkuk: “Write down the vision and make it plain on the tablets, so that whoever reads it may run.” Which promises from God's Word, or lessons from your past, do you need to “write down” to carry you through dark days?

Habakkuk 2:2

Discussion · 4/7

“If it lingers, wait for it, for it will surely come; it will not delay.” How can a promise seem to linger and yet not be late? Has a time of waiting drawn you closer to God or left you discouraged?

Habakkuk 2:3

Discussion · 5/7

Verse 4 shows two ways of living: the one who is puffed up, whose soul is not upright, and the righteous who “will live by his faithfulness”. Romans 1:17 stresses how we become righteous, Galatians 3:11 how we receive life, Hebrews 10:38 how we persevere. Which of these speaks most to you right now – and where do you live with a “puffed-up soul” instead of steadfast trust?

Habakkuk 2:4–5

Discussion · 6/7

The five woes hold up a mirror: heaping up what is not your own (v. 6), setting your nest on high (v. 9), building with blood (v. 12), humiliating others (v. 15), trusting mute idols (vv. 18–19). Which of these temptations do you recognise most in our culture – or in yourself?

Habakkuk 2:6–19

Discussion · 7/7

Peoples “toil only for the fire”, but “the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea”. And then: “But the LORD is in his holy temple – silence before him, all the earth!” How do these verses change the way you look at the news, and at where your time and energy go?

Habakkuk 2:13–20

For the week ahead

  • Choose one promise of God and literally write it down this week – on a card, your phone's lock screen or the fridge – where you will read it every day while you wait.
  • Take five minutes of silence each day before God with 2:20 in front of you: bring your inner noise, anxiety and complaints to a standstill before you say anything.

Pray together

Begin with a minute of silence together, as 2:20 invites. Then pray in twos or threes for the things each of you is waiting for, asking for the steadfast trust of 2:4. End by praying 2:14 together for your town and for the nations: that the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD.
