Yet I Will Rejoice · Session 1

How Long, LORD?

Habakkuk 1:1–17

To get started

Think of a time you waited a long while for an answer – an exam result, a reply to an application, news from a doctor. What was the waiting like, and what did you do with it?

Where we are in the story

Habakkuk lived in Judah at the end of the seventh century BC, probably under King Jehoiakim (about 609–598 BC), after the godly King Josiah had died at Megiddo and the country slid back into corruption, violence and bribed courts. At the same time Babylon was rising: Nineveh fell in 612 BC and Egypt was beaten at Carchemish in 605 BC. Most prophets speak to the people for God; Habakkuk speaks to God for the people. Tonight we hear a conversation: his first complaint (1:2–4), God's shocking answer (1:5–11) and his second complaint (1:12–17).

A look at the Hebrew

חָמָס

chamas · “violence”

In 1:2 Habakkuk uses two verbs for prayer: shiwwa', the persistent cry for help of someone in distress, and za'aq, the scream of a victim under attack. And in that scream he utters one word: chamas – cruelty, bloodshed and injustice. The same word returns in 1:3 and 1:9: the violence he complains about in Judah is met by the violence of the Chaldeans.

A look at the Hebrew

וְהִתַּמְּהוּ תְּמָהוּ

wehittammehu tamahu · “be astonished – be astounded”

God doubles the verb tamah (1:5). It does not mean being mildly surprised but being paralysed with shock and disbelief. While Habakkuk thought God was doing nothing, God says: “For I am doing a work in your days that you would not believe if it were told.” Acts 13:41 quotes this verse as a warning not to overlook God's saving work in Christ.

A look at the Hebrew

צוּר

tsur · “Rock”

In 1:12 Habakkuk calls God “Rock” – the only time in his book. Before he asks his second “Why?”, he anchors himself in who God is: from of old, my God, my Holy One, Rock, with eyes too pure to see evil. While the Babylonians sweep over the earth like a storm, he holds on to the immovable rock face (compare Deuteronomy 32:4).

The big idea

God welcomes our honest lament: he is never absent when heaven feels silent, he rules even over the powers that frighten us, and when his answer confuses us we can hold on to who he is – our Rock from of old.

Discussion · 1/7

Habakkuk does not hide his frustration behind pious phrases: “How long, LORD, shall I cry for help, and you do not hear?” Is it allowed to speak to God like this? What is the difference between complaining about God to other people and bringing the complaint directly to God?

Habakkuk 1:2

Discussion · 2/7

“Therefore instruction grows numb, and justice never comes forth.” Where do you see instruction and justice “growing numb” in our society today – and where are you in danger of growing spiritually cold or numb yourself because you are tired of waiting?

Habakkuk 1:3–4

Discussion · 3/7

God's answer is not what Habakkuk expected: “For look, I am raising up the Chaldeans.” Has God ever answered a prayer of yours in a way that caught you off guard, something that first looked like a disaster but later turned out to shape or rescue you?

Habakkuk 1:5–6

Discussion · 4/7

The Chaldean is a law to himself – “from himself come forth his justice and his dignity” – and he is the one “whose strength is his god”. In which areas of daily life do we live as if our own strength, cleverness or control were enough?

Habakkuk 1:7–11

Discussion · 5/7

In his second complaint Habakkuk starts with what he knows about God before he asks what he does not understand: “Are you not from of old, LORD, my God, my Holy One? We will not die.” Which of these truths do you find hardest to hold on to in a deep disappointment? Why?

Habakkuk 1:12–13

Discussion · 6/7

The oppressor treats people “like the fish of the sea”, and “he sacrifices to his net and burns incense to his dragnet”, because it makes his life rich. Where do people today become mere numbers – and which “nets” (work, qualifications, savings, skills) receive the trust that belongs to God alone in our lives?

Habakkuk 1:14–17

Discussion · 7/7

Where in your life or in our world are you crying out right now, “How long?” Have you put that into words before God? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

For the week ahead

  • Write your own honest “How long?” prayer this week – no masks, no polished words. Then, like Habakkuk in 1:12, add underneath three things you know for certain about who God is.
  • Look at one “net” you rely on (your job, your savings, your skills). Each day this week thank God for it as a gift and tell him in a sentence that your security is in him, not in it.

Pray together

Begin with a few minutes of honest lament: let anyone who wishes name, in a sentence, a situation where they are asking “How long?” – in our world or in their own life. Then pray together through the names Habakkuk gives God in 1:12 (from of old, my God, my Holy One, Rock) and ask him to keep your hearts from growing numb. End by praying for people who suffer violence and injustice today.
