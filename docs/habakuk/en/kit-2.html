Small-group kit · Session 2 / 3

Write Down the Vision

Present ▶

The big ideaWhile we wait at the guard post, God's promise is sure: the proud will not stand, but the righteous will live by his faithfulness – trusting God until the appointed time, when the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

What is the hardest thing for you to wait for in everyday life – a slow queue, a loading screen, a parcel, a reply to a message? What do you usually do while you wait?

Read together · Habakkuk 2:1–20

One person reads Habakkuk's words at the guard post (2:1), another the LORD's answer (2:2–5); then five readers each take one woe (2:6–8, 9–11, 12–14, 15–17, 18–20), and the whole group reads 2:20 together.

Where we are in the story

Last time Habakkuk asked how a holy God can use the cruel Babylonians to punish Judah (1:12–17). Now he climbs his guard post to wait for God's answer (2:1). The LORD gives him a vision to be written plainly on tablets: it will surely come at the appointed time, and the righteous will live by his faithfulness (2:2–5). Then the peoples oppressed by Babylon take up a taunt-song of five “Woes” that lay bare the empire's greed, pride, bloodshed, cruelty and idolatry (2:6–20).

A look at the Hebrew

  • 'emunah – אֱמוּנָה “faithfulness”: 2:4 reads “but the righteous will live by his faithfulness”. 'Emunah, from the root 'aman (behind our “Amen”), means steadfastness, reliability, persevering loyalty and unshakeable trust; in Exodus 17:12 Moses' hands stayed 'emunah – steady. The same root stands in 1:5 (“you would not believe”): in Hebrew, believing and being faithful are one family of words. Paul (Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11) and Hebrews 10:38 (“but my righteous one will live by faith”) read the word as faith – a lifelong, reliable clinging to God's promises.
  • kavod – כָּבוֹד “glory”: The root of kavod means “to be heavy, weighty”; its opposite, qalon, “disgrace” (2:16), comes from “to be light”. Babylon is “sated with disgrace instead of glory”: its power will vanish like a light mist, while the true weight and majesty of God will fill the earth “as the waters cover the sea” (2:14).
  • has – הַס “silence”: The chapter closes with the sound-word has! – like our “hush!” (2:20). The idols are mute; the living God in his holy temple brings the whole noisy, proud world to silence. Chapter 1 asked why God keeps silent (1:13); chapter 2 ends with Habakkuk himself falling silent in worship.

Discussion

  1. “I will stand at my guard post and take my place on the rampart; I will watch to see what he will say to me.” What does your own “guard post” look like? Do you make room to be still and listen – or do you do all the talking? (Habakkuk 2:1)
  2. Habakkuk also waits to hear “what I shall answer concerning my reproof” – he is ready to be corrected. Why is it hard to let God correct our perspective when we are the ones who feel wronged? (Habakkuk 2:1)
  3. God tells Habakkuk: “Write down the vision and make it plain on the tablets, so that whoever reads it may run.” Which promises from God's Word, or lessons from your past, do you need to “write down” to carry you through dark days? (Habakkuk 2:2)
  4. “If it lingers, wait for it, for it will surely come; it will not delay.” How can a promise seem to linger and yet not be late? Has a time of waiting drawn you closer to God or left you discouraged? (Habakkuk 2:3)
  5. Verse 4 shows two ways of living: the one who is puffed up, whose soul is not upright, and the righteous who “will live by his faithfulness”. Romans 1:17 stresses how we become righteous, Galatians 3:11 how we receive life, Hebrews 10:38 how we persevere. Which of these speaks most to you right now – and where do you live with a “puffed-up soul” instead of steadfast trust? (Habakkuk 2:4–5)
  6. The five woes hold up a mirror: heaping up what is not your own (v. 6), setting your nest on high (v. 9), building with blood (v. 12), humiliating others (v. 15), trusting mute idols (vv. 18–19). Which of these temptations do you recognise most in our culture – or in yourself? (Habakkuk 2:6–19)
  7. Peoples “toil only for the fire”, but “the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea”. And then: “But the LORD is in his holy temple – silence before him, all the earth!” How do these verses change the way you look at the news, and at where your time and energy go? (Habakkuk 2:13–20)

For the week ahead

  • Choose one promise of God and literally write it down this week – on a card, your phone's lock screen or the fridge – where you will read it every day while you wait.
  • Take five minutes of silence each day before God with 2:20 in front of you: bring your inner noise, anxiety and complaints to a standstill before you say anything.

Pray together

Begin with a minute of silence together, as 2:20 invites. Then pray in twos or threes for the things each of you is waiting for, asking for the steadfast trust of 2:4. End by praying 2:14 together for your town and for the nations: that the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD.

Notes for the leader

  • Begin with a short look back at session 1 and at 2:1: Habakkuk has poured out his complaint and now waits at his guard post. Day 3 ends here; days 4 and 5 cover the rest of the chapter.
  • Our translation of 2:4 reads “by his faithfulness”, following the Hebrew, while the New Testament quotes the verse as “by faith”. Don't let this turn into a debate of faith against faithfulness. Follow day 4: 'emunah is faithful, steadfast trust – a faith that holds on. It is not a work by which we earn righteousness; the righteous one knows he cannot save himself and therefore lives by trust in God.
  • Waiting is a tender topic. Some in the group may have waited years for healing, a child, a job or justice. Do not promise a timeline; the promise of 2:3 is that God's appointed time is sure, not that it is soon.
  • The woes are easy to aim only at corrupt leaders. Let that happen briefly, then turn to the mirror question, as day 5 does. The image of 2:15–16 (drunkenness, nakedness) is about humiliating others; explain it simply and move on.
  • Five readers for the woes keep the long chapter lively. If time is short, spend most of the discussion on 2:1–4 and choose one or two woes.

Scripture Habakkuk 2:1–20

1I will stand at my guard postand take my place on the rampart;I will watch to see what he will say to meand what I shall answer concerning my reproof.

2Then the LORD answered me and said:

Write down the visionand make it plain on the tablets,so that whoever reads it may run.3For the vision is still for the appointed time;it pants toward the end, and it will not deceive.If it lingers, wait for it,for it will surely come; it will not delay.4Look, he is puffed up – his soul is not upright within him;but the righteous will live by his faithfulness.5And moreover, wine is treacherous;an arrogant man does not stay at home.He opens his throat wide like the grave;he is like death and is never sated.He gathers to himself all the nationsand assembles to himself all the peoples.6Will not all of these take up a taunt against him,a mocking song, riddles about him, and say:Woe to the one who heaps up what is not his own –how long? –and loads himself heavy with pledges!7Will not those who bite you rise up suddenly,and those who make you tremble wake up,and you will become plunder for them?8Because you have plundered many nations,all who are left of the peoples will plunder you,because of human blood and violence to the land,to the city and all who live in it.9Woe to the one who gains evil gain for his house,to set his nest on high,to be snatched from the grip of evil!10You have counselled shame for your houseby cutting off many peoples,and you are sinning against your own life.11For a stone from the wall will cry out,and a rafter from the woodwork will answer it.12Woe to the one who builds a town with bloodand founds a city on injustice!13Look, is it not from the LORD of hoststhat peoples toil only for the fireand nations weary themselves for nothing?14For the earth will be filledwith the knowledge of the glory of the LORD,as the waters cover the sea.15Woe to the one who makes his neighbour drink,mixing in your wrath and making him drunk as well,in order to look on their nakedness!16You are sated with disgrace instead of glory.Drink, you too, and show your foreskin!The cup in the LORD's right hand will come around to you,and utter disgrace will come upon your glory.17For the violence done to Lebanon will cover you,and the destruction of the beasts that terrified them,because of human blood and violence to the land,to the city and all who live in it.18What use is a carved image,that its maker has carved it,a cast image and a teacher of lies?For its maker trusts in what he himself has formedwhen he makes mute idols.19Woe to the one who says to wood, "Wake up!""Awake!" to a silent stone.Can it teach?Look, it is overlaid with gold and silver,and there is no breath at all inside it.20But the LORD is in his holy temple –silence before him, all the earth!
