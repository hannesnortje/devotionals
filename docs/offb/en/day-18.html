The Accuser Is Thrown Down: The Christmas Story from Behind the Curtain

Scripture Revelation 12:1–18

1And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars. 2And she was pregnant, and she cries out in labour and in torment to give birth.

3And another sign appeared in heaven, and look, a great fiery red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns, and on its heads seven diadems. 4And its tail sweeps away a third of the stars of heaven, and it threw them to the earth. And the dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she gave birth it might devour her child. 5And she gave birth to a son, a male child, who is to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod. And her child was snatched up to God and to his throne. 6And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, so that there she might be fed for a thousand two hundred and sixty days.

7And war broke out in heaven: Michael and his angels made war against the dragon. And the dragon made war, and its angels, 8but it was not strong enough, and no place was found for them in heaven any longer. 9And the great dragon was thrown down, the ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world – he was thrown down to the earth, and his angels were thrown down with him.

10And I heard a loud voice in heaven saying:

Now have come the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our Godand the authority of his Christ,for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down,who accuses them before our God day and night.11And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamband by the word of their testimony,and they did not love their life even to death.12Therefore rejoice, you heavens,and you whose tent is in them!Woe to the earth and the sea,for the devil has come down to youwith great fury,because he knows that he has only a little time.

13And when the dragon saw that it had been thrown down to the earth, it pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child. 14And the two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman, so that she might fly into the wilderness to her place, where she is fed for a time and times and half a time, away from the face of the serpent. 15And the serpent spewed water like a river out of its mouth after the woman, so that the river would sweep her away. 16And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had spewed out of its mouth. 17And the dragon was angry with the woman and went off to make war against the rest of her offspring, those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus. 18And it took its stand on the sand of the sea.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday the seventh trumpet sounded: "The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and his Christ's". Today John draws the curtain back further and shows how it came about. "And a great sign appeared in heaven": a woman clothed with the sun, pregnant and in labour, and before her a great fiery red dragon, waiting to devour her child.

This is the Christmas story, but told from behind the curtain. On earth it looked like an ordinary birth in Bethlehem and a cruel king who had the children killed (Matthew 2:16). Behind the curtain it was the old conflict of Genesis 3:15, where God tells the serpent that there will be enmity between its offspring and the offspring of the woman. John calls the dragon by name: "the ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan" (v. 9). Its seven heads and ten horns come from the beasts of Daniel 7; the diadems are the signs of earthly rule. Anyone in Smyrna or Pergamum who heard these words understood: behind Rome's power stands an older and darker power.

The woman with the sun, the moon and twelve stars recalls Joseph's dream, in which the sun, moon and stars are Jacob and his family (Genesis 37:9) – Israel, the people from whom the Messiah is born. Her labour pains are those of Zion in Micah 4:9–10. But after the birth she is also the mother of "the rest of her offspring, those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus" (v. 17): the church. It is one people of God, from Abraham until today. The child is "to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod" (Psalm 2:9), and then he is "snatched up to God and to his throne". The whole life of Jesus, from the manger to the ascension, stands in a single sentence.

Then the woman flees into the wilderness, "where she has a place prepared by God". Here we hear the exodus. After Egypt Israel was led through the wilderness and fed with manna, and at Sinai God said: I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself (Exodus 19:4). Now the woman is given "the two wings of the great eagle". The church is on an exodus through this age – not out of creation, for "the earth helped the woman" (v. 16), but out of the dragon's power, on the way to the new creation.

Insights from the Greek

  • The accuser (ho katēgōr – ὁ κατήγωρ, v. 10): A word from the courtroom. It is exactly what the Hebrew hassatan means in Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3: "the adversary", the accuser in God's heavenly council. In Job 1:9 he asks: Is it for nothing that Job fears God? And the participle ho katēgorōn (ὁ κατηγορῶν) shows that he does it constantly: he "accuses them before our God day and night."
  • Thrown down (eblēthē – ἐβλήθη, vv. 9–13): Five times in five verses it rings out: thrown down, thrown down. The passive points to God as the one who does it. The dragon "was not strong enough, and no place was found for them in heaven any longer."
  • By the blood (dia to haima – διὰ τὸ αἷμα, v. 11): With the accusative, dia really means: on the basis of, because of. Believers do not conquer in their own strength; they conquer because the blood of the Lamb has already won.
  • A little time (oligon kairon – ὀλίγον καιρὸν, v. 12): The devil comes down to earth "with great fury, because he knows that he has only a little time." His fury is not the fury of a winner, but of a defeated enemy.

Core Theological Truths

  1. The cross was the decisive battle – in heaven too. Michael's war (Daniel 12:1) is the heavenly side of Good Friday and Easter. The song does not say that the dragon was defeated by angelic strength, but "by the blood of the Lamb". Jesus said it himself when his cross was near: now the ruler of this world will be driven out (John 12:31). In Job the accuser could still appear before God; after the cross he has no place there any longer.

  2. The accusation is answered by the blood, not by our record. In Zechariah 3 the high priest Joshua stands in filthy clothes before the angel of the LORD, and Satan stands at his right hand to accuse him. Joshua says nothing in his own defence. The LORD rebukes the accuser, and Joshua is given clean clothes: See, I have taken your guilt away from you (Zechariah 3:4). That is the gospel in a single scene: who will bring a charge against God's chosen ones? It is Christ who intercedes for us (Romans 8:33–34).

  3. The church is on its exodus: kept, fed, but in the wilderness. The thousand two hundred and sixty days are the same time as the witness of the two witnesses yesterday – the whole time between Christ's ascension and his return. The woman is not taken out of the wilderness but fed in it. God's people are not spared hardship, but they are carried through it.

  4. The dragon's fury is the sign of his defeat. He can no longer reach the child, so he fights against the woman's offspring. That explains the persecution of the churches in Asia – and of believers today. Yet the chapter ends with an almost comical picture: "And it took its stand on the sand of the sea." There it waits for helpers. Tomorrow we shall see who comes up out of the sea.

Living It Out

Many believers know the accuser's voice well. It whispers in the night: You are a hypocrite. Look at what you said again. God gave up on you long ago. The accuser often uses the truth – we really have sinned – but he uses it to drive us away from God, not towards him. That is the difference between the conviction the Spirit works, which leads to the cross, and Satan's accusation, which ends in despair.

Revelation 12 gives you the answer Joshua received in Zechariah 3. You do not have to conduct your own defence or polish your record. The accuser has been thrown down – not because you have become better, but by the blood of the Lamb. And the second weapon is "the word of their testimony": saying out loud what God has done.

The picture of the wilderness is a comfort too. Perhaps you are in a dry season – illness, loss, loneliness, a church that is struggling. The wilderness is not proof that God has abandoned you. It is the place he has "prepared", where he feeds you and carries you on eagles' wings.

Today, write down one accusation you hear again and again in your head. Next to it write: "for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down" (v. 10). Read it aloud when the voice comes back.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Which accusation do you hear most often in your own thoughts, and do you listen to it as though it were the last word (v. 10)?
  • Do you try to silence the accuser with your own good works, or do you take refuge in the blood of the Lamb (v. 11)?
  • Where are you in the wilderness at the moment, and where, if you look closely, can you see that God is feeding you there (vv. 6, 14)?
  • What would it mean for you not to love your life "even to death" (v. 11)?

Prayer

You are King, Lord God, and your Christ has the authority. I praise you that the accuser of your children has been thrown down and has no place before you any longer. Your Son was born, he died for me, and he sits on your throne.

You know the voices that accuse me. I admit that much of what they say is true: I have sinned, and my clothes are not clean. But I will no longer listen to the accuser as though he had the last word. I take refuge in the blood of the Lamb.

Thank you that you feed me in the wilderness and carry me on eagles' wings, on the way to the new creation. Give me courage to speak the word of my testimony, and not to love my life more than I love you. In Jesus' name, Amen.
