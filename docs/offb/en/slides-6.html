Behind the Curtain · Session 6

Heaven Is Listening

Revelation 8:1 – 9:21

To get started

Where do you find real silence in your week – and do you enjoy it, or does it make you uneasy?

Where we are in the story

Heaven has been full of songs: angels, elders, living creatures and a countless crowd. Now the Lamb opens the seventh seal – and there is silence. An angel offers incense with the prayers of all the saints, throws fire from the altar on the earth, and seven trumpets sound (chapters 8–9). The images come from the exodus: hail, blood, darkness and locusts, as in Egypt. For the small churches in Asia, under the power of Rome, this said: God still acts against every empire that behaves like Egypt.

A look at the Greek

σιγὴ

sigē · “silence”

An ordinary word for silence (8:1). It is not an empty silence, but the silence of a courtroom before the judge speaks – and of a Father who is listening. The prophets knew it as the silence before God acts (Habakkuk 2:20; Zephaniah 1:7).

A look at the Greek

τὸ τρίτον

to triton · “a third”

Twelve times in six verses (8:7–12). The seals struck a fourth (6:8), the trumpets a third, the bowls later everything. The judgements are limited. They are warnings, not yet the end.

A look at the Greek

οὐ μετενόησαν

ou metenoēsan · “they did not repent”

*Metanoeō* is a turning round of mind and life (9:20–21). It is the same verb Jesus used five times for the churches (2:5, 16, 21; 3:3, 19): the call to repent is addressed first to the church, then to the world.

The big idea

Heaven falls silent to hear the prayers of the saints, and those prayers come back to earth as God's action; the trumpets, like the plagues of Egypt, are limited warnings that call the world to repent – but judgement alone does not change the heart, and what the plagues cannot do, the Lamb does through his witnesses.

Discussion · 1/7

“There was silence in heaven for about half an hour.” Read Habakkuk 2:20 and Zephaniah 1:7. What kind of silence is this? When did you last become truly still before God, without speaking?

Revelation 8:1

Discussion · 2/7

The angel stands at the golden altar of incense, right in front of the curtain (Exodus 30:1–8; Luke 1:9–10), and the incense rises “with the prayers of the saints” before God. What does this picture say to small churches whose prayers no one in Rome heard – and to you, if you have given up on a prayer?

Revelation 8:3–4

Discussion · 3/7

The angel fills the censer “with the fire of the altar” and throws it on the earth – the same altar under which the souls cried “How long?” (6:9–10). Then the trumpets begin. Do you believe that your prayers are part of how God acts in the world? How would that change your intercession?

Revelation 8:5–6

Discussion · 4/7

Compare the first, second and fourth trumpets with the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 9:23–25; 7:20–21; 10:21–23). What does the exodus tell us about the purpose of the trumpets – for Rome, and for every power that oppresses God's people? What is God aiming at: the destruction of creation, or the liberation of his people?

Revelation 8:7–12

Discussion · 5/7

The locusts may harm “only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads”. The key, the authority and the five months – all are given. Their king Apollyon (“Destroyer”) is perhaps a dig at Apollo. What comfort did this give the churches in Asia? Where do you need to hear that evil is on a leash?

Revelation 9:1–11

Discussion · 6/7

“And the rest of humankind … did not even repent of the works of their hands”; like Pharaoh, they cling to idols “which can neither see nor hear nor walk” (Psalm 115:4–8). Which crisis in your life turned you to God – and which made you harder instead? What are the “works of your hands” that you trust more than God?

Revelation 9:20–21

Discussion · 7/7

In Israel the trumpet was the watchman's alarm (Ezekiel 33:3–6) – a God who warns has not yet given up on the world. How do you respond to news of disasters – with fear, with speculation, or with prayer and repentance? Do you hear the call to repent as a threat or as an invitation?

Revelation 8:13

For the week ahead

  • Sit in silence for five minutes before you pray, every day this week. Then write down one injustice you have long been praying about, and bring it before God again – knowing that heaven is listening.
  • Take ten minutes and ask yourself honestly: which “work of my hands” has taken my heart captive? Name it before God, and take one small step away from it – and towards him.

Pray together

Begin with a full minute of silence together – no words, only remembering who sits on the throne. Then let each person pray one sentence for a situation they have long prayed about, or for persecuted believers. Close with Jesus' words to the churches (3:20), and thank him that he always lives to intercede for us (Hebrews 7:25).
