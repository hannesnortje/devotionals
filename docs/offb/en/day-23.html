Behind the Curtain · Day 23 / 31

The Prostitute on the Beast: Why Are You Amazed?

Scripture Revelation 17:1–18

1And one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls came and spoke with me, saying, "Come, I will show you the judgement of the great prostitute who sits on many waters, 2with whom the kings of the earth committed harlotry, and those who live on the earth became drunk with the wine of her harlotry." 3And he carried me away in the Spirit into a wilderness. And I saw a woman sitting on a scarlet beast that was full of names of blasphemy and had seven heads and ten horns. 4And the woman was dressed in purple and scarlet and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearls; she had in her hand a golden cup full of abominations and the impurities of her harlotry, 5and on her forehead a name was written, a mystery: "Babylon the Great, the mother of prostitutes and of the abominations of the earth." 6And I saw the woman drunk with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the witnesses to Jesus. And when I saw her, I was greatly amazed.

7And the angel said to me, "Why are you amazed? I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast that carries her, which has the seven heads and the ten horns. 8The beast that you saw was, and is not, and is about to come up out of the abyss and go to destruction. And those who live on the earth, whose name has not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, will be amazed when they see the beast, because it was and is not and will be present. 9Here is the understanding that has wisdom: the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits. They are also seven kings: 10five have fallen, one is, the other has not yet come; and when he comes, he must remain only a little while. 11And the beast that was and is not is itself an eighth, and it belongs to the seven, and it goes to destruction. 12And the ten horns that you saw are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom, but they receive authority as kings for one hour together with the beast. 13These have one purpose, and they give their power and authority to the beast. 14These will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful." 15And he says to me, "The waters that you saw, where the prostitute sits, are peoples and crowds and nations and languages. 16And the ten horns that you saw, and the beast, these will hate the prostitute; they will make her devastated and naked, and they will eat her flesh and burn her up with fire. 17For God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose, to carry out one purpose, and to give their kingdom to the beast, until the words of God are fulfilled. 18And the woman that you saw is the great city that has a kingdom over the kings of the earth."

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

One of the seven angels with the bowls takes John aside: "Come, I will show you the judgement of the great prostitute who sits on many waters." At the seventh bowl God "remembered Babylon the Great" (16:19). Now the angel wants to show who she is.

First John hears: a great prostitute who sits on many waters, with whom "the kings of the earth committed harlotry". Ancient Babylon really did sit on many waters, on the Euphrates with its canals; Jeremiah addresses her in just that way (Jeremiah 51:13). But then the angel "carried me away in the Spirit into a wilderness", and John sees: "a woman sitting on a scarlet beast". As so often in Revelation, the seeing interprets the hearing. The city that sounds so mighty actually sits in a wilderness, and her throne is a beast – the same beast with seven heads and ten horns that we saw coming up out of the sea in chapter 13. And at the end of the chapter that very beast will tear her apart.

For the first readers the picture was not hard to recognise. The angel himself says: "the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits", and "the woman that you saw is the great city that has a kingdom over the kings of the earth." Rome was the well-known city on seven hills, and on a coin from the time of the emperor Vespasian the goddess Roma sits on the seven hills. She is "dressed in purple and scarlet and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearls", with a golden cup in her hand – the image of Jeremiah 51:7, where Babylon was a golden cup that made the whole earth drunk. But the cup is "full of abominations", and she is "drunk with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the witnesses to Jesus".

Yet Babylon is more than Rome. In the great story of the Bible she begins at Babel, where people wanted to build a tower reaching to heaven to make a name for themselves (Genesis 11:4). She destroyed Jerusalem and the temple and carried God's people into exile. The prophets also called Tyre, the trading city, a prostitute (Isaiah 23:17), and Nineveh "lovely and charming, mistress of sorceries" (Nahum 3:4). Babylon is every power in every age that seduces people with luxury and makes them forget to whom they belong.

Insights from the Greek

  • Prostitute / harlotry (tēs pornēs / tēs porneias – τῆς πόρνης / τῆς πορνείας, vv. 1–2): From pornē and porneia. In the prophets harlotry is the picture of unfaithfulness to God: giving your love and trust to other gods and powers (Hosea 2; Ezekiel 16). Here it is about the kings and nations who have bound themselves to Rome's power and wealth.
  • I was greatly amazed (ethaumasa ... thauma mega – ἐθαύμασα ... θαῦμα μέγα, v. 6): Literally, "I wondered with a great wonder." Even John is impressed. The angel asks: "Why are you amazed?" The same verb describes, in verse 8, those who live on the earth, who are amazed when they see the beast (compare 13:3). The danger of Babylon is not that she is repulsive, but that she is so impressive.
  • A mystery (mystērion – μυστήριον, v. 5): On her forehead is written "Babylon the Great, the mother of prostitutes". Over against her stand God's people with the name of the Lamb and of his Father on their foreheads (14:1).
  • Was, and is not, and will be present (ēn kai ouk estin kai parestai – ἦν καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν καὶ παρέσται, v. 8): A deliberate imitation of God's name, "who is and who was and who is coming" (1:4). The beast wants to be like God, but its "was" is only past, and its end is "destruction". (Many interpreters think here of the rumour that Nero would return; the seven kings of verses 9–11 are hard to match with particular emperors, and seven is probably the number of completeness.)

Core Theological Truths

  1. There are two women and two cities, and no neutral ground. The same angel who leads John to the prostitute here will later say to him: "Come, I will show you the bride, the wife of the Lamb" (21:9). Once he is carried away into a wilderness, the other time "to a great, high mountain" (21:10). The prostitute wears purple and gold; the bride is clothed "in fine linen, shining and clean" (19:8). And the woman of chapter 12, God's people, also fled into the wilderness – but there she is fed by God (12:6), while the prostitute gets drunk on blood. Revelation does not ask who the others are, but: to which woman do you belong?

  2. Babylon's splendour sits on a beast. Her wealth and glamour rest on violence, blasphemy and the blood of the witnesses. What looks like a royal throne is a beast that will in the end hate her and lay her waste (v. 16). In the end evil destroys its own allies too.

  3. God is in control, even of the evil that destroys itself. "For God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose … until the words of God are fulfilled." Even the struggle between the beast and the prostitute serves God's plan, and their authority lasts only "for one hour".

  4. The Lamb conquers, and his people are with him. "The Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings" – the title of God in Deuteronomy 10:17 and Daniel 2:47. And those who are with him are not described as powerful or rich, but as "called and chosen and faithful".

Living It Out

The most honest verse in the chapter may be verse 6: John was greatly amazed. If even the apostle could be impressed by Babylon, so can we. She does not appear as a monster but as a queen in purple with a golden cup. She does not whisper "deny your faith", but "you deserve better" and "everyone does it". She promises security, status and pleasure.

This is where the pattern of hearing and seeing helps. Babylon sounds impressive – many waters, kings, riches. But when God lets you look in the Spirit, you see the wilderness she sits in and the beast she rides. Look behind the glamour: what does it cost, and who pays for it? The angel's question – "Why are you amazed?" – is not a rebuke; he wants to open your eyes.

This does not mean that we should become suspicious of everything beautiful or successful, or point the finger at other people as "Babylon". The question is personal: what impresses me so much that it begins to draw my heart away from the Lamb? For one person it is money, for another recognition or a lifestyle everyone admires.

And then the good news: the Lamb conquers, and those with him are called, chosen and faithful. You do not become faithful by your own willpower; you have been called and chosen by the one who will not let you go.

This week, notice when you are "amazed" – when an advertisement, someone's life on social media or an offer at work stirs up longing in you. Then ask with the angel: why am I amazed? And thank God for one thing the Lamb has given you that Babylon can never give.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • What impresses you at the moment so much that, like John, you are amazed by it (v. 6)?
  • If you look behind the glamour of that thing, what "beast" might you see it riding on (v. 3)?
  • Whose name would be written on your forehead if your choices of the past week were the writing (v. 5; 14:1)?
  • What does it mean to you that those with the Lamb are called "called and chosen and faithful" (v. 14)?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Lamb of God, you are Lord of lords and King of kings. The powers of this world look great and glamorous, but they sit on a beast that goes to destruction. You alone conquer, and your kingdom remains for ever.

Forgive me for being so easily impressed by what glitters, and for sometimes drinking from Babylon's cup in my heart. Forgive me for the times I have given my love and my trust to things that cannot save me.

Open my eyes to see behind the glamour. Thank you that I am called and chosen, and that I belong to you. Keep me with you, so that I may stay faithful and one day be part of your bride, clothed in fine linen, shining and clean. Amen.
