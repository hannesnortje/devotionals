Small-group kit · Session 7 / 11

Sweet and Bitter

Present ▶

The big ideaGod's answer to a world that will not repent is his word in the mouth of his witnesses: the church eats the word, sweet and bitter, is measured and kept by God, bears witness in sackcloth, shares its Lord's death and resurrection – and the kingdom of the world has already become our Lord's and his Christ's.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of a food that you hated as a child but love now (or the other way round). What changed?

Read together · Revelation 10:1 – 11:19

Chapter 10: one narrator, one reader for the voice from heaven and the angel (10:4, 8, 9, 11). Chapter 11: one narrator; at 11:12 the whole group says “Come up here!”; everyone reads the songs of 11:15 and 11:17–18 together.

Where we are in the story

After the sixth trumpet we expect the seventh at once. But as between the sixth and the seventh seal, the story pauses. In chapter 7 God's people were sealed; in chapters 10 and 11 they are sent. John eats a little scroll, sweet in the mouth and bitter in the stomach, and must prophesy again; then he measures the temple and sees two witnesses who prophesy, are killed and stand up again. For the churches in Asia, living in Rome's cities, this was their calling: not to withdraw, but to bear witness.

A look at the Greek

  • chronos ouketi estai – χρόνος οὐκέτι ἔσται “there will be no more delay”: *Chronos* usually means “time”, and older translations read as if time itself would stop. But the oath echoes Daniel 12:7 and answers the “How long?” of 6:10: God will wait no longer (10:6).
  • kataphage – κατάφαγε “eat it up”: *Kataphagein* does not mean to taste but to eat up completely (10:9). The word must go into the prophet before it can come out of him – as with Ezekiel, for whom the scroll was sweet as honey and then bitter (Ezekiel 2:8–3:3, 14).
  • martysin – μάρτυσίν “witnesses”: We translate “witness”, not “martyr” (11:3). But this chapter shows how the two words grew together in the early church: whoever bears faithful witness sometimes pays the price – like Antipas (2:13), and like Jesus himself, “the faithful witness” (1:5).

Discussion

  1. Wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow over his head, the angel sets one foot on the sea and the other on the earth and swears by the one “who created heaven and what is in it, and the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it”. What do these images say about whose world this is? (Revelation 10:1–6)
  2. John is told: “Seal up what the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write it down!” What does this say to people who use Revelation as a puzzle for dates and speculation? Which questions about the future make you restless – and can you leave them with God? (Revelation 10:4)
  3. The souls under the altar were told “to rest a little longer” (6:11). Now the angel swears “that there will be no more delay”. Read Habakkuk 2:3. Where have you long been waiting for God, and what does this oath say to you? (Revelation 10:5–7)
  4. Read Ezekiel 3:1–3 and 3:14 alongside Revelation 10:9–10: “Take it and eat it up; it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey.” Do you read the Bible more like someone who tastes or like someone who eats? Which part of God's word is sweet to you at the moment, and which part bitter? (Revelation 10:8–11)
  5. John measures “the temple of God and the altar and those who worship in it”, but the outer court is trampled for forty-two months. In the New Testament the church is God's temple (1 Corinthians 3:16). Do you sometimes feel like the court that is trampled? What does it mean to you that God has measured the inner part? (Revelation 11:1–2)
  6. “These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands” – and the lampstands are the churches (1:20). Their signs are those of Elijah and Moses, and they stand not by might nor by power, but by God's Spirit (Zechariah 4:6). Where are you tempted to stay silent because your witness would make you unpopular? (Revelation 11:3–10)
  7. The witnesses are killed and raised, and – unlike after the plagues (9:20–21) – the rest give glory to the God of heaven – through the testimony. The seventh trumpet sings: “The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and his Christ's”. Where would it make a difference today if you truly believed that? (Revelation 11:11–19)

For the week ahead

  • Choose one short passage – Revelation 10:8–11, for example – and read it slowly and aloud every day for three days. “Chew” on it. Then share one sentence from it with someone who needs it.
  • Think of one person around you who does not yet know Christ. Pray for him or her by name every day this week, and ask God for one opportunity to share something of your hope – gently, not fearfully.

Pray together

Read 11:15 aloud together. Then pray in pairs: thank God that the kingdom of the world belongs to him and to his Christ; ask him for hunger for his word and courage when witness costs something; pray by name for the person each of you thought of. Close together with Zechariah 4:6: not by might nor by power, but by your Spirit.

Notes for the leader

  • Two devotionals (days 16–17). Chapter 11 is one of the hardest in the book; don't let the group get lost in who the two witnesses are. Our reading: they are the witnessing church (the lampstands, 1:20), shown with the features of Moses and Elijah, and two because the law requires two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15).
  • Other readings: futurist interpreters take the forty-two months as a literal three and a half years at the end of history, with two individual prophets – according to some Moses and Elijah themselves. Name this fairly in a sentence. In this series, the forty-two months / 1,260 days are Daniel's “a time and times and half a time” (Daniel 7:25; 12:7): the measured, limited time of the church's witness between Christ's first and second coming. Don't calculate.
  • Some members may know the older rendering of 10:6 (“that there should be time no longer”). The context is about God's waiting, not about the end of the calendar.
  • The angel of 10:1 has features of Christ (1:16); some think he is Christ himself, but John calls him an angel. Don't spend long on it.
  • Handle with care: some in the group carry the pain of witness that seems in vain – a shrinking congregation, a child far from God, being the only honest one at work. Don't promise success; the chapter promises vindication (“Come up here!”), not popularity.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 12 tells the Christmas story from behind the curtain – and the accuser is thrown down.

Scripture Revelation 10:1 – 11:19

1And I saw another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, and the rainbow was over his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire, 2and he had in his hand a little scroll, opened. And he set his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the earth, 3and he cried out with a loud voice, as a lion roars. And when he cried out, the seven thunders spoke with their own voices. 4And when the seven thunders had spoken, I was about to write; and I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Seal up what the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write it down!"

5And the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and on the earth raised his right hand to heaven 6and swore by the one who lives for ever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, and the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it, that there will be no more delay, 7but that in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he is about to blow the trumpet, the mystery of God is fulfilled, as he announced the good news to his servants the prophets.

8And the voice that I had heard from heaven spoke with me again and said, "Go, take the scroll that lies open in the hand of the angel who is standing on the sea and on the earth." 9And I went to the angel and told him to give me the little scroll. And he says to me, "Take it and eat it up; it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey." 10And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it up, and it was sweet as honey in my mouth; and when I had eaten it, my stomach was made bitter. 11And they say to me, "You must prophesy again about many peoples and nations and languages and kings."

1And a reed like a staff was given to me, and I was told, "Get up and measure the temple of God and the altar and those who worship in it. 2But leave out the court outside the temple, and do not measure it, for it has been given to the nations, and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months. 3And I will grant my two witnesses that they prophesy for a thousand two hundred and sixty days, clothed in sackcloth."

4These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth. 5And if anyone wants to harm them, fire comes out of their mouth and devours their enemies; and if anyone should want to harm them, this is how he must be killed. 6They have the authority to shut the sky, so that no rain falls during the days of their prophecy; and they have authority over the waters, to turn them into blood and to strike the earth with every plague, as often as they want.

7And when they have finished their testimony, the beast that comes up from the abyss will make war against them and conquer them and kill them. 8And their corpse lies in the street of the great city, which is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord too was crucified. 9And people from the peoples and tribes and languages and nations look at their corpse for three and a half days, and they do not allow their corpses to be laid in a tomb. 10And those who live on the earth are glad over them and rejoice, and they will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who live on the earth.

11And after the three and a half days a breath of life from God entered them, and they stood on their feet, and great fear fell on those who saw them. 12And they heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them, "Come up here!" And they went up to heaven in the cloud, and their enemies watched them. 13And in that hour there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell, and seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake; and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven.

14The second woe has passed; look, the third woe is coming soon.

15And the seventh angel blew the trumpet; and there were loud voices in heaven, saying:

The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and his Christ's,and he will reign for ever and ever.

16And the twenty-four elders who sit on their thrones before God fell on their faces and worshipped God, 17saying:

We give you thanks, Lord God, the Almighty,who is and who was,because you have taken your great powerand begun to reign.18And the nations were angry,and your wrath has come,and the time for the dead to be judged,and to give the reward to your servants the prophetsand to the saints and to those who fear your name,the small and the great,and to destroy those who destroy the earth.

19And God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen in his temple; and there were lightning and sounds and thunder and an earthquake and great hail.
