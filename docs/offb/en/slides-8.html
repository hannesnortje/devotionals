Behind the Curtain · Session 8

Whose Are You?

Revelation 12:1 – 13:18

To get started

Have you ever been fooled by a fake – a counterfeit product, a forged signature, a scam message? How did you find out?

Where we are in the story

The seventh trumpet has sounded: the kingdom of the world has become our Lord's. Now John draws the curtain back further and shows how it came about. Chapter 12 tells the Christmas story from behind the curtain: a woman, a child, a dragon – and the accuser thrown down. In chapter 13 the dragon calls up two beasts, from the sea and from the earth. For the churches in Asia the beast had a face: Rome, the imperial cult and the guilds, where you could not buy or sell without offering incense to the emperor.

A look at the Greek

ὁ κατήγωρ

ho katēgōr · “the accuser”

A word from the courtroom (12:10) – exactly what the Hebrew *hassatan* means in Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3: the adversary, the accuser in God's heavenly council. He accuses them “before our God day and night” – but now he has been thrown down.

A look at the Greek

ὡς ἐσφαγμένην

hōs esphagmenēn · “seemed slaughtered”

The same expression as for the Lamb in 5:6 (*hōs esphagmenon*). John wants us to notice the likeness – and the difference (13:3). The Lamb's wounds are for others; the beast's healing is for itself. Evil creates nothing new. It imitates.

A look at the Greek

χάραγμα

charagma · “mark”

A stamp or impression – also the emperor's stamp on coins and documents (13:16–17). Every coin in the hand of a trader in Thyatira bore the emperor's image and titles. The mark is “the name of the beast or the number of its name”: it says whom you belong to.

The big idea

Behind the curtain the dragon has already been thrown down by the blood of the Lamb; in his fury he works through beasts that imitate the Lamb and demand worship – but God's people, sealed before the beast ever appeared, conquer by the blood of the Lamb, the word of their testimony and endurance, for the question is not “when?” but “whose are you?”.

Discussion · 1/7

Read Genesis 3:15 and 37:9: what do they tell us about the woman, the child and the dragon? The child “is to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod” (Psalm 2:9) and is “snatched up to God and to his throne” – the whole life of Jesus in one sentence. What is different when we see Bethlehem from behind the curtain?

Revelation 12:1–6

Discussion · 2/7

Read Zechariah 3:1–5 and Job 1:9–11: the accuser could still stand before God. Now “the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down”. Which accusation do you hear most often in your own thoughts? How does the Spirit's conviction, which leads to the cross, differ from the accuser's voice, which ends in despair?

Revelation 12:7–10

Discussion · 3/7

They conquered “by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony”, and “they did not love their life even to death”. Do you try to silence the accuser with your own good works, or do you take refuge in the blood of the Lamb? What would it mean for you to speak the word of your testimony?

Revelation 12:11–12

Discussion · 4/7

The woman is given “the two wings of the great eagle” and is fed in the wilderness (Exodus 19:4). The church is on its exodus through this age – not out of creation, but out of the dragon's power. Where are you in the wilderness at the moment, and where can you see that God is feeding you there?

Revelation 12:13–18

Discussion · 5/7

Read Daniel 7:2–8 and 17: John's beast is leopard, bear and lion in one body. The crowd cries: “Who is like the beast, and who can make war against it?” – a counterfeit of Israel's song (Exodus 15:11). What did the beast look like for the churches in Asia? Which powers lay claim to people's whole heart today?

Revelation 13:1–8

Discussion · 6/7

One of its heads “seemed slaughtered to death”, and the second beast “had two horns like a lamb, and it spoke like a dragon”. Why is the convincing copy more dangerous for the church than the open enemy? Where are you drawn by something that looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon?

Revelation 13:3–15

Discussion · 7/7

The mark is given “on their right hand or on their forehead”, and without it no one can buy or sell. Compare God's seal (7:3) and the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:8): what you think and what you do. No one is unmarked. If someone had watched your week: whom do your hand and your forehead belong to?

Revelation 13:16–18

For the week ahead

  • Write down one accusation you hear again and again in your head. Next to it write: “for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down” (12:10). Read it aloud when the voice comes back.
  • Choose one “hand” choice (something you do) and one “forehead” pattern (something you think) where the beast is pulling at you. Pray about them every day this week with the words: I belong to the Lamb.

Pray together

Read 12:10–11 aloud together. Then let each person thank God in one sentence that they belong to the Lamb, and – if they want to – name one place where they feel the pressure to “buy or sell” by playing along. Close with the confession of the devotional: Who is a God like you? (Micah 7:18), and thank him that our names are written in “the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb” (13:8).
