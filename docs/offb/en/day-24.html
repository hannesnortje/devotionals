Come Out of Her, My People: The Fall of Babylon

Scripture Revelation 18:1–24

1After this I saw another angel coming down from heaven, who had great authority, and the earth was lit up by his glory. 2And he cried out with a mighty voice, saying:

Fallen, fallen is Babylon the Great!She has become a dwelling place of demons,a prison of every unclean spirit,a prison of every unclean bird,a prison of every unclean and hated beast.3For by the wine of the fury of her harlotryall the nations have fallen,and the kings of the earth committed harlotry with her,and the merchants of the earth grew richfrom the power of her luxury.

4And I heard another voice from heaven saying:

Come out of her, my people,so that you do not share in her sinsand do not receive any of her plagues.5For her sins have piled up to heaven,and God has remembered her crimes.6Repay her as she herself has repaid,and pay her back double for her works;in the cup in which she mixed, mix her a double portion.7As much as she glorified herself and lived in luxury,so much torment and grief give her.For in her heart she says:I sit as a queen;I am no widow,and I will never see grief.8That is why her plagues will come in a single day,pestilence and grief and famine,and she will be burned up with fire;for mighty is the Lord God who has judged her.

9And the kings of the earth who committed harlotry with her and lived in luxury will weep and mourn over her when they see the smoke of her burning. 10They will stand far off for fear of her torment and say:

Woe, woe, the great city,Babylon, the mighty city!For in one hour your judgement has come.

11And the merchants of the earth weep and grieve over her, because no one buys their cargo any more, 12cargo of gold and silver and precious stone and pearls, and fine linen and purple and silk and scarlet, and all kinds of citron wood and every article of ivory and every article of most precious wood and bronze and iron and marble, 13and cinnamon and spice and incense and perfumed oil and frankincense, and wine and olive oil and fine flour and wheat, and cattle and sheep, and horses and carriages, and bodies – and human souls.

14And the fruit your soul longed forhas gone from you,and all the luxuries and the splendourare lost to you,and they will never find them again.

15The merchants of these things, who grew rich from her, will stand far off for fear of her torment, weeping and grieving, 16and say:

Woe, woe, the great city,dressed in fine linen and purple and scarlet,and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearl!17For in one hour such great wealth has been devastated.And every ship's captain and everyone who sails anywhere, and sailors and all who make their living from the sea, stood far off

18and cried out when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, "Who is like the great city?" 19And they threw dust on their heads and cried out, weeping and grieving, and said:

Woe, woe, the great city,in which all who had ships on the seagrew rich from her costly wealth!For in one hour she has been devastated.20Rejoice over her, heaven,and you saints and apostles and prophets,for God has judged your case against her.

21And a mighty angel picked up a stone like a great millstone and threw it into the sea, saying:

So with violence Babylon, the great city, will be thrown down,and will never be found again.22And the sound of lyre players and musicians,of flute players and trumpeters,will never be heard in you again,and no craftsman of any craftwill ever be found in you again,and the sound of the millwill never be heard in you again,23and the light of a lampwill never shine in you again,and the voice of bridegroom and bridewill never be heard in you again.For your merchants were the nobles of the earth,for by your sorcery all the nations were deceived,24and in her was found the blood of prophets and of saintsand of all who have been slaughtered on the earth.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday we saw the great prostitute on the beast, so impressive that even John was amazed. Today an angel with great authority comes down from heaven, "and the earth was lit up by his glory". His first words sound like a result already known: "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the Great!" These are the words of Isaiah 21:9 about the fall of ancient Babylon.

John sees Babylon's fall through the eyes of three groups who stand far off and weep: the kings of the earth, the merchants and the sailors. The model is Ezekiel's lament over Tyre, the great trading city of the ancient world (Ezekiel 26–27), with its sailors who throw dust on their heads and ask who was like Tyre (Ezekiel 27:30–32).

Then comes the cargo list. It reads like the bill of lading of a ship docking at Ostia, Rome's port: "gold and silver and precious stone and pearls, and fine linen and purple and silk and scarlet", citron wood and ivory, some of it from Africa, cinnamon and spice from the East, wine, olive oil and wheat, cattle, horses and carriages. And then, at the very end: "and bodies – and human souls". "Bodies" was a trade word for slaves, and the empire ran on slave labour. Ezekiel also mentions the trade in human beings (Ezekiel 27:13), but somewhere in the middle of the list. John moves it to the end, where it strikes hardest, and he adds what the merchants did not want to see: these are not just bodies, they are souls.

For the churches in Asia this was no distant world. Ephesus and Smyrna were port cities that lived from this trade; in Thyatira the trade guilds, probably with their feasts in honour of the gods, were part of everyday working life (2:20); and Laodicea said, "I am rich, I have become wealthy, and I need nothing" (3:17). Here we hear the same voice from Babylon's heart: "I sit as a queen; I am no widow, and I will never see grief" – the words of Isaiah 47:7–8 about ancient Babylon. Babylon is not only out there. Her voice can speak in the church too.

Insights from the Greek

  • Come out of her, my people (Exelthate, ho laos mou, ex autēs – Ἐξέλθατε, ὁ λαός μου, ἐξ αὐτῆς, v. 4): The call of Jeremiah to the exiles in ancient Babylon (Jeremiah 51:6, 45; compare Isaiah 52:11). The Greek Old Testament uses the same verb for Israel's exodus from Egypt (e.g. Exodus 13:3): God's people are called out.
  • Share in (synkoinōnēsēte – συγκοινωνήσητε, v. 4): Related to koinōnia, fellowship or partnership. The danger is not only doing wrong, but becoming Babylon's partner – sharing her values, her fears and her hope.
  • Luxury (strēnous, estrēniasen – στρήνους, ἐστρηνίασεν, vv. 3, 7): From strēnos. Extravagant, self-satisfied luxury that makes you think you are safe. The kings and merchants "lived in luxury" and "grew rich from the power of her luxury".
  • Bodies – and human souls (sōmatōn, kai psychas anthrōpōn – σωμάτων, καὶ ψυχὰς ἀνθρώπων, v. 13): Here the Greek suddenly changes its construction, as though John were interrupting the list; our translation marks this with a dash. The Lamb "purchased people for God" by his blood (5:9); Babylon buys and sells them.
  • Sorcery (pharmakeia – φαρμακείᾳ, v. 23): The word of Nahum 3:4 about Nineveh, "mistress of sorceries". Babylon's power is not only economic; it is a spell by which "all the nations were deceived".

Core Theological Truths

  1. Babylon's heart is self-glorification and false security. "As much as she glorified herself and lived in luxury" – that is the core of her sin. She believes her wealth protects her from grief. But what feels like strength is poverty in God's eyes: "in a single day" her plagues will come (v. 8).

  2. Her wealth is built on souls and blood. The chapter does not end with money but with blood: "and in her was found the blood of prophets and of saints and of all who have been slaughtered on the earth." Babylon is luxury, violence and idolatry in one. God sees the people in the hold, and he hears the blood of everyone who has been slaughtered – as he heard the blood of Abel (Genesis 4:10).

  3. "Come out" is the call of a new exodus. As Israel was called out of Egypt and out of Babylon, so God's people are called out of the system of this world. The goal is not the wilderness, but the new Jerusalem, where God dwells with people (21:1–3). The letter to the Hebrews puts it this way: "So let us go out to him outside the camp, bearing his reproach. For here we have no city that lasts" (Hebrews 13:13–14).

  4. Babylon's silence makes room for the wedding of the Lamb. A mighty angel throws a millstone into the sea, as Jeremiah's scroll was thrown with a stone into the Euphrates (Jeremiah 51:63–64). Then it falls silent: no music, no craft, no mill, no lamp, and "the voice of bridegroom and bride will never be heard in you again" (compare Jeremiah 25:10). But tomorrow another song rings out: "for the wedding of the Lamb has come" (19:7).

Living It Out

This chapter is not a sermon against money or trade. Wheat and wine and olive oil are good gifts of God. The problem is the heart that says, "I sit as a queen … and I will never see grief." It is the voice we all know: if only I have enough, I will be safe; if the account is full, nothing can touch me. And without our noticing it can become ours too.

Notice what the merchants weep over. Not over the city, and not over the slaves, but "because no one buys their cargo any more". What we grieve over shows what our hearts are bound to.

Think, too, of the people in the list. Behind many of the things we buy and use there are people – workers in factories and on farms, people whose bodies and time are made cheap. And sometimes we ourselves become part of the cargo: when our worth is measured by what we produce. Babylon turns people into goods. The Lamb has purchased us with his blood, not to own us like a slave, but to set us free for God.

So "come out of her" does not mean fleeing into the wilderness. The first Christians went on living and working in Ephesus and Smyrna. It means living in the city without belonging to her: honest in your business, generous with what you have, with people rather than profits at the centre, and with your hope set on the city that lasts. This is the new exodus: not out of creation, but out of the power that corrupts it.

This week, do one thing that means "coming out". Give away something you would like to keep, or, with one purchase, ask who the people behind it are and pray for them. Not out of guilt, but as someone who already belongs to the Lamb.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Where do you hear the voice of verse 7 in your own heart: "I sit as a queen … I will never see grief"?
  • What would you grieve over most if it were lost tomorrow – and what does that say about what your heart is bound to (v. 11)?
  • Which people are perhaps only "cargo" to you – someone whose work you use without really seeing him or her (v. 13)?
  • What would "Come out of her, my people" mean for your daily life, your work and your spending (v. 4)?

Prayer

Mighty is the Lord God who has judged Babylon. You see every person sold as cargo, and you hear the blood of all who have been slaughtered on the earth. Thank you that the Lamb did not sell me, but purchased me for you with his blood.

Forgive me for the times I have said in my heart: I have enough; nothing can touch me. Forgive me for seeking security in what I own, and for sometimes seeing people only in terms of what they could do for me.

Call me out, Lord, as you called your people out of Egypt and out of Babylon. Teach me to live in this world without belonging to her, and keep my eyes on the city that lasts, where you will dwell with your people. In Jesus' name, Amen.
