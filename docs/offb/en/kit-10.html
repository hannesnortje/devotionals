Small-group kit · Session 10 / 11

Come Out of Her

Present ▶

The big ideaBabylon – the seductive power of every age that rides on the beast, lives in luxury, trades in bodies and souls and is drunk with the blood of the saints – looks impressive but falls in a single hour; God calls his people to come out of her, not out of the world, and invites them to the wedding of the Lamb, whose Rider, Faithful and True, conquers by the word of his mouth.

Timing

  • 5 min Welcome & opener
  • 15 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 45 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of an advertisement, a product or a lifestyle that once really impressed you. What made it so attractive – and did it keep its promise?

Read together · Revelation 17:1 – 19:21

The chapters are long: read 17:1–6 and 17:14–18, all of chapter 18 and all of chapter 19. In chapter 18 let three readers take the laments of the kings (vv. 9–10), the merchants (vv. 11–17a) and the sailors (vv. 17b–19); the whole group reads the Hallelujahs of 19:1–8 together.

Where we are in the story

At the last bowl the voice from the throne cried “It is done!”, and God “remembered Babylon the Great” (16:19). Chapters 17–19 show who she is. For the seven churches she was Rome on her seven hills, rich, glamorous and violent; in the Bible's great story she is every power since Babel that seduces people with luxury and makes them forget to whom they belong. Chapter 18 is the lament over her fall and the call to God's people to come out; chapter 19 answers with heaven's Hallelujah, the wedding of the Lamb and the Rider on the white horse – Christ's return, which still lies ahead of us.

A look at the Greek

  • Exelthate, ho laos mou, ex autēs – Ἐξέλθατε, ὁ λαός μου, ἐξ αὐτῆς “Come out of her, my people”: Jeremiah's call to the exiles in ancient Babylon (Jeremiah 51:6, 45). The Greek Old Testament uses the same verb for Israel's exodus from Egypt (Exodus 13:3). It is a new exodus – out of Babylon, not out of creation; the goal is the city where God dwells with people (21:1–3).
  • sōmatōn, kai psychas anthrōpōn – σωμάτων, καὶ ψυχὰς ἀνθρώπων “bodies – and human souls”: The last items on Babylon's cargo list. “Bodies” was a trade word for slaves; here the Greek suddenly changes its construction, as though John interrupts the list to say what the merchants did not want to see: these are souls. The Lamb purchased people for God (5:9); Babylon buys and sells them.
  • pistos … kai alēthinos – πιστὸς … καὶ ἀληθινός “Faithful and True”: Pistos is someone who keeps his word; alēthinos is the genuine thing as against the counterfeit. In a book full of counterfeits – a beast that looks as though slain (13:3), a false prophet with horns like a lamb (13:11) – the Rider is the Genuine One. It is how Jesus introduced himself to Laodicea (3:14).

Discussion

  1. John hears of “the great prostitute who sits on many waters”, then sees “a woman sitting on a scarlet beast”. How does the seeing interpret the hearing? What would the churches have recognised when the angel speaks of seven mountains and of “the great city that has a kingdom over the kings of the earth”? (Revelation 17:1–18)
  2. “And when I saw her, I was greatly amazed.” The angel asks: “Why are you amazed?” Babylon appears not as a monster but as a queen in purple with a golden cup. What impresses you so much at the moment that it begins to draw your heart away from the Lamb? Behind the glamour, what is it riding on – and who pays for it? (Revelation 17:3–7)
  3. The Old Testament in the New: read Jeremiah 51:6–8 and Isaiah 47:7–8 aloud and compare them with 18:2–8. What does it tell us that the voice from heaven uses Jeremiah's words, “Come out of her, my people”? What kind of exodus is this – out of the world, or out of something else? What would it mean for your work and your spending? (Revelation 18:2–8)
  4. In her heart Babylon says: “I sit as a queen; I am no widow, and I will never see grief.” Laodicea said: “I am rich, I have become wealthy, and I need nothing” (3:17). Where do you hear that voice in your own heart – “if I have enough, nothing can touch me”? (Revelation 18:7)
  5. The merchants do not weep over the city or over the slaves, but “because no one buys their cargo any more” – and the list ends with “bodies – and human souls”. What does our grief show about what our hearts are bound to? Who might be only “cargo” to us – people whose work we use without really seeing them? (Revelation 18:11–24)
  6. Hallelujah, “for the wedding of the Lamb has come, and his wife has made herself ready.” The prostitute bought her purple and gold; the bride is given fine linen, shining and clean. Do you see the church more as a failure or as the bride of the Lamb? And do you try to earn your robe, or receive it as a gift? (Revelation 19:1–10)
  7. The Rider is “called Faithful and True”; his robe is dipped in blood before any fight, and no battle is described – only the sword from his mouth. Which of God's promises do you find hard to believe at the moment? And when did you last fight with the beast's weapons – anger, manipulation, power – instead of the word? (Revelation 19:11–21)

For the week ahead

  • Choose one act of “coming out” this week: give away something you would like to keep, or, with one purchase, ask who the people behind it are and pray for them – not out of guilt, but as someone who already belongs to the Lamb.
  • Choose one conversation in which you are tempted to win with harsh words. Pray Hebrews 4:12 beforehand, and then speak one true word in love instead of the word that wounds.

Pray together

Read 19:6–8 aloud together. Then pray in pairs: name before God one thing that amazes you more than the Lamb; pray for people who are treated as cargo – exploited workers, trafficked people, those whose worth is measured by what they produce; and thank the Rider, Faithful and True, for one promise he has kept. Close together: “Hallelujah! For the Lord God, the Almighty has begun to reign.”

Notes for the leader

  • This is the fullest session: three long chapters and four devotionals (days 23–26). Follow the reading tip and choose five questions; 2, 4 and 6 are the heart of the evening.
  • Who is Babylon? For the first readers clearly Rome (the seven mountains, 17:9; the great city, 17:18), but in the Bible's story she is every power since Babel (Genesis 11:4) that seduces with luxury, violence and idolatry. Do not decode the seven kings (17:9–11) into a list of emperors, or the beast into a modern state, institution or church. Futurist readers expect an end-time Babylon or world system, preterist readers see only Rome; name both fairly in a sentence and return to the book's question: whose am I?
  • Handle “in or out” with care. Two women and two cities are a call to faithfulness, not a tool for labelling other people as Babylon. Nor is chapter 18 a sermon against money or trade – wheat, wine and olive oil are good gifts of God. The issue is the heart that says “I will never see grief”, and the people behind the goods.
  • The text calls Babylon a “prostitute” and speaks of “harlotry”. In the prophets this is the picture of unfaithfulness to God – giving your love and trust to other powers (Hosea 2; Ezekiel 16; Nahum 3:4). If someone is uneasy with the image, explain it briefly: it is about where we give our hearts.
  • 19:11–21 is Christ's return, which still lies ahead. The terrible supper of 19:17–18 takes up Ezekiel 39:17–20: evil is utterly and finally defeated. Point to the two suppers – everyone is first invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb (19:9). And 19:10 is a good moment to ask what we are tempted to worship in God's place: “Worship God!”
  • Point ahead: next week is the last session, chapters 20–22 – the dragon on a chain, all things made new, the bride who is a city, the river and the tree of life, and “Come, Lord Jesus”.

Scripture Revelation 17:1 – 19:21

1And one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls came and spoke with me, saying, "Come, I will show you the judgement of the great prostitute who sits on many waters, 2with whom the kings of the earth committed harlotry, and those who live on the earth became drunk with the wine of her harlotry." 3And he carried me away in the Spirit into a wilderness. And I saw a woman sitting on a scarlet beast that was full of names of blasphemy and had seven heads and ten horns. 4And the woman was dressed in purple and scarlet and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearls; she had in her hand a golden cup full of abominations and the impurities of her harlotry, 5and on her forehead a name was written, a mystery: "Babylon the Great, the mother of prostitutes and of the abominations of the earth." 6And I saw the woman drunk with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the witnesses to Jesus. And when I saw her, I was greatly amazed.

7And the angel said to me, "Why are you amazed? I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast that carries her, which has the seven heads and the ten horns. 8The beast that you saw was, and is not, and is about to come up out of the abyss and go to destruction. And those who live on the earth, whose name has not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, will be amazed when they see the beast, because it was and is not and will be present. 9Here is the understanding that has wisdom: the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits. They are also seven kings: 10five have fallen, one is, the other has not yet come; and when he comes, he must remain only a little while. 11And the beast that was and is not is itself an eighth, and it belongs to the seven, and it goes to destruction. 12And the ten horns that you saw are ten kings who have not yet received a kingdom, but they receive authority as kings for one hour together with the beast. 13These have one purpose, and they give their power and authority to the beast. 14These will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful." 15And he says to me, "The waters that you saw, where the prostitute sits, are peoples and crowds and nations and languages. 16And the ten horns that you saw, and the beast, these will hate the prostitute; they will make her devastated and naked, and they will eat her flesh and burn her up with fire. 17For God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose, to carry out one purpose, and to give their kingdom to the beast, until the words of God are fulfilled. 18And the woman that you saw is the great city that has a kingdom over the kings of the earth."

1After this I saw another angel coming down from heaven, who had great authority, and the earth was lit up by his glory. 2And he cried out with a mighty voice, saying:

Fallen, fallen is Babylon the Great!She has become a dwelling place of demons,a prison of every unclean spirit,a prison of every unclean bird,a prison of every unclean and hated beast.3For by the wine of the fury of her harlotryall the nations have fallen,and the kings of the earth committed harlotry with her,and the merchants of the earth grew richfrom the power of her luxury.

4And I heard another voice from heaven saying:

Come out of her, my people,so that you do not share in her sinsand do not receive any of her plagues.5For her sins have piled up to heaven,and God has remembered her crimes.6Repay her as she herself has repaid,and pay her back double for her works;in the cup in which she mixed, mix her a double portion.7As much as she glorified herself and lived in luxury,so much torment and grief give her.For in her heart she says:I sit as a queen;I am no widow,and I will never see grief.8That is why her plagues will come in a single day,pestilence and grief and famine,and she will be burned up with fire;for mighty is the Lord God who has judged her.

9And the kings of the earth who committed harlotry with her and lived in luxury will weep and mourn over her when they see the smoke of her burning. 10They will stand far off for fear of her torment and say:

Woe, woe, the great city,Babylon, the mighty city!For in one hour your judgement has come.

11And the merchants of the earth weep and grieve over her, because no one buys their cargo any more, 12cargo of gold and silver and precious stone and pearls, and fine linen and purple and silk and scarlet, and all kinds of citron wood and every article of ivory and every article of most precious wood and bronze and iron and marble, 13and cinnamon and spice and incense and perfumed oil and frankincense, and wine and olive oil and fine flour and wheat, and cattle and sheep, and horses and carriages, and bodies – and human souls.

14And the fruit your soul longed forhas gone from you,and all the luxuries and the splendourare lost to you,and they will never find them again.

15The merchants of these things, who grew rich from her, will stand far off for fear of her torment, weeping and grieving, 16and say:

Woe, woe, the great city,dressed in fine linen and purple and scarlet,and adorned with gold and precious stone and pearl!17For in one hour such great wealth has been devastated.And every ship's captain and everyone who sails anywhere, and sailors and all who make their living from the sea, stood far off

18and cried out when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, "Who is like the great city?" 19And they threw dust on their heads and cried out, weeping and grieving, and said:

Woe, woe, the great city,in which all who had ships on the seagrew rich from her costly wealth!For in one hour she has been devastated.20Rejoice over her, heaven,and you saints and apostles and prophets,for God has judged your case against her.

21And a mighty angel picked up a stone like a great millstone and threw it into the sea, saying:

So with violence Babylon, the great city, will be thrown down,and will never be found again.22And the sound of lyre players and musicians,of flute players and trumpeters,will never be heard in you again,and no craftsman of any craftwill ever be found in you again,and the sound of the millwill never be heard in you again,23and the light of a lampwill never shine in you again,and the voice of bridegroom and bridewill never be heard in you again.For your merchants were the nobles of the earth,for by your sorcery all the nations were deceived,24and in her was found the blood of prophets and of saintsand of all who have been slaughtered on the earth.

1After this I heard something like the loud voice of a great crowd in heaven, saying:

Hallelujah!The salvation and the glory and the power belong to our God,2for true and just are his judgements;for he has judged the great prostitutewho corrupted the earth with her harlotry,and he has avenged the blood of his servantsfrom her hand.

3And a second time they said:

Hallelujah!Her smoke goes up for ever and ever.

4And the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshipped God, who sits on the throne, saying:

Amen. Hallelujah!

5And a voice came out from the throne, saying:

Praise our God, all his servants,and you who fear him, the small and the great!

6And I heard something like the voice of a great crowd, and like the sound of many waters, and like the sound of mighty thunder, saying:

Hallelujah!For the Lord God, the Almighty has begun to reign.7Let us rejoice and be glad,and we will give him the glory,for the wedding of the Lamb has come,and his wife has made herself ready.8And it was given to her to clothe herselfin fine linen, shining and clean;for the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints.

9And he says to me, "Write: Blessed are those who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb." And he says to me, "These are the true words of God." 10And I fell down at his feet to worship him. And he says to me, "Do not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and with your brothers and sisters who hold to the testimony of Jesus. Worship God! For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy."

11And I saw heaven opened, and there was a white horse, and its rider is called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he judges and makes war. 12His eyes are a flame of fire, and on his head are many diadems, and he has a name written that no one knows except himself. 13And he is dressed in a robe dipped in blood, and his name is called The Word of God. 14And the armies that are in heaven followed him on white horses, dressed in fine linen, white and clean. 15And out of his mouth comes a sharp sword with which to strike down the nations, and he will shepherd them with an iron rod; and he treads the winepress of the wine of the fury of the wrath of God, the Almighty. 16And on his robe and on his thigh he has a name written: King of kings and Lord of lords.

17And I saw an angel standing in the sun, and he cried out with a loud voice, saying to all the birds that fly in midheaven, "Come, gather for the great supper of God, 18to eat the flesh of kings and the flesh of generals and the flesh of the powerful and the flesh of horses and their riders, and the flesh of all, free and slave, small and great." 19And I saw the beast and the kings of the earth and their armies gathered to make war on the rider of the horse and on his army. 20And the beast was captured, and with it the false prophet who had performed the signs before it, by which he deceived those who had received the mark of the beast and those who worshipped its image. The two of them were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulphur. 21And the rest were killed with the sword of the rider of the horse, the sword that came out of his mouth, and all the birds gorged themselves on their flesh.
