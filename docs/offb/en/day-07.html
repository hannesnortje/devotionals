Behind the Curtain · Day 7 / 31

Sardis – The Danger of a Good Reputation Without a Beating Heart

Scripture Revelation 3:1–6

1And to the angel of the church in Sardis write: The one who has the seven spirits of God and the seven stars says this: I know your works: you have the name of being alive, but you are dead. 2Wake up, and strengthen what remains, which was about to die, for I have not found your works complete before my God. 3So remember how you received and heard; keep it, and repent. If you do not wake up, I will come like a thief, and you will not know at all at what hour I will come upon you. 4But you have a few names in Sardis who have not soiled their clothes, and they will walk with me in white, because they are worthy. 5The one who conquers will in this way be dressed in white clothes, and I will never blot his name out of the book of life, and I will confess his name before my Father and before his angels. 6Whoever has an ear should hear what the Spirit says to the churches.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Today our journey takes us a good fifty kilometres south of Thyatira to Sardis – a city living off its faded glory. Centuries earlier Sardis had been the legendary capital of the ancient kingdom of Lydia, where the proverbially rich King Croesus reigned. Gold was literally panned from the nearby river Pactolus, and the very first coins of pure gold and silver are credited to the Sardis of Croesus.

The old city's citadel stood on top of a steep, crumbling ridge of rock that towered hundreds of metres above the Hermus valley. The people of Sardis believed their city was utterly impregnable. And according to the ancient historians, it was precisely this self-assured arrogance that brought about their downfall twice. In 546 BC King Cyrus of Persia besieged the city. Because the people of Sardis thought no one could climb the sheer cliffs, they left that side unguarded. Herodotus tells how a soldier in the Persian army watched a defender of Sardis drop his helmet over the edge and climb down a hidden path to fetch it. Soon afterwards the Persians climbed up by the same path, took the unguarded citadel by surprise and captured it like a "thief". According to Polybius something similar happened three centuries later (in 214 BC) under Antiochus the Great: once again climbers scaled the cliffs at a point where the guards were not at their posts. By the time John writes, Sardis was a wealthy but decadent city living on the fame of its past, while its heart had long since been hollowed out.

Insights from the Greek

Let us look at some key words of the Greek text:

  • Onoma – ὄνομα (name / reputation): In verse 1 Jesus says: "you have the name of being alive, but you are dead" (nekros – νεκρός). In the surrounding cities the church in Sardis had a brilliant reputation. It was active, well known and respected. But before Jesus it was a spiritual corpse – nothing more than a fine gravestone with an impressive inscription.
  • Grēgoreō – γρηγορέω (be watchful / wake up): In verses 2 and 3 the sharp military command rings out: grēgoreō – "Wake up and stay at your post!" Anyone in Sardis who knew the city's shameful history – guards who did not keep watch on the walls while the enemy climbed in – would have flinched at this very word.
  • Peplērōmena – πεπληρωμένα (complete / filled): In verse 2 Jesus says: "I have not found your works complete before my God." They were not without works; they had programmes and activities. But their works were like empty shells – attractive on the outside for people to see, but empty on the inside of Spirit-filled faith and real love for God.

Core Theological Truths

This cutting letter reveals four profound theological truths:

  1. The Holy Spirit is the only source of genuine spiritual life (v. 1): To a dead church Jesus does not introduce himself with a sword of judgement, but as "the one who has the seven spirits of God and the seven stars". The "seven spirits" refer to the Holy Spirit in his sevenfold fullness and life-giving power (cf. Isaiah 11:2; Zechariah 4:6). A church or a believer that leans on human organisation, tradition and reputation without the indwelling power of the Holy Spirit is merely running a religious mortuary. But the good news is this: Jesus holds in his hand the Spirit who brings dry bones back to life (Ezekiel 37)!
  2. Peace with the world is often a sign of spiritual death (vv. 1–2): Have you noticed what is completely missing from the letter to Sardis? There is no mention of persecution from outside (as in Smyrna and Pergamum), no mention of Jewish opposition, and no mention of false teachers like the Nicolaitans or Jezebel from within! Why did the devil leave Sardis alone? Because a dead church poses no threat to Satan's kingdom. Sardis was so comfortably woven into its pagan culture that there was no friction left.
  3. Spiritual renewal calls for five urgent steps of obedience (vv. 2–3): Jesus has not yet written the church off; there are still embers glowing ("what remains, which was about to die"). He gives five direct commands: - Wake up (grēgoreō – shake off spiritual slumber and complacency). - Strengthen what remains (stērizō – στηρίζω – prop up the faint sparks of faith that have not yet gone out, and breathe new life into them). - Remember how you received and heard (think back to the humble dependence with which you first received the gospel). - Keep it (obey the Word in practice). - Repent (break at once with your spiritual hypocrisy, before Christ arrives unexpectedly like a thief with his discipline).
  4. Unstained faithfulness leads to public honour before God's throne (vv. 4–5): Even in a spiritually dead city like Sardis the Lord knows the "few names" who refused to stain their spiritual clothes with the filth of the world. In the ancient world a worshipper often had to be ritually clean to enter a temple, and soiled clothes could keep him out. Jesus promises the conquerors three things: - They will walk with him in white – the symbol of complete purity, festive joy and the righteousness of Christ. - Their name will never be blotted out of the book of life. Ancient cities kept a register of citizens, and the name of a citizen who died or was condemned for a crime could be struck out. But even if the world strikes your name from its roll of honour, your name stands in the Lamb's register in the indelible ink of grace. - Jesus himself will confess your name "before my Father and before his angels" (cf. Matthew 10:32).

Living It Out

Sardis is a frightening diagnosis for believers today who have walked with the Lord for a long time. It is possible to be the perfect Christian on paper and in the eyes of your community, while your soul is running on "autopilot". People at church or at work may think you are a spiritual giant. You know all the right answers, you know how to pray nicely when someone asks you to, you give your contributions, and your name is on all the right committees. You have the name of being alive.

But when the front door closes behind you and no one is watching, there is no prayer life, no hunger for God's Word, no tears over sin and no dependence on the Holy Spirit. We live off spiritual experiences from ten or twenty years ago – just as Sardis lived off the glory of King Croesus's day. Our works look impressive to people, but before God they are not "complete" – empty shells without a beating heart of faith.

Why do we fall asleep so easily, like the guards on the cliffs of Sardis? Because we think we are safe. We think: "I've been baptised, haven't I? I gave my heart to the Lord years ago, I'm not a bad person." And while we rest on our reputation, the enemy climbs over our walls by the unguarded paths of pride, lukewarmness and spiritual laziness.

Hear the Lord's wake-up call today: Wake up! Do not be content with a second-hand faith or a fine religious façade. He who holds the fullness of the Holy Spirit in his hand stands ready today to breathe his breath of life once more into the dying parts of your spiritual life.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • If you were to compare honestly what other people think of your spiritual life (your "name") with what your relationship with God really looks like in secret, how wide would the gap between the two be?
  • Are you living off old spiritual memories and victories from your past, or are you experiencing a fresh, living dependence on the Holy Spirit today?
  • Which spiritual discipline or conviction in your life is like what "was about to die" (v. 2), and needs you to wake up today and deliberately strengthen it again?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you hold the sevenfold fullness of the Holy Spirit in your hand. Today I bow before you with my heart laid bare. You are not deceived by my outward religious show or by the good opinion others may have of me.

Forgive me for every time I cared more about my reputation before people than about the real state of my heart before you. Forgive me for spiritual sleep, for prayers that have become empty words, and for works I do out of habit without the power of your Spirit. Shake me awake today, Lord! Breathe new life by your Holy Spirit into every part of my faith that is bleeding to death.

Wash my clothes white in your blood and give me the grace to walk unstained through this world. Thank you for the unshakeable promise that my name is written safely in your book of life. Let my life today not be merely a dead name, but a living testimony to your resurrection power. Amen.
