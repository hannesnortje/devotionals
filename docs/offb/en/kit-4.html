Small-group kit · Session 4 / 11

Who Is Worthy?

Present ▶

The big ideaBehind the visible power of Rome John is shown the throne in heaven, where the Creator is worshipped, and in the middle of the throne a Lamb standing, as though slaughtered: God rules history, and his power looks like the cross.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Have you ever heard someone described before you met them – and then been completely surprised when you saw them? What had you expected?

Read together · Revelation 4:1 – 5:14

One narrator reads John's words; others read the songs: the four living creatures (4:8), the elders (4:11 and 5:9–10), the angels (5:12). The whole group reads the song of every creature together (5:13) and answers with the living creatures: “Amen.”

Where we are in the story

In the cities of the seven churches Rome's throne was visible everywhere: temples for the emperor, his image on coins and in the market squares; Pergamum was the place “where the throne of Satan is” (2:13). After the seven letters a door opens in heaven, and the curtain is drawn back: chapter 4 shows the throne where the Creator is worshipped, chapter 5 the sealed scroll and the Lamb who alone is worthy to open it. Everything that follows in the book – seals, trumpets, bowls – comes from this room.

A look at the Greek

  • axios ei – Ἄξιος εἶ “worthy are you”: According to Suetonius, the emperor Domitian wished to be addressed as “our lord and god”. In the elders' song – “Worthy are you, our Lord and God” (4:11) – many hear a quiet, courageous counter-voice. In 5:9 the same words are sung to the Lamb: he receives the worship that belongs to God.
  • arnion – ἀρνίον “Lamb”: With one exception (the beast of 13:11) John uses this word only for Christ – 29 times. Behind it stand the Passover lamb of Exodus 12 and the Servant led like a lamb to the slaughter (Isaiah 53:7). The Lamb is standing “as though slaughtered” (hōs esphagmenon): alive, but still bearing the wounds (5:6).
  • ēgorasas – ἠγόρασας “you purchased”: A word of the market, which could also describe buying slaves (5:9). By his blood the Lamb has bought people for God from every nation and made them a kingdom and priests – what God promised Israel at Sinai (Exodus 19:6).

Discussion

  1. After Christ knocking at a closed door in Laodicea, now “a door stood open in heaven”, and John hears “Come up here”. What would it mean for believers in cities full of emperor temples to be shown the throne first – before any seals or plagues? Does your day usually begin with the news or with the throne? (Revelation 4:1–2)
  2. John stands in a long line. Read Isaiah 6:1–3, Ezekiel 1:26–28 and Job 1:6–12 (or 1 Kings 22:19). What do these scenes behind the curtain have in common? What do they say about who really decides what happens on earth – and about the accuser, who can do nothing without God's permission? (Revelation 4:2–8)
  3. The elders sing “Worthy are you, our Lord and God” – the very title Domitian claimed – and the reason is creation: “for you created all things”. In your decisions about time, money and attention, what do you actually call “worthy”? (Revelation 4:9–11)
  4. The elders cast their crowns before the throne. What “crown” are you holding tightly – a title, an achievement, your reputation, even your own faithfulness? What freedom might there be in laying it down? (Revelation 4:10)
  5. John weeps, because no one is found worthy to open the scroll. These are the tears of every believer who wonders whether suffering has any meaning. Which “scroll” in your life seems closed and sealed? And what does it mean that the golden bowls of incense “are the prayers of the saints”? (Revelation 5:1–8)
  6. John hears “the Lion from the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered” – and sees “a Lamb standing, as though slaughtered”. What did people expect from the Lion? What does it mean that the seeing interprets the hearing – that God's power looks like the cross? Where do you long for a Lion rather than the Lamb? (Revelation 5:5–7)
  7. “by your blood you purchased people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation” – and “they reign on the earth”, in the present tense. How does the Lamb's people reign: like their King, by witness and self-giving love? What would that look like in one conflict or frustration of yours this week? (Revelation 5:9–14)

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week, before you unlock your phone, read 4:8–11 aloud. Then name one “throne” that frightens you at the moment and deliberately lay it before the throne on which he sits.
  • Choose one place where you would love to “roar” – a conflict, an injustice, a frustration – and ask: how would the Lamb conquer here? Then take one small step of self-giving love in that direction.

Pray together

Begin with worship, not requests: read the songs of 4:8, 4:11 and 5:12 aloud together (or sing a song based on them). Then let each person name one “sealed scroll” and place it in the hands of the Lamb, trusting that our prayers are kept before the throne. Close by reading 5:13 together and answering “Amen.”

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers two devotionals (days 10–11) and two chapters. The heart of the evening is 5:5–6 (question 6): John hears “Lion” and sees a Lamb. Keep time for it; it is the key to everything that follows in the series.
  • Some (especially dispensational readings) take “Come up here” (4:1) as a picture of the church being taken up before the events of chapters 6–19, partly because the word “church” does not appear again until 22:16. Name this fairly. The devotional reads it as a call to John, like the prophets before him, to see the present reality behind the curtain. Keep the focus on the throne and the Lamb.
  • The twenty-four elders are probably the whole people of God – twelve tribes and twelve apostles (cf. 21:12–14) – though others think of angelic beings. The four living creatures recall Ezekiel's cherubim and Isaiah's seraphim and probably represent the whole of living creation. Mention briefly; do not spend long.
  • The scroll probably stands for God's purpose for history – his judgement on evil and the coming of his kingdom. Do not try to decode the seals yet; that is next session (chapters 6–7).
  • Handle with care: “the Lamb conquers by self-giving love” must not be heard as “put up with abuse”. The Lamb's way is faithful witness and refusing to hit back – not staying silent in harm or leaving the vulnerable unprotected. If someone in the group is in a harmful situation, talk with them afterwards.
  • Plan the prayer time as worship. Revelation 4–5 is full of songs; if your group sings, choose a hymn or song based on these chapters.

Scripture Revelation 4:1 – 5:14

1After this I looked, and look, a door stood open in heaven, and the first voice, which I had heard speaking with me like a trumpet, said, "Come up here, and I will show you what must take place after this." 2At once I was in the Spirit. And look, a throne stood in heaven, and on the throne someone was seated. 3And the one seated there looked like jasper and carnelian, and around the throne was a rainbow that looked like an emerald. 4And around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and on the thrones sat twenty-four elders, dressed in white clothes, with golden crowns on their heads. 5And from the throne come lightning and sounds and thunder, and seven torches of fire are burning before the throne; these are the seven spirits of God. 6And before the throne there was something like a sea of glass, like crystal. And in the middle of the throne and around the throne were four living creatures, full of eyes in front and behind. 7The first living creature was like a lion, the second living creature was like a young bull, the third living creature had a face like a human being, and the fourth living creature was like a flying eagle. 8And the four living creatures, each of them with six wings, are full of eyes all around and within. And day and night they never rest, saying:

Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty,who was and who is and who is coming.

9And whenever the living creatures give glory and honour and thanks to the one seated on the throne, who lives for ever and ever, 10the twenty-four elders fall down before the one seated on the throne and worship the one who lives for ever and ever, and they cast their crowns before the throne, saying:

11Worthy are you, our Lord and God,to receive the glory and the honour and the power,for you created all things,and by your will they existed and were created.

1And I saw in the right hand of the one seated on the throne a scroll written inside and on the back, sealed with seven seals. 2And I saw a mighty angel proclaiming with a loud voice, "Who is worthy to open the scroll and to break its seals?" 3And no one in heaven or on earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll or to look into it. 4And I wept and wept, because no one was found worthy to open the scroll or to look into it. 5And one of the elders says to me, "Do not weep. Look, the Lion from the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered, so that he can open the scroll and its seven seals."

6And I saw in the middle of the throne and of the four living creatures, and in the middle of the elders, a Lamb standing, as though slaughtered. He had seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth. 7And he came and took it out of the right hand of the one seated on the throne. 8And when he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each holding a lyre and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints. 9And they sing a new song:

Worthy are you to take the scrolland to open its seals,for you were slaughtered,and by your blood you purchased people for Godfrom every tribe and language and people and nation,10and you made them a kingdom and priests to our God,and they reign on the earth.

11And I looked, and I heard the voice of many angels around the throne and the living creatures and the elders, and their number was ten thousands of ten thousands and thousands of thousands, 12saying with a loud voice:

Worthy is the Lamb who was slaughteredto receive the power and wealth and wisdom and strengthand honour and glory and praise.

13And every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and on the sea, and everything in them, I heard saying:

To the one seated on the throne and to the Lambbe the praise and the honour and the glory and the mightfor ever and ever.

14And the four living creatures said, "Amen." And the elders fell down and worshipped.
