Behind the Curtain · Day 2 / 31

The Glorified King among the Lampstands

Scripture Revelation 1:9–20

9I, John, your brother, who shares with you in the affliction and the kingdom and the endurance in Jesus, was on the island called Patmos because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. 10On the Lord's day I came to be in the Spirit, and behind me I heard a loud voice like a trumpet, 11saying, "Write what you see in a book and send it to the seven churches: to Ephesus, to Smyrna, to Pergamum, to Thyatira, to Sardis, to Philadelphia and to Laodicea." 12And I turned to see the voice that was speaking with me. And when I had turned, I saw seven golden lampstands, 13and in the middle of the lampstands one like a son of man, dressed in a robe that reached to his feet, with a golden sash around his chest. 14His head and his hair were white like white wool, like snow, and his eyes were like a flame of fire; 15his feet were like burnished bronze, as if glowing in a furnace, and his voice was like the sound of many waters. 16In his right hand he had seven stars, out of his mouth came a sharp two-edged sword, and his face was like the sun shining in its power.

17When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. And he placed his right hand on me and said, "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last, 18and the Living One. I was dead, and look, I am alive for ever and ever, and I have the keys of death and of Hades. 19So write down what you have seen, what is, and what is going to take place after this. 20As for the mystery of the seven stars that you saw in my right hand, and the seven golden lampstands: the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven lampstands are the seven churches."

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

John finds himself on Patmos, a small, rocky island of volcanic origin in the Aegean Sea, some 60 to 70 kilometres off the coast of Asia Minor. The Romans often banished troublesome people to islands, and according to ancient church tradition John was sent to Patmos in this way – a place of isolation and oblivion. He himself says only that he was there "because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus". But no Roman power could shut out the Spirit of God. On "the Lord's day" (probably Sunday, the day of Christ's resurrection) John's physical limitations are overtaken by a heavenly reality.

Before Jesus addresses his messages to the seven particular churches, he first gives an overwhelming vision of himself. Why? Because the church's greatest problem in times of crisis (and of prosperity) is too small a view of Jesus. John uses images from the Old Testament (especially Daniel 7 and 10, and Ezekiel 1) to paint a portrait of who the risen Christ really is.

Insights from the Greek

Let us explore some key terms from the original Greek:

  • "Who shares with you" (synkoinōnos – συγκοινωνός, v. 9): In verse 9 John does not call himself an exalted apostle above the churches, but your brother, a synkoinōnos (someone who has an intimate share in something together with others). He shows that for a Christian three things belong inseparably together: affliction (thlipsis – θλῖψις: pressure from outside), kingdom (basileia – βασιλεία: reigning together with Christ), and endurance (hypomonē – ὑπομονή: courageous staying power under heavy loads). You cannot have the kingdom without being willing to go through pressure with endurance.
  • "Lampstands" (lychniai – λυχνίαι, vv. 12, 20): This was not the one seven-branched lampstand (the menorah) of the Old Testament tabernacle and temple (Exodus 25:31–37; cf. Zechariah 4:2), but seven separate golden lampstands. The church has no light of her own; she is simply the stand that must hold up the light of Christ in a dark world.
  • "Sharp two-edged sword" (rhomphaia distomos – ῥομφαία δίστομος, v. 16): The word for the sword that comes out of Jesus' mouth is not machaira (μάχαιρα), the ordinary word for a shorter sword or dagger, but rhomphaia – originally a long, heavy weapon (the word is often linked with the Thracian long blade), and here distomos, literally "two-mouthed", razor-sharp on both sides. (Revelation uses both words, so the difference should not be pressed too far.) Isaiah 49:2 says of the Servant of the LORD: "He made my mouth a sharp sword." It symbolises the absolute power of his spoken Word to cut lies open and to pass righteous judgement.

Core Theological Truths

Every physical detail of this vision carries a specific theological truth for the churches:

  1. Jesus is present in the midst of his church (vv. 12–13). He stands in the middle of the seven golden lampstands, and in 2:1 he is described as the one "who walks among the seven golden lampstands". He is not an absent landlord who has left his church to her fate. His long robe and the golden sash around his chest recall the dress of the high priest, and also that of a king. Just as the priests in the temple tended the lamps every day, trimmed the wicks and added oil, so Jesus walks among his churches to examine them, purify them and keep them burning.
  2. His divine wisdom, knowledge and power (vv. 14–16).
    • Hair white as snow: In Daniel 7:9 God (the "Ancient of Days") is described with hair white like wool. By applying this feature to Jesus, the text declares that Jesus possesses eternal wisdom and is himself God.
    • Eyes like a flame of fire: Nothing is hidden from him. His gaze burns through every religious mask, all hypocrisy and every excuse.
    • "His feet were like burnished bronze, as if glowing in a furnace, and his voice was like the sound of many waters": He stands immovable to trample down evil, and his voice drowns out all the noise and propaganda of the world.
  3. Absolute security and victory over death (vv. 16–18). John falls overwhelmed at his feet – according to the church's tradition the same John who had leaned against Jesus' chest at the Last Supper! Then that same pierced right hand that holds the seven stars touches him tenderly. Jesus combines his awe-inspiring majesty with intimate gentleness: "Do not be afraid." He has "the keys of death and of Hades". Not Rome, not illness, and not the devil decides when a believer's life ends; Jesus opens and shuts.

Living It Out

We often live with a shrunken version of Jesus in our minds. We think of him only as the gentle Shepherd in a children's Bible picture or the suffering Man on the cross, and we forget that today he is the glorified King whose face shines like the sun in its power. When our picture of Jesus is small, our problems look enormous. But when we see him as John saw him on Patmos, our fears shrink to their true size.

Think of the picture in verses 16 and 17: the hand that holds the stars (the angels of the churches – many understand them as the leaders or messengers of the church) is the same hand that rests on the shoulder of a trembling, overwhelmed human being. This means that no power can snatch you out of his right hand, and that his immense strength always goes together with his personal compassion for you.

At the same time this vision challenges us. If Jesus walks today with "eyes like a flame of fire" through the corridors of your heart and your church, what does he see? We can fool other people with pious words and a neat appearance on a Sunday morning, but before him everything lies open and bare. Yet his purpose with the sharp sword of his Word is not to destroy his children, but to cut away everything that keeps us from shining brightly as his golden lampstands.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • John calls himself one who shares in the affliction, the kingdom and the endurance. How are you handling the "pressure" (thlipsis) in your life at the moment – is it making you bitter, or is it growing patient endurance in you?
  • Is there any area of your life that you are trying to hide from others, about which you need to realise afresh today that it lies completely open before Jesus' "eyes like a flame of fire"?
  • Which specific fear in your life needs to hear today that Jesus holds "the keys of death and of Hades" in his own hand?

Prayer

Glorified Lord Jesus, as I look at this vision of you this morning, I realise how small and limited my thoughts about you often are. I bow in reverence before your majesty. You are the First and the Last, and the Living One who has conquered death for ever.

Search my heart today with your eyes like a flame of fire; show me where I wear masks, and with the two-edged sword of your Word cut away everything that does not bring you honour. Thank you that in my own "Patmos" moments of isolation or struggle you do not stand far off, but walk among us.

Please lay your right hand of grace on my life today and speak your peace over my fears. Help me today to let your light shine brightly like a pure golden lampstand wherever I go. Amen.
