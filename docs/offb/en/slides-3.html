Behind the Curtain · Session 3

Wake Up, Hold Fast, Open the Door

Revelation 3:1–22

To get started

What is the difference between a house that looks good in photos and a house that is good to live in? Have you ever been surprised by the difference?

Where we are in the story

The messenger's road ends with three more cities. Sardis was proud of its past and of a citadel it thought impregnable – yet it was taken twice because no one kept watch. Philadelphia was young and small, on a fertile plain shaken again and again by earthquakes. Laodicea was rich, with banks, a textile trade and a medical school, but its water arrived lukewarm. Three churches, three dangers – reputation, weakness, comfort – and the seven letters end with a throne (3:21).

A look at the Greek

ὄνομα

onoma · “name”

Name means reputation: “you have the name of being alive, but you are dead” (3:1). Sardis was well known, active and respected – and before Christ a fine gravestone with an impressive inscription. Yet the same letter also knows “a few names” who have not soiled their clothes (3:4).

A look at the Greek

μικρὰν δύναμιν

mikran dynamin · “little power”

Not a rebuke for weak faith, but a description of a small, poor church without influence (3:8). And yet it kept Christ's word and did not deny his name – and the one who holds the key of David set an open door before it.

A look at the Greek

χλιαρός

chliaros · “lukewarm”

The word occurs only here in the New Testament (3:16). Many interpreters hear the water of the Lycus valley behind it: hot, healing springs at Hierapolis, cold drinking water at Colossae – and lukewarm water at Laodicea that healed no one and quenched no one's thirst.

The big idea

Christ sees past reputation, size and wealth: he wakes up a church that only looks alive, opens a door for a church with little power, and knocks at the door of a church that thinks it needs nothing – and to all three he offers a place with him on his throne.

Discussion · 1/7

Each reporter: what did your city pride itself on (Sardis: its unconquerable citadel; Philadelphia: its name and its new imperial names; Laodicea: its banks, its black wool and its eye-salve)? Where does Christ's letter touch exactly that point?

Revelation 3:1–18

Discussion · 2/7

Sardis: “you have the name of being alive, but you are dead.” No persecution, no false teacher – can peace with the world be a sign of spiritual sleep? How wide is the gap between your “name” and your life with God when no one is watching – and what would “Wake up, and strengthen what remains” mean for you?

Revelation 3:1–4

Discussion · 3/7

Philadelphia: Christ has “the key of David, who opens and no one will shut, and who shuts and no one opens” (Isaiah 22:20–23). What did that key mean for Eliakim, and what does it mean that Jesus holds it? Which door has been shut in your face – and which door may he have opened?

Revelation 3:7–8

Discussion · 4/7

“You have little power, and yet you have kept my word and have not denied my name.” What does this say to small churches and to people who feel too ordinary to matter? Why would a pillar “in the temple of my God” mean so much in a city of earthquakes?

Revelation 3:8–12

Discussion · 5/7

“I too will keep you from the hour of testing”. The devotional points to John 17:15: not taken out of the world, but kept from the evil one – kept through it and brought out of it. What difference does it make whether we expect God to spare us trouble or to keep us in it?

Revelation 3:10–11

Discussion · 6/7

Laodicea: “I am rich, I have become wealthy, and I need nothing”. Christ offers to sell what only he has (cf. Isaiah 55:1: buy without money and without price). Where has comfort or success made you feel, without noticing, that you need nothing? What would gold refined by fire, white clothes and salve for your eyes be for you?

Revelation 3:15–19

Discussion · 7/7

“Look, I am standing at the door and knocking.” The Lord of the church stands outside his own church – and does not break the door down. Which room of your life is he still knocking at? And what does it mean that the one who opens is invited to sit with him on his throne?

Revelation 3:20–22

For the week ahead

  • Choose one spiritual habit that is “about to die” in your life – prayer, Bible reading, worship, generosity – and strengthen it deliberately this week, at a fixed time each day.
  • Open a door: name the area of your life where Jesus is still standing outside, tell him so in prayer, and give him the place of honour there – perhaps with one concrete change in how you spend your time or your money.

Pray together

Pray for the three churches and for ourselves: for wakefulness (Sardis), for faithfulness with little power (Philadelphia), for honest dependence (Laodicea). Then keep a minute of silence with verse 20, and let each person open one door to Christ in silence. Close by thanking him that he loves those he reproves (3:19).
