Small-group kit · Session 8 / 11

Whose Are You?

Present ▶

The big ideaBehind the curtain the dragon has already been thrown down by the blood of the Lamb; in his fury he works through beasts that imitate the Lamb and demand worship – but God's people, sealed before the beast ever appeared, conquer by the blood of the Lamb, the word of their testimony and endurance, for the question is not “when?” but “whose are you?”.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Have you ever been fooled by a fake – a counterfeit product, a forged signature, a scam message? How did you find out?

Read together · Revelation 12:1 – 13:18

Three readers: a narrator, a second reader for the loud voice in heaven (12:10–12, read slowly as a song of victory), and a third for the crowd and the second beast (13:4, 14). Pause briefly between the two chapters.

Where we are in the story

The seventh trumpet has sounded: the kingdom of the world has become our Lord's. Now John draws the curtain back further and shows how it came about. Chapter 12 tells the Christmas story from behind the curtain: a woman, a child, a dragon – and the accuser thrown down. In chapter 13 the dragon calls up two beasts, from the sea and from the earth. For the churches in Asia the beast had a face: Rome, the imperial cult and the guilds, where you could not buy or sell without offering incense to the emperor.

A look at the Greek

  • ho katēgōr – ὁ κατήγωρ “the accuser”: A word from the courtroom (12:10) – exactly what the Hebrew *hassatan* means in Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3: the adversary, the accuser in God's heavenly council. He accuses them “before our God day and night” – but now he has been thrown down.
  • hōs esphagmenēn – ὡς ἐσφαγμένην “seemed slaughtered”: The same expression as for the Lamb in 5:6 (*hōs esphagmenon*). John wants us to notice the likeness – and the difference (13:3). The Lamb's wounds are for others; the beast's healing is for itself. Evil creates nothing new. It imitates.
  • charagma – χάραγμα “mark”: A stamp or impression – also the emperor's stamp on coins and documents (13:16–17). Every coin in the hand of a trader in Thyatira bore the emperor's image and titles. The mark is “the name of the beast or the number of its name”: it says whom you belong to.

Discussion

  1. Read Genesis 3:15 and 37:9: what do they tell us about the woman, the child and the dragon? The child “is to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod” (Psalm 2:9) and is “snatched up to God and to his throne” – the whole life of Jesus in one sentence. What is different when we see Bethlehem from behind the curtain? (Revelation 12:1–6)
  2. Read Zechariah 3:1–5 and Job 1:9–11: the accuser could still stand before God. Now “the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down”. Which accusation do you hear most often in your own thoughts? How does the Spirit's conviction, which leads to the cross, differ from the accuser's voice, which ends in despair? (Revelation 12:7–10)
  3. They conquered “by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony”, and “they did not love their life even to death”. Do you try to silence the accuser with your own good works, or do you take refuge in the blood of the Lamb? What would it mean for you to speak the word of your testimony? (Revelation 12:11–12)
  4. The woman is given “the two wings of the great eagle” and is fed in the wilderness (Exodus 19:4). The church is on its exodus through this age – not out of creation, but out of the dragon's power. Where are you in the wilderness at the moment, and where can you see that God is feeding you there? (Revelation 12:13–18)
  5. Read Daniel 7:2–8 and 17: John's beast is leopard, bear and lion in one body. The crowd cries: “Who is like the beast, and who can make war against it?” – a counterfeit of Israel's song (Exodus 15:11). What did the beast look like for the churches in Asia? Which powers lay claim to people's whole heart today? (Revelation 13:1–8)
  6. One of its heads “seemed slaughtered to death”, and the second beast “had two horns like a lamb, and it spoke like a dragon”. Why is the convincing copy more dangerous for the church than the open enemy? Where are you drawn by something that looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon? (Revelation 13:3–15)
  7. The mark is given “on their right hand or on their forehead”, and without it no one can buy or sell. Compare God's seal (7:3) and the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:8): what you think and what you do. No one is unmarked. If someone had watched your week: whom do your hand and your forehead belong to? (Revelation 13:16–18)

For the week ahead

  • Write down one accusation you hear again and again in your head. Next to it write: “for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down” (12:10). Read it aloud when the voice comes back.
  • Choose one “hand” choice (something you do) and one “forehead” pattern (something you think) where the beast is pulling at you. Pray about them every day this week with the words: I belong to the Lamb.

Pray together

Read 12:10–11 aloud together. Then let each person thank God in one sentence that they belong to the Lamb, and – if they want to – name one place where they feel the pressure to “buy or sell” by playing along. Close with the confession of the devotional: Who is a God like you? (Micah 7:18), and thank him that our names are written in “the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb” (13:8).

Notes for the leader

  • Two devotionals (days 18–19). Both chapters are full of images; spend most of the time on 12:10–11 (the accuser thrown down) and 13:16–17 (the mark). These carry the pastoral weight.
  • 666: name the two main readings briefly – the words “Nero Caesar” in Hebrew letters add up to 666 (and in a Latin spelling to 616, the number in a few early manuscripts), or a “human number”, six falling short three times of seven. John calls for the wisdom of discernment, not of code-breakers. Do not decode the number to modern persons, and do not let the group speculate about chips, barcodes or dates.
  • The mark is about belonging, not fear. Some members may have grown up with frightening teaching about the mark; reassure them: God's seal came first (7:3), and the mark of the beast is not something you receive by accident – it grows through a thousand small choices of the hand and the forehead.
  • Other readings: futurists expect a future individual ruler and a literal mark at the end of history; preterists see the chapter fulfilled in Rome in the first century. Our series: Rome in John's day, and every power in every age that demands worship. Name the views fairly in a sentence and return to the question “whose are you?”.
  • “In or out” is a call to faithfulness, not “us against them”. Don't let the group name political parties or leaders as the beast; the question is turned on ourselves first – Laodicea's lukewarm middle way (3:15–16) is also no neutral ground.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 14 – the Lamb on Mount Zion with those who have his name and his Father's name on their foreheads.

Scripture Revelation 12:1 – 13:18

1And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars. 2And she was pregnant, and she cries out in labour and in torment to give birth.

3And another sign appeared in heaven, and look, a great fiery red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns, and on its heads seven diadems. 4And its tail sweeps away a third of the stars of heaven, and it threw them to the earth. And the dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she gave birth it might devour her child. 5And she gave birth to a son, a male child, who is to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod. And her child was snatched up to God and to his throne. 6And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, so that there she might be fed for a thousand two hundred and sixty days.

7And war broke out in heaven: Michael and his angels made war against the dragon. And the dragon made war, and its angels, 8but it was not strong enough, and no place was found for them in heaven any longer. 9And the great dragon was thrown down, the ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world – he was thrown down to the earth, and his angels were thrown down with him.

10And I heard a loud voice in heaven saying:

Now have come the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our Godand the authority of his Christ,for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down,who accuses them before our God day and night.11And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamband by the word of their testimony,and they did not love their life even to death.12Therefore rejoice, you heavens,and you whose tent is in them!Woe to the earth and the sea,for the devil has come down to youwith great fury,because he knows that he has only a little time.

13And when the dragon saw that it had been thrown down to the earth, it pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child. 14And the two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman, so that she might fly into the wilderness to her place, where she is fed for a time and times and half a time, away from the face of the serpent. 15And the serpent spewed water like a river out of its mouth after the woman, so that the river would sweep her away. 16And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had spewed out of its mouth. 17And the dragon was angry with the woman and went off to make war against the rest of her offspring, those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus. 18And it took its stand on the sand of the sea.

1And I saw a beast coming up out of the sea, with ten horns and seven heads, and on its horns ten diadems, and on its heads names of blasphemy. 2And the beast that I saw was like a leopard, and its feet were like a bear's, and its mouth like a lion's mouth. And the dragon gave it its power and its throne and great authority. 3And one of its heads seemed slaughtered to death, and its death wound was healed. And the whole earth followed the beast in amazement, 4and they worshipped the dragon, because it had given the authority to the beast, and they worshipped the beast, saying, "Who is like the beast, and who can make war against it?"

5And it was given a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies, and it was given authority to act for forty-two months. 6And it opened its mouth in blasphemies against God, to blaspheme his name and his tent – those whose tent is in heaven. 7And it was allowed to make war against the saints and to conquer them, and it was given authority over every tribe and people and language and nation. 8And all who live on the earth will worship it – everyone whose name has not been written in the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb from the foundation of the world.

9If anyone has an ear, he should hear. 10If anyone is for captivity, into captivity he goes; if anyone is to be killed with the sword, with the sword he will be killed. Here is the endurance and the faith of the saints.

11And I saw another beast coming up out of the earth, and it had two horns like a lamb, and it spoke like a dragon. 12And it exercises all the authority of the first beast in its presence, and it makes the earth and those who live on it worship the first beast, whose death wound was healed. 13And it performs great signs, so that it even makes fire come down from heaven to the earth before people. 14And it deceives those who live on the earth by the signs that it was allowed to perform in the presence of the beast, telling those who live on the earth to make an image for the beast that has the wound of the sword and came to life. 15And it was allowed to give breath to the image of the beast, so that the image of the beast would even speak and would cause all who do not worship the image of the beast to be killed. 16And it makes everyone, the small and the great, the rich and the poor, the free and the slaves, to be given a mark on their right hand or on their forehead, 17so that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark: the name of the beast or the number of its name. 18Here is wisdom: whoever has understanding should calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a human being. And its number is six hundred and sixty-six.
