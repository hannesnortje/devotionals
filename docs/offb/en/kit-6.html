Small-group kit · Session 6 / 11

Heaven Is Listening

Present ▶

The big ideaHeaven falls silent to hear the prayers of the saints, and those prayers come back to earth as God's action; the trumpets, like the plagues of Egypt, are limited warnings that call the world to repent – but judgement alone does not change the heart, and what the plagues cannot do, the Lamb does through his witnesses.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Where do you find real silence in your week – and do you enjoy it, or does it make you uneasy?

Read together · Revelation 8:1 – 9:21

Begin with half a minute of silence after 8:1 before reading on. Then one narrator reads, and six readers each take one trumpet (8:7, 8, 10, 12; 9:1, 13); a last reader takes the eagle's cry (8:13) and 9:20–21.

Where we are in the story

Heaven has been full of songs: angels, elders, living creatures and a countless crowd. Now the Lamb opens the seventh seal – and there is silence. An angel offers incense with the prayers of all the saints, throws fire from the altar on the earth, and seven trumpets sound (chapters 8–9). The images come from the exodus: hail, blood, darkness and locusts, as in Egypt. For the small churches in Asia, under the power of Rome, this said: God still acts against every empire that behaves like Egypt.

A look at the Greek

  • sigē – σιγὴ “silence”: An ordinary word for silence (8:1). It is not an empty silence, but the silence of a courtroom before the judge speaks – and of a Father who is listening. The prophets knew it as the silence before God acts (Habakkuk 2:20; Zephaniah 1:7).
  • to triton – τὸ τρίτον “a third”: Twelve times in six verses (8:7–12). The seals struck a fourth (6:8), the trumpets a third, the bowls later everything. The judgements are limited. They are warnings, not yet the end.
  • ou metenoēsan – οὐ μετενόησαν “they did not repent”: *Metanoeō* is a turning round of mind and life (9:20–21). It is the same verb Jesus used five times for the churches (2:5, 16, 21; 3:3, 19): the call to repent is addressed first to the church, then to the world.

Discussion

  1. “There was silence in heaven for about half an hour.” Read Habakkuk 2:20 and Zephaniah 1:7. What kind of silence is this? When did you last become truly still before God, without speaking? (Revelation 8:1)
  2. The angel stands at the golden altar of incense, right in front of the curtain (Exodus 30:1–8; Luke 1:9–10), and the incense rises “with the prayers of the saints” before God. What does this picture say to small churches whose prayers no one in Rome heard – and to you, if you have given up on a prayer? (Revelation 8:3–4)
  3. The angel fills the censer “with the fire of the altar” and throws it on the earth – the same altar under which the souls cried “How long?” (6:9–10). Then the trumpets begin. Do you believe that your prayers are part of how God acts in the world? How would that change your intercession? (Revelation 8:5–6)
  4. Compare the first, second and fourth trumpets with the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 9:23–25; 7:20–21; 10:21–23). What does the exodus tell us about the purpose of the trumpets – for Rome, and for every power that oppresses God's people? What is God aiming at: the destruction of creation, or the liberation of his people? (Revelation 8:7–12)
  5. The locusts may harm “only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads”. The key, the authority and the five months – all are given. Their king Apollyon (“Destroyer”) is perhaps a dig at Apollo. What comfort did this give the churches in Asia? Where do you need to hear that evil is on a leash? (Revelation 9:1–11)
  6. “And the rest of humankind … did not even repent of the works of their hands”; like Pharaoh, they cling to idols “which can neither see nor hear nor walk” (Psalm 115:4–8). Which crisis in your life turned you to God – and which made you harder instead? What are the “works of your hands” that you trust more than God? (Revelation 9:20–21)
  7. In Israel the trumpet was the watchman's alarm (Ezekiel 33:3–6) – a God who warns has not yet given up on the world. How do you respond to news of disasters – with fear, with speculation, or with prayer and repentance? Do you hear the call to repent as a threat or as an invitation? (Revelation 8:13)

For the week ahead

  • Sit in silence for five minutes before you pray, every day this week. Then write down one injustice you have long been praying about, and bring it before God again – knowing that heaven is listening.
  • Take ten minutes and ask yourself honestly: which “work of my hands” has taken my heart captive? Name it before God, and take one small step away from it – and towards him.

Pray together

Begin with a full minute of silence together – no words, only remembering who sits on the throne. Then let each person pray one sentence for a situation they have long prayed about, or for persecuted believers. Close with Jesus' words to the churches (3:20), and thank him that he always lives to intercede for us (Hebrews 7:25).

Notes for the leader

  • Two dense chapters (days 14–15). Don't try to explain every detail of the locusts or the horses; the chapters work by overwhelming images. Keep the group on three things: the prayers rise, the judgements are limited, and people do not repent.
  • Avoid matching the trumpets to today's disasters (or even guessing which trumpet has just sounded). Some interpreters think the first readers may have thought of Vesuvius (AD 79) at the burning mountain; futurist interpreters expect literal plagues in a future period before Christ's return. Mention this fairly if asked, then return to the call to prayer and repentance.
  • The locusts and the two hundred million horsemen invite decoding (helicopters, modern armies). Gently point out that John himself says he heard the number (9:16): it is an army too large to grasp, painted with Joel 2 and the smoke of Sodom (Genesis 19:24, 28), not a code.
  • Some Bibles read an angel instead of an eagle in 8:13; older translations follow later manuscripts. Our translation follows the SBL Greek text. If someone notices, explain briefly and move on.
  • Handle with care: some members may have prayed for years without seeing an answer – for a child, an illness, an injustice. The point of 8:3–5 is not that prayer works like a lever, but that no prayer is lost. Don't promise outcomes.
  • Point ahead: next week the pause between the sixth and seventh trumpet – the little scroll and the two witnesses: God's answer to a world that will not repent is his word in the mouth of his witnesses.

Scripture Revelation 8:1 – 9:21

1And when he opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour. 2And I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them.

3And another angel came and stood at the altar, holding a golden censer. And much incense was given to him, so that he would put it with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne. 4And the smoke of the incense went up with the prayers of the saints from the hand of the angel before God. 5And the angel took the censer and filled it with the fire of the altar and threw it on the earth; and there were thunder and sounds and lightning and an earthquake.

6And the seven angels who had the seven trumpets made themselves ready to blow the trumpet.

7And the first blew the trumpet; and there came hail and fire, mixed with blood, and it was thrown on the earth. And a third of the earth was burnt up, and a third of the trees were burnt up, and all the green grass was burnt up.

8And the second angel blew the trumpet; and something like a great mountain burning with fire was thrown into the sea. And a third of the sea became blood, 9and a third of the creatures in the sea that had life died, and a third of the ships were destroyed.

10And the third angel blew the trumpet; and a great star fell from heaven, burning like a torch, and it fell on a third of the rivers and on the springs of water. 11And the name of the star is called Wormwood. And a third of the waters became wormwood, and many people died from the waters, because they had been made bitter.

12And the fourth angel blew the trumpet; and a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon and a third of the stars, so that a third of them was darkened, and for a third of the day there was no light, and likewise for the night.

13And I looked, and I heard an eagle flying in midheaven, saying with a loud voice, "Woe, woe, woe to those who live on the earth, because of the remaining blasts of the trumpet of the three angels who are about to blow!"

1And the fifth angel blew the trumpet; and I saw a star that had fallen from heaven to the earth, and the key to the shaft of the abyss was given to him. 2And he opened the shaft of the abyss, and smoke went up from the shaft like the smoke of a great furnace, and the sun and the air were darkened by the smoke of the shaft. 3And out of the smoke came locusts onto the earth, and they were given authority like the authority that the scorpions of the earth have. 4And they were told not to harm the grass of the earth or any green thing or any tree, but only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads. 5And they were allowed not to kill them, but to torment them for five months; and their torment was like the torment of a scorpion when it stings a person. 6And in those days people will seek death and will not find it; they will long to die, and death flees from them.

7And the shapes of the locusts were like horses prepared for war; and on their heads was something like crowns, like gold, and their faces were like human faces. 8And they had hair like women's hair, and their teeth were like lions' teeth. 9And they had breastplates like breastplates of iron, and the rumble of their wings was like the rumble of chariots with many horses rushing into war. 10And they have tails like scorpions, and stings, and in their tails is their authority to harm people for five months. 11They have as king over them the angel of the abyss; his name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in Greek he has the name Apollyon.

12The first woe has passed; look, two woes are still coming after this.

13And the sixth angel blew the trumpet; and I heard a voice from the horns of the golden altar that is before God, 14saying to the sixth angel, who had the trumpet, "Release the four angels who are bound at the great river, the Euphrates!" 15And the four angels were released, who had been made ready for the hour and day and month and year, to kill a third of humankind. 16And the number of the mounted troops was twice ten thousands of ten thousands; I heard their number. 17And this is how I saw the horses in the vision and those who sat on them: they had breastplates fiery red and hyacinth blue and sulphur yellow; and the heads of the horses were like lions' heads, and out of their mouths come fire and smoke and sulphur. 18By these three plagues a third of humankind was killed, by the fire and the smoke and the sulphur that come out of their mouths. 19For the authority of the horses is in their mouths and in their tails; for their tails are like serpents, with heads, and with them they do harm.

20And the rest of humankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not even repent of the works of their hands, so as to stop worshipping the demons and the idols of gold and silver and bronze and stone and wood, which can neither see nor hear nor walk. 21And they did not repent of their murders or of their sorceries or of their sexual immorality or of their thefts.
