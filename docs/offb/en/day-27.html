Behind the Curtain · Day 27 / 31

The Dragon on a Chain: The Thousand Years and the Book of Life

Scripture Revelation 20:1–15

1And I saw an angel coming down from heaven, holding in his hand the key of the abyss and a great chain. 2And he seized the dragon, the ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years, 3and threw him into the abyss and locked it and sealed it over him, so that he would deceive the nations no more until the thousand years were completed. After that he must be released for a short time.

4And I saw thrones, and they sat on them, and judgement was given to them. And I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded because of the testimony of Jesus and because of the word of God, and who had not worshipped the beast or its image and had not received the mark on their foreheads or on their hands. And they lived and reigned with Christ for a thousand years. 5The rest of the dead did not live until the thousand years were completed. This is the first resurrection. 6Blessed and holy is the one who has a share in the first resurrection. Over these the second death has no authority, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him for a thousand years.

7And when the thousand years are completed, Satan will be released from his prison 8and will go out to deceive the nations at the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them for the war; their number is like the sand of the sea. 9And they went up across the breadth of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city. And fire came down from heaven and consumed them. 10And the devil, who deceived them, was thrown into the lake of fire and sulphur, where the beast and the false prophet are as well, and they will be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

11And I saw a great white throne and the one seated on it. Earth and heaven fled from his face, and no place was found for them. 12And I saw the dead, the great and the small, standing before the throne, and books were opened. And another book was opened, which is the book of life. And the dead were judged by what was written in the books, according to their works. 13And the sea gave up the dead who were in it, and death and Hades gave up the dead who were in them, and they were judged, each one according to their works. 14And death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death, the lake of fire. 15And if anyone was not found written in the book of life, he was thrown into the lake of fire.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

An angel comes down from heaven, "holding in his hand the key of the abyss and a great chain". The beast and the false prophet have already been captured without a fight (19:20). Now the third of the evil trio has his turn: "the dragon, the ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan." The angel seizes him and binds him for a thousand years.

Chapter 20 does not necessarily follow chapter 19 in time. As so often in Revelation, the vision goes back and shows the same period from another angle. In chapter 12 we already saw the dragon thrown down when the child was "snatched up to God and to his throne" (12:5, 9) – the victory of the cross, the resurrection and the ascension. The Lord Jesus himself spoke like this. He says that no one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his goods unless he first binds the strong man (Mark 3:27). And before the cross: Now the ruler of this world will be driven out; and when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself (John 12:31–32).

The thousand years, then, are not a period on a calendar, but the time in which we now live, between Christ's first and second coming: a full, long time measured out by God (ten times ten times ten), over against Satan's "short time" at the end. Christ reigns now, and his people reign with him. Other Christians read the chapter differently: premillennialists – among them already church fathers such as Justin and Irenaeus, and later the dispensationalists – expect a literal thousand-year kingdom on earth after Christ's return, while postmillennialists expect a golden age of the gospel before his coming. Here we follow the line known in the church since Augustine (The City of God, book 20).

The first readers knew of brothers and sisters who had been "beheaded because of the testimony of Jesus and because of the word of God." What had become of those who had lost their lives for their witness? Of the souls under the altar (6:9)? John now sees them on thrones: "And they lived and reigned with Christ for a thousand years." To the world they had lost; with Christ they reign.

Insights from the Greek

  • Bound him (edēsen – ἔδησεν, v. 2): From deō, "bind" – the same verb Jesus uses in Mark 3:27 for binding the strong man. The binding of Satan begins at the cross.
  • So that he would deceive the nations no more (hina mē planēsē eti ta ethnē – ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη, v. 3): The text does not say that Satan can do nothing any more, but that he can no longer deceive the nations. For centuries the nations lay in darkness, and God was known only in Israel. Now the gospel goes out to every tribe and language and people and nation (5:9), and Satan cannot stop it.
  • They lived (ezēsan – ἔζησαν, vv. 4–5): The verb can mean "came to life" or "lived". Our translation chooses the plain "lived". John sees tas psychas (τὰς ψυχὰς), "the souls", of the faithful: they are dead to the world, but they live with Christ.
  • The first resurrection (hē anastasis hē prōtē – ἡ ἀνάστασις ἡ πρώτη, vv. 5–6): Whoever belongs to Christ already shares in his resurrection. Jesus says that whoever hears his word and believes has passed from death to life (John 5:24), and Paul writes that God raised us up with Christ and seated us with him in the heavenly places (Ephesians 2:6). That is why "the second death has no authority" – as Smyrna was promised (2:11).
  • The book of life (tē biblō tēs zōēs – τῇ βίβλῳ τῆς ζωῆς, v. 15): The picture comes from the Old Testament: Moses asks God rather to blot him out of his book (Exodus 32:32–33), and Daniel hears that everyone found written in the book will be delivered (Daniel 12:1). In Revelation it is "the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb" (13:8).

Core Theological Truths

  1. Christ reigns now, and Satan is on a chain. Being bound does not mean that evil is quiet; a dog on a chain can still bark and bite within the reach of its chain. But he cannot stop the gospel. That there are churches today in almost every country of the world is itself the proof of the chain.

  2. The faithful dead are not lost. The world beheaded them; God seated them on thrones. The picture comes from Daniel 7:9, 22, where thrones are set up and judgement is given to the saints of the Most High. Whoever dies in Christ does not go into nothingness, but to the throne.

  3. The last rebellion is short, and the end comes from the throne. At the end Satan is released "for a short time", and he gathers Gog and Magog – Ezekiel's picture of the last enemy from the north (Ezekiel 38–39) – against "the camp of the saints and the beloved city." The church is like Israel in the wilderness: a camp, a pilgrim people. Again there is no battle: "fire came down from heaven and consumed them" (cf. 2 Kings 1:10–12).

  4. We are judged according to our works, but saved by the Lamb's book. Before the great white throne everyone stands, "the great and the small". The books are opened, and our lives count (Psalm 62:12). But there is another book, and it decides. Whoever is written in it is there not because their works were good enough, but because the Lamb was slain. And then "death and Hades" themselves are thrown into the lake of fire: the last enemy to be destroyed is death (1 Corinthians 15:26).

Living It Out

It does not always feel as though Satan is bound. We see war, persecution and broken families, and our own struggle with sin. Revelation 20 does not deny this. But it shows us what the news does not: the gospel is still going out. People in countries where the church was unknown for centuries pray to Jesus today in their own language. The chain holds.

For anyone grieving a loved one who died in faith, this chapter is a gentle comfort. They are not gone. John sees them living and reigning with Christ. The second death has no authority over them – nor over you, if you belong to him.

And the judgement? Many believers become afraid when they read about the great white throne. The books of our works tell the truth about us, and that is good: God takes our lives seriously. But the last word comes from another book. Jesus told his disciples to rejoice, not because the spirits were subject to them, but because their names were written in heaven (Luke 10:20).

This week, pray every day for one people or country where the gospel is still little known, and thank God for one loved one who died in faith and now lives with Christ.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Do you live as if Satan were free and Christ bound, or the other way round (vv. 2–4)?
  • Which door for the gospel has God opened around you that you have not yet used (v. 3)?
  • How do the words "they lived and reigned with Christ" change the way you think about your loved ones who have died (v. 4)?
  • For your salvation, do you trust in the books of your works or in the book of life (vv. 12, 15)?

Prayer

You have bound the strong man, Lord Jesus, and broken open his house. You reign now, and your people reign with you. Thank you that Satan cannot stop the gospel, and that people from every nation call on your name today.

Too easily I live as if evil had the upper hand, and I fear the judgement although you have already paid for my sin. Forgive me where my works have dishonoured you, and teach me to take seriously that my life counts before your throne.

Thank you for my loved ones who died in faith and now live with you. Thank you that my name is written in the book of life of the Lamb, not because I deserved it, but because you were slain. Use me to carry your gospel further, until death itself is destroyed. In your name, Amen.
