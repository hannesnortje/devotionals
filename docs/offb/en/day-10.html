A Door Open in Heaven: The Throne Behind the Curtain

Scripture Revelation 4:1–11

1After this I looked, and look, a door stood open in heaven, and the first voice, which I had heard speaking with me like a trumpet, said, "Come up here, and I will show you what must take place after this." 2At once I was in the Spirit. And look, a throne stood in heaven, and on the throne someone was seated. 3And the one seated there looked like jasper and carnelian, and around the throne was a rainbow that looked like an emerald. 4And around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and on the thrones sat twenty-four elders, dressed in white clothes, with golden crowns on their heads. 5And from the throne come lightning and sounds and thunder, and seven torches of fire are burning before the throne; these are the seven spirits of God. 6And before the throne there was something like a sea of glass, like crystal. And in the middle of the throne and around the throne were four living creatures, full of eyes in front and behind. 7The first living creature was like a lion, the second living creature was like a young bull, the third living creature had a face like a human being, and the fourth living creature was like a flying eagle. 8And the four living creatures, each of them with six wings, are full of eyes all around and within. And day and night they never rest, saying:

Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty,who was and who is and who is coming.

9And whenever the living creatures give glory and honour and thanks to the one seated on the throne, who lives for ever and ever, 10the twenty-four elders fall down before the one seated on the throne and worship the one who lives for ever and ever, and they cast their crowns before the throne, saying:

11Worthy are you, our Lord and God,to receive the glory and the honour and the power,for you created all things,and by your will they existed and were created.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday we stood in Laodicea, where the risen Christ stands outside the door of his own church and knocks (3:20). Today the picture is turned round: "After this I looked, and look, a door stood open in heaven." The one who wanted to come in at Laodicea now invites John to come in to him: "Come up here, and I will show you what must take place after this."

Think of the first readers: seven small churches in the Roman province of Asia, probably in the reign of the emperor Domitian (AD 81–96). In their cities Rome's throne was visible everywhere – temples for the emperor, his image on coins and in market squares. Pergamum was the place "where the throne of Satan is" (2:13). Anyone who saw the world only from the street could come to just one conclusion: Rome rules.

Then Revelation 4 draws back the curtain. That is what apokalypsis (1:1) means: an unveiling of what is happening behind the scenes. It may sound almost like a conspiracy theory – a hidden power struggle behind the official version that the emperor is lord – but it is the opposite: God himself opens the door, not to make us suspicious and afraid, but so that we may worship and endure. And the first thing John sees behind the curtain is not a timetable or a battlefield, but a throne: "and on the throne someone was seated."

John stands in a long line here. Whenever God's people suffered under earthly thrones, he drew back the curtain of his heavenly council a little way. Job sees only his losses, but the reader sees the heavenly council, where the accuser can do nothing without God's permission (Job 1–2). King Ahab sits on his throne in Samaria, but the prophet Micaiah son of Imlah sees the LORD on his throne with all the host of heaven around him (1 Kings 22:10, 19). The message is the same every time: the thrones of the earth do not have the last word.

Insights from the Greek

  • A door standing open (thyra ēneōgmenē – θύρα ἠνεῳγμένη, v. 1): The verb form describes a door that has been opened and stays open. John does not force heaven open; God has already done it.
  • What must take place (ha dei genesthai – ἃ δεῖ γενέσθαι, v. 1): The language of Daniel 2:28–29. Dei ("must") is not about a calendar but about God's plan: history is neither blind chance nor the whim of emperors.
  • Throne (thronos – θρόνος): Fourteen times in eleven verses. Everything stands around, before or in the middle of the throne: it is the centre of reality.
  • Worthy are you, our Lord and God (Axios ei, ho kyrios kai ho theos hēmōn – Ἄξιος εἶ, ὁ κύριος καὶ ὁ θεὸς ἡμῶν, v. 11): According to the Roman writer Suetonius (Domitian 13), Domitian wished to be addressed as "our lord and god". Many interpreters therefore hear in the elders' song a quiet, courageous counter-voice. And the reason for their praise is not power but creation: hoti sy ektisas ta panta (ὅτι σὺ ἔκτισας τὰ πάντα), "for you created all things".

Core Theological Truths

  1. The throne in heaven stands firm, even when the earth shakes. Everything that happens in the rest of the book – the seals, the trumpets, the bowls – comes from this throne. For people who saw the emperor's image on their coins every day, this was the best news of all: Rome is great, but Rome is not seated on the throne.

  2. What happens on earth is understood from the hidden scene. Satan may have a throne in Pergamum, but not in heaven, and he can do nothing that God does not permit. That does not make suffering hurt less, but it is never outside God's hand.

  3. The whole creation worships the Creator. The four living creatures recall Ezekiel's cherubim (Ezekiel 1:10; 10:14–15) and the seraphim of Isaiah 6:2–3; many interpreters think the lion, the bull, the human being and the eagle represent the whole of living creation. The twenty-four elders are probably the whole people of God – twelve tribes and twelve apostles (cf. 21:12–14) – though others think of angelic beings. The Bible's story begins with the Creator (Genesis 1), and it ends at the same throne: "Look, I am making all things new" (21:5).

  4. The crowns go back to the throne – and Christ shares that throne. The elders cast their crowns (stephanoi, victors' wreaths) before the throne. Everything they have received, they give back. And at the end of the seven letters Jesus said that he "sat down with my Father on his throne" (3:21). Tomorrow we shall see who stands in the middle of this throne.

Living It Out

Most of us live like the believers on the streets of those cities. We see the news, the budget, the diagnosis, and every day we hear an official version of who is in charge: the market, politics, the employer, or simply our own fears. Revelation 4 does not deny that these thrones exist. It simply puts them where they belong – far below the throne on which someone is seated.

That is why worship is not an escape from reality but the most honest look at it. When a small congregation sings on Sunday, it joins a song that goes on "day and night", even when you feel nothing of it. For the first readers it took courage to sing "Worthy are you, our Lord and God" in a city where the emperor claimed the same honour. For us it is no different: what you call "worthy" decides where your time, your money and your heart go.

And then there are the crowns. Each of us holds something tightly: a title, an achievement, our reputation, even our own hard-won faithfulness. The elders show us the freedom of laying it down. You do not have to keep your own throne standing; there is already one who reigns, and the door to him stands open.

Start tomorrow at the open door, before you even unlock your phone: read Revelation 4:8–11, name one "throne" that frightens you at the moment, and deliberately lay it before the throne on which he sits.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Which earthly "throne" seems to rule your life at the moment – a deadline, money, an illness, someone's opinion? What changes when you look at it through the open door of verse 1?
  • Does your day usually begin with the news or with the throne (v. 2)?
  • Which "crown" are you holding so tightly that you would find it hard to cast it before the throne (v. 10)?
  • In your decisions about time, money and attention, what do you actually call "worthy" (v. 11)?

Prayer

Holy Lord God, the Almighty, who was and who is and who is coming: today I stand with John at the open door and look up to your throne. You created all things, and by your will I exist. You alone are worthy – not the rulers of this world, not my fears, and not I myself.

I confess that I live as if the thrones of this earth had the last word, and that I cling to my little crowns as if my life depended on them. Teach me to lay them down before you.

Thank you that you have opened the door, and that the Lord Jesus, who sits with you on your throne, invites me to come in. Give me eyes of faith to see behind the curtain, and a heart that worships with heaven, so that today I may live faithfully and without fear. In Jesus' name, Amen.
