Small-group kit · Session 9 / 11

The Song on the Shore

Present ▶

The big ideaAfter the dragon and the beasts, the curtain opens on the Lamb standing on Zion with his people, who bear his name and sing the song of Moses and of the Lamb before a single bowl is poured out – God's judgement on evil is real and just, but those who belong to the Lamb are kept, and the end of evil has already been decided.

Timing

  • 5 min Welcome & opener
  • 15 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Is there a song that carries you through hard times – one you sing or hum when things are difficult? What does it do for you?

Read together · Revelation 14:1 – 16:21

Three readers, one per chapter. In 14:6–11 let three voices take the three angels; the whole group reads the song of 15:3–4 together; in chapter 16 the reader pauses briefly after each bowl, and the group reads the answer of the altar (16:7) aloud.

Where we are in the story

In chapter 13 the dragon and his two beasts seemed to rule the world, and no one could buy or sell without the beast's mark. Chapters 14–16 draw back the curtain again: the Lamb stands on Mount Zion with the whole people of God, angels proclaim an eternal gospel and the harvest of the earth is gathered in. Then come the seven last plagues: before a single bowl is poured out, the conquerors sing on the shore of the sea of glass, and then the bowls fall like the plagues of Egypt on the kingdom of the beast – until the voice from the throne cries, “It is done!”

A look at the Greek

  • gegrammenon epi tōn metōpōn autōn – γεγραμμένον ἐπὶ τῶν μετώπων αὐτῶν “written on their foreheads”: The verb form describes something that has been written and stays written. The mark of the beast has to be enforced (13:16); the name of the Lamb and of his Father is a lasting gift (14:1; cf. 7:3; 22:4). No one is unmarked – everyone belongs to someone.
  • tēn ōdēn Mōyseōs … kai tēn ōdēn tou arniou – τὴν ᾠδὴν Μωϋσέως … καὶ τὴν ᾠδὴν τοῦ ἀρνίου “the song of Moses … and the song of the Lamb”: Probably not two songs but one: the song of the exodus (Exodus 15), now sung about a greater exodus. The blood of the Passover lamb brought Israel out of Egypt; the blood of the Lamb has purchased people for God from every nation (5:9).
  • Gegonen – Γέγονεν “It is done!”: The perfect tense: something has happened and stands. The kings are still gathering for war, yet the outcome is already decided. The same word sounds again at the new creation (21:6).

Discussion

  1. After the beast's mark: “And I looked, and there was the Lamb, standing on Mount Zion”. The dragon stood on the sand of the sea (12:18); the Lamb stands on the rock of Zion. What would this have meant to a believer in Pergamum who felt almost alone in not bowing down? Where do you need this look behind the curtain today? (Revelation 14:1)
  2. The 144,000 have “his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads” and follow the Lamb wherever he goes. Which label (job, income, looks, failures) weighs on you most, and what changes when his name is written over it? Where are you reluctant to follow the Lamb at the moment? (Revelation 14:1–5)
  3. Before the fall of Babylon and the solemn warning, an angel flies with “an eternal gospel” for every nation: “Fear God and give him glory”. Why do you think the gospel comes first? And what does verse 13 say to someone who has lost a loved one in the Lord? (Revelation 14:6–13)
  4. The conquerors stand beside the sea of glass and sing “the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb”. Read Exodus 15:1–2, 11 and Jeremiah 10:6–7 aloud. What links do you see between Israel on the shore of the Red Sea and God's people here? What is the greater exodus the Lamb has brought about? (Revelation 15:2–4)
  5. The song comes before the bowls, and there is not a word in it about the singers' own suffering or courage – every line is about God. Where are you standing at the moment: still in the sea, or already on the shore? What would it mean to sing “just and true are your ways” there already? (Revelation 15:3–4)
  6. The bowls bring back the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 7–10). Twice we read that “they did not repent”, and three times that they blasphemed God. What does Pharaoh's story teach about why judgement alone does not change a heart? When something hurts, do you become softer or harder towards God? (Revelation 16:1–11)
  7. The altar says: “Yes, Lord God, the Almighty, true and just are your judgements.” And Christ says: “Look, I am coming like a thief. Blessed is the one who stays awake and keeps his clothes”. How do these voices comfort those who suffer injustice? What does “staying awake” mean for you – without calculating dates? (Revelation 16:5–17)

For the week ahead

  • Tonight write on a card: I belong to the Lamb. Put it by the mirror, and each morning read 15:3–4 aloud and thank God for one concrete work he has done in your life.
  • Take two minutes one evening to ask where you have fallen asleep this week – in prayer, honesty or love. Then pray for one person or place that suffers injustice, using the words of 16:7.

Pray together

Begin by reading 15:3–4 aloud together, or sing a song of praise the group knows. Then pray in twos or threes: thank the Lamb that his name is written over your life, name one situation in which you feel you are still in the sea, and pray for one place in the world that suffers injustice. Close together with 16:7: “Yes, Lord God, the Almighty, true and just are your judgements.”

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers three chapters and three devotionals (days 20–22). Chapter 16 is hard; do not skip it, but protect time for questions 4 and 5 – the song on the shore is the heart of the evening.
  • The 144,000 (14:1–5) are, as in 7:4–9, the whole people of God, not an elite group or a single nation. “Virgins” (14:4) is the Old Testament picture of soldiers keeping themselves pure for holy war (Deuteronomy 23:9–14; 1 Samuel 21:5) and of Israel following her God faithfully (Jeremiah 2:2) – it does not mean that only the unmarried belong to the Lamb.
  • The bowls are not the next chapter in a calendar but the same reality as the trumpets (8–9), now in full seriousness: God's patience with evil has an end. Some group members may read them as a future period just before Christ's return (futurist), others only as Rome's judgement (preterist); name these views fairly in a sentence and come back to the pastoral message. Armageddon (16:16) is most likely “the mountain of Megiddo”, a symbol of the final revolt – the battle itself is never described. Steer away from maps and news headlines.
  • Handle the language of wrath with care (14:10–11, 19–20; 16). Some people grew up with preaching that made them afraid. Stress what the devotionals stress: this is comfort for those who suffer, not a weapon to threaten others; God's wrath is his faithfulness to the victims; those sealed by God do not stand under it (7:3; Exodus 8:22). Read the chapter about our own hearts, not about other people.
  • The new exodus runs through these chapters – the seal on the forehead, the song by the sea, the tent of witness (15:5; Exodus 40:34–35), the plagues. Let the group discover it in question 4 rather than explaining it in advance.
  • Point ahead: next week chapters 17–19 – the prostitute on the beast, the fall of Babylon, the wedding of the Lamb and the Rider called Faithful and True.

Scripture Revelation 14:1 – 16:21

1And I looked, and there was the Lamb, standing on Mount Zion, and with him a hundred and forty-four thousand who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads. 2And I heard a sound from heaven like the sound of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder; and the sound that I heard was like lyre players playing on their lyres. 3And they sing something like a new song before the throne and before the four living creatures and the elders; and no one could learn the song except the hundred and forty-four thousand, who have been purchased from the earth. 4These are the ones who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are virgins. These are the ones who follow the Lamb wherever he goes. These were purchased from among people as firstfruits for God and for the Lamb, 5and no lie was found in their mouth; they are without blemish.

6And I saw another angel flying in midheaven, with an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who are settled on the earth and to every nation and tribe and language and people. 7He said with a loud voice, "Fear God and give him glory, for the hour of his judgement has come; and worship the one who made heaven and earth and sea and the springs of water!"

8And another, a second angel, followed, saying, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the Great, who has made all the nations drink of the wine of the fury of her harlotry!"

9And another, a third angel, followed them, saying with a loud voice, "If anyone worships the beast and its image and receives a mark on his forehead or on his hand, 10he too will drink of the wine of the fury of God, poured undiluted into the cup of his wrath, and he will be tormented with fire and sulphur before holy angels and before the Lamb. 11And the smoke of their torment goes up for ever and ever, and they have no rest day or night, those who worship the beast and its image, and whoever receives the mark of its name." 12Here is the endurance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and faith in Jesus.

13And I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Write: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on." "Yes," says the Spirit, "so that they may rest from their toil, for their works go with them."

14And I looked, and there was a white cloud, and seated on the cloud was one like a son of man, with a golden crown on his head and a sharp sickle in his hand. 15And another angel came out of the temple, crying out with a loud voice to the one seated on the cloud, "Send out your sickle and reap, for the hour to reap has come, for the harvest of the earth is dry and ripe." 16And the one seated on the cloud swung his sickle over the earth, and the earth was reaped.

17And another angel came out of the temple in heaven, and he too had a sharp sickle. 18And another angel came from the altar, the one who has authority over the fire, and he called with a loud voice to the one who had the sharp sickle, saying, "Send out your sharp sickle and gather the clusters from the vine of the earth, for its grapes are ripe." 19And the angel swung his sickle over the earth and gathered the vintage of the vine of the earth and threw it into the great winepress of the fury of God. 20And the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood came out of the winepress up to the horses' bridles, for a distance of a thousand six hundred stadia.

1And I saw another sign in heaven, great and wonderful: seven angels with seven plagues, the last ones, for with them the fury of God is completed.

2And I saw something like a sea of glass mixed with fire, and those who conquer the beast and its image and the number of its name, standing beside the sea of glass, holding lyres of God. 3And they sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying:

Great and wonderful are your works,Lord God, the Almighty;just and true are your ways,King of the ages.4Who will not fear you, Lord,and glorify your name?For you alone are holy.For all the nations will comeand worship before you,for your righteous acts have been revealed.

5And after this I looked, and the temple of the tent of witness in heaven was opened, 6and the seven angels who had the seven plagues came out of the temple, dressed in clean, shining linen, with golden sashes around their chests. 7And one of the four living creatures gave the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the fury of God, who lives for ever and ever. 8And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power, and no one could enter the temple until the seven plagues of the seven angels were completed.

1And I heard a loud voice from the temple saying to the seven angels, "Go and pour out the seven bowls of the fury of God on the earth!"

2And the first went and poured out his bowl on the earth; and a foul and evil sore came on the people who had the mark of the beast and who worshipped its image.

3And the second poured out his bowl into the sea; and it became blood like that of a dead man, and every living soul died, everything that was in the sea.

4And the third poured out his bowl into the rivers and the springs of water; and they became blood. 5And I heard the angel of the waters say:

Just are you, who is and who was, the Holy One,because you have judged these things;6for they poured out the blood of saints and prophets,and you have given them blood to drink.They deserve it.

7And I heard the altar say:

Yes, Lord God, the Almighty,true and just are your judgements.

8And the fourth poured out his bowl on the sun; and it was allowed to scorch people with fire. 9And the people were scorched with fierce heat, and they blasphemed the name of God, who has authority over these plagues, and they did not repent and give him glory.

10And the fifth poured out his bowl on the throne of the beast; and its kingdom was plunged into darkness, and people gnawed their tongues in pain, 11and they blasphemed the God of heaven because of their pains and their sores, and they did not repent of their works.

12And the sixth poured out his bowl on the great river, the Euphrates; and its water was dried up, so that the way would be prepared for the kings from the rising of the sun. 13And I saw coming out of the mouth of the dragon and out of the mouth of the beast and out of the mouth of the false prophet three unclean spirits like frogs. 14For they are spirits of demons that perform signs, and they go out to the kings of the whole world, to gather them for the war of the great day of God, the Almighty. 15– "Look, I am coming like a thief. Blessed is the one who stays awake and keeps his clothes, so that he does not walk around naked and people see his shame." – 16And they gathered them at the place that is called in Hebrew Armageddon.

17And the seventh poured out his bowl into the air; and a loud voice came out of the temple, from the throne, saying, "It is done!" 18And there were lightning and sounds and thunder, and there was a great earthquake, such as there had never been since people were on the earth, so great an earthquake, so mighty. 19And the great city split into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell. And God remembered Babylon the Great, to give her the cup of the wine of the fury of his wrath. 20And every island fled, and mountains were not found. 21And great hail, each stone as heavy as a talent, comes down from heaven on the people; and the people blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail, for its plague is exceedingly great.
