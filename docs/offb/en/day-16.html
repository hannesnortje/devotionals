The Little Scroll: Sweet in the Mouth, Bitter in the Stomach

Scripture Revelation 10:1–11

1And I saw another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, and the rainbow was over his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire, 2and he had in his hand a little scroll, opened. And he set his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the earth, 3and he cried out with a loud voice, as a lion roars. And when he cried out, the seven thunders spoke with their own voices. 4And when the seven thunders had spoken, I was about to write; and I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Seal up what the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write it down!"

5And the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and on the earth raised his right hand to heaven 6and swore by the one who lives for ever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, and the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it, that there will be no more delay, 7but that in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he is about to blow the trumpet, the mystery of God is fulfilled, as he announced the good news to his servants the prophets.

8And the voice that I had heard from heaven spoke with me again and said, "Go, take the scroll that lies open in the hand of the angel who is standing on the sea and on the earth." 9And I went to the angel and told him to give me the little scroll. And he says to me, "Take it and eat it up; it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey." 10And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it up, and it was sweet as honey in my mouth; and when I had eaten it, my stomach was made bitter. 11And they say to me, "You must prophesy again about many peoples and nations and languages and kings."

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday the chapter ended with hard words: after all the plagues, people did not repent. We expect the seventh trumpet to sound now. But as between the sixth and the seventh seal (chapter 7), another pause comes. In chapter 7 God's people were sealed; in chapters 10 and 11 they are sent. God's answer to a world that will not turn round is not more locusts, but his word in the mouth of his witnesses.

John saw "another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, and the rainbow was over his head, and his face was like the sun, and his feet were like pillars of fire". Every feature comes from Scripture. The pillar of cloud and fire led Israel through the wilderness (Exodus 13:21); the rainbow surrounds God's throne (4:3; Ezekiel 1:28) and recalls God's covenant with the earth after the flood (Genesis 9:13); the face like the sun is the face of Christ himself (1:16). Some therefore think that this is Christ himself; but John calls him an angel, a messenger who carries the glory of the one who sent him. He sets one foot on the sea and the other on the earth – the whole of creation is under the authority of the God who sends him.

Then he cries out "as a lion roars", and the seven thunders answer. Amos said: the lion has roared – who will not fear? The Lord GOD has spoken – who can but prophesy? (Amos 3:8). But when John is about to write, a voice says: "Seal up what the seven thunders have spoken, and do not write it down!" There are things that even the prophet may not pass on.

Then the angel raises his right hand and swears "by the one who lives for ever and ever". This echoes Daniel 12:7, where a man clothed in linen raises his hands to heaven and swears that the end will still take a time, times and half a time. Here the oath is different: "that there will be no more delay". For the souls under the altar, who had been told "to rest a little longer" (6:11), this is the answer to their "How long?"

And then John himself must take the little scroll and eat it. Ezekiel had the same experience. In exile God gave him a scroll, written on both sides with laments and mourning and woe, and said: eat this scroll. It was sweet as honey in his mouth – but afterwards he went away in bitterness of spirit (Ezekiel 2:8–3:3, 14). Jeremiah knew this too: God's words were a joy to him (Jeremiah 15:16), and yet the same word brought him persecution.

Insights from the Greek

  • A little scroll, opened (biblaridion ēneōgmenon – βιβλαρίδιον ἠνεῳγμένον, v. 2): The same verb form as the door in heaven that stood open (4:1). Many interpreters think that this is the scroll of chapter 5, which the Lamb has meanwhile opened and which is now given to John; others see a smaller, separate message. Either way, it is open, and it is meant for him.
  • Seal up (Sphragison – Σφράγισον, v. 4): Daniel had to seal up his visions until the time of the end (Daniel 12:4, 9). At the end of Revelation John will hear that he must not seal up the words of this book (22:10). But the seven thunders remain sealed: not everything is meant for us to know.
  • No more delay (chronos ouketi estai – χρόνος οὐκέτι ἔσται, v. 6): Chronos usually means "time". Older translations had "that there should be time no longer", which was often read as if time itself would stop. But the context – the oath of Daniel 12:7 and the "How long?" of 6:10 – is about God's waiting, not about the end of the calendar: God will wait no longer.
  • Take it and eat it up (Labe kai kataphage auto – Λάβε καὶ κατάφαγε αὐτό, v. 9): Kataphagein does not mean to taste but to eat up completely. The word must go into the prophet before it can come out of him.
  • You must prophesy again (Dei se palin prophēteusai – Δεῖ σε πάλιν προφητεῦσαι, v. 11): The same dei ("must") as in 4:1, "what must take place after this". The witness of God's people is not a side issue; it is part of God's plan for history.

Core Theological Truths

  1. God's answer to a hard world is his word. Where plagues did not bring repentance, God sends prophets "about many peoples and nations and languages and kings" – the same nations from which the Lamb purchased people (5:9). This was the calling of the churches in Rome's cities: not to withdraw, but to bear witness.

  2. Not everything is ours to know. The sealed thunders are a rebuke to anyone who wants to use Revelation as a puzzle for speculation and dates. God shows us enough to be faithful, not enough to control the future. He asks for trust, not calculation.

  3. God is not late. The angel swears that "the mystery of God is fulfilled, as he announced the good news to his servants the prophets" (cf. Amos 3:7). The verb for "announce the good news" is the verb of the gospel. In the New Testament the mystery of God is his plan in Christ, in which the nations become heirs together with Israel (Ephesians 3:6). Habakkuk had already learnt to wait for a vision that will surely come and not delay (Habakkuk 2:3).

  4. The word of God is sweet and bitter. Sweet, because it is the word of the Lamb's victory and of God's faithfulness. Bitter, because carrying it costs something: it includes judgement on the world and suffering for the witnesses, as chapter 11 will show. So it was for Jesus too, whose word was joy for the poor and yet led him to the cross.

Living It Out

Many of us read the Bible like someone sampling at a buffet: a verse here, a comforting thought there. That is not wrong, but John is told to eat up the scroll. God's word wants to go into us, shape our thinking and become part of us – and that takes time, repetition and silence.

When that happens, you will taste both the sweetness and the bitterness. The sweetness: the promise that you need not be afraid (1:17), the forgiveness of the Lamb. The bitterness: the word that says you must forgive the one who hurt you; the truth you have to speak at work or in your family and that may cost you something. If the Bible is only sweet to you, you may be reading only half of it. If it is only bitter, you have forgotten the Lamb.

The seven thunders are a comfort as well. You do not have to know everything about the future. You only have to be faithful with what God has actually shown. And if you have long been waiting for an answer, remember the angel's oath: with God there is no delay that breaks his promise.

This week choose one short passage – Revelation 10:8–11, for example – and read it slowly and aloud every day for three days. "Chew" on it. Then share one sentence from it with someone who needs it.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Do you read the Bible more like someone who tastes or like someone who eats (vv. 9–10)?
  • Which part of God's word is sweet to you at the moment, and which part bitter (v. 10)?
  • Which "sealed thunders" – questions about the future that God has not answered – make you restless (v. 4)? Can you leave them with him?
  • To which "peoples and nations and languages" – the people around you – is God sending you as a witness (v. 11)?

Prayer

Lord God, Creator of heaven and earth and sea, you live for ever and ever, and your word stands firm. Thank you that you are never late, and that the mystery of your plan in Christ is being fulfilled, as you promised your prophets.

Forgive me that I only taste your word and avoid the bitter parts. Forgive my curiosity about what you have sealed, and my slowness to be faithful with what you have shown.

Give me a hunger for your word. Let it be sweet in my mouth, and give me the courage to bear the bitterness when witness costs something. Send me to the people around me, so that they may hear the good news of the Lamb. In the name of the Lamb, Amen.
