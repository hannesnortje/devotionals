Behind the Curtain · Day 12 / 31

The Lamb Opens the Seals: Who Can Stand?

Scripture Revelation 6:1–17

1And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seven seals, and I heard one of the four living creatures say with a voice like thunder, "Come!" 2And I looked, and there was a white horse, and its rider had a bow; and a crown was given to him, and he went out conquering and to conquer.

3And when he opened the second seal, I heard the second living creature say, "Come!" 4And another horse came out, fiery red; and its rider was allowed to take peace from the earth, so that people would slaughter one another; and a great sword was given to him.

5And when he opened the third seal, I heard the third living creature say, "Come!" And I looked, and there was a black horse, and its rider had a pair of scales in his hand. 6And I heard something like a voice in the middle of the four living creatures, saying, "A quart of wheat for a denarius, and three quarts of barley for a denarius, but do not harm the oil and the wine!"

7And when he opened the fourth seal, I heard the voice of the fourth living creature say, "Come!" 8And I looked, and there was a pale green horse, and the name of its rider was Death, and Hades followed with him. And they were given authority over a fourth of the earth, to kill with sword and with famine and with pestilence and by the wild beasts of the earth.

9And when he opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slaughtered because of the word of God and because of the testimony they had. 10And they cried out with a loud voice, "How long, holy and true Master, will you not judge and avenge our blood on those who live on the earth?" 11And each of them was given a white robe, and they were told to rest a little longer, until their fellow servants and their brothers and sisters, who were about to be killed as they themselves had been, were complete in number.

12And I saw when he opened the sixth seal: there was a great earthquake, and the sun became black like sackcloth made of hair, and the whole moon became like blood, 13and the stars of the sky fell to the earth, as a fig tree drops its unripe figs when it is shaken by a strong wind. 14And the sky receded like a scroll being rolled up, and every mountain and island was moved from its place. 15And the kings of the earth and the nobles and the generals and the rich and the powerful and every slave and free person hid themselves in the caves and among the rocks of the mountains, 16and they say to the mountains and the rocks, "Fall on us and hide us from the face of the one seated on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb, 17for the great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?"

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday we saw the Lamb take the scroll from the hand of the one seated on the throne, while the whole of heaven sang. Today he begins to open it. "And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seven seals." What follows are some of the best-known and most feared images in the Bible: four horses and their riders, souls under the altar, and a sky rolled up like a scroll.

The images do not come out of nowhere. Zechariah saw horses of different colours, sent out by the LORD to patrol the earth (Zechariah 1:8–11; 6:1–8). And Ezekiel names four judgements that God brings on a land that has turned away: sword, famine, wild beasts and pestilence (Ezekiel 14:21) – exactly the list of verse 8. The first readers did not read this as fantasy. They knew war (the Jewish War and the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70, the civil war of AD 69), they knew famine (Acts 11:28), and on Rome's eastern frontier the Parthians, mounted archers, were a constant threat.

How do we read these seals? They are not a timetable of disasters that will begin only at the end of history. Jesus himself said that wars, famines and earthquakes would mark the whole time until his return, but that this is not yet the end (Mark 13:7–8). The first four seals show the world as it is between Christ's first and second coming – and who holds it in his hand. (Other interpreters, especially futurists, place the seals in a future period just before the return of Christ.) Only the sixth seal takes us right up to the great day itself.

Insights from the Greek

  • Come! (Erchou – Ἔρχου, vv. 1, 3, 5, 7): Each time one of the living creatures at the throne summons the rider. The horses do not charge out of their own accord; they come only when the Lamb opens a seal and heaven calls.
  • Was given (edothē – ἐδόθη, vv. 2, 4, 8, 11): The crown, the sword and the authority are given to the riders. The passive points to God as the one who permits – and limits. The black horse may not harm "the oil and the wine", and Death is given authority "over a fourth of the earth", not over the whole earth.
  • Slaughtered (esphagmenōn – ἐσφαγμένων, v. 9): The souls under the altar are those "who had been slaughtered because of the word of God". It is the same word as for the Lamb (5:6). The witnesses share the lot of their Lord. In the temple the blood of the sacrifices was poured out at the base of the altar (Leviticus 4:7); so their lives have been offered to God.
  • Master (despotēs – δεσπότης, v. 10): The word for a lord with full authority over his household and his servants. The martyrs are not crying out into emptiness; they call to the one who is truly in charge.
  • Who can stand? (tis dynatai stathēnai – τίς δύναται σταθῆναι, v. 17): The question of Nahum 1:6: before his fury who can stand?

Core Theological Truths

  1. The Lamb holds the seals in his hand – even in a world full of war and hunger. The conqueror on the white horse, the sword, the costly bread and death are real and terrible. (Some see the first rider as Christ himself, since he rides a white horse in 19:11; but because he rides out with the other three, many read him as conquest and war, perhaps even as a counterfeit of Christ.) Yet none of the riders rides out unless the Lamb opens a seal. History is not out of control.

  2. God hears the cry of his suffering people. "How long, holy and true Master" is the prayer of Habakkuk ("How long, LORD, shall I cry for help", Habakkuk 1:2) and of the psalms (Psalm 79:5, 10). God does not rebuke them for it. He gives each of them "a white robe" and tells them "to rest a little longer". Their cause is not forgotten; it is simply not yet the end.

  3. The great day is coming, and no one can stand in their own strength. The sixth seal uses the prophets' language for the day of the LORD: the sun black, the moon like blood, the sky rolled up like a scroll (Joel 2:31; Isaiah 34:4). Those who hide are the whole pyramid of Rome – kings, nobles, generals, the rich – but also "every slave and free person". They cry, as in Hosea 10:8, "Fall on us". Before the face of God there is no rank any more.

  4. The wrath of the Lamb is the wrath of the one who was slaughtered for the world. It sounds like a contradiction, but it is both the most serious and the most hopeful word in the chapter. The Judge is the same one who gave his blood. And the question of verse 17 receives its answer tomorrow: a great crowd that no one could count, "standing before the throne and before the Lamb" (7:9).

Living It Out

We do not have to look far to see the four riders. The news is full of war, of prices that put bread beyond people's reach, of disease and death. That can paralyse us with fear, or send us searching for signs and dates to work out how near the end is. Revelation 6 gives a different answer: look at who opens the seals. The Lamb who was slaughtered for the world does not let chaos have the last word.

This also gives us the freedom to pray honestly. The souls under the altar are not polite and content; they cry, "How long?" Perhaps there is an injustice in your life that has gone on for years, a child living far from God, an illness that will not lift. You may cry it out before the throne. Your cry is not ignored, and the "little longer" has an end that God knows.

And then the great question: "who can stand?" Not the powerful with their caves and rocks, and not we with our good intentions. Only those who stand with the Lamb, who have washed their robes in his blood (7:14). You do not have to flee from the face of the one seated on the throne; in Christ you may run to him.

When you read the news this week, choose one place of war or hunger and pray for it – and say it aloud: the Lamb holds the seals in his hand.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Which of the "riders" – war, economic pressure, illness, death – frightens you most at the moment (vv. 2–8)? What changes when you remember who opens the seals?
  • What have you long been crying "How long?" about (v. 10)? Do you dare to lay it honestly before God?
  • Where are you tempted to keep your witness quiet so that you need not pay the price of the souls under the altar (v. 9)?
  • On whom or what are you relying to stand on the great day (v. 17)?

Prayer

Lamb of God, you alone are worthy to open the seals. The wars, the hunger and the death of this world frighten me, but none of it is outside your hands. You are the holy and true Master, and you know the end of every "little longer".

I live so easily in fear, Lord, and I keep my faith quiet when witness would cost something. Hear the cry of my heart, and the cry of your children who suffer today because of the word of God. How long, Lord? I lay my questions before your throne.

Thank you that I need not hide on the great day, because you were slaughtered and my robes have been washed in your blood. Let me stand with you today, faithful and without fear, until I stand with the great crowd before the throne and before the Lamb. Amen.
