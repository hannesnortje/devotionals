Look, I Am Making All Things New: A New Heaven and a New Earth

Scripture Revelation 21:1–8

1And I saw a new heaven and a new earth; for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea is no more. 2And I saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband. 3And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying:

Look, God's tent is with people,and he will spread his tent with them,and they will be his peoples,and God himself will be with them,4and he will wipe away every tear from their eyes,and death will be no more,nor grief nor crying nor pain will be any more.The former things have passed away.

5And the one seated on the throne said, "Look, I am making all things new." And he says, "Write, for these words are faithful and true." 6And he said to me, "It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life as a gift. 7The one who conquers will inherit these things, and I will be his God, and he will be my son. 8But for the cowardly and the faithless and the abominable and murderers and the sexually immoral and sorcerers and idolaters and all liars, their share will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulphur, which is the second death."

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday earth and heaven fled from the face of the one seated on the great white throne, and death itself was thrown into the lake of fire. Today John sees what remains when everything evil is gone: "And I saw a new heaven and a new earth; for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea is no more."

The Bible's first sentence says that in the beginning God created the heavens and the earth (Genesis 1:1). Now, at the end, the same two words stand again, but with the word "new" beside them. Isaiah promised it: God will create a new heaven and a new earth, and the former things will no longer be remembered (Isaiah 65:17). The story that began in Genesis 3 with a curse and with tears ends here with a God who will "wipe away every tear from their eyes".

And the sea? In the Old Testament the sea is often the picture of chaos and threat; out of the sea came Daniel's beasts (Daniel 7:3), and in Revelation the beast of chapter 13. And the sea was Babylon's trade route: when she fell, the sailors stood far off and wept, for across the sea the wealth of the world sailed to Rome (18:17–19). In the new creation there is no more chaos, no more separation – and no market that buys and sells everything.

Then comes the heart of the vision. "Look, God's tent is with people, and he will spread his tent with them." This is the language of the exodus. In the wilderness God told Moses to make him a sanctuary, so that he might dwell in their midst (Exodus 25:8), and his glory filled the tent (Exodus 40:34). The exodus from Egypt was never just an escape; it led into the promised land and to a God who dwells with his people. So it is with the new exodus of Revelation. When Babylon fell, the voice from heaven called: "Come out of her, my people" (18:4). It is an exodus out of Babylon, not out of creation. The goal is not that we escape to a heaven far away, but that God comes down and dwells with us.

Insights from the Greek

  • New (kainon – καινὸν, v. 1): Kainos often points not just to something young or recent, but to something new in kind. Interpreters differ on how we should picture it – a wholly new creation, or the old creation purified and renewed – but the point is the same: the first order, marked by sin, death and tears, is over.
  • Passed away (apēlthan – ἀπῆλθαν, vv. 1, 4): The same verb stands in the lament over Babylon: "the fruit your soul longed for has gone from you" (18:14, apēlthen – ἀπῆλθεν). Babylon's luxury was the first to pass away. Now the whole first heaven and earth go, and everything Babylon had for sale goes with them.
  • God's tent (hē skēnē tou theou – ἡ σκηνὴ τοῦ θεοῦ, v. 3): The word for the tabernacle in the wilderness. John 1:14 uses its verb: the Word became flesh and pitched his tent among us. What began in Jesus becomes true here for ever. And the promise is in the plural: "his peoples" – people from all the nations (cf. Zechariah 2:11).
  • Look, I am making all things new (Idou kaina poiō panta – Ἰδοὺ καινὰ ποιῶ πάντα, v. 5): This is one of the few places in the book where the one seated on the throne speaks himself (cf. 1:8). He does not just make new things; he makes all things new. He does not throw his creation away. And the verb is in the present tense: whoever is in Christ is already a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17).
  • As a gift (dōrean – δωρεάν, v. 6): Freely, without payment – as in Isaiah 55:1, where God invites the thirsty to come and buy without money.

Core Theological Truths

  1. God does not give up his creation; he makes it new. The Bible does not end with souls escaping to a cloud, but with a new heaven and a new earth. His last word about the world is not destruction but renewal.

  2. The goal of everything is God dwelling with his people. Eden, the tabernacle, the temple, Jesus pitching his tent among us, the church as the temple of the Spirit – every step in the story points here. "And God himself will be with them." This is the promised land of the new exodus.

  3. Babylon's wealth belongs to the things that pass away. Babylon staked everything on the first heaven and the first earth, and all she had for sale – even people – passes away with them. In the new creation the water of life is given as a gift. For here we have no city that lasts; rather, we are seeking the one that is to come (Hebrews 13:14).

  4. The thirsty receive it as a gift; the one who conquers inherits everything. "The one who conquers will inherit these things" is the last of the promises to the conquerors of chapters 2–3, and it is the promise to David's son (2 Samuel 7:14) that now holds for every believer: "I will be his God, and he will be my son." The list in verse 8 begins with "the cowardly": not people who are afraid and weak, but people who out of fear gave up Christ and chose Babylon. It is a warning, but the door stands open: whoever is thirsty may come.

Living It Out

Some tears are not wiped away in this life. The child who was never born, the marriage that broke, the loved one who died of cancer, the body that no longer obeys. Revelation 21 does not give an explanation for every tear. It gives something better: a promise that God himself will wipe each one away – not an angel, God himself.

This vision also helps us look honestly at what we invest in. Our time, our money and our dreams often go to things that pass away with the first earth. Creation is not bad – God is making it new – but our hearts should not hang on Babylon's luxury. Whoever knows that the new creation is coming can live today with open hands.

And because he says "I am making all things new" in the present tense, you do not have to wait to taste something of it. Every time someone comes to faith, every time a broken relationship is healed, every time a church welcomes a stranger, the new creation is already beginning.

Write down today one tear you are still carrying, and read Revelation 21:3–5 beside it. Then say to God: This one too you will wipe away.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • What tear or loss are you carrying just now, and what does it mean to you that God himself will wipe it away (v. 4)?
  • Do you invest more in things that pass away with the first earth than in the city that is coming (v. 1)?
  • Where can you be a sign of the new creation this week – at home, at work, in your church (v. 5)?
  • Are you thirsty for the water of life, or are you still trying to pay for what God gives as a gift (v. 6)?

Prayer

Lord God, the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end: you created the heavens and the earth, and you are making all things new. I worship you, for you do not abandon your creation, and your purpose is to dwell with your people yourself.

Forgive me that my heart clings so easily to the things of the first earth – to possessions, security and the luxury of Babylon. Forgive me for the times I kept silent out of fear, and for the lies I have believed about what will make me happy.

Thank you that you will wipe away every tear, even those I am still carrying today. Thank you for the water of life that you give as a gift. Lead me on the way of the new exodus, out of Babylon, to the day when your tent is spread among us and you will be my God. In Jesus' name, Amen.
