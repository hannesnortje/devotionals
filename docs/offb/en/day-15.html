Behind the Curtain · Day 15 / 31

The Army from the Abyss: When People Love the Darkness

Scripture Revelation 9:1–21

1And the fifth angel blew the trumpet; and I saw a star that had fallen from heaven to the earth, and the key to the shaft of the abyss was given to him. 2And he opened the shaft of the abyss, and smoke went up from the shaft like the smoke of a great furnace, and the sun and the air were darkened by the smoke of the shaft. 3And out of the smoke came locusts onto the earth, and they were given authority like the authority that the scorpions of the earth have. 4And they were told not to harm the grass of the earth or any green thing or any tree, but only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads. 5And they were allowed not to kill them, but to torment them for five months; and their torment was like the torment of a scorpion when it stings a person. 6And in those days people will seek death and will not find it; they will long to die, and death flees from them.

7And the shapes of the locusts were like horses prepared for war; and on their heads was something like crowns, like gold, and their faces were like human faces. 8And they had hair like women's hair, and their teeth were like lions' teeth. 9And they had breastplates like breastplates of iron, and the rumble of their wings was like the rumble of chariots with many horses rushing into war. 10And they have tails like scorpions, and stings, and in their tails is their authority to harm people for five months. 11They have as king over them the angel of the abyss; his name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in Greek he has the name Apollyon.

12The first woe has passed; look, two woes are still coming after this.

13And the sixth angel blew the trumpet; and I heard a voice from the horns of the golden altar that is before God, 14saying to the sixth angel, who had the trumpet, "Release the four angels who are bound at the great river, the Euphrates!" 15And the four angels were released, who had been made ready for the hour and day and month and year, to kill a third of humankind. 16And the number of the mounted troops was twice ten thousands of ten thousands; I heard their number. 17And this is how I saw the horses in the vision and those who sat on them: they had breastplates fiery red and hyacinth blue and sulphur yellow; and the heads of the horses were like lions' heads, and out of their mouths come fire and smoke and sulphur. 18By these three plagues a third of humankind was killed, by the fire and the smoke and the sulphur that come out of their mouths. 19For the authority of the horses is in their mouths and in their tails; for their tails are like serpents, with heads, and with them they do harm.

20And the rest of humankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not even repent of the works of their hands, so as to stop worshipping the demons and the idols of gold and silver and bronze and stone and wood, which can neither see nor hear nor walk. 21And they did not repent of their murders or of their sorceries or of their sexual immorality or of their thefts.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

After the first four trumpets – hail, blood, bitter water and darkness, as in Egypt – an eagle in midheaven cried, "Woe, woe, woe". Now the first two of those woes arrive. The exodus line continues, but the curtain opens further. At the plagues in Egypt God said that he would bring judgement on all the gods of Egypt (Exodus 12:12). The plagues were never just natural disasters; they were a war against the powers behind Pharaoh. In Revelation 9 we see those powers themselves. For the first time the army of the abyss steps forward – and at the end of the chapter it is said outright: behind the idols stand demons (v. 20).

At the fifth trumpet John sees "a star that had fallen from heaven to the earth, and the key to the shaft of the abyss was given to him". In Revelation stars often stand for angels (1:20), and in 20:1 an angel comes with the same key. Others think of a fallen power, like the morning star of Isaiah 14:12 that fell from heaven. Either way, the key is given. Even the abyss opens only when God allows it.

From the shaft smoke rises "like the smoke of a great furnace" – the smoke of Sodom (Genesis 19:28) – and "the sun and the air were darkened". Out of the darkness come locusts: the eighth plague of Egypt (Exodus 10:12–15), merged with the prophet Joel's locust army charging like horses, with teeth like lions (Joel 1:4–6; 2:4–11). But these locusts do not eat grass. They are to harm "only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads". Their king has a name in two languages: Abaddon, Hebrew for "destruction" (Job 26:6), and Apollyon, Greek for "destroyer". Many interpreters hear a play on Apollo, one of Rome's great gods. The locust was one of Apollo's symbols, and emperors such as Nero liked to be portrayed as Apollo. What Rome celebrated as glory is, seen from heaven, the king of the abyss.

At the sixth trumpet a voice sounds "from the horns of the golden altar" – the same altar where yesterday the prayers rose. Four angels "bound at the great river, the Euphrates" are released. For John's readers the Euphrates was the eastern frontier of the empire, beyond which the dreaded Parthian horsemen waited; for Israel it was the river across which Assyria and Babylon had come (Isaiah 8:7). Then John says, "I heard their number" – twice ten thousands of ten thousands, an army too large to grasp, with fire, smoke and sulphur as over Sodom (Genesis 19:24).

For the churches in Asia the message was clear: the powers behind Rome are real, and they are cruel – but they are on a leash, and they can strike only those who do not belong to God. And then come the most shocking words of the chapter: "And the rest of humankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not even repent of the works of their hands". Like Pharaoh, whose heart grew harder after every plague (Exodus 7:13; 9:35), the world clings to its idols.

Insights from the Greek

  • I heard their number (ēkousa ton arithmon autōn – ἤκουσα τὸν ἀριθμὸν αὐτῶν, v. 16): John used exactly the same words when he counted the sealed: "And I heard the number of those who were sealed" (7:4). Two armies, both counted: the hundred and forty-four thousand of the Lamb, and the countless horsemen of the abyss. This is John's way of thinking in opposites – light or darkness, life or death, seal or no seal. There is no third army. Even the locusts are a counterfeit: crowns "like gold", faces "like human faces", teeth "like lions' teeth" – a distorted echo of the living creatures at the throne (4:7).
  • The abyss (tēs abyssou – τῆς ἀβύσσου, vv. 1–2): In the Greek Old Testament abyssos is the primeval deep (tehom) of Genesis 1:2, the chaos before God's creating word, and in Jesus' day the place to which the demons did not want to be sent (Luke 8:31). Behind the locusts stands the power of chaos that wants to undo creation.
  • Torment (basanismos – βασανισμὸς, vv. 5–6): A word for pain that goes deep and will not stop; the same word family is used for the woman in torment in childbirth (12:2). The locusts may not kill, only torment for five months – a limited time. And then: in those days "people will seek death and will not find it". Without God even death becomes an enemy that runs away – the opposite of the believer, who has already passed from death to life (John 5:24).
  • They did not repent (oude metenoēsan – οὐδὲ μετενόησαν, v. 20; ou metenoēsan – οὐ μετενόησαν, v. 21): Metanoeō is a turning round of mind and life. It is the same verb that Jesus used in five of the seven letters (2:5, 16, 21; 3:3, 19). The call to repent is addressed first to the church, then to the world.
  • Sorceries (pharmakōn – φαρμάκων, v. 21): The word comes from pharmakon, a remedy, poison or potion. In the ancient world it was the magic by which people tried to manipulate the gods and fate – like Nineveh, the "mistress of sorceries" (Nahum 3:4).

Core Theological Truths

  1. Two armies, two marks, no neutral ground. Revelation 9 pulls back the curtain on a cosmic war. There are only two sides: those with God's seal and those without it. The locusts may not touch God's people (v. 4), as John writes elsewhere: the evil one cannot touch the one born of God (1 John 5:18). The question is not whether you are in this war, but on which side.

  2. Evil devours its own. Notice whom the destroyer torments: not God's people, but those who serve him. The king of the abyss is a cruel master. This is what Paul means when he says three times that God gives people over to what they themselves have chosen (Romans 1:24, 26, 28). The darkness is not neutral; it consumes the people who serve it.

  3. People love the darkness rather than the light. Why do people not repent? Not because they have seen too few signs. John had already said it in his Gospel: the light has come into the world, and people loved darkness rather than light (John 3:19); though Jesus had done so many signs, they did not believe in him (John 12:37). People become like the idols they worship – blind, deaf and lame (Psalm 115:4–8) – and behind the idols stand demons (Deuteronomy 32:17). Nor does idolatry stay in the temple: it leads to murders, sorceries, sexual immorality and thefts – God's commandments broken one after another.

  4. God does not force love – the Lamb wins hearts. Plagues can break a person, but they cannot make anyone love. It is not that God is powerless, but that love cannot be forced. That is why the story does not end here: in chapters 10 and 11 God sends his witnesses, and afterwards people "gave glory to the God of heaven" (11:13). It is not fear but God's kindness that leads to repentance (Romans 2:4). The Lamb conquers through witness and self-giving, not through locusts.

Living It Out

For the churches in Asia this chapter pulled back the curtain: behind Apollo's temples, the emperor's glory and the idols in the marketplace stands the king of the abyss. And yet they were safe – not because they did not suffer, but because they were sealed. The destroyer could not touch them. We live in the same war, even if the front line looks different.

Revelation 9 first asks us: whose side are you on? There is no neutral ground between the army of the Lamb and the army of the abyss. But if you belong to Christ, you need not fear the powers of darkness. They are real, but they are on a leash, and they cannot touch you.

Second, it asks: what do you love more? The people in Revelation 9 did not reject God because they could not see him, but because they loved the darkness more. That is in our hearts too. The works of our hands – our career, our savings, our reputation, the phone in our hand – can neither see nor hear nor walk, and yet we cling to them as though our life depended on them. And slowly we become like them: deaf to God, blind to our neighbour. Crises alone do not change us. How often has an illness, an accident or a loss shaken us awake – and a few weeks later everything was back as it was?

The good news is that God does not win with locusts but with a Lamb. Jesus did not only tell the churches to repent; he also said, "Look, I am standing at the door and knocking" (3:20). Repentance is not a price we pay to put God in a good mood; it is turning back to someone who is already waiting for us. Joel's locusts ended with the same invitation: tear your hearts, return to the LORD, for he is gracious and compassionate (Joel 2:13).

Ask yourself honestly today: which work of my hands has taken my heart captive? Name it before God, step out of the darkness, and take one small step towards the light.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Do you live as though there were neutral ground between the Lamb and the abyss? Where do you try to stand on both sides (v. 4; 7:4)?
  • What, honestly, do you love more than the light? Which works of your hands do you trust more than God (v. 20)?
  • Which crisis in your life turned you to God – and which one made you harder instead (v. 20)?
  • In what ways do you try, as with pharmaka, to control your life and your fate yourself instead of trusting God (v. 21)?

Prayer

Lord God, you hold the keys of heaven and of the abyss. Nothing happens outside the limits you set, and your people are marked with your seal. Thank you that I belong to you, and that the powers of darkness cannot touch me, for they are on your leash.

My heart is harder than I want to admit, Lord. I often love the darkness more than the light, and I cling to the works of my hands – things that can neither see nor hear nor save. I confess that I have sometimes become like my idols: deaf to your voice and blind to my neighbour. Forgive me.

Lord Jesus, Lamb of God, you win hearts not with fear but with your love. Knock at my door again today – I will open. Draw me out of the darkness into your light. Amen.
