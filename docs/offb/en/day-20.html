Behind the Curtain · Day 20 / 31

The Lamb on Mount Zion: His Name on Our Foreheads

Scripture Revelation 14:1–20

1And I looked, and there was the Lamb, standing on Mount Zion, and with him a hundred and forty-four thousand who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads. 2And I heard a sound from heaven like the sound of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder; and the sound that I heard was like lyre players playing on their lyres. 3And they sing something like a new song before the throne and before the four living creatures and the elders; and no one could learn the song except the hundred and forty-four thousand, who have been purchased from the earth. 4These are the ones who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are virgins. These are the ones who follow the Lamb wherever he goes. These were purchased from among people as firstfruits for God and for the Lamb, 5and no lie was found in their mouth; they are without blemish.

6And I saw another angel flying in midheaven, with an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who are settled on the earth and to every nation and tribe and language and people. 7He said with a loud voice, "Fear God and give him glory, for the hour of his judgement has come; and worship the one who made heaven and earth and sea and the springs of water!"

8And another, a second angel, followed, saying, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the Great, who has made all the nations drink of the wine of the fury of her harlotry!"

9And another, a third angel, followed them, saying with a loud voice, "If anyone worships the beast and its image and receives a mark on his forehead or on his hand, 10he too will drink of the wine of the fury of God, poured undiluted into the cup of his wrath, and he will be tormented with fire and sulphur before holy angels and before the Lamb. 11And the smoke of their torment goes up for ever and ever, and they have no rest day or night, those who worship the beast and its image, and whoever receives the mark of its name." 12Here is the endurance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and faith in Jesus.

13And I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Write: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on." "Yes," says the Spirit, "so that they may rest from their toil, for their works go with them."

14And I looked, and there was a white cloud, and seated on the cloud was one like a son of man, with a golden crown on his head and a sharp sickle in his hand. 15And another angel came out of the temple, crying out with a loud voice to the one seated on the cloud, "Send out your sickle and reap, for the hour to reap has come, for the harvest of the earth is dry and ripe." 16And the one seated on the cloud swung his sickle over the earth, and the earth was reaped.

17And another angel came out of the temple in heaven, and he too had a sharp sickle. 18And another angel came from the altar, the one who has authority over the fire, and he called with a loud voice to the one who had the sharp sickle, saying, "Send out your sharp sickle and gather the clusters from the vine of the earth, for its grapes are ripe." 19And the angel swung his sickle over the earth and gathered the vintage of the vine of the earth and threw it into the great winepress of the fury of God. 20And the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood came out of the winepress up to the horses' bridles, for a distance of a thousand six hundred stadia.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday was a heavy day. The dragon on the sand, the beast from the sea, the beast from the earth, and a mark without which no one can buy or sell. A reader in Pergamum or Thyatira might have wondered whether anyone was left who had not bowed down. Then John says: "And I looked, and there was the Lamb, standing on Mount Zion". The dragon stands on the sand of the sea; the Lamb stands on the rock of Zion.

Zion was the mountain of Jerusalem, the place where God installed his king (Psalm 2:6) and where, the prophets said, he himself would reign: "and the LORD will reign over them on Mount Zion from now on and for ever" (Micah 4:7). John does not mean the hill on the map. The letter to the Hebrews says to ordinary believers: "But you have approached Mount Zion and the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem" (Hebrews 12:22). Zion is the place where God's people stand with their King – already now.

With the Lamb stand "a hundred and forty-four thousand who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads." We met them in chapter 7: the whole people of God, sealed before the storm. Here the seal is given its content. It is a name. In Israel the high priest wore a golden plate on his forehead with the words "Holy to the LORD" (Exodus 28:36–38). Now the whole people bear the Name, as a kingdom of priests. And in the last chapter of the Bible it rings out again: "his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4).

The rest of the chapter is a preview of everything still to come – the eternal gospel, the fall of Babylon, the two harvests – and it begins with the Lamb and his people.

Insights from the Greek

  • Written on their foreheads (gegrammenon epi tōn metōpōn autōn – γεγραμμένον ἐπὶ τῶν μετώπων αὐτῶν, v. 1): The verb form describes something that has been written and stays written. The name of the Lamb is a lasting gift. (The SBLGNT has "his name and his Father's name"; older editions only the Father's name.)
  • Those who follow the Lamb (hoi akolouthountes tō arniō – οἱ ἀκολουθοῦντες τῷ ἀρνίῳ, v. 4): The present tense describes a way of life: they keep following, "wherever he goes". It is Jesus' own call to his disciples: Follow me. Of the beast's worshippers it was said that "the whole earth followed the beast in amazement" (13:3); these people follow the Lamb.
  • Firstfruits (aparchē – ἀπαρχὴ, v. 4): The first sheaf of the harvest, dedicated to God as the pledge of the whole harvest (Leviticus 23:10). Jeremiah calls Israel the firstfruits of God's harvest (Jeremiah 2:3). The Lamb's people are God's first sheaf from humanity.
  • An eternal gospel (euangelion aiōnion – εὐαγγέλιον αἰώνιον, v. 6): The only time the word "gospel" occurs in Revelation – and it is proclaimed to "every nation and tribe and language and people". Even in the shadow of the beast the door is still open.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Those who belong to the Lamb bear his name. Yesterday's question – whose are you? – receives its answer today: the name of the Father and the Son, given to those "who have been purchased from the earth" (v. 3; cf. 5:9). It is not enforced by threats but given by grace. The names of this world will pass away; his name remains.

  2. The Lamb's people are an army that fights as the Lamb fights. That they are "virgins" does not mean that only the unmarried belong to the Lamb. The Old Testament uses the picture of soldiers keeping themselves pure for holy war (Deuteronomy 23:9–14; 1 Samuel 21:5) and of Israel as the bride who faithfully follows her God (Jeremiah 2:2). And "no lie was found in their mouth" – like the remnant of whom Zephaniah says that they speak no lie (Zephaniah 3:13). Against the beast's deception stands a people of the truth.

  3. The judgement is serious, but the gospel comes first. The first angel cries, "Fear God and give him glory", and worship the one who made all things – as Jonah confessed to the sailors that he feared the God of heaven, "he who made the sea and the dry land" (Jonah 1:9). Only then come the fall of Babylon (Isaiah 21:9) and the warning to those who worship the beast, in the language of Sodom's fire and sulphur (Genesis 19:24). God warns because he wants people to turn back.

  4. The Son of Man will reap. On the white cloud sits "one like a son of man" (Daniel 7:13) with a sickle. The prophet Joel had already said: Send out the sickle, for the harvest is ripe (Joel 3:13). Many interpreters read the grain harvest (vv. 14–16) as the gathering of God's people, and the grapes in the winepress (vv. 17–20) as judgement (cf. Isaiah 63:1–6). The winepress is trodden "outside the city", over an area as large as the land itself – and some recall that Jesus too suffered outside the city (Hebrews 13:12).

Living It Out

We live in an age of labels. Your job, your income, your looks and your number of followers tell the world who you are. Many of us carry those labels heavily, as if they were written on our foreheads. Revelation 14 says: another name has been written over you, and it is neither your achievement nor your failure. It is the name of the Father and of the Lamb.

To bear that name is first of all a comfort. On the days when you feel that the beast is stronger and your faith is small, you still stand with the Lamb on Zion. It is not you who keep yourself there. He has purchased you.

But it is also a calling. Of those who bear his name it is said: "These are the ones who follow the Lamb wherever he goes." That sometimes means going where you would rather not: to the difficult conversation, to forgiveness, to the people others avoid. And it means being a person of the truth in a world full of half-truths.

And then there is the comfort of verse 13 for everyone who has lost a loved one in the Lord: "Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on." They rest, and their works go with them. Nothing done in love for the Lamb is lost.

Watch your words tomorrow. When you notice that you want to tell a small lie to look good or to avoid trouble, tell the truth instead, gently – and thank the Lamb that you may bear his name.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Which of the world's "labels" weighs most heavily on you, and what changes when you write the name of the Father and the Lamb over it (v. 1)?
  • Where is the Lamb leading you at the moment that you would rather not follow (v. 4)?
  • Is there a "small lie" in your mouth that you have grown so used to that you no longer notice it (v. 5)?
  • How does verse 13 comfort you about someone you have lost – or about your own death?

Prayer

You stand on Mount Zion, Lamb of God, and I may stand with you. Thank you that you have purchased me from the earth, and that your name and your Father's name are written over my life. No mark of this world is stronger than that.

I confess that I wear the world's labels as if they were my true name. Forgive me for the lies, great and small, and for the times I did not want to follow you where you went.

Teach me to follow you wherever you go, with a mouth without a lie and a heart that belongs to you. And when one day I die in the Lord, let me rest in the knowledge that you do not forget my small works of love. In Jesus' name, Amen.
