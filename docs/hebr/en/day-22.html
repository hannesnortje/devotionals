Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 22 / 28

The Nature of Faith and the First Heroes of Faith: Abel, Enoch and Noah

Scripture Hebrews 11:1–7

1Now faith is the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen. 2For by it the people of old were attested. 3By faith we understand that the worlds were fashioned by God's word, so that what is seen did not come into being out of things that appear.

4By faith Abel offered God a greater sacrifice than Cain. Through it he was attested as righteous, God himself testifying about his gifts; and through it, though he died, he is still speaking. 5By faith Enoch was taken up so that he would not see death, and "he was not found, because God had taken him up". For before he was taken up, it was testified of him that he had pleased God. 6And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever approaches God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him. 7By faith Noah, when he was warned by God about things not yet seen, in reverence built an ark for the salvation of his house. Through it he condemned the world, and he became heir of the righteousness that accords with faith.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With chapter 11 we enter the last major section of the letter to the Hebrews (chapters 11 to 13). Yesterday, at the end of chapter 10, the author quoted Habakkuk 2:4 – "but my righteous one will live by faith" – and declared that we are not of those who draw back to destruction, but of those who have faith to the preserving of the soul (10:38–39). Now he draws back the curtain on the famous "Hall of Fame of Faith".

For the persecuted Jewish Christians, the greatest temptation was to stare only at what the physical eye could see: the power of Rome, the loss of their earthly possessions, and the apparent security of the earthly temple in Jerusalem. The author now shows them that the greatest heroes of their own Old Testament history never lived by what their physical eyes could see, but by God's invisible promises. He starts at the very beginning of Genesis (chapters 1 to 9) – long before Abraham or the law of Moses existed – with creation and the three early witnesses before the flood: Abel, Enoch and Noah.

Insights from the Greek

Let us dig into the riches of the original Greek terms and the theological foundations of these seven verses:

  1. The ground and proof of the unseen (hypostasis – ὑπόστασις and elenchos – ἔλεγχος, vv. 1–2): Verse 1 does not give a dry dictionary definition of faith; it shows what faith does: it is "the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen"*. - Hypostasis: We have already met this rich Greek word in Hebrews 1:3 ("being") and 3:14 ("assurance"). In some Greek papyri of the first centuries, hypostasis is used for the documents that prove ownership – a kind of title deed to a piece of land. Even if you have not yet gone to live on that plot, you hold in your hand the legal title that guarantees it belongs to you. Faith is the spiritual title deed that makes God's future promises so real and tangible to us today that we can build our lives on them! - Elenchos*: This word is a legal term for conclusive evidence that settles a case in court beyond all doubt, or for an inner conviction that works like a sixth sense. Where our physical eyes perceive the visible world, faith is the spiritual eye with which we see the invisible kingdom of God clearly. - Were attested (emartyrēthēsan – ἐμαρτυρήθησαν, v. 2): In the world people look for the approval of their contemporaries, but "the people of old" (hoi presbyteroi – οἱ πρεσβύτεροι, literally the elders) received through their faith a good testimony from God himself that his favour rested on them.

  2. The invisible origin of the visible cosmos (katērtisthai tous aiōnas – κατηρτίσθαι τοὺς αἰῶνας, v. 3): The very first place where faith enlightens our understanding is the origin of the universe (Genesis 1). "By faith we understand" (nooumen – νοοῦμεν: we grasp with spiritual insight) "that the worlds were fashioned by God's word". "The worlds" (aiōnas – the ages, time and space) were fashioned by the spoken word (rhēmati – ῥήματι) of God. The Greek verb for "fashion" is katartizō: to fit something together precisely, to equip, to order in perfect harmony (the same word is used in Hebrews 13:21 for the way God equips us to do his will). And notice the end of v. 3: the visible universe (to blepomenon – τὸ βλεπόμενον) did not come into being out of things that were already visible (ek phainomenōn – ἐκ φαινομένων). The ultimate reality behind everything we can see and touch is not blind matter but the invisible, eternal word of God!

  3. Abel: faith that worships and goes on speaking after death (pleiona thysian – πλείονα θυσίαν, v. 4): Now the author moves through Genesis in order and shows how the walk of faith unfolds: in Abel we see the worship of faith (Genesis 4:1–10). By faith Abel offered God a "greater" (pleiona: fuller, richer, more valuable) sacrifice than Cain. Why did God accept Abel's lamb and reject Cain's fruit of the ground? Not simply because of what was on the menu, but because Abel came with a heart of faith that acknowledged that, as a sinner, he needed atonement through a blood sacrifice, while Cain came with a self-righteous attitude of mere duty. God himself testified about Abel's gifts and declared him righteous (dikaios – δίκαιος). And although Abel was the very first martyr for the faith when his brother murdered him, the author says: "through it, though he died, he is still speaking" (eti lalei – ἔτι λαλεῖ). In Genesis 4:10 Abel's blood cried out to God from the ground, but his faith still speaks to us today, in the present tense, and testifies that it is worth serving God on his terms, even if it costs you your life.

  4. Enoch: faith that walks with God and delights his heart (euarestēkenai – εὐαρεστηκέναι and misthapodotēs – μισθαποδότης, vv. 5–6): If Abel shows us the offering of faith, Enoch shows us the daily walk of faith (Genesis 5:21–24). The Hebrew text of Genesis 5 says that Enoch walked with God for three hundred years. The Greek Septuagint, however, rendered that Hebrew expression as "Enoch pleased God" (euērestēsen tō theō) – and our author seizes on exactly that verb! Enoch's close fellowship with the Lord in the middle of a corrupt world before the flood was so precious to God that God "had taken him up" (metatithēmi – μετατίθημι: transferred to heaven) without his tasting death. From Enoch's life, v. 6 formulates an unshakeable ground rule of the spiritual life: "without faith it is impossible (adynaton – ἀδύνατον) to please him". And what does this faith involve when we approach God? Two things: - We must believe that he exists (hoti estin – ὅτι ἔστιν, literally "that he is": not just a cold intellectual acknowledgement that there is a Supreme Being, but that he is the living "I AM" of Exodus 3:14, truly present). - We must believe that he rewards those who earnestly seek him (ekzētousin – ἐκζητοῦσιν). He is a misthapodotēs – a generous Rewarder. And what is the greatest reward God gives to the one who seeks him? Himself! (See Genesis 15:1, where God says to Abram: "I am your shield; your reward will be very great.")

  5. Noah: faith that builds in reverence in the dark (chrēmatistheis – χρηματισθείς and eulabētheis – εὐλαβηθείς, v. 7): In Abel we see faith that worships, in Enoch faith that walks, and in Noah faith that works and perseveres (Genesis 6–9). Noah acted "when he was warned by God about things not yet seen" (chrēmatistheis – an official oracle or revelation from God's side) – a worldwide flood at a time when no one had ever experienced such a judgment. How did Noah respond? He did not argue or wait for a weather forecast; in reverence (eulabētheis – related to eulabeia – εὐλάβεια, the same deep godly fear that Hebrews 5:7 testifies of Jesus!) he hammered away at the ark, plank by plank, for decades, "for the salvation of his house". Through his obedient faith he did two things: first, "he condemned the world" (katekrinen – κατέκρινεν: his obedience left their wilful unbelief without excuse), and second, "he became heir of the righteousness that accords with faith". Genesis 6:9 is the first place in Scripture where anyone is called "righteous" – and it is Noah!

Core Theological Truths

  1. Faith makes the unseen certain. It is the assurance of what is hoped for and the proof of things that are not seen – the title deed of God's promises in our hand.
  2. Reality rests on God's word. Behind everything we can see and touch stands not blind matter but the invisible, eternal word of God.
  3. Faith worships, walks and works. Abel shows the worship of faith, Enoch the daily walk of faith, Noah the work and perseverance of faith.
  4. Without faith it is impossible to please God. Whoever approaches God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him.

Living It Out

Hebrews 11:1–7 frees us from the suffocating tyranny of the visible world and, through Abel, Enoch and Noah, gives us a threefold road map for our daily walk with the Lord.

Think first about how you see reality (vv. 1–3). Every day we are bombarded with news, figures and visible crises that shout at us that only what you can see and measure really matters. But when you look through the lens of vv. 1 and 3, you realize that this visible world is temporary and that God's promises are the hypostasis – the firm title deed – under your feet. You don't need to see today how God is going to cut through a particular knot in your future or your family in order to stand on it in peace already; you hold the signed title deed of his word in your hand!

Look secondly at the heart of your relationship with the Father (vv. 4–6). Many believers live with the constant fear that God is always slightly disappointed or irritated with them. But look at v. 6: how do you delight God's heart (euaresteō)? Not by flawless perfection in your own strength, but by faith! When today, like Abel, you come to him acknowledging that you rely only on the blood of the Lamb, and when, like Enoch, you walk towards his throne expecting not a stingy judge but a generous Rewarder who loves to give himself to those who earnestly seek him (ekzēteō), then the Father smiles on you!

Look thirdly at Noah's hammer in your own house (v. 7). Notice the moving detail in v. 7: in reverence Noah built the ark "for the salvation of his house". In a culture that mocked God's warnings, Noah held his course and built a spiritual refuge for his wife and children. What a call for us today! The world around us may think it old-fashioned or foolish to be still before the word every day, to choose integrity over profit, and to build your house on the rock of Christ. But every daily act of the obedience of faith by which you lead your family to Christ is like one more plank you nail in place against the storms of this age.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Your spiritual title deed (hypostasis): Which particular promise of God are you holding on to at the moment, while your physical eyes can see no sign yet of its fulfilment? Do you treat that promise as a "wish" or as a legal title deed?
  • How do you see God when you pray (v. 6)? When you approach God today, do you come merely out of duty, like Cain, or with the firm faith that he is present and that he is a generous Rewarder of those who seek him with all their heart?
  • Building your "ark" (v. 7): Where is the Lord asking you at the moment, like Noah, to keep building in reverence and obedience – in your personal devotion, your work or for the blessing of your household – even if there is not yet a cloud on the horizon?

Prayer

Eternal and invisible Creator God, I worship you today as the One who, by the power of your single spoken word, prepared and ordered the universe and the ages out of nothing. Thank you that my life is not left to blind chance or to what my limited eyes can see, but that your word is the firm title deed and pledge of my hope.

Lord, I want to please you today with a living, childlike faith. Like Abel I come to you with no merit of my own, but only on the ground of the precious blood of the perfect Lamb, Jesus Christ. Like Enoch I long to walk with you today, step by step, in close fellowship, with the joyful conviction that you are truly present and that you richly reward everyone who earnestly seeks you.

And as with Noah, I ask you to fill my heart with a holy reverence for your word. Where the world around me lives for the moment and ignores your warnings, give me the courage and perseverance to keep building faithfully in faith every day, for the blessing and shelter of my household and of the people you have entrusted to me. Open my spiritual eyes wide today to the invisible things of your kingdom. Amen.
