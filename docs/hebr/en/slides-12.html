Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 12

The Race and the Mountain

Hebrews 12:1–29

To get started

Think of a time you finished something long and hard – a race, a climb, a project – when you felt like giving up halfway. What kept you going?

Where we are in the story

Chapter 11 ended with a great gallery of witnesses; now the baton is placed in our hands. The first readers were worn out, like long-distance runners with a cramp halfway through a marathon. In chapter 12 the author gives them new strength with three pictures: a stadium (verses 1–3), a family home where children are raised (verses 4–13), and two mountains – Sinai and Zion (verses 18–24) – followed by the last warning of the letter (verses 25–29).

For the sake of the joy that lay before him he endured the cross, despising the shame, and he has taken his seat at the right hand of the throne of God.

A look at the Greek

ἀφορῶντες

aphorōntes · “with our eyes fixed on”

The prefix apo- (away from) means that you deliberately turn your eyes away from everything else to fix them on one point: Jesus, “the pioneer of faith and the one who brings it to perfection” (12:2). No runner wins by looking at his own feet or at the crowd.

A look at the Greek

παιδεία

paideia · “discipline”

From pais (child): the whole process of raising a child, forming character and loving correction. A court's punishment looks back at the offence in order to repay it – and our punishment has already fallen on Jesus; the Father's paideia looks forward to the child's growth (12:5–11).

A look at the Greek

πανήγυρις

panēgyris · “festal gathering”

A great national festival, as at the Olympic Games when the whole city celebrated. At Sinai the angels surrounded the awe-inspiring giving of the law; at Zion they celebrate the redemption of sinners (12:22).

The big idea

With our eyes fixed on Jesus we run the race with endurance and accept the Father's discipline as love; and because we have approached Mount Zion, not Sinai, we receive a kingdom that cannot be shaken and worship God with gratitude, reverence and awe.

Discussion · 1/7

We have “such a vast cloud of witnesses surrounding us” – not spectators waiting for us to fall, but witnesses who testify through their own race that God's promises hold. Which “weight” – perhaps not even a sin – is slowing you down at the moment?

Hebrews 12:1

Discussion · 2/7

“For the sake of the joy that lay before him he endured the cross, despising the shame.” What does it mean in practice to keep your eyes fixed on Jesus when you feel overwhelmed by opposition, injustice or your own weakness?

Hebrews 12:2–3

Discussion · 3/7

Verse 5 warns against two opposite reactions to hardship: “do not make light of the discipline of the Lord” and “do not lose heart”. Which of the two is more your tendency? How does it change things to see hardship as the Father's training rather than his punishment?

Hebrews 12:4–10

Discussion · 4/7

“Later, however, it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.” Can you tell of a hard season in which you only saw the fruit later?

Hebrews 12:11–13

Discussion · 5/7

Esau “sold his birthright for a single meal”, and a root of bitterness can defile many. What “single meal” tempts us today? And how can we, as a group, “see to it” for one another without becoming judgemental?

Hebrews 12:14–17

Discussion · 6/7

Compare the two mountains: what do you see, hear and feel at Sinai (verses 18–21) and at Zion (verses 22–24)? When you pray, do you approach God more like Sinai or like Zion?

Hebrews 12:18–24

Discussion · 7/7

The blood of Jesus “speaks better than Abel”, and “since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be thankful”. What is being shaken around you at the moment, and what would thankful worship look like as your answer?

Hebrews 12:24–29

For the week ahead

  • Name one “weight” and put it down this week in a concrete way – an evening without a screen, a commitment you hand back, a worry you give to God in prayer each morning.
  • If a root of bitterness has begun to grow towards someone, take one step this week towards peace: pray for that person by name, send a message, or ask for a conversation.

Pray together

Begin by reading verse 2 aloud together. Keep a minute of silence in which each person lays down a weight before God. Then pray for those in the group who feel tired in the race, thank God that in Christ we have approached Mount Zion, and close by reading verses 28–29 together.
