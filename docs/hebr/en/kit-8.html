Small-group kit · Session 8 / 13

Shadow and Reality

Present ▶

The big ideaWe have a high priest who has sat down in the true tent and is mediator of a better covenant, whose promises rest on God's own “I will”: he writes his laws on our hearts and remembers our sins no more.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Have you ever seen an architect's model or a sketch of a house before it was built? How did the real building compare with it?

Read together · Hebrews 8:1–13

Two readers: one reads verses 1–7 and verse 13; the other reads the Jeremiah quotation in verses 8–12 as the voice of the Lord – slowly, with a short pause after each “I will”.

Where we are in the story

In chapter 7 the author showed that the person of our high priest, according to the order of Melchizedek, is far higher than the Levitical priests. Now chapter 8 turns to his ministry: a better sanctuary and a better covenant. For the first readers, cut off from the temple in Jerusalem and perhaps mocked for having no temple, no priest and no altar, this was a burning question. The author answers with the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament, Jeremiah 31:31–34, quoted as the Greek translation has it.

A look at the Greek

  • kephalaion – κεφάλαιον “the main point”: Not just a summary but the crowning point of the whole argument (8:1). And the core is a seated high priest: the earthly priests had to stand every day because their work was never finished (cf. 10:11); ours has taken his seat.
  • hypodeigma kai skia – ὑπόδειγμα καὶ σκιά “copy and shadow”: A hypodeigma is a copy or sketch; Moses had to build according to the typos, the pattern or blueprint shown on the mountain (8:5). Like a shadow on the ground or an architect's scale model, the tent worship was good and God-given, but it pointed to a greater reality: Christ's ministry in heaven.
  • diathēkē kainē – διαθήκη καινή “a new covenant”: Kainos is often distinguished from neos: not only new in time but new in kind (the distinction is not strict – in 12:24 Hebrews also uses nea). Its promises are better because God no longer says “If you …” but “I will!” (8:8–12).

Discussion

  1. The main point is this: “we have such a high priest, who took his seat at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens”. Why would the author make a seated priest the core of his whole argument? (Hebrews 8:1–2)
  2. The earthly priests serve “a copy and shadow of the heavenly things”. The first readers had lost the visible temple. Which visible things – forms, rituals, human approval – do we lean on for our spiritual security? (Hebrews 8:3–5)
  3. If the first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no need for a second. Read verse 8: “For he finds fault with them when he says”. Where did the fault lie – and what did the old covenant not give? (Hebrews 8:6–9)
  4. “I will put my laws into their minds, and I will write them on their hearts.” Do you experience God's will at the moment more like tablets of stone – an outward burden – or like something the Spirit is writing within you? What makes the difference? (Hebrews 8:10)
  5. “For they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest.” Under the old covenant ordinary people depended on prophets, priests and kings. What does it mean for the youngest and the oldest in our group that each can know the Lord personally? (Hebrews 8:11)
  6. Verse 12 begins with “For”: everything rests on it. “For I will be merciful toward their unrighteous deeds, and I will remember their sins no more.” The Greek uses a double negative – never, ever again. Is there a sin you have confessed long ago but still accuse yourself of? What would change if you believed this verse? (Hebrews 8:12)
  7. “What is becoming old and growing aged is close to disappearing.” How can we honour the first covenant as God-given and still not go back to the shadows? (Hebrews 8:13)

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week pray verse 10 for one specific area of your life where you are pushing yourself with clenched teeth: ask the Lord to write his will there on your heart and to change your desires from within.
  • Write down one old, confessed sin that you keep digging up. Read verse 12 aloud over it, thank God that he will never again use it against you – and then tear up the paper.

Pray together

Read verses 10–12 aloud together. Then pray in twos or threes: thank Jesus that he has sat down as our high priest in the true tent, ask him to write his laws on your hearts, and thank the Father, in your own words, for the promise of verse 12. Leave a moment of silence for each person to receive it personally.

Notes for the leader

  • This session draws on a single devotional (day 15) for the whole chapter, so there is time for the Jeremiah quotation. Choose five or six questions; do not skip the question on verse 12.
  • Hebrews quotes Jeremiah as the Greek (Septuagint) has it. In 8:9 our text reads “and I disregarded them”, where the Hebrew of Jeremiah 31:32 says although I was their husband (or master). If someone's Bible reads differently, this is the reason – our translation follows the Greek of Hebrews on purpose.
  • The devotional is clear that the fault lay not with God or his holy law but with sinful people (8:8). The tent was built at God's instruction (8:5). Keep the evening from turning into contempt for the Old Testament or for the Jewish people.
  • Verse 12 does not mean that the all-knowing God suffers memory loss; it means that he promises never again to use our confessed sin against us or to treat us according to our past. For some, the question on verse 12 touches deep guilt – allow silence and offer to pray with anyone afterwards.
  • Verse 13: many think the letter was written before AD 70, when the temple was indeed close to disappearing. That is interesting background, but don't let dating questions take over the discussion.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 9 – a guided tour of the earthly tent, and then “But Christ …”: through his own blood.

Scripture Hebrews 8:1–13

1The main point of what we are saying is this: we have such a high priest, who took his seat at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens, 2a minister in the sanctuary and in the true tent, which the Lord pitched, not a human being. 3For every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices; so it is necessary for this one too to have something to offer. 4Now if he were on earth, he would not even be a priest, since there are those who offer the gifts according to the law. 5They worship at a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, just as Moses was instructed when he was about to complete the tent: "See," he says, "that you make everything according to the pattern that was shown you on the mountain." 6But as it is, he has obtained a ministry that is as much superior as he is also mediator of a better covenant, which has been enacted on better promises.

7For if that first covenant had been faultless, no place would have been sought for a second. 8For he finds fault with them when he says:

See, days are coming, says the Lord,when I will bring about a new covenantwith the house of Israel and with the house of Judah,9not like the covenant that I made with their fatherson the day when I took hold of their handto lead them out of the land of Egypt;for they did not remain in my covenant,and I disregarded them, says the Lord.10For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israelafter those days, says the Lord:I will put my laws into their minds,and I will write them on their hearts;and I will be their God,and they shall be my people.11And they shall not teach, each one his fellow citizenand each one his brother, saying: Know the Lord,for they shall all know me,from the least of them to the greatest.12For I will be merciful toward their unrighteous deeds,and I will remember their sins no more.

13By saying "new", he has made the first one old; and what is becoming old and growing aged is close to disappearing.
