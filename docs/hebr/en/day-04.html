Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 4 / 28

Crowned with Glory: Humanity's Destiny and Our Substitute

Scripture Hebrews 2:5–9

5For it was not to angels that he subjected the world to come, about which we are speaking. 6Rather, someone has testified somewhere:

What is a human being, that you remember him,or a son of man, that you look after him?7You made him a little lower than the angels;with glory and honour you crowned him;8you subjected everything under his feet.For in subjecting everything to him, he left nothing that is not subject to him. As it is, we do not yet see everything subjected to him.

9But we do see the one who was made a little lower than the angels, Jesus, crowned with glory and honour because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

In chapter 1 the author showed us that Jesus is the eternal Son of God, highly exalted above the angels. But that raised a huge stumbling block in the minds of his Jewish readers: If Jesus is so great and divine, why did he become a frail human being, suffer, and die a humiliating death on a Roman cross? To the Jewish mind a crucified Messiah was a scandal (1 Corinthians 1:23). How can someone who descended lower than the angels and died still be greater than the angels?

To answer this deep question, the author takes us back to humanity's original purpose in creation by quoting Psalm 8:4–6. He shows that Jesus' incarnation and suffering were not a tragic accident or a sign of weakness, but God's brilliant rescue plan to win back humanity's lost crown.

He introduces the psalm with a deliberately vague formula: "Rather, someone has testified somewhere". Every Jewish reader knew exactly where it was written, and so the emphasis falls on God as its true author rather than on the human writer.

Insights from the Greek

Let us explore the literary and linguistic treasures in these verses:

  • "The world to come" (oikoumenēn tēn mellousan – οἰκουμένην τὴν μέλλουσαν, v. 5): The author resumes his comparison with the angels: God did not subject "the world to come" (the new, redeemed earth and kingdom under the Messiah's rule) to angels. To whom does it belong, then? To answer, he quotes David's words from Psalm 8.
  • "A little" (brachy ti – βραχύ τι, vv. 7 and 9): Brachy ti can express degree ("a little lower") or time ("for a little while"). In Psalm 8 it is about humanity's place in creation: "You made him a little lower than the angels; with glory and honour you crowned him." When the author applies the same words to Jesus in verse 9, many interpreters read them in terms of time: for a little while, during his earthly life.
  • "We do not yet see" (oupō horōmen – οὔπω ὁρῶμεν, v. 8): According to Psalm 8, God placed everything without exception under humanity's feet. But then the author voices the painful reality we all experience every day: "As it is, we do not yet see everything subjected to him."
  • "But we do see … Jesus" (blepomen Iēsoun – βλέπομεν Ἰησοῦν, v. 9a): Here lies one of the most powerful contrasts in the whole New Testament. In verse 8 the author uses the word horōmen, in verse 9 the verb blepomen. Some interpreters see a difference here: we do not yet see the fulfilled creation with our physical eyes, but we fix our spiritual gaze on him with insight and focus: "But we do see the one who was made a little lower than the angels, Jesus". Notice that this is the very first time in the letter that the author uses his human name, Jesus (meaning "Yahweh saves").
  • "Taste death" (geusētai thanatou – γεύσηται θανάτου, v. 9b): The purpose of his humiliation is described with a moving expression: "so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone" (chariti theou – χάριτι θεοῦ). He did not merely dip his fingertips into death. In biblical idiom, "to taste death" means to experience death really and fully: he drained the bitter cup of death and of God's judgement on sin on our behalf.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Humanity's original calling. In Genesis 1:26–28 God created human beings as his image, to rule as his deputies over creation. God crowned humanity with glory and honour to govern the physical creation. Angels were never destined to rule over creation – human beings were!
  2. The tragic dissonance of the fall. When Adam and Eve fell into sin, the crown rolled off humanity's head. Instead of ruling over creation in harmony and authority, we are plagued by sickness, natural disasters, temptation, sin and finally our greatest enemy: death. We are kings who have lost our throne and become slaves in our own kingdom.
  3. Jesus, the last Adam, has stepped into our shoes. We do not stare at our own broken rule; we look at Jesus, the last Adam (1 Corinthians 15:45). Jesus walked exactly the path of Psalm 8. He who is eternally God was made a little lower than the angels by taking on a mortal human body.
  4. Through suffering to the crown. And precisely because of his willing suffering to the point of death (cf. Philippians 2:8–9), he is now exalted as a human being and crowned with glory and honour at the Father's right hand! Where we humans failed, a true human being now sits on the throne of the universe.

Living It Out

When we look at our world and our own lives today, the feeling of verse 8 often dominates our thoughts: "As it is, we do not yet see everything subjected to him." We see broken relationships, cancer diagnoses, economic pressure, injustice in society and our own stubborn shortcomings. It often feels as if chaos is winning and we are utterly powerless.

If you look only at your circumstances, you will become discouraged and begin to drift. But Hebrews 2 gives us the secret of perseverance: shift your gaze from what you do not yet see (v. 8) to the one you can indeed see by faith (v. 9): "But we do see … Jesus!"

Think about what this means for you day by day:

  • Your humanity has eternal value: You are not a cosmic accident or an insignificant speck of dust in a cold universe. God remembers you and looks after you (v. 6). In Christ your human destiny is being restored, so that one day you will reign with him in the world to come.
  • Your suffering is not the end of the story: For Jesus the road to the crown of glory and honour led through the valley of suffering and death. If you are going through trials now, it does not mean that God has abandoned you; you are simply following in the footsteps of your King on the way to glory.
  • The sting of death has been drawn: Because Jesus drank ("tasted") the bitter poisoned cup of death to the bottom for you, you never need to fear spiritual death or the judgement of God.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Where are your eyes fixed today? Are you letting the things in your life that still seem "out of control" and broken (v. 8) paralyse your faith – or are you deliberately choosing to fix your spiritual eyes on the crowned Jesus (v. 9)?
  • Wonder at God's care: When did you last pause over David's question: "What is a human being, that you remember him?" Do you really grasp that the Creator of the galaxies knows you by name and has compassion on you?
  • Are you living from grace? Verse 9 says that it is by the grace of God that Jesus tasted death for everyone. How does this truth change the way you deal with your own fear of failure or of death today?

Prayer

Lord, our Lord, how wonderful is your name in all the earth! With the psalmist I cry out in wonder today: What is a human being, that you remember him, and who am I, that the Creator of the universe should look after me?

Lord Jesus, I confess that I so often stare at the brokenness of this world. When I see sin, pain and uncertainty raging around me, my heart easily becomes afraid and discouraged because I do not yet see everything subjected to you. But today I choose to turn my eyes away from the storm and fix them on you. I see you, Jesus – crowned with glory and honour at the right hand of the Father!

Thank you that you were willing to be made lower than the angels for a little while, to become human in my place, and by the indescribable grace of God to taste the bitter cup of death to the last drop on my behalf. Because you have conquered death, I need fear nothing. Fill me today with new hope and courage as I look forward to the world to come, where I will reign with you. Amen.
