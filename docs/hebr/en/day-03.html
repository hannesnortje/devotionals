Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 3 / 28

The Danger of Drifting: Do Not Disregard a Salvation This Great

Scripture Hebrews 2:1–4

1That is why we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away. 2For if the word spoken through angels proved binding, and every transgression and disobedience received a just penalty, 3how shall we escape if we disregard a salvation this great? It began when the Lord spoke of it, and it was confirmed to us by those who heard, 4while God added his testimony by signs and wonders and various mighty works and by distributions of the Holy Spirit according to his will.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

In chapter 1 the author painted a breathtaking theological portrait of Jesus Christ as the eternal Creator-King – one who is infinitely exalted above all the angels. Now he makes a sudden, urgent turn. In the structure of the letter to the Hebrews we come here to the very first of five well-known warning passages (Hebrews 2:1–4; 3:7–4:13; 5:11–6:12; 10:19–39; 12:14–29). Here we see the heart of a true shepherd: deep theology is never meant merely to flatter our intellect; it demands a life-changing response. The little word "That is why" (Dia touto – Διὰ τοῦτο) at the start of verse 1 is the hinge: precisely because Christ is greater than any angel or prophet, an even greater responsibility rests on us who have heard his voice.

To make his point, the author uses a classic Jewish rabbinic way of reasoning known as qal wahomer (קַל וָחֹמֶר, literally "light and heavy": if something is true in a lesser case, how much more is it true in a greater one!):

  • The lesser: "The word spoken through angels" refers to the law of Moses at Sinai (Acts 7:53). Under the old covenant, deliberate transgression and disobedience were punished strictly and justly (e.g. Numbers 15:30–31).
  • The greater: If God's covenant word given through servants (angels) was taken so seriously, "how shall we escape" if we ignore the final word of salvation spoken by the Son himself?

Insights from the Greek

Let us look at the rich nautical and legal vocabulary of the Greek text:

  • "Pay attention" (prosechein – προσέχειν) and "drift away" (pararyōmen – παραρυῶμεν, v. 1): The author uses two powerful Greek verbs from ancient seafaring. First he says we "must pay much closer attention to what we have heard" (prosechein). In everyday speech this means to fix your full attention on something, but in seafaring the word meant to hold a ship on course towards a harbour and moor it there safely (to drop anchor). The opposite is to "drift away" (pararyōmen). The word describes a boat that has not been properly tied up and quietly drifts with the current past the safe harbour – or a precious ring that slips unnoticed off a finger, or water seeping from a leaking jar. Notice that the danger for these believers was not a sudden, dramatic rebellion against God, but a slow, unnoticed drifting caused by spiritual tiredness and neglect.
  • "Binding" (bebaios – βέβαιος, v. 2): That word "proved binding" – bebaios is a legal term for a binding, guaranteed contract.
  • "Transgression" (parabasis – παράβασις) and "disobedience" (parakoē – παρακοή, v. 2): Parabasis means stepping over a clear boundary line; parakoē means closing your ears and refusing to listen.
  • "Disregard" (amelēsantes – ἀμελήσαντες, v. 3): The word for "disregard" means to be indifferent, not to care, or to brush something aside as unimportant. The same word is used in Matthew 22:5 for the invited guests who ignored the king's wedding invitation in order to see to their own fields and businesses.
  • "Added his testimony" (synepimartyrountos – συνεπιμαρτυροῦντος, v. 4): God himself acted as co-witness – through supernatural signs (sēmeiois – σημείοις), wonders (terasin – τέρασιν), mighty works (dynamesin – δυνάμεσιν) and the sovereign distribution of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Deep theology demands a response. Precisely because Christ is greater than any angel or prophet, an even greater responsibility rests on us who have heard his voice.
  2. The great danger is slow drifting. The danger is not first of all dramatic rebellion, but indifference and neglect.
  3. The threefold foundation of "a salvation this great" (vv. 3b–4). Why is this salvation (sōtērias – σωτηρίας) so great and so impossible to escape? Because it rests on an unshakeable threefold testimony: - The origin (the Lord): It began with the preaching, the death on the cross and the resurrection of the Lord Jesus himself. - The transmission (the eyewitnesses): It was reliably confirmed by the apostles who heard and saw Jesus first-hand (which also suggests that the author of Hebrews was a second-generation Christian). - The divine seal (Father and Spirit): God himself added his testimony by signs and wonders, mighty works and distributions of the Holy Spirit.

Living It Out

Nobody wakes up one morning with a deliberate plan to shipwreck their faith. Spiritual decline almost always happens through drifting. Drifting away from the truth takes no effort at all; you only have to pull in your oars, loosen your anchor and let the current of our busy, secular culture do the rest.

When we are under pressure – whether through disappointment, busy schedules, financial stress or social opposition – the first things we often let slip are our daily anchor points: stillness before the Lord, meditating on his word, and the fellowship of believers. We do not openly reject the gospel; we simply get used to it. We treat "a salvation this great", which cost the Son of God his life, with a shrug of indifference while we spend all our energy on passing things.

This passage does not call us to panic, but to holy watchfulness. If we are not to drift, we must deliberately row against the current and moor our hearts daily (prosechein) to the gospel we have heard.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Check your spiritual course: If you compare your love for Christ and your hunger for his word today with where you were a year ago, are you anchoring more deeply, or quietly drifting away with the current?
  • Which undercurrents are pulling at you? Which specific things in your daily routine (e.g. too much screen time, career ambition, worry, or entertainment) act like a silent undercurrent drawing your attention away from the Lord?
  • The value of your salvation: Where in your life may you have grown used to the grace of God? How can you "pay attention" to the gospel again today with fresh wonder?

Prayer

Holy Father, thank you for the loving seriousness of your word today. You know my human heart, and you know how easily my attention is divided and how quickly I can begin to drift on the currents of this world without even noticing.

Forgive me, Lord, for the times when I have treated your precious gospel – a salvation this great, bought with the blood of your own Son – with lukewarmness or indifference. Forgive me when passing worries and earthly distractions became more important to me than the eternal word spoken through Jesus.

I pray that by your Holy Spirit you will shake my spiritual senses awake today. Help me, with new discipline and deep wonder, to pay attention to what I have heard. Be the unshakeable anchor of my soul in every storm and every busy season. Keep my feet on your path and hold my heart close to your cross. Amen.
