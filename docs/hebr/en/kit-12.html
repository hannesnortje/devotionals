Small-group kit · Session 12 / 13

The Race and the Mountain

Present ▶

The big ideaWith our eyes fixed on Jesus we run the race with endurance and accept the Father's discipline as love; and because we have approached Mount Zion, not Sinai, we receive a kingdom that cannot be shaken and worship God with gratitude, reverence and awe.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of a time you finished something long and hard – a race, a climb, a project – when you felt like giving up halfway. What kept you going?

Read together · Hebrews 12:1–29

Use four readers: verses 1–13 (the race and the Father's discipline), 14–21 (Esau and Mount Sinai – read 18–21 slowly and heavily), 22–24 (Mount Zion – read with joy, so the contrast is heard), 25–29 (the kingdom that cannot be shaken).

Where we are in the story

Chapter 11 ended with a great gallery of witnesses; now the baton is placed in our hands. The first readers were worn out, like long-distance runners with a cramp halfway through a marathon. In chapter 12 the author gives them new strength with three pictures: a stadium (verses 1–3), a family home where children are raised (verses 4–13), and two mountains – Sinai and Zion (verses 18–24) – followed by the last warning of the letter (verses 25–29).

A look at the Greek

  • aphorōntes – ἀφορῶντες “with our eyes fixed on”: The prefix apo- (away from) means that you deliberately turn your eyes away from everything else to fix them on one point: Jesus, “the pioneer of faith and the one who brings it to perfection” (12:2). No runner wins by looking at his own feet or at the crowd.
  • paideia – παιδεία “discipline”: From pais (child): the whole process of raising a child, forming character and loving correction. A court's punishment looks back at the offence in order to repay it – and our punishment has already fallen on Jesus; the Father's paideia looks forward to the child's growth (12:5–11).
  • panēgyris – πανήγυρις “festal gathering”: A great national festival, as at the Olympic Games when the whole city celebrated. At Sinai the angels surrounded the awe-inspiring giving of the law; at Zion they celebrate the redemption of sinners (12:22).

Discussion

  1. We have “such a vast cloud of witnesses surrounding us” – not spectators waiting for us to fall, but witnesses who testify through their own race that God's promises hold. Which “weight” – perhaps not even a sin – is slowing you down at the moment? (Hebrews 12:1)
  2. “For the sake of the joy that lay before him he endured the cross, despising the shame.” What does it mean in practice to keep your eyes fixed on Jesus when you feel overwhelmed by opposition, injustice or your own weakness? (Hebrews 12:2–3)
  3. Verse 5 warns against two opposite reactions to hardship: “do not make light of the discipline of the Lord” and “do not lose heart”. Which of the two is more your tendency? How does it change things to see hardship as the Father's training rather than his punishment? (Hebrews 12:4–10)
  4. “Later, however, it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.” Can you tell of a hard season in which you only saw the fruit later? (Hebrews 12:11–13)
  5. Esau “sold his birthright for a single meal”, and a root of bitterness can defile many. What “single meal” tempts us today? And how can we, as a group, “see to it” for one another without becoming judgemental? (Hebrews 12:14–17)
  6. Compare the two mountains: what do you see, hear and feel at Sinai (verses 18–21) and at Zion (verses 22–24)? When you pray, do you approach God more like Sinai or like Zion? (Hebrews 12:18–24)
  7. The blood of Jesus “speaks better than Abel”, and “since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be thankful”. What is being shaken around you at the moment, and what would thankful worship look like as your answer? (Hebrews 12:24–29)

For the week ahead

  • Name one “weight” and put it down this week in a concrete way – an evening without a screen, a commitment you hand back, a worry you give to God in prayer each morning.
  • If a root of bitterness has begun to grow towards someone, take one step this week towards peace: pray for that person by name, send a message, or ask for a conversation.

Pray together

Begin by reading verse 2 aloud together. Keep a minute of silence in which each person lays down a weight before God. Then pray for those in the group who feel tired in the race, thank God that in Christ we have approached Mount Zion, and close by reading verses 28–29 together.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers two devotionals (days 25–26) and three big pictures. If time is short, focus on verses 1–3 and 18–24, and choose one question from the discipline passage.
  • Verses 5–11 can be painful for people who had a harsh or abusive father, and the words “he flogs every son” can sound frightening. Stay with the devotional: paideia is not punishment, it looks forward in love, and God disciplines “for our good”. Don't suggest that a particular illness or loss is God's punishment for a particular sin.
  • 12:17 (Esau “found no place for repentance”) can frighten sensitive believers who fear they have gone too far. The day 26 devotional says that Esau's tears were only regret over his loss, not true repentance towards God. Someone who is troubled about this is showing the opposite of Esau's indifference; offer a personal conversation if needed.
  • Verses 25–29 are the letter's last warning. The devotional holds both together: the God of Zion is the same consuming fire as the God of Sinai, but in Christ our fear becomes grateful reverence. Don't let either side drop out.
  • Translation: in 12:2 our text has “the pioneer of faith and the one who brings it to perfection” instead of the familiar wording of older Bibles; it stays close to the Greek archēgos (already in 2:10) and teleiōtēs. The series title comes from this verse.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 13, the last session – love in practice, outside the camp, and the great shepherd.

Scripture Hebrews 12:1–29

1Therefore, since we too have such a vast cloud of witnesses surrounding us, let us lay aside every weight and the sin that so readily wraps itself around us, and let us run with endurance the race that lies before us, 2with our eyes fixed on Jesus, the pioneer of faith and the one who brings it to perfection. For the sake of the joy that lay before him he endured the cross, despising the shame, and he has taken his seat at the right hand of the throne of God. 3Consider him who endured such opposition from sinners against themselves, so that you do not grow weary and lose heart in your souls.

4In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of blood, 5and you have forgotten the encouragement that addresses you as sons:

My son, do not make light of the discipline of the Lord,and do not lose heart when you are corrected by him;6for the Lord disciplines the one he loves,and he flogs every son whom he accepts.

7It is for discipline that you endure. God is treating you as sons. For what son is there whom a father does not discipline? 8But if you are without the discipline that all have come to share in, then you are illegitimate and not sons. 9Besides, we had the fathers of our flesh to discipline us, and we respected them. Shall we not much more submit to the Father of spirits and live? 10For they disciplined us for a few days as seemed good to them, but he does it for our good, so that we may share in his holiness. 11All discipline, for the moment, seems to be a matter not of joy but of sorrow; later, however, it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.

12Therefore lift up again the drooping hands and the weakened knees, 13and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be put out of joint but rather be healed.

14Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, without which no one will see the Lord. 15See to it that no one falls short of the grace of God, that no root of bitterness springs up and causes trouble, and through it many are defiled, 16that no one is sexually immoral or godless like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. 17For you know that later, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected; for he found no place for repentance, although he sought it with tears.

18For you have not approached something that could be touched, and blazing fire, and darkness and gloom and storm, 19and the sound of a trumpet and a voice speaking words, whose hearers begged that no further word be spoken to them, 20for they could not bear the command, "Even if an animal touches the mountain, it shall be stoned." 21And so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, "I am full of fear and trembling." 22But you have approached Mount Zion and the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and myriads of angels, the festal gathering 23and assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in the heavens, and God, the judge of all, and the spirits of the righteous who have been made perfect, 24and Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and the blood of sprinkling that speaks better than Abel.

25See that you do not refuse the one who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused the one who warned them on earth, much less shall we, if we turn away from the one who warns from heaven. 26His voice shook the earth then, but now he has promised:

Once more I will shake not only the earthbut also heaven.

27This "Once more" shows that what is shaken will be removed, as things that have been made, so that what is not shaken may remain. 28Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be thankful, and so worship God in a way that pleases him, with reverence and awe; 29for our God is indeed a consuming fire.
