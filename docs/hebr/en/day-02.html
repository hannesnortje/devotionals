Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 2 / 28

Exalted above the Angels: The Eternal King and Ministering Spirits

Scripture Hebrews 1:5–14

5For to which of the angels did he ever say:

You are my Son;today I have fathered you?And again:I will be a father to him,and he will be a son to me?

6And when he again brings the firstborn into the world, he says:

And let all God's angels worship him.

7With regard to the angels he says:

He makes his angels winds,and his ministers a flame of fire.

8But with regard to the Son:

Your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever,and the sceptre of uprightness is the sceptre of your kingdom.9You have loved righteousness and hated lawlessness;therefore God, your God, has anointed youwith the oil of rejoicing beyond your partners.

10And:

In the beginning, Lord, you laid the foundation of the earth,and the heavens are the works of your hands.11They will perish, but you remain;they will all wear out like a garment,12and like a cloak you will roll them up;like a garment they will also be changed.But you are the same,and your years will never come to an end.

13But to which of the angels has he ever said:

Sit at my right handuntil I make your enemiesa footstool for your feet?

14Are they not all ministering spirits, sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation?

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

To modern readers it may sound strange that the author of Hebrews devotes a whole chapter to proving that Jesus is greater than the angels. We are rarely tempted to exalt angels above Christ. But in the Jewish world of the first century – especially in certain mystical and apocalyptic movements such as the Qumran community – angels played an enormous role. Jewish tradition held that the law of Moses was given on Mount Sinai through the mediation of angels (cf. Deuteronomy 33:2; Acts 7:53; Galatians 3:19). Angels were seen as the dazzling, awe-inspiring messengers and go-betweens between a holy God and a sinful humanity. Some writings from Qumran even give the archangel Michael a leading role in God's final victory.

So to convince his readers that the new covenant in Christ is infinitely better than the old covenant of Moses, the author had to show that the mediator of the new covenant is far above the heavenly messengers of the old. He does it in a typically Jewish way: by threading together a charaz (חרז, a "string of pearls") of seven Old Testament quotations from the Greek Septuagint. With each pearl from the Psalms, Deuteronomy and Samuel he builds an irrefutable case.

Insights from the Greek

Let us explore the theological and linguistic depths of this comparison:

  • "The firstborn" (prōtotokon – πρωτότοκον, vv. 5–6): The author quotes Psalm 2:7 and 2 Samuel 7:14, two classic messianic royal texts. In the Old Testament angels are sometimes collectively called "sons of God" (e.g. Job 1:6) because God created them, but to no single angel did God ever say: "You are my Son". The phrase "today I have fathered you" does not refer in the New Testament to a point at which Jesus was created (he is eternal, after all), but to his public vindication and enthronement as the risen Messiah-King (cf. Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4). In verse 6 Jesus is called "the firstborn" (prōtotokos). In ancient culture and in biblical Greek, prōtotokos refers primarily to rank, authority and the right of inheritance, not merely to origin in time (just as David is called "firstborn" in Psalm 89:27, although he was the youngest son of Jesse). The quotation in verse 6 comes from Deuteronomy 32:43 as it reads in the Septuagint (cf. Psalm 97:7); the line is missing from the Hebrew text of Deuteronomy 32.
  • "Let them worship him" (proskynēsatōsan – προσκυνησάτωσαν, v. 6): The proof of Jesus' divine rank lies in the command to the angels: they must worship him. In Jewish monotheism only God himself may be worshipped.
  • "Winds" (pneumata – πνεύματα, v. 7) and "O God" (ho theos – ὁ θεός, v. 8): Here we meet a sharp contrast (from Psalm 104:4 and Psalm 45:6–7). Angels are changeable and subordinate; God makes his angels "winds" and his ministers "a flame of fire" to carry out his orders at lightning speed. They are created instruments. But when the Father speaks to the Son, he addresses him directly as God: "Your throne, O God (ho theos), stands for ever and ever."
  • "Lawlessness" (anomian – ἀνομίαν, v. 9): The Son has "loved righteousness and hated lawlessness". The psalm goes on to speak of his anointing "with the oil of rejoicing beyond your partners".
  • "Cloak" (peribolaion – περιβόλαιον) and "the same" (ho autos – ὁ αὐτός, vv. 10–12): Next the author applies Psalm 102:25–27 – a passage that in the Hebrew Old Testament speaks directly of the LORD (YHWH) – to Jesus without hesitation. At the end of history Christ will roll up the present cosmos like an old cloak and replace it with a new heaven and earth. But in the midst of a universe that grows old and changes, Jesus stands firm: "But you are the same (ho autos), and your years will never come to an end."
  • "Ministering spirits" (leitourgika pneumata – λειτουργικὰ πνεύματα, vv. 13–14): The chapter's climax reaches back to Psalm 110:1 (the Old Testament verse most often quoted in the New Testament). No angel was ever told to come and sit at God's right hand. The ancient image of enemies as a "footstool" goes back to the practice of a victorious king placing his foot on the neck of his defeated enemy as a sign of total subjection. What then is the angels' true position? Verse 14 calls them leitourgika pneumata: "liturgical" or "ministering spirits". They are heaven's support staff!

Core Theological Truths

  1. The Son is unique. No angel was ever addressed as Son. The Son receives the worship of the angels – something due to God alone.
  2. The Son is God. "Your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever" is one of the most powerful and direct declarations of Christ's full deity in all of Scripture. While the angels serve, the Son reigns with "the sceptre of uprightness". His kingship rests on his moral perfection.
  3. The Son is the unchanging Creator. The physical universe, however impressive and permanent it may seem to us, is subject to decay. It will wear out like an old garment. But he remains the same.
  4. The Son is seated in victory; the angels are sent out to serve. And whom do they serve? They are sent out to serve us: "those who are going to inherit salvation".

Living It Out

In our modern world we may not bow before angels, but every day we are tempted to exalt "substitutes" above Christ. Sometimes the supernatural, spiritual experiences, or even great spiritual leaders and traditions fascinate us more than the person of Jesus himself. At other times we fix our eyes on earthly rulers, political systems or economic structures, as if they were sitting on the throne. Hebrews 1 sets our perspective straight: everything and everyone in creation is only a changeable servant or a temporary garment that wears out. Only Jesus' throne stands for ever and ever.

This passage also offers great comfort to an uncertain heart. First, your stability lies in his unchangeableness. We live in a world where everything "wears out". Bodies grow old, relationships change, economies falter and seasons shift. When you feel as if the rug is being pulled from under your feet, hold on to verse 12: "But you are the same." The Jesus who was faithful yesterday is on the throne today, and his years will never come to an end.

Second, realise how precious you are to God in Christ. Think of the breathtaking reality of verse 14: mighty heavenly beings – God's flames of fire – are sent out by the King to stand by you, to protect you and to help you on your way to the eternal inheritance. You never walk through your trials alone; heaven's resources are actively involved in keeping you.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Good things in place of the best: Are there "good" spiritual things in your life (ministry, traditions, spiritual books, or emotional experiences) that have subtly taken the place of intimate worship of Jesus himself?
  • Where do you look for unchanging security? Which "garments" of this world (status, money, health, comfort) are you clinging to as if they will last for ever, when only Christ remains unchanging?
  • Love for righteousness: Verse 9 says the Son loved righteousness and hated lawlessness. As you grow closer to him, is your heart also beginning to love what he loves and hate what he hates – or are you making peace with small compromises?

Prayer

Eternal King Jesus, with all the angels of God I fall down in worship before you today. You are not merely a messenger or a good teacher; your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever, and the sceptre of uprightness is the sceptre of your kingdom.

Lord, in a world where everything around me keeps changing, ageing and wearing out like an old garment, I anchor my soul anew today in who you are. You are the same – yesterday, today and for ever. When my circumstances are shaken, remind me that you are the one who laid the foundations of the earth, and that at this very moment you are seated in full victory at the right hand of the Father.

Purify my heart, so that like you I may love righteousness and hate every form of sin and lawlessness. Fill me through your Spirit with the oil of rejoicing. And thank you, Father, for the indescribable grace that you send even your mighty angels as ministering spirits to carry me and keep me on the way to eternal salvation. I trust you with my life. Amen.
