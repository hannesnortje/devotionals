Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 7

A Priest for Ever

Hebrews 7:1–28

To get started

Have you ever had to start again with a new doctor, teacher or adviser because the old one left? What was lost?

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 6 Jesus entered behind the curtain as our forerunner, high priest for ever according to the order of Melchizedek. Now the author finally opens the subject he has been circling since 5:6–10. For Jewish believers it raised a fair question: how can Jesus be a priest if he came from the tribe of Judah and not from Levi? The author answers from the only two places in the Old Testament where Melchizedek appears: Genesis 14:18–20 and Psalm 110:4.

Read together · Hebrews 7:1–2

1For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, met Abraham as he was returning from the defeat of the kings and blessed him, 2and Abraham gave him a tenth of everything. His name means first "king of righteousness", and then he is also king of Salem, that is, "king of peace".

Read together · Hebrews 7:3–4

3Without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but made like the Son of God, he remains a priest for all time.

4See how great he is, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth of the best of the spoils.

Read together · Hebrews 7:5–7

5Those of the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment in the law to collect a tenth from the people, that is, from their brothers and sisters, though these too have come from Abraham's body. 6But this man, who does not trace his descent from them, collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises. 7Beyond all dispute, the lesser is blessed by the better.

Read together · Hebrews 7:8–10

8Here, mortal men receive tithes; there, one of whom it is testified that he lives. 9And one might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid a tenth through Abraham, 10for he was still in his ancestor's body when Melchizedek met him.

Read together · Hebrews 7:11–12

11Now if perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood – for on the basis of it the people received the law – what further need would there have been for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, and not to be named according to the order of Aaron? 12For when the priesthood is changed, there is of necessity a change of law as well.

Read together · Hebrews 7:13–16

13For the one of whom these things are said belonged to another tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar. 14For it is clear that our Lord has arisen from Judah, and in connection with that tribe Moses said nothing about priests.

15And it is even more evident if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek, 16who has become a priest not according to a law of a fleshly commandment but according to the power of an indestructible life.

17For it is testified:

You are a priest for everaccording to the order of Melchizedek.

18For on the one hand, a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness 19– for the law made nothing perfect – and on the other hand, a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God.

Read together · Hebrews 7:20–21

20And it was not without a sworn oath. For the others became priests without a sworn oath, 21but he with a sworn oath, through the one who said to him:

The Lord has swornand will not change his mind:You are a priest for ever.

Read together · Hebrews 7:22–25

22So much the more has Jesus become the guarantor of a better covenant.

23The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office; 24but he, because he remains for ever, holds his priesthood permanently. 25That is why he is able to save completely those who approach God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them.

Read together · Hebrews 7:26–27

26For it was fitting that we should have such a high priest: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and become higher than the heavens. 27He has no need, like the high priests, to offer up sacrifices day after day, first for his own sins and then for those of the people; for this he did once for all when he offered up himself.

Read together · Hebrews 7:28–28

28For the law appoints as high priests men who have weakness, but the word of the sworn oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son, made perfect for ever.

A look at the Greek

ἀγενεαλόγητος

agenealogētos · “without genealogy”

Genesis introduces almost every important figure with ancestors, birth and death – but not Melchizedek. He is not an angel or an appearance of Christ; the author reads the silence of Scripture: in the text Melchizedek is “made like the Son of God”, a portrait painted in advance of the Original (7:3).

A look at the Greek

ἔγγυος

engyos · “guarantor”

Found only here in the New Testament (7:22). A mediator negotiates between two parties; a guarantor puts his own life, property and honour at stake that the terms will be kept – if we fail, the guarantor pays. Jesus was appointed with God's sworn oath, and so he guarantees a better covenant.

A look at the Greek

εἰς τὸ παντελές

eis to panteles · “completely”

The phrase has a double shade: completely, to the deepest corner of our brokenness, and for all time, to the very end. He can save like this “since he always lives to intercede for them” (7:25).

The big idea

Melchizedek, without genealogy and greater than Abraham, points to Jesus: a priest not by descent but by the power of an indestructible life and by God's oath, who always lives to intercede for us and who offered himself once for all.

Discussion · 1/7

Melchizedek is first “king of righteousness” and then “king of peace”. Why does the order matter? Where do we look for peace without letting righteousness touch the matter?

Hebrews 7:1–2

Discussion · 2/7

Genesis is silent about Melchizedek's parents, birth and death, and the author sees in that silence a portrait of the Son of God. How does it free us that Jesus' priesthood – and his blessing – do not depend on pedigree, background or a spiritual CV?

Hebrews 7:3

Discussion · 3/7

“See how great he is.” Abraham gave a tenth of the best of the spoils – centuries before the law, out of gratitude. What would it mean for you to give Jesus the best part of your time and your heart rather than the leftovers?

Hebrews 7:4–10

Discussion · 4/7

Verse 19 says “the law made nothing perfect”, but “a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God”. Where are you tempted to rely on a list of rules instead of drawing near?

Hebrews 7:11–19

Discussion · 5/7

Jesus became a priest “according to the power of an indestructible life” and with a sworn oath. What does his resurrection – and God's oath – have to do with your confidence before God?

Hebrews 7:16–22

Discussion · 6/7

The former priests were many “because they were prevented by death from continuing in office”, but Jesus “holds his priesthood permanently”. What does it mean for you that your high priest never retires and never has to be replaced?

Hebrews 7:23–24

Discussion · 7/7

“He is able to save completely those who approach God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them.” Is there a part of your life you secretly think is beyond his reach? What does verse 25 say to it?

Hebrews 7:25–28

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week, remind yourself that Jesus is interceding for you today, and bring him one thing you fear you cannot manage.
  • When old guilt comes back this week, remember that he did it “once for all when he offered up himself” – and refuse to add your own self-punishment to his sacrifice.

Pray together

Read verses 24–25 aloud together. Then pray in pairs: thank Jesus that he is your guarantor and that he is praying for you now, and name before him one area you think is beyond his reach. Close with thanks for his once-for-all sacrifice (v. 27).
