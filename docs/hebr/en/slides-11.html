Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 11

By Faith

Hebrews 11:1–40

To get started

Have you ever paid for something long before you could enjoy it – a ticket, a plot of land, a holiday? What did it feel like to hold the proof in your hand while you were still waiting?

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 10 the author reminded his tired, persecuted readers that they are not of those who draw back, but of those who have faith. Now he opens a gallery of witnesses from their own Scriptures – from Abel, Enoch and Noah, through Abraham and the patriarchs, to Moses, Rahab and nameless martyrs. None of them lived by what their eyes could see; all of them lived by God's promises. Tonight we read the whole of chapter 11.

A look at the Greek

ὑπόστασις

hypostasis · “assurance”

We met this word in 1:3 (“being”) and 3:14 (“assurance”). In some Greek papyri of the first centuries it is used for documents that prove ownership – a kind of title deed. Faith holds the title deed of God's promises before we have moved in (11:1).

A look at the Greek

ἀσπάζομαι

aspazomai · “greeted”

To welcome something with joy, to embrace it with open arms – like sailors who see the hills of home on the horizon and cheer, though they are still miles from the harbour. The patriarchs died without the fulfilment, but they saw the promises from afar and greeted them (11:13).

A look at the Greek

καρτερέω

kartereō · “held on”

Like a soldier who stays standing, strong and unshaken. This is the secret of Moses' staying power: when you see the invisible King with the eye of faith, the fury of an earthly pharaoh shrinks to nothing (11:27).

The big idea

Faith treats God's promises as a title deed in our hand: it makes us pilgrims on the way to the city whose builder is God, it values the reproach of the Christ above the treasures of Egypt, and it holds on – whether God delivers us or carries us through suffering.

Discussion · 1/7

“Now faith is the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen.” The devotional compares this assurance to a title deed. What is the difference between treating a promise of God as a title deed and treating it as a wish?

Hebrews 11:1–3

Discussion · 2/7

Abel shows the worship of faith, Enoch its daily walk, Noah its work and perseverance. Verse 6 says that whoever approaches God must believe that he exists and that he “rewards those who earnestly seek him”. How does that picture of God change the way you pray?

Hebrews 11:4–7

Discussion · 3/7

Abraham “went out, not knowing where he was going”. When have you had to take a step of obedience without seeing the whole map? What helped you take it?

Hebrews 11:8–12

Discussion · 4/7

The patriarchs confessed that they were “strangers and temporary residents on the earth”. Where do we drive our tent pegs too deep into this world? And what does it mean to you that “God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God”?

Hebrews 11:13–16

Discussion · 5/7

On Moriah Abraham “reckoned that God is able even to raise from the dead”. What is the “Isaac” in your life – a good gift from God that you may be clinging to more tightly than to the Giver?

Hebrews 11:17–22

Discussion · 6/7

Moses “considered the reproach of the Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt”. What are today's treasures of Egypt, and what helps us see through “the fleeting enjoyment of sin”?

Hebrews 11:23–28

Discussion · 7/7

In verses 33–35 some shut the mouths of lions; then the text turns: “Others were tortured and refused to accept release”. Both groups stand in the same list, and of the suffering ones it says “the world was not worthy of them”. How can this help someone in a long, hard season?

Hebrews 11:32–40

For the week ahead

  • Write down one promise of God you are holding on to without yet seeing it fulfilled. Read it every morning this week as a title deed, and thank God for it before you see anything change.
  • Name one tent peg that holds you too tightly to this world – a possession, an ambition or a worry – and do one concrete thing this week to loosen it: give something away, share, or hand it over to God in prayer.

Pray together

Begin by reading verse 1 aloud together. Thank God for the people of faith who went before you, in the Bible and in your own life. Then pray for anyone in the group who is in an “Others” season – waiting, suffering or not yet seeing an answer – and ask that all of you may hold on as those who see the invisible One.
