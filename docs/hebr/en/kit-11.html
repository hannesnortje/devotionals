Small-group kit · Session 11 / 13

By Faith

Present ▶

The big ideaFaith treats God's promises as a title deed in our hand: it makes us pilgrims on the way to the city whose builder is God, it values the reproach of the Christ above the treasures of Egypt, and it holds on – whether God delivers us or carries us through suffering.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 15 min Read the passage
  • 5 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Have you ever paid for something long before you could enjoy it – a ticket, a plot of land, a holiday? What did it feel like to hold the proof in your hand while you were still waiting?

Read together · Hebrews 11:1–40

Use four readers: verses 1–7 (faith and the first witnesses), 8–22 (Abraham to Joseph), 23–31 (Moses to Rahab), 32–40 (the heroes and the nameless). Let the whole group say the words “By faith” together each time they come.

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 10 the author reminded his tired, persecuted readers that they are not of those who draw back, but of those who have faith. Now he opens a gallery of witnesses from their own Scriptures – from Abel, Enoch and Noah, through Abraham and the patriarchs, to Moses, Rahab and nameless martyrs. None of them lived by what their eyes could see; all of them lived by God's promises. Tonight we read the whole of chapter 11.

A look at the Greek

  • hypostasis – ὑπόστασις “assurance”: We met this word in 1:3 (“being”) and 3:14 (“assurance”). In some Greek papyri of the first centuries it is used for documents that prove ownership – a kind of title deed. Faith holds the title deed of God's promises before we have moved in (11:1).
  • aspazomai – ἀσπάζομαι “greeted”: To welcome something with joy, to embrace it with open arms – like sailors who see the hills of home on the horizon and cheer, though they are still miles from the harbour. The patriarchs died without the fulfilment, but they saw the promises from afar and greeted them (11:13).
  • kartereō – καρτερέω “held on”: Like a soldier who stays standing, strong and unshaken. This is the secret of Moses' staying power: when you see the invisible King with the eye of faith, the fury of an earthly pharaoh shrinks to nothing (11:27).

Discussion

  1. “Now faith is the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen.” The devotional compares this assurance to a title deed. What is the difference between treating a promise of God as a title deed and treating it as a wish? (Hebrews 11:1–3)
  2. Abel shows the worship of faith, Enoch its daily walk, Noah its work and perseverance. Verse 6 says that whoever approaches God must believe that he exists and that he “rewards those who earnestly seek him”. How does that picture of God change the way you pray? (Hebrews 11:4–7)
  3. Abraham “went out, not knowing where he was going”. When have you had to take a step of obedience without seeing the whole map? What helped you take it? (Hebrews 11:8–12)
  4. The patriarchs confessed that they were “strangers and temporary residents on the earth”. Where do we drive our tent pegs too deep into this world? And what does it mean to you that “God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God”? (Hebrews 11:13–16)
  5. On Moriah Abraham “reckoned that God is able even to raise from the dead”. What is the “Isaac” in your life – a good gift from God that you may be clinging to more tightly than to the Giver? (Hebrews 11:17–22)
  6. Moses “considered the reproach of the Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt”. What are today's treasures of Egypt, and what helps us see through “the fleeting enjoyment of sin”? (Hebrews 11:23–28)
  7. In verses 33–35 some shut the mouths of lions; then the text turns: “Others were tortured and refused to accept release”. Both groups stand in the same list, and of the suffering ones it says “the world was not worthy of them”. How can this help someone in a long, hard season? (Hebrews 11:32–40)

For the week ahead

  • Write down one promise of God you are holding on to without yet seeing it fulfilled. Read it every morning this week as a title deed, and thank God for it before you see anything change.
  • Name one tent peg that holds you too tightly to this world – a possession, an ambition or a worry – and do one concrete thing this week to loosen it: give something away, share, or hand it over to God in prayer.

Pray together

Begin by reading verse 1 aloud together. Thank God for the people of faith who went before you, in the Bible and in your own life. Then pray for anyone in the group who is in an “Others” season – waiting, suffering or not yet seeing an answer – and ask that all of you may hold on as those who see the invisible One.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers three devotionals (days 22–24) and 40 verses. Don't try to discuss every name; the four reading parts give the structure, and four or five questions are enough.
  • 11:11: our translation follows the Greek base text, in which Abraham is the subject (“together with Sarah herself”); many older Bibles make Sarah the subject. If someone notices the difference, explain it briefly – verse 12 also speaks of “one man” – and point out that Sarah is still included. Don't let it become the topic of the evening.
  • The most sensitive point is verses 35–38. Some people believe that great faith always brings healing or rescue. The day 24 devotional says that those who suffered did not have less faith than those who saw miracles. Handle this gently with anyone who is ill, grieving or has prayed long without an answer, and don't promise outcomes.
  • Abel's sacrifice: the devotional explains God's acceptance by the faith of Abel's heart. Genesis says little about the reason, so don't spend long on speculation about the offerings.
  • Verses 39–40 prepare for next week: the witnesses of chapter 11 are made perfect together with us. Point ahead: chapter 12 – the race, the Father's discipline and the two mountains, Sinai and Zion.

Scripture Hebrews 11:1–40

1Now faith is the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen. 2For by it the people of old were attested. 3By faith we understand that the worlds were fashioned by God's word, so that what is seen did not come into being out of things that appear.

4By faith Abel offered God a greater sacrifice than Cain. Through it he was attested as righteous, God himself testifying about his gifts; and through it, though he died, he is still speaking. 5By faith Enoch was taken up so that he would not see death, and "he was not found, because God had taken him up". For before he was taken up, it was testified of him that he had pleased God. 6And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever approaches God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him. 7By faith Noah, when he was warned by God about things not yet seen, in reverence built an ark for the salvation of his house. Through it he condemned the world, and he became heir of the righteousness that accords with faith.

8By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance; and he went out, not knowing where he was going. 9By faith he lived as a guest in the land of the promise as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, co-heirs of the same promise. 10For he was waiting for the city that has the foundations, whose designer and builder is God. 11By faith he received power to father offspring – together with Sarah herself – even when he was past the age for it, since he considered the one who had promised faithful. 12Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, descendants were born as many as the stars of heaven and as countless as the sand on the shore of the sea.

13In faith all these died without having received the promises, but they saw them from afar and greeted them, and confessed that they were strangers and temporary residents on the earth. 14For people who speak like this make it clear that they are seeking a homeland. 15If they had been thinking of the one they had left, they would have had opportunity to return. 16But as it is, they long for a better one, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God; for he has prepared a city for them.

17By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac. He who had received the promises was ready to offer up his only son, 18he to whom it had been said, "Through Isaac offspring will be named for you." 19He reckoned that God is able even to raise from the dead; hence he did receive him back – as a symbol. 20By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, also concerning things to come. 21By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and he worshipped, leaning on the top of his staff. 22By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, mentioned the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave instructions about his bones.

23By faith Moses, after he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they were not afraid of the king's edict. 24By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, 25choosing rather to be mistreated along with the people of God than to have the fleeting enjoyment of sin, 26for he considered the reproach of the Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, because he had his eyes fixed on the reward. 27By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the fury of the king; for he held on as though he saw the one who is invisible. 28By faith he instituted the Passover and the applying of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them. 29By faith they crossed the Red Sea as on dry land; when the Egyptians attempted it, they were swallowed up. 30By faith the walls of Jericho fell after they had been encircled for seven days. 31By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who disobeyed, because she had welcomed the spies in peace.

32And what more shall I say? Time would fail me if I told of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets, 33who through faith conquered kingdoms, practised righteousness, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions, 34quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight. 35Women received back their dead by resurrection. Others were tortured and refused to accept release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection. 36Still others experienced mocking and flogging, and even chains and prison. 37They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they died by the sword; they went about in sheepskins and goatskins, in want, afflicted, mistreated – 38the world was not worthy of them – wandering in deserts and mountains, in caves and holes in the ground.

39And all these, though they were attested through their faith, did not receive the promise, 40since God had foreseen something better for us, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us.
