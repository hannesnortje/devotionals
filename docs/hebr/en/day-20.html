Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 20 / 28

The New and Living Way: Let Us Approach with Confidence and Hold Fast

Scripture Hebrews 10:19–25

19So, brothers and sisters, since we have confidence to enter the sanctuary by the blood of Jesus, 20by the new and living way that he inaugurated for us through the curtain, that is, his flesh, 21and since we have a great priest over the house of God, 22let us approach with a true heart in full assurance of faith, with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water. 23Let us hold fast to the confession of hope, unwavering, for the one who made the promise is faithful. 24And let us consider one another, so as to spur one another on to love and good works, 25not abandoning our own meeting together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day drawing near.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Hebrews 10:19 is the great hinge of the whole letter to the Hebrews. From 1:1 to 10:18 the author has laid a masterly theological foundation: who Christ is (greater than the prophets, the angels, Moses and Aaron) and what he has done as our eternal High Priest in the heavenly sanctuary. He has exhorted his readers before (2:1–4; 3:7–4:13; 5:11–6:12), but now, with the words "So, brothers and sisters, since we have …" (Echontes oun, adelphoi – Ἔχοντες οὖν, ἀδελφοί), he moves decisively from doctrine to our duty – from the wonderful position Christ has won for us to the practical way of life that must flow from it (10:19 to 13:25).

In the original Greek, verses 19 to 25 form one single, beautifully constructed sentence. It rests on two great spiritual possessions ("since we have …" – vv. 19–21) and flows into three powerful exhortations ("Let us …" – vv. 22–25), which together form the classic Christian triad of faith (v. 22), hope (v. 23) and love (vv. 24–25)!

Under the old covenant any ordinary Israelite (or even an ordinary priest) who dared to step behind the curtain into the Most Holy Place would have died on the spot. Even the high priest entered only once a year with fear and trembling. When Jesus' body was broken and torn on the cross, the massive curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom (Matthew 27:51). The double cleansing of verse 22 points to the consecration of priests in the Old Testament (Exodus 29:4, 21; Leviticus 8) and to the new-covenant prophecy of Ezekiel 36:25–26.

Insights from the Greek

Let us look at the riches of the Greek terms:

  • "Confidence" (parrēsia – παρρησία, v. 19): We "have confidence to enter the sanctuary by the blood of Jesus". Parrēsia is a free, unhindered and fully legal right of access!
  • "The new and living way" (hodon prosphaton kai zōsan – ὁδὸν πρόσφατον καὶ ζῶσαν, v. 20): The Greek word for "new", prosphatos, is especially striking: originally it means "fresh, recently made". Here it points to something that always stays fresh and never grows old or loses its power. Christ's sacrifice on the cross remains for ever as fresh and powerful before God as on the day his blood flowed on Golgotha – and because he is risen, it is a living way.
  • "Inaugurated" (enkainizō – ἐγκαινίζω, v. 20): Jesus "inaugurated" this way for us, that is, opened it for the first time.
  • "Let us approach" (proserchōmetha – προσερχώμεθα, v. 22): A present-tense form meaning: let us keep drawing near to God, continually and as a habit of life.
  • "With a true heart" (meta alēthinēs kardias – μετὰ ἀληθινῆς καρδίας, v. 22): a sincere, undivided heart, free of hypocrisy.
  • "In full assurance of faith" (en plērophoria pisteōs – ἐν πληροφορίᾳ πίστεως, v. 22): the inner confidence of someone fully convinced.
  • "Unwavering" (aklinē – ἀκλινῆ, v. 23): We are to hold fast to the public confession (homologia – ὁμολογία) of our hope, "unwavering" (katechōmen … aklinē – κατέχωμεν … ἀκλινῆ). The word aklinēs literally means "without bending or leaning over" – like an upright pillar that refuses to snap under tremendous wind pressure.
  • "Let us consider one another" (katanoōmen allēlous – κατανοῶμεν ἀλλήλους, v. 24): In Hebrews 3:1 the author told us to "consider closely" Jesus (katanoeō – κατανοέω: to reflect deeply on, to study); now he uses exactly the same verb for our brothers and sisters!
  • "Spur on" (paroxysmos – παροξυσμός, v. 24): From this word comes our medical term "paroxysm". It is usually used in a negative sense for a violent outburst, irritation or quarrel (like the sharp disagreement between Paul and Barnabas in Acts 15:39). But here the author beautifully turns it around: we are to "provoke" one another in a good way, to shake each other awake and set each other on fire (like spurs that make a horse leap forward) to love and to serve!
  • "Meeting together" and "encouraging" (episynagōgē – ἐπισυναγωγή; parakaleō – παρακαλέω, v. 25): We are not to abandon our own meeting together, but to encourage one another.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Our two great possessions: the open way and the great priest (vv. 19–21). First, by the blood of Jesus we have confidence to enter the sanctuary, "by the new and living way that he inaugurated for us through the curtain, that is, his flesh". His incarnation and death on the cross removed the barrier between God and humanity for ever. Second, we have "a great priest over the house of God": we are not walking into an empty throne room; our living King-Priest stands ready at the Father's right hand to welcome us.
  2. Call 1 (faith): "Let us approach" (v. 22). The first response to the open curtain is to draw near, "with a true heart in full assurance of faith". This inner confidence rests on a double cleansing: - Inward: "with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience" (by the blood of Christ, which washes away our deepest sense of guilt). - Outward: "and our bodies washed with pure water" (the outward sign of Christian baptism, which testifies to the inner renewal and sanctification by the Holy Spirit and the Word; cf. Ephesians 5:26; Titus 3:5). In Christ every believer is now a consecrated priest who may go straight into the Most Holy Place!
  3. Call 2 (hope): "Let us hold fast" (v. 23). Having drawn near to God upwards in faith, we look forwards to the future in hope. How can we stay upright when everything around us is shaking? Not because our own grip is so strong, but "for the one who made the promise is faithful" (pistos – πιστὸς)! Our holding on rests on his unfailing character.
  4. Call 3 (love): "Let us consider one another" (vv. 24–25). The third call looks sideways at our fellow believers. We are to look attentively and compassionately at one another's lives and think creatively about how we can support each other spiritually, "so as to spur one another on to love and good works". But that cannot happen if we isolate ourselves. That is why verse 25 warns against "abandoning our own meeting together, as is the habit of some" (probably out of fear of persecution or spiritual lukewarmness). We are to encourage one another, "and all the more as you see the Day drawing near" (the return of Christ and the impending judgement on Jerusalem).

Living It Out

Hebrews 10:19–25 gives us the perfect blueprint for a healthy, balanced spiritual life in a hostile and exhausting world.

First, look at your vertical relationship with God (vv. 19–22). How many Christians today still live as if the curtain had been sewn up again! When you pray, you may feel as if you have to shout to God from outside the camp over a high wall, hoping he might listen if you have lived well enough today. But the gospel declares that the curtain has been torn open for ever through Jesus' broken body. The way to the Father's heart is prosphatos – always fresh, living and wide open through Jesus' blood. You do not have to stand at a distance and knock timidly at the door; with a cleansed conscience you may walk today with parrēsia (childlike confidence) straight up to your Father's throne.

Second, look at your horizontal relationship with other believers (vv. 24–25). In our modern, individualistic and digital culture the temptation is greater than ever to practise the Christian faith as a private "solo sport". We think: "All I need is my Bible, my laptop and my personal relationship with the Lord; I don't need the trouble of other people's failings."

But notice the text: in the long run you cannot live out verse 22 ("let us approach") and verse 23 ("let us hold fast to the confession of hope") if you ignore verses 24 and 25! A coal that rolls out of the fire soon dies and goes cold; in the same way a believer's hope and love cool down in isolation. To "consider" one another (katanoeō) means more than sitting next to someone on Sunday and chatting about the weather. It means deliberately investing in one another's lives – in our families, our home groups and our church community – so that, as iron sharpens iron (Proverbs 27:17), we can spur one another on (paroxysmos) to practical love and endurance as our King's coming draws ever nearer.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Are you walking on the "new and living way"? Do you make daily use of the privilege of entering God's presence with confidence through Jesus' blood – or do you let an old, guilty conscience keep you in the outer court?
  • Is your pillar bending under pressure (aklinēs – ἀκλινής)? In which area of your life is your confession of hope under tremendous pressure right now? How does the truth of verse 23 – "for the one who made the promise is faithful" – help you to stay upright?
  • Holy spurring on (paroxysmos): Who are the people you are specifically "considering" at the moment, in order to spur them on to love and good works? And do you let other believers live close enough to you to encourage you when you grow tired?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, with deep wonder and gratitude I come with confidence today into your holy presence. I come not on the basis of my own merit, but only through the precious blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that he tore open for me through his body broken on the cross.

Lord Jesus, my great Priest over the house of God, thank you that you have sprinkled my heart clean from an evil conscience. Help me today to live close to you with a sincere, undivided heart and in full assurance of faith. Where the storms of life blow against me, give me the grace to hold fast to the confession of my hope, upright and unwavering, because I know that you who promised are completely faithful.

Set me free, too, from spiritual selfishness and isolation. Open my eyes today to consider with compassion and wisdom the people you send across my path – in my own home and in the body of Christ. Make me a spark that spurs others on to love and good works, and let us encourage and hold on to one another day by day as we watch with joyful expectation your great Day drawing near. Amen.
