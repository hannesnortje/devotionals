One Sacrifice for All Time: The Perfect Obedience That Makes Holy

Scripture Hebrews 10:1–18

1For the law has only a shadow of the good things to come, not the very form of the realities. So it can never, by the same sacrifices that they offer continually year after year, make perfect those who approach. 2Otherwise, would they not have stopped being offered? For the worshippers, once cleansed, would no longer have any sins on their conscience. 3But in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sins year after year. 4For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.

5Therefore, when he comes into the world, he says:

Sacrifice and offering you did not want,but a body you have prepared for me;6in burnt offerings and sin offeringsyou took no pleasure.7Then I said: See, I have come –in the scroll of the book it is written of me –to do your will, O God.

8Above he says: "Sacrifices and offerings and burnt offerings and sin offerings you did not want, nor did you take pleasure in them" – these are offered according to the law. 9Then he has said: "See, I have come to do your will." He takes away the first in order to establish the second. 10By this will we have been made holy through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

11Every priest stands day after day ministering and offering again and again the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. 12But this one offered one sacrifice for sins for all time and took his seat at the right hand of God, 13and from then on he waits until his enemies are made a footstool for his feet. 14For by one offering he has made perfect for all time those who are made holy. 15And the Holy Spirit also testifies to us; for after saying:

16This is the covenant that I will make with themafter those days, says the Lord:I will put my laws on their hearts,and I will write them on their minds,17and their sins and their lawless deedsI will remember no more.

18Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With Hebrews 10:1–18 we reach the closing chord of the great doctrinal exposition of Christ as High Priest that began in chapter 5. The author now ties all the golden threads of his argument – about the tent, the priesthood, the covenant and the sacrificial blood – into one irrefutable conclusion: Christ's once-for-all sacrifice on the cross accomplished everything that the centuries-long Old Testament sacrificial system could never achieve.

For a Jewish believer who had grown up with the impressive rituals of the temple – the thousands of lambs at Passover, the morning and evening sacrifices and the solemn Day of Atonement – it was almost unthinkable that the whole system should come to an end. But the author shows that the endless repetition of those sacrifices was the very proof of their built-in powerlessness. If a medicine really cures a disease, you stop taking the pills; the fact that you have to keep taking them year after year shows that it only manages the symptoms without curing the cause.

To show that God himself never intended animal sacrifices to be the final solution, the author quotes David's words from Psalm 40:6–8 (40:7–9 in the Hebrew; in the Greek Septuagint it is Psalm 39) and places them directly in the mouth of the Son as he came into the world in his incarnation. In the Hebrew text of Psalm 40:6 it literally says: "Ears you have dug for me" (to listen obediently). The Greek text that Hebrews quotes reads: "but a body you have prepared for me" – possibly a figure of speech in which the part (the ears) stands for the whole (the body).

Insights from the Greek

The author's choice of words opens up the insights of this passage:

  • "Shadow" versus "form" (skia – σκιά; eikōn – εἰκών, v. 1): In verse 1 the author draws a fine distinction: the law has "only a shadow of the good things to come, not the very form of the realities". A skia is a flat, dark outline on the ground with no life or depth, whereas an eikōn represents the three-dimensional, tangible reality and form itself.
  • "Take away" (aphaireō – ἀφαιρέω, v. 4): "For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins."
  • "Sins on their conscience" and "reminder" (syneidēsin hamartiōn – συνείδησιν ἁμαρτιῶν; anamnēsis – ἀνάμνησις, vv. 2–3): Instead of wiping out the sense of guilt, so that the worshippers "would no longer have any sins on their conscience", every Day of Atonement worked more like an annual direct-debit notice: "in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sins year after year". The people were reminded that their debt was still outstanding!
  • "Prepared" (katērtisō – κατηρτίσω, v. 5): "But a body you have prepared for me." God prepared a body for the Son and made it ready for his task.
  • "Take away" and "establish" (anaireō – ἀναιρέω; stēsē – στήσῃ, v. 9): With the words "See, I have come to do your will", Christ abolishes the first system (anaireō – to take away or abolish), as verse 9 says: "He takes away the first in order to establish the second."
  • "Once for all" (ephapax – ἐφάπαξ, v. 10): We have been made holy "through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all".
  • "Stands" and "take away" (hestēken – ἕστηκεν; perielein – περιελεῖν, v. 11): The earthly priests stand day after day, caught in an exhausting routine of sacrifices "which can never take away sins". Perielein literally means "take away from around", like a dirty garment that is taken right off and thrown away.
  • "Took his seat" (ekathisen – ἐκάθισεν, v. 12): After a single sacrifice Jesus took his seat at the right hand of God! The phrase eis to diēnekes (εἰς τὸ διηνεκὲς, "for all time") can go in Greek with "offered" or with "took his seat"; our translation takes it with the sacrifice, as in verse 14. Both readings stress the same finality.
  • Two tenses side by side (teteleiōken – τετελείωκεν; tous hagiazomenous – τοὺς ἁγιαζομένους, v. 14): "For by one offering he has made perfect for all time those who are made holy." Teteleiōken ("he has made perfect") is a perfect tense: a position before God that is already settled. Tous hagiazomenous ("those who are made holy") is a present participle: our daily process of growth – we are being made holy.
  • "Forgiveness" (aphesis – ἄφεσις, v. 18): The final legal verdict in verse 18 uses the word for release or cancellation of a debt: "Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin."

Core Theological Truths

  1. The shadow could never replace the reality. Because animal blood stands on a completely different moral and spiritual level from human beings (animals have no moral awareness and cannot willingly choose to suffer in a person's place), the blood of bulls and goats could never really take away sins.
  2. God seeks a body that does his will, not mere rituals. God never wanted mere mechanical rituals and the slaughter of unwilling animals; he required a human life of complete, loving obedience. That is why Jesus says: "See, I have come … to do your will, O God." And verse 10 gives the glorious result: through the willing obedience of the Son to the Father's will we have been made holy through the offering of Jesus' body, once for all.
  3. The seated King-Priest has finished his work. In verses 11 and 12 the author paints an unforgettable contrast between the standing priests and Jesus, who took his seat. His sitting down is the exclamation mark behind the "It is finished!" of Golgotha. And verse 14 beautifully sums up the paradox of the Christian life: your righteous standing before God is already 100% perfect and settled, while the Spirit is now at work day by day to bring your way of life into line with your already perfect status!
  4. The Holy Spirit seals it: no further sacrifice is needed. To close his whole argument, the author calls the Holy Spirit himself as chief witness and once more quotes the core promises of Jeremiah 31 (which he began in chapter 8): the law on the heart and the assurance that "their sins and their lawless deeds I will remember no more". The account is closed, the receipt is stamped, and the altar of self-atonement has been torn down for ever.

Living It Out

Hebrews 10:1–18 pulls the rug out from under one of our biggest spiritual traps: the temptation to set up our own little "side altars" next to the cross of Christ.

When we have messed up in daily life, our human pride finds it very hard simply to go to the Lord and, on the basis of verses 14 and 18, receive full and immediate forgiveness. We feel we must first "do penance" for a while. We walk around for days with a heavy heart, deny ourselves joy, or try to make up for our failure with extra religious zeal. We think this is humility, but in reality it is unbelief! If verse 18 says that "there is no longer any offering for sin", then every attempt on your part to pay for your sin by punishing yourself is an insult to the sufficiency of Jesus' once-for-all sacrifice.

Further, verses 5 to 9 teach us a profound lesson about what God is really looking for in our daily lives: not mere outward religious rituals, but a body willing to do his will. It is so easy to give God "sacrifices and offerings" – to go to church on Sundays, give a donation or say a quick prayer – while keeping our own will and our own kingdom intact for the rest of the week. But just as Jesus made his physical body available with the words "See, I have come to do your will", Romans 12:1 and Hebrews 10 call us to put our everyday bodies, our time, our work and our family life at his disposal as a living offering of thanks.

Finally, live today in the peace of the balance of verse 14: you do not need to lose heart when you see how much growth ("those who are made holy") still has to happen in your character, because in God's eyes you already stand perfect for all time through Christ's one sacrifice ("he has made perfect for all time").

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Sin-consciousness or forgiveness-consciousness? Are you still walking around with a constant, paralysing sense of guilt (v. 2) about things you have already confessed – or are you resting in the knowledge that Christ has taken away your sin for ever like an old garment (perielein)?
  • Rituals or obedience? Do you sometimes try to make up with outward religious duties ("sacrifices and offerings") for an area of your life where you are really refusing to say with Christ: "See, I have come … to do your will, O God" (v. 7)?
  • Perfect and still growing (v. 14): How does knowing that your position before God is already perfect for all time in Christ give you the confidence and energy to work wholeheartedly with the Spirit today at your daily growth in holiness?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, today I stand amazed at the perfection of your plan of redemption. Where no human effort and no thousands of sacrifices could ever wipe out the stain of guilt from my soul, you yourself have opened the way.

Lord Jesus, I thank you that you came in obedience to the Father and, with the body prepared for you, paid the full price on the cross. Thank you that you no longer have to stand like an earthly priest whose work is never done, but that after your one perfect sacrifice you took your seat at the right hand of God. I praise you that through that one sacrifice I am made perfect for all time before the Father, while your Spirit makes me holy day by day.

Forgive me when, through my own self-reproach and penance, I still try to add something to your work on the cross, as if your blood were not enough. Because there is now full forgiveness, I lay down today every "offering" of my own for guilt. Take my life, my body and my will into your service again today; let the song of my heart be: Here I am to do your will, O God! Amen.
