Small-group kit · Session 13 / 13

The Great Shepherd

Present ▶

The big ideaGrateful worship in a kingdom that cannot be shaken becomes practical love, contentment and the courage to go out to Jesus outside the camp; we offer God the sacrifices of praise and sharing – and the God of peace, who brought up the great shepherd from the dead, himself equips us to do his will.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 5 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 20 min Application & prayer

To get started

When a long time together comes to an end – a trip, a course, a job – what do you most want the others to take with them?

Read together · Hebrews 13:1–25

Use three readers: verses 1–9, 10–19 and 22–25. Let the whole group read verse 8 together, and read verses 20–21 together as a blessing at the end of the reading.

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 12 the author said that we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken and should worship God with thankfulness, reverence and awe. Chapter 13 shows what that worship looks like on an ordinary weekday: love, hospitality, marriage and money (verses 1–6), leaders and the unchanging Christ (7–9), going out to Jesus outside the camp (10–14), the sacrifices of praise and sharing (15–19), and finally a great blessing and personal greetings (20–25). Tonight we finish the letter – and this series.

A look at the Greek

  • ou mē se anō oud' ou mē se enkatalipō – οὐ μή σε ἀνῶ οὐδʼ οὐ μή σε ἐγκαταλίπω “never … never”: In 13:5 the Greek piles up five negatives: freely rendered: I will never, ever loosen my grip on you, and I will never, ever abandon you. Our security lies not in what we have, but in whom we have – that is why we can be content (13:5–6).
  • exō tēs parembolēs – ἔξω τῆς παρεμβολῆς “outside the camp”: On the Day of Atonement the bodies of the sin offerings were burned outside the camp (Leviticus 16:27). Jesus suffered outside the gate. For the first readers the camp was the safe, respected world of the temple – but Jesus is outside (13:11–13).
  • katartizō – καταρτίζω “equip”: Used in medicine for setting a broken bone and by fishermen for mending nets (Matthew 4:21); in 11:3 the same verb describes the worlds being fashioned by God's word. What God asks of us, he himself works in us (13:21).

Discussion

  1. Verses 1–4 name four directions of love: love for brothers and sisters, hospitality to strangers, compassion for prisoners and the mistreated, and honour for marriage. Which of these is strong in our group, and which needs our attention? (Hebrews 13:1–4)
  2. “Be content with what you have” – and the reason given is not a budget but a promise: “I will never let you go and never abandon you.” Why does contentment rest on a person rather than on possessions? Where are you uneasy about money or the future? (Hebrews 13:5–6)
  3. “Jesus Christ is yesterday and today the same – and for ever.” Think of your own yesterday, today and future: where do you most need to hear this sentence? (Hebrews 13:7–8)
  4. “It is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods.” What are today's “foods” – rules, achievements or forms we use to feel in control? What does it look like to let grace strengthen your heart? (Hebrews 13:9)
  5. “So let us go out to him outside the camp, bearing his reproach.” What is the comfortable “camp” in our setting, and what could going out to Jesus look like for you this month? (Hebrews 13:10–14)
  6. Verses 15–16 name two sacrifices: “the fruit of lips that confess his name” and “Do not forget to do good and to share”. Which comes more naturally to you – the lips or the hands? And how can we make the work of those who “keep watch over your souls” a joy rather than a groaning? (Hebrews 13:15–19)
  7. Looking back over the whole series: Hebrews has shown us Jesus as the Son through whom God has spoken, the merciful high priest, the forerunner, the pioneer of faith and now the great shepherd. Which picture of Jesus has become most important to you, and why? (Hebrews 13:20–21)

For the week ahead

  • Choose one of the directions of love in verses 1–3 and put it into practice this week: invite someone for a meal, visit or write to someone in prison, hospital or a care home, or share something you have with someone in need.
  • As the series ends, choose the one verse from Hebrews that has spoken to you most. Write it out, put it where you will see it every day, and tell one person this week why it matters to you.

Pray together

Thank God together for what he has done in the group through this series. Pray for your leaders and for one another, as the author asks in verse 18. Close by turning to one another and speaking verses 20–21 as a blessing over the group, and end with the last words of the letter: “Grace be with you all.”

Notes for the leader

  • This is the last session: plan extra time for looking back over the series (question 7 and the prayer), even if you choose fewer of the other questions. Verse 2 invites hospitality – consider ending with a meal together.
  • 13:4: some in the group are single, divorced or widowed, or live in a painful marriage. Keep the focus on marriage as precious and to be honoured by all, and make sure no one leaves feeling judged. Offer a personal conversation where needed.
  • 13:17: some people have been hurt by church leaders. The text describes leaders who keep watch over souls and will give account; the day 28 devotional explains “listen” as letting yourself be persuaded by their word from Scripture. Don't let the evening turn into criticism of particular leaders.
  • 13:10 (“We have an altar”): the day 27 devotional reads this as Christ's sacrifice on the cross, in which believers share by faith. If the question of the Lord's Supper comes up, note this reading and leave a longer discussion for another time.
  • 13:23 mentions “our brother Timothy”, and some will conclude that Paul wrote the letter. Hebrews names no author; the author calls the whole letter a “word of encouragement” and says he has written “only briefly” – it takes about an hour to read aloud.
  • Closing the series: thank the group, ask what they have learned and what they would like to do next, and suggest reading the whole letter aloud in one sitting at home.

Scripture Hebrews 13:1–25

1Let love for one another as brothers and sisters continue. 2Do not forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing so some have entertained angels without knowing it. 3Remember those in prison as though you were in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you yourselves also are in the body. 4Let marriage be held in honour by all, and the marriage bed be kept undefiled; for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers. 5Let your way of life be free from the love of money; be content with what you have. For he himself has said, "I will never let you go and never abandon you." 6So we can say with courage:

The Lord is my helper, I will not be afraid.What can a human being do to me?

7Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith. 8Jesus Christ is yesterday and today the same – and for ever. 9Do not be carried away by all kinds of strange teachings; for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those who lived by them. 10We have an altar from which those who worship at the tent have no right to eat. 11For the bodies of the animals whose blood is brought by the high priest into the sanctuary for sin are burned outside the camp. 12That is why Jesus too suffered outside the gate, in order to make the people holy through his own blood. 13So let us go out to him outside the camp, bearing his reproach. 14For here we have no city that lasts; rather, we are seeking the one that is to come. 15Through him, then, let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name. 16Do not forget to do good and to share, for with such sacrifices God is pleased.

17Listen to your leaders and defer to them, for they keep watch over your souls as people who will give account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no benefit to you. 18Pray for us; for we are convinced that we have a good conscience, wanting to live well in every respect. 19I encourage you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you sooner.

20Now may the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep by the blood of an eternal covenant, our Lord Jesus, 21equip you with everything good to do his will, working in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory for ever. Amen.

22I encourage you, brothers and sisters: bear with this word of encouragement, for I have written to you only briefly. 23You should know that our brother Timothy has been released; if he comes soon, I will see you with him. 24Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those from Italy greet you. 25Grace be with you all.
