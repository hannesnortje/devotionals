Small-group kit · Session 1 / 13

The Final Word

Present ▶

The big ideaGod has spoken his final word in his Son, who radiates his glory, carries all things, has made purification for sins and now reigns far above the angels – so we can rest in his finished work and anchor our lives in the one who remains the same.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

When you receive important news, how do you most want to hear it – by text message, as a rumour, or from the person themselves? Why?

Read together · Hebrews 1:1–14

Read in two parts: one reader takes verses 1–4 (a single long sentence in Greek – read it slowly, without stopping); in verses 5–14 one reader reads the linking words and a second reader the seven Old Testament quotations.

Where we are in the story

Hebrews names no author and opens without a greeting. It reads like a sermon to a congregation of Jewish Christians (perhaps in or near Rome, cf. 13:24) who are under heavy pressure and tempted to go back to the visible temple service, the priesthood and the law of Moses. Tonight we read chapter 1: God's final word in the Son (vv. 1–4), and a string of seven Old Testament quotations showing that the Son is far above the angels (vv. 5–14).

A look at the Greek

  • apaugasma – ἀπαύγασμα “radiance”: The radiance or shining-out of light. Just as you cannot separate the sun from its rays, Christ is the visible radiance of God's invisible glory – he does not merely reflect God's light as the moon reflects the sun, he radiates God's own glory (1:3).
  • charaktēr – χαρακτήρ “very stamp”: The source of our word character: the exact impression a signet ring makes in warm wax, or the image struck on a coin, showing every finest line of the original. Christ is the exact expression of God's being (hypostasis, 1:3).
  • leitourgika pneumata – λειτουργικὰ πνεύματα “ministering spirits”: Literally liturgical, serving spirits. No angel was ever told to sit at God's right hand (Psalm 110:1); the angels are heaven's support staff – sent out to serve those who are going to inherit salvation (1:14).

Discussion

  1. In Greek, verses 1–4 are a single sentence. In the past God spoke “in many parts and in many ways”; now “he has spoken to us through the Son”. What is the difference between receiving messages from God and receiving his Son as his final word? (Hebrews 1:1–2)
  2. Christ is “the radiance of his glory and the very stamp of his being”. If you want to know what God is like, where do you look – and what do you see in Jesus? (Hebrews 1:3)
  3. “He carries all things by the powerful word.” The devotional describes this carrying as moving something forward to a destination, not balancing a dead weight like Atlas. Which part of your life feels as if it is falling apart – and what changes if he is carrying it? (Hebrews 1:3)
  4. The tabernacle had no chair for the priests; their work was never finished. But “when he himself had made purification for sins, he took his seat”. Where are you still standing – trying to earn God's favour – instead of resting in his finished work? (Hebrews 1:3–4)
  5. The first readers were tempted to fall back on what was visible, familiar and impressive. We may not bow before angels today – which substitutes fascinate us more than Jesus himself? (Hebrews 1:4–7)
  6. The Father says to the Son: “Your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever.” What does it mean for us that the one who was crucified is addressed as God and reigns? (Hebrews 1:8–9)
  7. “But you are the same, and your years will never come to an end.” Bodies grow old, relationships change, economies falter. Where do you need the unchanging Christ this week? And how does verse 14 comfort you – angels “sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation”? (Hebrews 1:10–14)

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week, before you look at the news or your phone, read Hebrews 1:3 and name one burden you are handing over to the one who carries all things.
  • Name one area where you still try to do enough for God before you dare to come to him. Thank him in so many words that the purification is finished and that he has sat down.

Pray together

Begin by reading verses 1–3 aloud together, slowly. Then pray in pairs: worship the Son for who he is (the radiance of God's glory, the very stamp of his being, the one who carries all things, the King), name one thing that feels out of control, and give it to him. Close by thanking God that his angels serve those who are going to inherit salvation.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers two devotionals (days 1–2). The chapter is dense; choose four or five questions and keep time for the last one.
  • Authorship: Hebrews names no author. If the group asks who wrote it, simply say that the letter does not tell us, and move on – it is not the topic of the evening.
  • Verse 6: firstborn (prōtotokos) is about rank, authority and the right of inheritance, not about Jesus having a beginning – David is called firstborn in Psalm 89:27, although he was Jesse's youngest son. In the same way “today I have fathered you” (v. 5) points in the New Testament to his enthronement as the risen Messiah-King (Acts 13:33; Romans 1:4), not to a moment when he was created.
  • Hebrews quotes the Old Testament in its Greek form (the Septuagint), and our translation renders the quotations as Hebrews has them. The line in verse 6 comes from Deuteronomy 32:43 in the Septuagint and is missing from the Hebrew text. If someone compares with their Old Testament and notices differences, that is the reason.
  • Angels can become a long side-track (guardian angels, experiences, speculation). Bring the group back to the chapter's point: the angels serve; the Son reigns and is worshipped.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 2 – the danger of drifting, and Jesus, who is not ashamed to call us brothers and sisters.

Scripture Hebrews 1:1–14

1In many parts and in many ways God spoke in the past to the fathers through the prophets; 2at the end of these days he has spoken to us through the Son. Him he appointed heir of everything, and through him he also made the worlds. 3He is the radiance of his glory and the very stamp of his being, and he carries all things by the powerful word. When he himself had made purification for sins, he took his seat at the right hand of the Majesty on high, 4and so he has become as much better than the angels as the name he has inherited is superior to theirs.

5For to which of the angels did he ever say:

You are my Son;today I have fathered you?And again:I will be a father to him,and he will be a son to me?

6And when he again brings the firstborn into the world, he says:

And let all God's angels worship him.

7With regard to the angels he says:

He makes his angels winds,and his ministers a flame of fire.

8But with regard to the Son:

Your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever,and the sceptre of uprightness is the sceptre of your kingdom.9You have loved righteousness and hated lawlessness;therefore God, your God, has anointed youwith the oil of rejoicing beyond your partners.

10And:

In the beginning, Lord, you laid the foundation of the earth,and the heavens are the works of your hands.11They will perish, but you remain;they will all wear out like a garment,12and like a cloak you will roll them up;like a garment they will also be changed.But you are the same,and your years will never come to an end.

13But to which of the angels has he ever said:

Sit at my right handuntil I make your enemiesa footstool for your feet?

14Are they not all ministering spirits, sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation?
