The Sacrifice of Praise and the Great Shepherd: Equipped by the God of Peace

Scripture Hebrews 13:15–25

15Through him, then, let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name. 16Do not forget to do good and to share, for with such sacrifices God is pleased.

17Listen to your leaders and defer to them, for they keep watch over your souls as people who will give account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no benefit to you. 18Pray for us; for we are convinced that we have a good conscience, wanting to live well in every respect. 19I encourage you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you sooner.

20Now may the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep by the blood of an eternal covenant, our Lord Jesus, 21equip you with everything good to do his will, working in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory for ever. Amen.

22I encourage you, brothers and sisters: bear with this word of encouragement, for I have written to you only briefly. 23You should know that our brother Timothy has been released; if he comes soon, I will see you with him. 24Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those from Italy greet you. 25Grace be with you all.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With Hebrews 13:15–25 we reach the majestic destination of our journey through the whole letter to the Hebrews. In yesterday's passage (13:10–14) the author called us to leave the earthly shadow-temple behind for good and to go out to Jesus "outside the camp". Now a practical question arises: If we no longer bring animal sacrifices to an earthly altar, what sacrifices do believers of the new covenant bring?

The author answers this question by pointing us to the true spiritual sacrifices of thanksgiving and service (verses 15–16) and to our relationship with spiritual leaders and our prayer for one another (verses 17–19). He then closes with one of the mightiest blessings (benedictions) in the whole of Scripture (verses 20–21) and a last personal greeting (verses 22–25).

Insights from the Greek

Let us dig into the riches of the Greek terms and the theological structure of these closing verses:

  • The two sacrifices of the new covenant: lips and hands (thysia aineseōs and koinōnia – θυσία αἰνέσεως, κοινωνία, vv. 15–16): Because Jesus' once-for-all sacrifice on the cross has ended all sin offerings for ever (10:18), all that remains for the believing priesthood are offerings of thanksgiving and praise! Under the old covenant (Leviticus 7:12) the praise offering (todah) was a voluntary thank offering for God's deliverance, and a later rabbinic tradition (Leviticus Rabbah 9:7) says that in the age to come all other offerings will cease except the todah praise offering. The author shows that we now bring God "continually" (dia pantos – διὰ παντὸς: in every season, not only at the festivals in Jerusalem), through Jesus our High Priest, two inseparable sacrifices:
    • Upward with our mouths (v. 15): "Through him, then, let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name" (thysian aineseōs – θυσίαν αἰνέσεως; karpon cheileōn – καρπὸν χειλέων, an expression from Hosea 14:2 (14:3 in the Septuagint); homologountōn – ὁμολογούντων).
    • Outward with our hands (v. 16): Praise with the lips without an open hand towards people in need is empty. That is why verse 16 immediately adds: "Do not forget to do good and to share, for with such sacrifices God is pleased" (eupoiias – εὐποιΐας, doing good; koinōnias – κοινωνίας, sharing what you have in practical and financial ways; thysiais – θυσίαις, sacrifices; euaresteitai – εὐαρεστεῖται, is pleased). Our praise of God and our generosity towards people are the true incense that fills the heavenly sanctuary!
  • Spiritual leadership, accountability and a good conscience (agrypneō and stenazō – ἀγρυπνέω, στενάζω, vv. 17–19): In verse 7 the author spoke of leaders who had already died; in verse 17 he speaks of their present, living spiritual leaders (hēgoumenois – ἡγουμένοις). He calls the church to listen to them (peithesthe – πείθεσθε: let yourselves be persuaded by their word from Scripture) and to defer to them (hypeikete – ὑπείκετε: willingly give way to their leading). Why? Look at the moving picture of a shepherd's task: "for they keep watch over your souls as people who will give account" (agrypnousin – ἀγρυπνοῦσιν, literally: they stay awake, giving up their night's rest; logon – λόγον, account). Spiritual leadership is not a title of honour but a heavy responsibility before the judgement seat of Christ. When church members are stubborn, leaders do their work "with groaning" (stenazontes – στενάζοντες: with a heavy, sighing heart), and that harms the church itself spiritually – it would be "of no benefit to you" (alysiteles – ἀλυσιτελές). In verses 18–19 the author humbly asks for prayer and testifies to a "good conscience" (kalēn syneidēsin – καλὴν συνείδησιν) – the same cleansed conscience he wrote about in chapters 9 and 10, now visible in living well (kalōs anastrephesthai – καλῶς ἀναστρέφεσθαι) in every respect.
  • The great blessing: the God of peace and the great shepherd (ho poimēn ho megas and katartizō – ὁ ποιμὴν ὁ μέγας, καταρτίζω, vv. 20–21): In verses 20 and 21 the author gathers all the great themes of the whole letter into one breathtaking closing prayer. It is the only place in the whole letter to the Hebrews that speaks explicitly of Jesus' resurrection from the dead!
    • The source: "The God of peace" (ho theos tēs eirēnēs – ὁ θεὸς τῆς εἰρήνης). Because the war between a holy God and sinful people was settled on the cross, we now approach him as the God of shalom.
    • The mighty act: He "brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep by the blood of an eternal covenant" (anagagōn – ἀναγαγών; ton poimena tōn probatōn ton megan – τὸν ποιμένα τῶν προβάτων τὸν μέγαν). Here the author reaches back to Isaiah 63:11, which speaks of how God brought up Moses, the shepherd of his flock, out of the sea. Moses was only an earthly shepherd; Jesus is the great shepherd (cf. John 10:11), who went through death and was raised from the grave by the worth of his own covenant blood, to lead his flock for ever!
    • The equipping within us (katartizō): And what does the author ask this God of the resurrection to do in us? May he "equip you with everything good to do his will" (katartisai – καταρτίσαι)! The Greek verb katartizō (which we also met in 11:3, where the worlds were fashioned by God's word) is a rich term, used in medicine for splinting and setting a broken bone, and by fishermen for mending and preparing torn nets (Matthew 4:21). And how does he equip us? We do not have to squeeze it out of our own human strength: he does it, "working in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ" (poiōn en hēmin – ποιῶν ἐν ἡμῖν)! What God requires of us, he himself works in us by his Spirit!
  • A word of encouragement and the last greeting (logos tēs paraklēseōs – λόγος τῆς παρακλήσεως, vv. 22–25): The author modestly calls his whole masterly letter a "word of encouragement" (tou logou tēs paraklēseōs – τοῦ λόγου τῆς παρακλήσεως – the same expression that Acts 13:15 uses for a sermon in the synagogue), which he has written "only briefly" (dia bracheōn – διὰ βραχέων) – it takes about an hour or less to read the whole letter aloud! He shares the good news that their brother Timothy has been released (probably from prison), passes on the greetings of the believers "from Italy", and ends with the simplest and yet richest blessing imaginable: "Grace be with you all."

Core Theological Truths

  1. The sacrifice of the cross has ended all sin offerings; now we bring praise and good deeds. The fruit of our lips and the sharing of our hands are the sacrifices with which God is pleased.
  2. Spiritual leadership is a heavy responsibility. Leaders keep watch over our souls as people who will give account; we listen to them and pray for them.
  3. The risen Jesus is the great shepherd. The God of peace brought him up from the dead by the blood of an eternal covenant to lead his flock for ever.
  4. What God asks of us, he himself works in us. He equips us with everything good and works in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ.

Living It Out

At the end of our long journey of twenty-eight days through the whole letter to the Hebrews, these last verses leave us with three unforgettable life lessons for the road ahead.

First, look at how your whole everyday life becomes a sanctuary (vv. 15–16). Sometimes we think that "worship" only happens when we sing a song on Sunday, and that the rest of our week is "secular". But Hebrews 13:15–16 tears down that division: every time you choose, in the middle of daily pressure, not to complain but to speak through Jesus a word of thanks or praise over his name ("the fruit of lips"), incense rises to God's throne. And every time you use your time, your knowledge or your means to do practical good and to share with others (koinōnia), you lay on the altar a sacrifice in which the Father takes deep pleasure!

Second, look at the comfort of the great shepherd who rose from the dead (v. 20). Throughout the letter we have come to know Jesus as our great High Priest in heaven; now, on the very last page, we also meet him as our great shepherd on the ground beside us! A high priest intercedes for you before God's throne; a shepherd walks with you through the deep valleys of the shadow of death, looks for you when you wander, and carries you on his shoulders when you are tired. And because the God of peace brought him up from the grave by the blood of an eternal covenant, there is no dark valley in your life where your shepherd has not already won the victory.

Third, look at the secret of Christian service in verse 21: may he equip you, "working in us what is pleasing in his sight"! Do you sometimes feel broken like a limb put out of joint (12:13), or torn like an old fishing net, when you look at the calling before you? Do not lose heart! The same God who fashioned the worlds (katartizō, 11:3) is the God who mends your broken places, equips you and by his Spirit himself works in you the strength and the willingness to do his will. We rest in his finished work, and we work out of his indwelling strength!

So our journey through Hebrews ends where it began: with the Son through whom God "at the end of these days … has spoken to us" (1:2). Keep your eyes fixed on Jesus – he is yesterday and today the same, and for ever.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • The sacrifices of lips and hands (vv. 15–16): Is my daily life marked at present by a continual sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving on my lips, and by practical sharing with my hands?
  • Joy for spiritual leaders (vv. 17–18): Do I pray faithfully for those who give spiritual guidance in my life and minister the Word – and does my attitude make it a joy for them to keep watch over my soul?
  • Equipped by the great shepherd (vv. 20–21): Where do I feel inadequate today for the task or calling God has entrusted to me? How does it change my outlook to know that the God of peace himself is working in me what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ?

Prayer

God of peace, at the end of this rich journey through your Word I bow before you with a heart overflowing with worship. Today, through Jesus Christ, I bring you the sacrifice of praise of my lips that confess your holy name, and I ask you to open my life and my hands to do good tirelessly and to share with those around me, for your pleasure.

I thank you that you brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep, our Lord Jesus, by the precious blood of an eternal covenant! Lord Jesus, you are not only my High Priest in heaven, but also my faithful shepherd on every step of my earthly pilgrimage. Bless and strengthen everyone you have appointed to keep watch over our souls and to minister your Word; grant that they may fulfil their service with joy and a good conscience.

And now, God of peace, take my life into your hands: restore what is broken, and equip me fully with everything good to do your will. By your Spirit work in my inmost being what is pleasing in your sight, through Jesus Christ – to whom be all praise, honour and glory, yesterday, today and for ever! Amen.
