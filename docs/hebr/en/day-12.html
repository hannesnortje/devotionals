Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 12 / 28

God's Unchangeable Oath: The Sure and Firm Anchor of Our Soul

Scripture Hebrews 6:13–20

13For when God made his promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself, 14saying: "I will surely bless you and surely multiply you." 15And so, after waiting patiently, Abraham obtained the promise. 16For people swear by someone greater than themselves, and for them an oath is the end of every dispute, as confirmation. 17In the same way God, wanting to show even more clearly to the heirs of the promise how unchangeable his purpose was, guaranteed it with an oath, 18so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before us. 19We have this hope as an anchor of the soul, sure and firm, and it enters the inner place behind the curtain, 20where Jesus has entered as a forerunner on our behalf, having become high priest for ever according to the order of Melchizedek.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

At the end of yesterday's passage (Hebrews 6:12) the author called us not to become sluggish, but to "imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises". Now, in verses 13 to 20, he takes up the greatest Old Testament example of such patient faith: the patriarch Abraham.

To Jewish Christians shaken by uncertainty and persecution, the author shows that our hope does not rest on wishful thinking or human optimism, but on the rock-solid character of God himself. With these verses he also closes the long section of exhortation that began in Hebrews 5:11, and he masterfully brings us back into the main stream of his argument: Jesus as our eternal high priest according to the order of Melchizedek (v. 20).

Insights from the Greek

Let us explore the historical, linguistic and theological richness of this passage:

  • Abraham on Mount Moriah and God's oath by himself (vv. 13–15): The author quotes Genesis 22:16–17 – the dramatic moment on Mount Moriah just after Abraham had been willing to offer his only son of promise, Isaac: "I will surely bless you and surely multiply you." Abraham had already had to wait twenty-five years from God's first promise of offspring in Genesis 12 until Isaac was finally born ("after waiting patiently" – makrothymēsas – μακροθυμήσας). And on Moriah God confirms his promise in an extraordinary way: he swears an oath. In ancient legal practice (v. 16) one swore by someone greater than oneself (such as by God's name) to settle every "dispute" (antilogia – ἀντιλογία) once and for all. But because there is no one in the universe greater than God, he swore by himself. In doing so God put his own deity, his honour and his very being on the line as a guarantee!
  • The "two unchangeable things" and God's guarantee (mesiteuō – μεσιτεύω and ametathetos – ἀμετάθετος, vv. 17–18a): God's word alone is already completely reliable; he never needs to swear an oath to be honest. Why then did he do it? Verse 17 says he did it for the sake of our human weakness and doubt! He wanted "to show even more clearly to the heirs of the promise how unchangeable his purpose was" (to ametatheton – τὸ ἀμετάθετον – a word used in ancient legal language of a will or agreement that could not be cancelled or altered). So God "guaranteed it with an oath". The Greek verb in verse 17 is emesiteusen (ἐμεσίτευσεν, from mesiteuō), related to mesitēs, "mediator" (8:6; 9:15): to step in as guarantor or mediator. God, as it were, came and stood between himself and us as his own guarantee! And so our faith now rests on "two unchangeable things" (dia dyo pragmatōn ametathetōn – διὰ δύο πραγμάτων ἀμεταθέτων), "in which it is impossible for God to lie" (adynaton – ἀδύνατον): first, his unfailing promise, and second, his irrevocable oath.
  • Fleeing to the city of refuge (katapheugō – καταφεύγω, v. 18b): Through this double guarantee "we who have fled for refuge" (hoi kataphygontes – οἱ καταφυγόντες) "might have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before us". In the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) the verb katapheugō is used, among other things, for someone who fled to one of Israel's six cities of refuge (see Numbers 35; Deuteronomy 19:5; Joshua 20) to be protected from the avenger of blood. We have fled from the judgement on our sin to Christ, our true city of refuge, and now hold on with both hands to the firm hope stretched out before us.
  • The anchor of the soul that is cast upwards (ankyra tēs psychēs – ἄγκυρα τῆς ψυχῆς, v. 19): In verse 19 the author uses one of the best-known images of early Christianity: our hope is "an anchor of the soul, sure (asphalē – ἀσφαλῆ – it cannot slip) and firm (bebaian – βεβαίαν – it cannot break)". (Many anchors have been found on early Christian graves in the catacombs of Rome; the anchor was a familiar symbol outside the church as well, but Christians probably linked it with this verse.) But notice how the author turns the picture of an earthly anchor completely on its head! An ordinary ship's anchor is thrown downwards to the bottom of the passing sea. Our spiritual anchor, however, is cast upwards and inwards: it "enters the inner place behind the curtain" (eis to esōteron tou katapetasmatos – εἰς τὸ ἐσώτερον τοῦ καταπετάσματος) – straight into the heavenly Most Holy Place, where it takes hold at the throne of God himself!
  • Jesus our forerunner (prodromos – πρόδρομος, v. 20): How did our anchor get there behind the curtain? Through Jesus, who "has entered as a forerunner on our behalf". The Greek word prodromos fits beautifully with both the ship picture of verse 19 and the temple picture:
  • A picture from seafaring: A well-known sermon illustration (for which, however, there is no clear ancient evidence) tells of harbours with a dangerous sandbank or reef across the entrance. When a storm was blowing and the tide was low, a large cargo ship could not enter without running aground. Then a small, nimble rowing boat – the "forerunner" – would take the big ship's heavy anchor, row over the dangerous reef into the calm harbour and set the anchor there in firm ground. Afterwards the big ship could gradually haul itself safely into the harbour along that anchor rope. Whether or not it happened exactly like that, the picture captures the author's point perfectly!
  • In the temple service: Under the old covenant the high priest was never a forerunner into the Most Holy Place. Once a year he went behind the curtain in fear, and no one could ever follow him there. But because Jesus has become "high priest for ever according to the order of Melchizedek", he has gone ahead as our prodromos to open the way, so that we can all follow him into God's presence!

Core Theological Truths

  1. Our hope rests on God's character, not on our optimism. The believer's hope is grounded in the rock-solid character of God, who swore by himself.
  2. God stoops to our weakness. God's word is enough, yet for the sake of our doubt he confirmed his promise with an oath and made himself guarantor.
  3. Two unchangeable things secure our hope. God's promise and God's oath – and it is impossible for God to lie.
  4. Christ is our city of refuge. We have fled to him for refuge and hold fast to the hope set before us.
  5. Where the forerunner is, his followers will be too. Jesus has entered behind the curtain as forerunner on our behalf; our hope is anchored there, with our eternal high priest according to the order of Melchizedek.

Living It Out

When the storms of life blow up – when a medical report shocks you, when financial or work pressure mounts, or when the world around you feels unstable – our emotions are tossed about like a ship on a rough sea. If in those moments your "anchor" is lying inside the boat (in your own feelings, your clever plans or your own staying power), or is fastened in the loose sand of earthly security, you will drift away (Hebrews 2:1).

Hebrews 6:13–20 teaches us that Christian hope is completely different from the way the world uses the word. In everyday speech we say, "I hope it rains tomorrow" or "I hope everything works out" – by which we really mean that we are unsure and keeping our fingers crossed. Biblical hope (elpis – ἐλπίς), by contrast, is a blood-bought, oath-bound certainty about the future, because it is grounded on two unchangeable things in which God cannot lie: his promise and his oath.

Think today about the overwhelming comfort of verses 19 and 20 for your own life:

  • You do not have to wait for the storm to die down in order to be safe. An anchor proves its worth precisely when the wind blows and the waves pound against the bow. Your circumstances may be shaking you violently today, but the cable of your faith is fastened to an invisible Rock behind the heavenly curtain.
  • Where the forerunner is, his followers will be too. Because Jesus as our prodromos has already entered the heavenly sanctuary with our human nature and fastened the anchor of our salvation there, our final safe arrival in the harbour is guaranteed. Every day his grace draws you a little closer to the harbour where your forerunner is already waiting for you.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • What is your anchor fastened to today? When you look at the things that give you peace (or steal it), is your soul's anchor cast into temporary earthly securities that can shift, or is it fixed behind the curtain with Christ?
  • Doubting God's character: When you struggle to believe that God will keep his promises for your future and your salvation, do you realize that God has given his own name and oath as a guarantee – and that it is impossible for him to lie?
  • Abraham's long waiting room: Abraham had to wait patiently for decades (makrothymēsas) between the promise and its fulfilment. How does knowing that God's purpose is unchangeable (ametathetos) help you to hold on today while you are still waiting for his timing?

Prayer

Unchanging and faithful God, I worship you today as the One for whom it is impossible to lie. Thank you that you know my fragile, doubting human heart so well that you have not only given me your precious promises, but have also guaranteed them with an irrevocable oath by your own name.

Lord Jesus, I thank you that I may flee to you as my only safe city of refuge. When the storms of this life beat against my soul and my own strength fails me, I choose today not to look at the waves, but to hold fast to the unshakeable hope that lies before me. Thank you that this hope is a sure and firm anchor for my soul that can never break or slip.

I praise you, O Christ, that as my forerunner you have already gone ahead behind the heavenly curtain and fastened my life there to the Father's throne. Because you are there as my eternal high priest according to the order of Melchizedek, I know that you will draw me safely into the eternal harbour. Give me today the patience of Abraham, to wait on you in calm trust. Amen.
