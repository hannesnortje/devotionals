Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 6 / 28

Greater than Moses: The Servant in the House and the Son over the House

Scripture Hebrews 3:1–6

1Therefore, holy brothers and sisters, partners in a heavenly calling, consider closely the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus. 2He is faithful to the one who appointed him, as Moses also was in his house. 3For he has been counted worthy of greater glory than Moses, just as the builder of a house has greater honour than the house itself. 4For every house is built by someone, but the one who built all things is God. 5Moses was faithful in all his house as a servant, to testify to the things that were to be spoken later, 6but Christ as a Son over his house. And we are his house, if we hold fast to the confidence and the pride we take in hope.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

In the first two chapters the author has shown us that Jesus is exalted above the prophets and the angels. Now he comes to the very greatest human figure in Israel's history: Moses. For a first-century Jew, Moses was the undisputed giant of faith. He was the man who led Israel out of Egypt with signs and wonders, who received the Ten Commandments at Sinai, the author of the Torah, and the man with whom God spoke "mouth to mouth" and "face to face" (Numbers 12:6–8; Deuteronomy 34:10). Some Jewish traditions even placed Moses above the angels.

Had the author of Hebrews tried to exalt Jesus by running Moses down, his Jewish readers would have stopped listening at once. But look at the masterly wisdom and respect with which he goes about it: he fully affirms Moses' greatness and faithfulness, and then shows how the glory of Jesus surpasses that of Moses at every level. Just as the architect of a building is infinitely more important than one of the stones in its wall, so Christ is greater than Moses.

Insights from the Greek

Let us dig into the riches of the Greek terms in these six verses:

  • Our dignity and our task (katanoēsate – κατανοήσατε, v. 1): The author addresses the discouraged believers with two exalted titles: they are "holy brothers and sisters" (set apart for God through Christ) and "partners in a heavenly calling" (metochoi – μέτοχοι – partners who share together). Then he gives the central command of the passage: "consider closely … Jesus." The Greek verb is katanoēsate (from katanoeō – κατανοέω). It does not mean taking a quick glance at something; it means fixing your thoughts deeply on it, studying it carefully, pondering it and getting to the bottom of it. What are we to focus on so intently? On Jesus in his double office as "the apostle and high priest of our confession" (apostolos – ἀπόστολος, archiereus – ἀρχιερεύς, homologia – ὁμολογία – what we confess together as true). This is the only place in the whole New Testament where Jesus is called an "apostle"! An apostolos is an official envoy sent out with the full authority of the sender. Under the old covenant Moses was the "apostle" sent by God to Pharaoh (in the Greek Septuagint, Exodus 3:10 uses exactly the verb apostellō – ἀποστέλλω), while his brother Aaron was the high priest.
  • The house and its builder (oikos – οἶκος and kataskeuazō – κατασκευάζω, vv. 2–4): The author draws on Numbers 12:7, where God himself testifies that Moses is faithful in all God's house. The Greek word for "house" (oikos) has a double meaning: it refers to a building (such as the tabernacle or the temple), but also to a household, family or dynasty (the people of God). Both Moses and Jesus were faithful (pistos – πιστός) to God. But Jesus is the builder (ho kataskeuasas – ὁ κατασκευάσας – creator, architect and outfitter) of the house! And verse 4 draws the line straight through to Jesus' deity: "but the one who built all things is God."
  • The servant in the house and the Son over the house (therapōn – θεράπων versus huios – υἱός, vv. 5–6a): In verse 5 Moses is called a "servant". The author does not use the ordinary Greek word for a slave (doulos – δοῦλος), but the very honourable word therapōn. A therapōn was a trusted, honoured servant, a personal confidant of his master. Moses' high calling as therapōn in the house was "to testify to the things that were to be spoken later" – in other words, everything Moses did and wrote (the tabernacle, the sacrifices, the exodus) was a prophetic preview pointing to the future coming of Christ. But look at the contrast in verse 6: Moses was a servant in (en – ἐν) the house, but Christ is there "as a Son over his house" – over (epi – ἐπί) the house! The servant carries out orders within the household; the Son is the rightful heir and ruler to whom the whole house belongs.
  • We are his house (parrēsia – παρρησία and kauchēma – καύχημα, v. 6b): Parrēsia means open boldness and unashamed assurance in standing before God; kauchēma means a jubilant pride or joy in the hope that awaits us. (The words ‘firm to the end’, which older translations have here in verse 6, are not in the oldest manuscripts; the author does use them in verse 14.)

Core Theological Truths

  1. Jesus is apostle and high priest in one person. Under the old covenant Moses was the one sent and Aaron was the high priest. Under the new covenant Jesus fulfils both offices perfectly in one person: as apostle he comes from God to us to make the Father known; as high priest he goes to God on our behalf to make atonement.
  2. Christ is the divine builder, not part of the building. Moses himself was part of the "house" (the community of faith); he was a redeemed sinner within Israel who needed grace himself. Moses is an honourable part of the building, but Christ is the divine architect who brought the whole household – and the universe – into being.
  3. The Son rules over the house. The servant's task was to point ahead; the Son is the fulfilment and the owner of the house.
  4. We are his house – the evidence of true faith. Under the new covenant God's temple is no longer a building of stone and gold in Jerusalem: "And we are his house …" As believers we form the living household and temple in which God dwells. Then the author closes with a conditional sentence that prepares us directly for tomorrow's warning: "if we hold fast to the confidence and the pride we take in hope." This does not teach that we earn our salvation by our own perseverance; rather, it shows that perseverance is the evidence of genuine faith. Whoever has truly been built into his house by the builder holds fast to him to the end.

Living It Out

How does this passage speak to us today? When life gets hard, we all look for fixed points. Sometimes we make the mistake of building our faith on "servants in the house" – wonderful spiritual leaders, pastors, theological systems or church traditions. Like Moses, these people and structures are precious gifts from God, meant to point to Jesus like signposts. But when we pitch our tent at the signpost instead of looking at the destination itself, we will be disappointed, because every human leader is himself only a broken stone in the building.

Hebrews 3:1 gives us the simple, powerful remedy for spiritual weariness and confusion: Katanoēsate – fix your thoughts deeply and wholly on Jesus!

  • When you feel that God is far away and unknown, look to Jesus as your apostle: he was sent by the Father to you to open God's heart to you perfectly.
  • When you feel guilty and unworthy, look to Jesus as your high priest: he stands before the Father on your behalf and pleads his own blood for your acquittal.
  • When you are worried about the church, your family or your own spiritual growth, look to Jesus as the builder and Son over the house: you do not have to hold up the roof of your life in your own strength. You belong to his household, and the Son rules over his own house.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Where does your attention go (katanoeō)? If you look honestly at what you think about and dwell on most during the day, does Jesus as your apostle and high priest receive your deepest focus – or only the leftover moments of your attention?
  • Servants or the Son: Have you perhaps tied your spiritual security to a human leader, a congregation or a tradition ("servants in the house"), instead of anchoring yourself directly to the Son who rules over the house?
  • Confidence and joyful hope: What do your parrēsia (confidence) and your kauchēma (pride/joy in hope) look like today? Have disappointments made you shrink back into silence, or are you still holding on to his promises with glad expectation?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, I thank you that you have called me by grace to be a holy child of yours and a partner in the heavenly calling. Thank you that I am not a stranger or an outsider, but part of your own household – the living temple that you inhabit by your Spirit.

Lord Jesus, today I obey the call of your word and fix my thoughts and the eyes of my heart on you anew. You are the perfect apostle who came from heaven to me to reveal the Father's love to me, and you are the faithful high priest who intercedes for me before the throne of grace. You are infinitely greater than every earthly leader or prophet, for you are the builder of all things and the eternal Son who rules over your house.

Forgive me when my eyes wander to people or circumstances. Build my life and my family according to your perfect architect's plan. Give me, through your Spirit, the strength to hold fast unshakeably to the end to the confidence and the joyful pride of the hope I have in you. Amen.
