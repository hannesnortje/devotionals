Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 8

Shadow and Reality

Hebrews 8:1–13

To get started

Have you ever seen an architect's model or a sketch of a house before it was built? How did the real building compare with it?

Where we are in the story

In chapter 7 the author showed that the person of our high priest, according to the order of Melchizedek, is far higher than the Levitical priests. Now chapter 8 turns to his ministry: a better sanctuary and a better covenant. For the first readers, cut off from the temple in Jerusalem and perhaps mocked for having no temple, no priest and no altar, this was a burning question. The author answers with the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament, Jeremiah 31:31–34, quoted as the Greek translation has it.

A look at the Greek

κεφάλαιον

kephalaion · “the main point”

Not just a summary but the crowning point of the whole argument (8:1). And the core is a seated high priest: the earthly priests had to stand every day because their work was never finished (cf. 10:11); ours has taken his seat.

A look at the Greek

ὑπόδειγμα καὶ σκιά

hypodeigma kai skia · “copy and shadow”

A hypodeigma is a copy or sketch; Moses had to build according to the typos, the pattern or blueprint shown on the mountain (8:5). Like a shadow on the ground or an architect's scale model, the tent worship was good and God-given, but it pointed to a greater reality: Christ's ministry in heaven.

A look at the Greek

διαθήκη καινή

diathēkē kainē · “a new covenant”

Kainos is often distinguished from neos: not only new in time but new in kind (the distinction is not strict – in 12:24 Hebrews also uses nea). Its promises are better because God no longer says “If you …” but “I will!” (8:8–12).

The big idea

We have a high priest who has sat down in the true tent and is mediator of a better covenant, whose promises rest on God's own “I will”: he writes his laws on our hearts and remembers our sins no more.

Discussion · 1/7

The main point is this: “we have such a high priest, who took his seat at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens”. Why would the author make a seated priest the core of his whole argument?

Hebrews 8:1–2

Discussion · 2/7

The earthly priests serve “a copy and shadow of the heavenly things”. The first readers had lost the visible temple. Which visible things – forms, rituals, human approval – do we lean on for our spiritual security?

Hebrews 8:3–5

Discussion · 3/7

If the first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no need for a second. Read verse 8: “For he finds fault with them when he says”. Where did the fault lie – and what did the old covenant not give?

Hebrews 8:6–9

Discussion · 4/7

“I will put my laws into their minds, and I will write them on their hearts.” Do you experience God's will at the moment more like tablets of stone – an outward burden – or like something the Spirit is writing within you? What makes the difference?

Hebrews 8:10

Discussion · 5/7

“For they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest.” Under the old covenant ordinary people depended on prophets, priests and kings. What does it mean for the youngest and the oldest in our group that each can know the Lord personally?

Hebrews 8:11

Discussion · 6/7

Verse 12 begins with “For”: everything rests on it. “For I will be merciful toward their unrighteous deeds, and I will remember their sins no more.” The Greek uses a double negative – never, ever again. Is there a sin you have confessed long ago but still accuse yourself of? What would change if you believed this verse?

Hebrews 8:12

Discussion · 7/7

“What is becoming old and growing aged is close to disappearing.” How can we honour the first covenant as God-given and still not go back to the shadows?

Hebrews 8:13

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week pray verse 10 for one specific area of your life where you are pushing yourself with clenched teeth: ask the Lord to write his will there on your heart and to change your desires from within.
  • Write down one old, confessed sin that you keep digging up. Read verse 12 aloud over it, thank God that he will never again use it against you – and then tear up the paper.

Pray together

Read verses 10–12 aloud together. Then pray in twos or threes: thank Jesus that he has sat down as our high priest in the true tent, ask him to write his laws on your hearts, and thank the Father, in your own words, for the promise of verse 12. Leave a moment of silence for each person to receive it personally.
