Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 1

The Final Word

Hebrews 1:1–14

To get started

When you receive important news, how do you most want to hear it – by text message, as a rumour, or from the person themselves? Why?

Where we are in the story

Hebrews names no author and opens without a greeting. It reads like a sermon to a congregation of Jewish Christians (perhaps in or near Rome, cf. 13:24) who are under heavy pressure and tempted to go back to the visible temple service, the priesthood and the law of Moses. Tonight we read chapter 1: God's final word in the Son (vv. 1–4), and a string of seven Old Testament quotations showing that the Son is far above the angels (vv. 5–14).

A look at the Greek

ἀπαύγασμα

apaugasma · “radiance”

The radiance or shining-out of light. Just as you cannot separate the sun from its rays, Christ is the visible radiance of God's invisible glory – he does not merely reflect God's light as the moon reflects the sun, he radiates God's own glory (1:3).

A look at the Greek

χαρακτήρ

charaktēr · “very stamp”

The source of our word character: the exact impression a signet ring makes in warm wax, or the image struck on a coin, showing every finest line of the original. Christ is the exact expression of God's being (hypostasis, 1:3).

A look at the Greek

λειτουργικὰ πνεύματα

leitourgika pneumata · “ministering spirits”

Literally liturgical, serving spirits. No angel was ever told to sit at God's right hand (Psalm 110:1); the angels are heaven's support staff – sent out to serve those who are going to inherit salvation (1:14).

The big idea

God has spoken his final word in his Son, who radiates his glory, carries all things, has made purification for sins and now reigns far above the angels – so we can rest in his finished work and anchor our lives in the one who remains the same.

Discussion · 1/7

In Greek, verses 1–4 are a single sentence. In the past God spoke “in many parts and in many ways”; now “he has spoken to us through the Son”. What is the difference between receiving messages from God and receiving his Son as his final word?

Hebrews 1:1–2

Discussion · 2/7

Christ is “the radiance of his glory and the very stamp of his being”. If you want to know what God is like, where do you look – and what do you see in Jesus?

Hebrews 1:3

Discussion · 3/7

“He carries all things by the powerful word.” The devotional describes this carrying as moving something forward to a destination, not balancing a dead weight like Atlas. Which part of your life feels as if it is falling apart – and what changes if he is carrying it?

Hebrews 1:3

Discussion · 4/7

The tabernacle had no chair for the priests; their work was never finished. But “when he himself had made purification for sins, he took his seat”. Where are you still standing – trying to earn God's favour – instead of resting in his finished work?

Hebrews 1:3–4

Discussion · 5/7

The first readers were tempted to fall back on what was visible, familiar and impressive. We may not bow before angels today – which substitutes fascinate us more than Jesus himself?

Hebrews 1:4–7

Discussion · 6/7

The Father says to the Son: “Your throne, O God, stands for ever and ever.” What does it mean for us that the one who was crucified is addressed as God and reigns?

Hebrews 1:8–9

Discussion · 7/7

“But you are the same, and your years will never come to an end.” Bodies grow old, relationships change, economies falter. Where do you need the unchanging Christ this week? And how does verse 14 comfort you – angels “sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation”?

Hebrews 1:10–14

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week, before you look at the news or your phone, read Hebrews 1:3 and name one burden you are handing over to the one who carries all things.
  • Name one area where you still try to do enough for God before you dare to come to him. Thank him in so many words that the purification is finished and that he has sat down.

Pray together

Begin by reading verses 1–3 aloud together, slowly. Then pray in pairs: worship the Son for who he is (the radiance of God's glory, the very stamp of his being, the one who carries all things, the King), name one thing that feels out of control, and give it to him. Close by thanking God that his angels serve those who are going to inherit salvation.
