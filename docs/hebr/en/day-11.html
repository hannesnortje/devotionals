Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Day 11 / 28

Earnestness and Perseverance: Inheriting the Promises through Faith and Patience

Scripture Hebrews 6:4–12

4For it is impossible for those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift and have become partners of the Holy Spirit, 5who have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the age to come, 6and who have fallen away, to be renewed again to repentance – crucifying the Son of God again for themselves and holding him up to contempt. 7For land that drinks the rain that often falls on it, and produces vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is cultivated, receives a blessing from God. 8But if it bears thorns and thistles, it fails the test and is close to being cursed, and its end is to be burned.

9But even though we speak like this, dear friends, we are convinced of better things in your case, things that belong to salvation. 10For God is not unjust; he will not forget your work and the love you have shown for his name when you served the saints, as you still serve them. 11And we long for each one of you to show the same eagerness for the full assurance of hope to the end, 12so that you do not become sluggish, but imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Hebrews 6:4–12 is without doubt one of the most debated, and sometimes most feared, passages in the whole New Testament. Down the centuries many sincere and sensitive believers have read verses 4 to 6 and wondered whether they have stumbled too far to be forgiven by God. To understand these verses rightly, however, we must read them within the historical setting of the letter and in the light of the farming picture of verses 7–8.

The author is speaking here to Jewish Christians who were considering openly denying Christ and returning to the temple sacrifices of Judaism to escape persecution. To go back to the synagogue would have meant declaring, together with Jesus' opponents, that Jesus was a false Messiah and a blasphemer who deserved his death on the cross. The author warns them that such a final, deliberate rejection of Christ leaves no other lifeboat. Yet right after this thunderclap, the sun of pastoral tenderness breaks brightly through in verse 9: he calls them "dear friends" (agapētoi – ἀγαπητοί) and assures them that he sees the fruit of genuine salvation in their lives!

Insights from the Greek

Let us dig into the richness of the Greek terms and the theological structure of this passage:

  • The five privileges of the community of faith (vv. 4–5): The author describes people who had experienced enormous spiritual blessings within the visible covenant community (just as the wilderness generation of chapter 3 ate the manna and saw the pillar of cloud):
  • Once enlightened (phōtisthentas – φωτισθέντας): They had received the knowledge of the light of the gospel and had stepped out of the darkness of ignorance in their understanding.
  • Tasted the heavenly gift (geusamenous – γευσαμένους): They had come to know at first hand the gracious gift of God that came down from heaven.
  • Become partners of the Holy Spirit (metochous – μετόχους): They had shared in the common working and power of the Spirit in the congregation.
  • Tasted the goodness of the word of God (kalon theou rhēma – καλὸν θεοῦ ῥῆμα): They had heard the powerful proclamation of God's spoken promises and experienced their goodness.
  • The powers of the age to come (dynameis mellontos aiōnos – δυνάμεις μέλλοντος αἰῶνος): They had seen the miracles and spiritual gifts (see Hebrews 2:4) that broke into the present like a foretaste of God's coming kingdom.
  • The impossibility of renewal after open rejection (parapiptō – παραπίπτω and anastaurountas – ἀνασταυροῦντας, v. 6): What happens when someone who has experienced all this light and power at close quarters deliberately has "fallen away" (parapesontas – παραπεσόντας – a total, calculated falling away and betrayal of the truth, not a believer stumbling into sin out of weakness)? The author says it is impossible for them "to be renewed again to repentance – crucifying the Son of God again for themselves and holding him up to contempt" (anastaurountas … paradeigmatizontas – παραδειγματίζοντας). Notice the present participles (crucifying, holding up): repentance is impossible as long as someone persists in the attitude that rejects Jesus afresh and shouts with his crucifiers that he is an impostor. If you reject the only high priest and the only final sacrifice of the cross, there is no "Plan B" for salvation. (And for anyone who is afraid today of having committed this sin: the very fact that your heart is tender, that you grieve over your sin and long for Jesus, is the unfailing proof that you are not in the hardened state of verse 6!)
  • The parable of the land: the same rain, different harvests (vv. 7–8): To explain verses 4–6 the author uses a picture from nature (reminiscent of Isaiah 5 and Jesus' parable of the sower). Two pieces of land receive exactly the same gracious showers from heaven (the blessings of verses 4–5). One drinks in the rain and produces useful vegetation for the farmer. The other drinks the same rain but pushes up only thorns and thistles. The point is clear: a tree is known by its fruit. Outward exposure to spiritual rain is not enough; where true new birth has taken place, the ground will in time bring forth the fruit of repentance and love.
  • Things that belong to salvation (echomena sōtērias – ἐχόμενα σωτηρίας, vv. 9–10): In verse 9 the tone changes at once. This is the only place in the whole letter where the author addresses his readers as "dear friends" (agapētoi). He is "convinced of better things in your case" (like the fruitful land of verse 7) – "things that belong to salvation" (echomena sōtērias – things inseparably bound up with true salvation). How does he know that they are truly saved and not thorns and thistles? Verse 10 gives the answer: by their work and the love they have shown for God's name. (Older translations read "labour of love" here; those words are found in some manuscripts, but not in the Greek text we follow – compare 1 Thessalonians 1:3.) They did not just say with their mouths that they knew God; under difficult circumstances they served their fellow believers ("the saints") practically and sacrificially, and they still do! And God is just: he never forgets a single cup of cold water or act of love shown for the sake of his name.
  • Faith and patience to the end (plērophoria – πληροφορία and makrothymia – μακροθυμία, vv. 11–12): The author's heart's desire is for each one of them "to show the same eagerness for the full assurance of hope to the end" (spoudēn – σπουδήν; plērophorian tēs elpidos – πληροφορίαν τῆς ἐλπίδος – like a sailing ship whose sails are filled with wind). He warns them not to become "sluggish" (nōthroi – νωθροί – the same word as in 5:11!) again, but to "imitate" (mimētai – μιμηταί) the heroes of faith who inherit the promises through two things: faith (pistis – πίστις) and patience (makrothymia – a long breath that keeps on believing even when a long time passes between God's promise and its fulfilment).

Core Theological Truths

  1. Privilege is not the same as fruit. A person can experience the light, the gift, the working of the Spirit and the powers of the age to come at close quarters – and still reject Christ.
  2. There is no other sacrifice than Christ. Whoever rejects the only high priest and the only sacrifice of the cross has no "Plan B"; as long as someone keeps crucifying the Son of God again, repentance is impossible.
  3. A tree is known by its fruit. The same rain falls on all the land, but true new birth becomes visible in a harvest of repentance and love.
  4. God does not forget the love shown in his name. God is not unjust; every hidden act of service to the saints is known to him.
  5. The promises are inherited through faith and patience. True faith has a long breath and holds fast to the full assurance of hope to the end.

Living It Out

This passage holds up both a mirror and a tremendous encouragement for us today.

First, the mirror of verses 7–8 asks us: What am I doing with the spiritual rain that falls on my life? We have more access to Bibles, sermons, Christian books and devotionals than any generation before us. We are soaked in "rain". But the test of our spiritual life does not lie in how much rain falls on us or how moved we feel during a worship song; it lies in the harvest our life produces. Are we letting the word sink into the soil of our hearts and bring forth practical fruit of humility, obedience and service?

Second, verse 10 gives every discouraged servant of the Lord an indescribable comfort: "For God is not unjust; he will not forget your work …" Perhaps you feel exhausted today. You serve your family, you help other believers in need, you pray in secret, and you do your work with integrity – and sometimes it feels as if no one notices or appreciates it. Hear the promise of Scripture today: the just God of the universe sees every hidden act of love you do for the sake of his name. It is written in the books of heaven.

Finally, verse 12 reminds us that God's promises are received not only through faith, but through faith joined to patience (makrothymia). We live in an instant culture that wants everything at once. When God's promise or answer is slow in coming, we easily become sluggish and discouraged. But true faith has staying power; it holds on to God's character even in the long waiting room between promise and fulfilment.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Rain and harvest: What specific "useful vegetation" (in your character, your family or your service to others) has the spiritual rain you have received over the past few months produced in your life?
  • The work of love: Are you serving other believers in practical ways for the sake of the Lord's name at present (v. 10)? And how does it encourage you to know that God himself sees and remembers every sacrifice you make?
  • Faith with a long breath (makrothymia): In which area of your life have you been waiting a long time on the Lord, and are you tempted to give up? What does it mean today to keep holding on to his promise with patience?

Prayer

Faithful and righteous Father, I thank you for the abundant rain of your grace that falls so often on my life. Thank you that you have enlightened me with the truth of the gospel, that I could taste the sweetness of your good word and may know the power of your Holy Spirit within me.

Lord, keep my heart from ever taking your grace for granted. Let the soil of my life stay soft and receptive, so that every drop of your word yields a rich harvest of obedience and fruit to your glory. Thank you for the deep comfort that you never forget the work and love done in your name. Renew my strength where I have grown weary of serving.

Today I pray for the gift of patience. Where I am still waiting for the fulfilment of your promises and the road feels long, keep me from sluggishness. Give me a persevering faith that holds fast to my Redeemer to the end, with the full assurance of hope. Amen.
