Eyes Fixed on Jesus · Session 10

The New and Living Way

Hebrews 10:1–39

To get started

Think of something you had to repeat again and again – a medicine, a repair, a chore. How do you know when a problem has been fixed for good?

Where we are in the story

Chapter 10 brings the great teaching on Christ as high priest to its closing chord: the yearly sacrifices only reminded people of their sins, but Christ's one offering has done what they never could (10:1–18). Then, at 10:19, the letter turns from what Christ has done to how we are to live: three times “let us”. A fourth serious warning follows (10:26–31), and then warm encouragement that leads into the great faith chapter, Hebrews 11.

A look at the Greek

τετελείωκεν … τοὺς ἁγιαζομένους

teteleiōken … tous hagiazomenous · “he has made perfect … those who are made holy”

Two tenses side by side in 10:14: a perfect tense – our standing before God is already settled – and a present participle – we are being made holy day by day. Already perfect in God's eyes, and still growing.

A look at the Greek

πρόσφατος

prosphatos · “new”

Originally fresh, recently made. The way Jesus inaugurated through the curtain (10:20) always stays fresh: his sacrifice is as powerful before God today as on the day of the cross – and because he is risen, it is a living way.

A look at the Greek

παρρησία

parrēsia · “confidence”

A free, unhindered right of access: we have confidence to enter the sanctuary by the blood of Jesus (10:19). In 10:35 the same word returns: don't throw it away – like a soldier who throws away his shield and flees.

The big idea

By one offering Christ has made perfect for all time those who are made holy; so let us approach God, hold fast to our hope and consider one another – and not throw away our confidence, for the one who is coming will come.

Discussion · 1/7

If a medicine really cures, you stop taking it. “In these sacrifices there is a reminder of sins year after year” – but verse 18 says: “Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin.” Where do we set up little side altars next to the cross – punishing ourselves or doing penance before we accept forgiveness?

Hebrews 10:1–4

Discussion · 2/7

“See, I have come to do your will.” God was looking not for mere rituals but for a body willing to do his will. What would it look like to put your everyday body – your time, your work, your family life – at his disposal this week?

Hebrews 10:5–10

Discussion · 3/7

Every priest stands day after day; this one offered one sacrifice and took his seat. “For by one offering he has made perfect for all time those who are made holy.” How does this verse help when you are discouraged by how slowly you change?

Hebrews 10:11–18

Discussion · 4/7

Verses 19–23 rest on two possessions – the open way and a great priest – and call us to approach and to hold fast, “for the one who made the promise is faithful”. Why does it matter that our holding on rests on his faithfulness rather than on the strength of our grip?

Hebrews 10:19–23

Discussion · 5/7

“And let us consider one another, so as to spur one another on to love and good works.” The same verb is used in 3:1 for considering Jesus. What would it look like for us as a group to consider one another in this way – and who among us needs encouraging right now?

Hebrews 10:24–25

Discussion · 6/7

Verses 26–31 are among the most serious words in the letter: “To fall into the hands of the living God is a fearful thing.” Yet straight after them the author writes: “But remember the earlier days”. Why do warning and encouragement belong together here?

Hebrews 10:26–31

Discussion · 7/7

The readers had once accepted the loss of their possessions with joy, knowing they had a better possession, one that lasts. “So do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward.” Which earlier struggle, through which God carried you, gives you courage for the present one?

Hebrews 10:32–39

For the week ahead

  • Name one side altar in your life – a way you try to pay for your own failures – and each time you notice it this week, read verse 18 and receive forgiveness instead.
  • Consider one person this week – perhaps someone who has been missing from your meetings – and spur them on with a visit, a call or a message of encouragement.

Pray together

Begin by reading verses 22–24 aloud together. Then pray in three movements: approach God with thanks for the open way through Jesus' blood; hold fast – bring to God the places where your hope is under pressure; consider one another – pray by name for one person in the group. Close together with verse 39.
