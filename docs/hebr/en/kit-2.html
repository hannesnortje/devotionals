Small-group kit · Session 2 / 13

Do Not Drift

Present ▶

The big ideaBecause the Son is so great, we must not drift away from a salvation this great: Jesus became one of us, tasted death for everyone by the grace of God, broke the power of the devil, and is now the merciful and faithful high priest who helps us when we are tested.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Have you ever been on a lilo, a boat or a paddleboard and suddenly noticed how far the current had carried you without your noticing? What happened?

Read together · Hebrews 2:1–18

Three readers: verses 1–4 (the warning), verses 5–9 (Psalm 8 and Jesus crowned – pause briefly before “But we do see”), verses 10–18 (our brother and high priest).

Where we are in the story

Chapter 1 showed the Son exalted far above the angels. Now the author turns to the first of five warning passages: if the word spoken through angels at Sinai was binding, how much more the salvation the Lord himself spoke of (vv. 1–4). Then he answers a stumbling block for his Jewish readers – why would the Son become a frail human being and die? – with Psalm 8, showing Jesus as our pioneer, our brother and our high priest (vv. 5–18).

A look at the Greek

  • pararyōmen – παραρυῶμεν “drift away”: A seafaring word for a boat that has not been properly moored and quietly drifts with the current past the safe harbour – or a ring that slips unnoticed off a finger. Its opposite in the same verse, prosechein (pay attention), meant holding a ship on course and mooring it. The danger is not sudden rebellion but slow, unnoticed drifting (2:1).
  • archēgos – ἀρχηγός “pioneer”: A pioneer, trailblazer or champion – like the sailor in a well-known sermon illustration who swims through the breakers to the rocky shore and ties a rescue line, so that everyone on board can cross safely. God made this pioneer “perfect through sufferings”: teleioō means fully qualified for his task, not morally improved (2:10).
  • boētheō – βοηθέω “help”: Made up of boē (a cry for help) and theō (to run): to rush towards a cry for help. Because he himself suffered when he was tested, Jesus “is able to help those who are being tested” (2:18).

Discussion

  1. “How shall we escape if we disregard a salvation this great?” The danger here is not open rebellion but drifting through tiredness and neglect. Which undercurrents in our daily lives pull our attention away from the Lord? (Hebrews 2:1–3)
  2. Verses 3–4 give a threefold foundation for this salvation: the Lord who first spoke of it, those who heard him, and God who added his testimony. Why does it help to know that our faith rests on this testimony and not on our feelings? (Hebrews 2:3–4)
  3. “As it is, we do not yet see everything subjected to him.” Where do you feel this most painfully – in the world, in your family, in yourself? (Hebrews 2:5–8)
  4. “But we do see … Jesus, crowned with glory and honour.” What changes when we shift our gaze from what we do not yet see to the one we do see by faith? (Hebrews 2:8–9)
  5. Jesus was made a little lower than the angels “so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone”. He did not merely dip his fingertips into death; he drained the cup. What does that mean to you? (Hebrews 2:9)
  6. “He is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters.” When do you feel like the black sheep of God's family? How does verse 11 speak into that? (Hebrews 2:10–13)
  7. Verse 15 speaks of people “held in slavery all their lives” through fear of death, and verse 18 promises help to “those who are being tested”. Where does fear – of illness, loss, the future – dictate our decisions? And when you are tempted, do you fight alone first, or call on your high priest straight away? (Hebrews 2:14–18)

For the week ahead

  • Check your anchor points: choose one daily habit you have let slip (stillness before the Lord, reading the word, fellowship with believers) and moor yourself to it again this week.
  • When temptation or fear comes this week, make verse 18 your first response: call to Jesus at once with a short prayer for help, before you try to fight it in your own strength.

Pray together

Read verses 17–18 aloud together. Pray in threes: confess where you have been drifting, thank Jesus that he is not ashamed to call you his brother or sister, and bring him one area where fear or temptation is pressing on you. Close by praying for people you know who are drifting away.

Notes for the leader

  • Three devotionals (days 3–5) and eighteen verses make a full evening. If time is short, use questions 1, 4, 6 and 7.
  • This is the first of five warning passages (2:1–4; 3:7–4:13; 5:11–6:12; 10:19–39; 12:14–29). Its tone is pastoral urgency, not panic. Keep the emphasis of the day 3 devotional: holy watchfulness, and deliberately mooring our hearts to the gospel every day.
  • 2:9 “by the grace of God”: our translation follows the majority reading (chariti theou). Some manuscripts and the SBL Greek text read chōris theou (apart from God). Mention it only if someone's Bible reads differently; don't let it become the topic of the evening.
  • 2:10 does not mean that Jesus was ever sinful or morally imperfect (see 4:15). Teleioō means bringing someone to the full goal, fully qualifying him for a task – here, to be the Redeemer and high priest of suffering people. Expect this question.
  • Fear of death (v. 15) can touch people who are ill, grieving or caring for a dying relative. Handle it gently: the verse is about being set free, not a rebuke for feeling afraid.
  • Point ahead: next week chapter 3 – Jesus greater than Moses, and the call “Today, if you hear his voice”.

Scripture Hebrews 2:1–18

1That is why we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away. 2For if the word spoken through angels proved binding, and every transgression and disobedience received a just penalty, 3how shall we escape if we disregard a salvation this great? It began when the Lord spoke of it, and it was confirmed to us by those who heard, 4while God added his testimony by signs and wonders and various mighty works and by distributions of the Holy Spirit according to his will.

5For it was not to angels that he subjected the world to come, about which we are speaking. 6Rather, someone has testified somewhere:

What is a human being, that you remember him,or a son of man, that you look after him?7You made him a little lower than the angels;with glory and honour you crowned him;8you subjected everything under his feet.For in subjecting everything to him, he left nothing that is not subject to him. As it is, we do not yet see everything subjected to him.

9But we do see the one who was made a little lower than the angels, Jesus, crowned with glory and honour because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone.

10For it was fitting for him, for whose sake everything exists and through whom everything exists, in bringing many sons to glory, to make the pioneer of their salvation perfect through sufferings. 11For the one who makes holy and those who are made holy are all from one. That is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters, 12saying:

I will declare your name to my brothers and sisters;in the midst of the assembly I will sing your praise.

13And again:

I will put my trust in him.And again:See, here am I, and the children God has given me.

14Since, then, the children share in blood and flesh, he too in the same way took part in the same things, so that through death he might break the power of the one who holds sway over death, that is, the devil, 15and set free all those who through fear of death were held in slavery all their lives. 16For surely he does not take hold of angels; it is the offspring of Abraham he takes hold of. 17Therefore he had to be made like the brothers and sisters in every respect, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest before God, to make atonement for the sins of the people. 18For because he himself suffered when he was tested, he is able to help those who are being tested.
