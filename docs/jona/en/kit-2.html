Small-group kit · Session 2 / 4

From the Depths

Present ▶

The big ideaAt the lowest point of his flight, Jonah discovers that God was in charge all along – and that salvation belongs to the LORD alone.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & Hebrew
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of a time when you were completely stuck – a flat tyre, a lost key, a cancelled flight. What went through your mind?

Read together · Jonah 2:1–11

Let one person read 2:1–2 and 2:11 (the narrator), and another read the psalm (2:3–10) slowly, as a prayer.

Where we are in the story

Jonah has been thrown into the sea, and the storm has stopped. But the story is not over: the LORD appoints a great fish. From inside it, in total darkness, Jonah prays. Note: this chapter follows the Hebrew verse numbers – English Bibles number 2:1 as 1:17 and 2:2–11 as 2:1–10.

A look at the Hebrew

  • manah – מָנָה “appoint”: God appoints the great fish (2:1) – and later the plant, the worm and the wind (4:6–8). The fish is not an accident or a punishment but God's chosen means of rescue.
  • chesed – חֶסֶד “faithful love”: God's loyal covenant love that never gives up. “Those who cling to worthless idols abandon their faithful love” (2:9): whatever we cling to instead of God robs us of it.
  • yeshu'ah – יְשׁוּעָה “salvation”: “Salvation belongs to the LORD!” (2:10). The name Yeshua – Jesus – comes from the same root.

Discussion

  1. How do you read the great fish: as punishment or as rescue? What does the word “appointed” suggest? (Jonah 2:1)
  2. Jonah says to God, “You had thrown me into the deep” – although the sailors threw him. What does it mean to see God's hand behind our crisis? (Jonah 2:4)
  3. “I am driven away from before your eyes. Yet I will look again towards your holy temple.” What is the “yet” of faith, and where have you needed it? (Jonah 2:5)
  4. “To the roots of the mountains I went down … But you brought my life up from the pit.” How does this verse complete the “going down” we saw in chapter 1? (Jonah 2:7)
  5. Jonah says, “I remembered the LORD” when his soul was fainting. What helps you to remember God when you are exhausted? (Jonah 2:8)
  6. Jonah praises God before he is out of the fish. Why is praise in the dark so powerful – and so hard? (Jonah 2:10)
  7. Jesus calls his death and resurrection “the sign of Jonah” (Matthew 12:40). Where do you see the gospel in this chapter?

For the week ahead

  • Learn one verse of Jonah's psalm by heart this week, so that you carry God's word with you into “dark” days.
  • Name one “worthless idol” you cling to for security, and each day say aloud instead: “Salvation belongs to the LORD!”

Pray together

Read 2:3 together aloud. Then let each person who wishes name one situation where they need a “yet” of faith, and pray for each other briefly. Close by saying together: “Salvation belongs to the LORD!”

Notes for the leader

  • Some will want to discuss whether the fish story is literal. Acknowledge the question briefly, then steer towards what the text emphasises: God's sovereignty and Jonah's prayer.
  • Explain the verse numbering at the start, so that people using English Bibles are not confused.
  • Jonah's psalm echoes many Psalms. If your group knows them, invite them to spot the echoes (e.g. Psalm 42:7 and 31:6 in English Bibles).
  • Some members may be in a real “belly of the fish” – illness, loss, unemployment. Be gentle; don't suggest that every crisis is a punishment.
  • Point ahead: Jonah promised to keep his vows (2:10). Next week we see whether he keeps them.

Scripture Jonah 2:1–11

1But the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. (English Bibles: 1:17) 2Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish 3and said:

I called out of my distress to the LORD,and he answered me;from the belly of the grave I cried for help –you heard my voice.4You had thrown me into the deep,into the heart of the seas,and the current surrounded me;all your breakers and your wavespassed over me.5And I – I said, "I am driven awayfrom before your eyes.Yet I will look againtowards your holy temple."6Waters closed around me up to my throat,the deep surrounded me,seaweed was wrapped around my head.7To the roots of the mountains I went down;the earth – its bars closed behind me for ever.But you brought my life up from the pit,LORD my God.8When my soul was fainting within me,I remembered the LORD,and my prayer came to you,into your holy temple.9Those who cling to worthless idolsabandon their faithful love.10But I, with a voice of thanksgiving,will sacrifice to you;what I have vowed I will pay.Salvation belongs to the LORD!

11Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto the dry land.
