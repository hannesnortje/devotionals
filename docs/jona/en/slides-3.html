Journey through Jonah · Session 3

Overturned

Jonah 3:1–10

To get started

Have you ever been given a second chance – a second attempt at an exam, a job or a friendship? What did it feel like?

Where we are in the story

Jonah has just been vomited out onto the dry land. Chapter 3 opens almost word for word like chapter 1: the word of the LORD comes to Jonah a second time, with the same assignment. This time Jonah gets up and goes – and a sermon of just five Hebrew words sets off the greatest turning of a city in the Bible.

Read together · Jonah 3:1–4

1The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time: 2"Get up, go to Nineveh, the great city, and cry out to it the message that I am telling you." 3So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the LORD had said. Now Nineveh was a great city – great even before God – a three days' journey across. 4Jonah began to go into the city, one day's journey, and he cried out and said, "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned!"

Read together · Jonah 3:5–7

5And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least. 6When the word reached the king of Nineveh, he got up from his throne, took off his royal robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in the ashes. 7And he had it cried out in Nineveh and said: "By decree of the king and his great men: let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock taste anything; let them not graze, and let them not drink water.

Read together · Jonah 3:8–10

8Let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out to God with all their strength. Let each one turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands. 9Who knows? God may turn and relent; he may turn from his burning anger, so that we do not perish." 10And God saw what they did, that they turned from their evil way. And God relented of the evil that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it.

A look at the Hebrew

שֵׁנִית

shenit · “a second time”

One small word holds an ocean of grace. God could have chosen another prophet, but his word comes to Jonah again: his calling rests on his faithfulness, not on our failure.

A look at the Hebrew

נֶהְפָּכֶת

nehpakhet · “overturned”

From hafakh, the word used for the destruction of Sodom (Genesis 19:25) – but it can also mean "turned around", "completely changed". Nineveh was indeed overturned within forty days: not by fire, but by repentance.

A look at the Hebrew

שׁוּב

shuv · “turn”

The hinge of chapter 3. The people are to turn from their evil way (v. 8); the king hopes God may turn from his burning anger (v. 9); and when they turn, God relents of the evil he had said he would do (v. 10).

The big idea

God speaks to his runaway prophet a second time, and a violent city believes God and turns – God's grace overturns hearts, not only cities.

Discussion · 1/7

Put 1:1–3 and 3:1–3 side by side. What is the same, and what has changed? Notice the shift from "cry out against it" (1:2) to "cry out to it the message that I am telling you" (3:2).

Jonah 3:1

Discussion · 2/7

Jonah's whole sermon is: "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned!" What is missing from it? Why do you think it worked anyway?

Jonah 3:4

Discussion · 3/7

"Overturned" can mean destroyed or turned around. In what sense was Nineveh "overturned" after all?

Jonah 3:10

Discussion · 4/7

The turning starts among ordinary people and then reaches the throne. Look at the four things the king does in verse 6. What does each one say?

Jonah 3:6

Discussion · 5/7

The king says, "Who knows? God may turn and relent." Compare the captain in 1:6: "Perhaps that god will give a thought to us." What do these two pagans understand about approaching God that Jonah seems to have forgotten?

Jonah 3:9

Discussion · 6/7

"And God saw what they did, that they turned from their evil way." What does real turning look like in verses 8 and 10? What might "the violence that is in his hands" be for us today?

Jonah 3:8

Discussion · 7/7

Nineveh was "a great city – great even before God". Whom have we written off as beyond God's reach? What would change if we saw them as great before God?

Jonah 3:3

For the week ahead

  • Name one "point of disobedience" you need to go back to – something God asked of you that you left undone – and take the first "one day's journey" step this week.
  • Choose one person or group you have written off. Pray for them by name every day this week.

Pray together

Thank God together that his word comes "a second time". Then hold a minute of silence in which each person brings before God what is in their hands that they need to let go of. Close by praying aloud, briefly and by name, for people and places that seem beyond reach.
