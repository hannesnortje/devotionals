Journey through Jonah · Session 1

On the Run

Jonah 1:1–16

To get started

As a child, what did you do to get out of a chore you didn't want to do – and did it work?

Where we are in the story

Jonah was a well-known prophet in Israel who had announced good news for his own nation (2 Kings 14:25). Now God sends him to Nineveh, a capital of the brutal Assyrian Empire – Israel's most feared enemy. Tonight we follow Jonah from the call, onto a ship heading the opposite way, and into a storm.

Read together · Jonah 1:1–3

1The word of the LORD came to Jonah son of Amittai: 2"Get up, go to Nineveh, the great city, and cry out against it, for their evil has come up before my face." 3So Jonah got up – to flee to Tarshish, away from the face of the LORD. He went down to Joppa and found a ship bound for Tarshish. He paid the fare and went down into it, to go with them to Tarshish, away from the face of the LORD.

Read together · Jonah 1:4–5

4But the LORD hurled a great wind onto the sea, and there was a great storm on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break apart. 5The sailors were afraid, and each cried out to his own god. They hurled the cargo that was in the ship into the sea to lighten it for themselves. But Jonah had gone down into the innermost part of the ship, had lain down, and had fallen into a deep sleep.

Read together · Jonah 1:6–7

6The captain came to him and said to him, "What are you doing asleep? Get up, call out to your god! Perhaps that god will give a thought to us, and we will not perish." 7Then they said to one another, "Come, let us cast lots, so that we may know on whose account this evil has come upon us." So they cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

Read together · Jonah 1:8–10

8Then they said to him, "Tell us, please, on whose account this evil has come upon us! What is your work, and where do you come from? What is your country, and from what people are you?" 9He said to them, "A Hebrew am I, and the LORD, the God of heaven, I fear – he who made the sea and the dry land." 10Then the men feared with a great fear and said to him, "What is this you have done!" For the men knew that he was fleeing from the face of the LORD, because he had told them.

Read together · Jonah 1:11–13

11And they said to him, "What shall we do to you, so that the sea will calm down for us?" For the sea was growing more and more stormy. 12He said to them, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea, and the sea will calm down for you. For I know that it is on my account that this great storm has come upon you." 13The men rowed hard to get back to dry land, but they could not, for the sea was growing more and more stormy against them.

Read together · Jonah 1:14–16

14Then they called out to the LORD and said, "Please, LORD, do not let us perish for this man's life, and do not lay innocent blood on us! For you, LORD, have done as you pleased." 15Then they picked Jonah up and hurled him into the sea, and the sea stopped its raging. 16Then the men feared the LORD with a great fear; they offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows.

A look at the Hebrew

קוּם

qum · “get up”

God says “Get up, go” (1:2). Jonah does get up – but to flee (1:3). And the pagan captain later shakes him with the very same word: “Get up, call out to your god!” (1:6).

A look at the Hebrew

יָרַד

yarad · “go down”

Jonah goes down to Joppa, down into the ship, down into its innermost part (1:3, 5). Every step away from God's call is a step downward, while Nineveh's evil “has come up” before God (1:2).

A look at the Hebrew

טוּל

tul · “hurl”

The LORD hurls a great wind (1:4), the sailors hurl the cargo (1:5) and finally hurl Jonah (1:15). The same verb shows who is really in control of the storm.

The big idea

God's call does not let go of us when we run – he sends a storm of grace that brings us, and those around us, back to him.

Discussion · 1/7

What exactly does God ask Jonah to do, and what does Jonah do instead? Look closely at the words in verses 2 and 3.

Jonah 1:2–3

Discussion · 2/7

Everything seems to go smoothly for Jonah: a ship is ready and he can pay the fare. Why is an “open door” not always proof of God's will?

Jonah 1:3

Discussion · 3/7

While the sailors pray and work, Jonah sleeps. What does this picture tell us about spiritual numbness?

Jonah 1:5–6

Discussion · 4/7

Jonah says, “A Hebrew am I, and the LORD, the God of heaven, I fear.” In what way is his confession right, and in what way does his life contradict it?

Jonah 1:9

Discussion · 5/7

The men “rowed hard” to reach land (literally, they “dug”). Where do we try to solve with our own strength something only obedience can solve?

Jonah 1:13

Discussion · 6/7

The storm starts with men who fear the sea and ends with men who “feared the LORD with a great fear”. What has God done through Jonah's disobedience?

Jonah 1:16

Discussion · 7/7

What is your “Nineveh” – and your “Tarshish” – at the moment? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

For the week ahead

  • Name one specific “Nineveh” (a conversation, an act of forgiveness, a task) and take one small step towards it this week.
  • Notice one “Tarshish” you use to escape God's voice (busyness, screens, overwork) and set aside ten quiet minutes each day instead.

Pray together

Pray in pairs or threes: thank God that he pursues us even when we run, and ask him for the courage to “get up” in the one area each person named. Close by praying together for people who are suffering because of our own “storms”.
