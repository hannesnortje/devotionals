The Siege of Nineveh: When Human Power Collapses

Scripture Nahum 2:2–8

2One who scatters has come up against you.Guard the rampart!Watch the road!Brace your loins!Gather all your strength! (English Bibles: 2:1; from here on subtract one from the verse number)3For the LORD restores the majesty of Jacoblike the majesty of Israel;for plunderers have plundered themand ruined their vine branches.4The shield of his warriors is reddened,the men of valour are clothed in scarlet;the chariots flash with the fire of steelon the day he makes ready,and the cypress spears are brandished.5In the streets the chariots rage,they rush to and fro in the squares;their appearance is like torches,like lightning they dart about.6He remembers his nobles;they stumble as they go;they hurry to her wall,and the protective cover is set up.7The gates of the rivers are opened,and the palace melts away.8And it is fixed: she is stripped, she is carried away,and her slave women moanlike the sound of doves,beating on their breasts.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

(A note on verse numbers: today's passage is 2:2–8 in the Hebrew text, which we follow; English Bibles number it 2:1–7. Subtract one from our verse number to find it in an English Bible.)

In chapter 1 Nahum announced that God would judge Nineveh. Now, in chapter 2, he shows how it will happen. This passage reads like a vivid eyewitness report of an ancient battle – but the remarkable thing is that Nahum probably wrote these words decades before Nineveh actually fell! In 612 BC a coalition of Babylonians (under Nabopolassar) and Medes (under Cyaxares) besieged invincible Nineveh and destroyed it – and it happened as Nahum announces here.

Nineveh was an architectural and military marvel of the ancient world. Its inner city wall was some 12 kilometres long and very high – ancient writers describe it as so wide that three chariots could drive side by side on top of it. In places it was surrounded by a broad moat fed by the Tigris and the Khosr rivers. To any human observer, Nineveh was utterly untouchable.

Insights from the Hebrew

  • One who scatters (mefits – מֵפִיץ, v. 2, English 2:1): "One who scatters has come up against you." The Hebrew text reads mefits, "one who scatters"; some interpreters, with a small change, read "a shatterer" (a war club, cf. Jeremiah 51:20).
  • Majesty (ge'on – גְּאוֹן, v. 3, English 2:2): "For the LORD restores the majesty of Jacob" – ge'on means majesty, honour or glory.
  • Scarlet and red (magen … me'oddam – מָגֵן … מְאָדָּם; metulla'im – מְתֻלָּעִים, v. 4, English 2:3): The shield (magen) is reddened, and the soldiers are metulla'im, "clothed in scarlet" (from tola'at, the worm from which the scarlet dye was made).
  • The fire of steel (peladot – פְּלָדוֹת, v. 4): A word found only here – flashing iron or fire-steel, the gleaming steel fittings of the chariots.
  • Nobles and protective cover (addirav – אַדִּירָיו; hassokhekh – הַסֹּכֵךְ, v. 6, English 2:5): The addirim are the nobles and elite officers. The sokhekh is a covering: a mobile armoured roof or shield wall used in sieges.
  • Melts away (namog – נָמוֹג, from mug, v. 7, English 2:6): To dissolve or collapse – the same verb used of the hills that "melt away" before the LORD in 1:5.
  • It is fixed (wehutsav – וְהֻצַּב, v. 8, English 2:7): Literally "it has been set firm": Nineveh's fate is decided.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Divine irony against human pride (v. 2, English 2:1): Nahum begins with biting, holy sarcasm aimed at Assyria's commanders: "Guard the rampart! Watch the road! Brace your loins! Gather all your strength!" The prophet dares Nineveh to put up its best defence, because "one who scatters" is on his way. Although the Babylonian and Median armies were the earthly instruments, the wider context of the book shows that God himself is the Commander-in-Chief behind this attack. Human preparation, however impressive, is useless when you are fighting against God's purpose.
  2. The spiritual reason for the judgement: restoring the vine (v. 3, English 2:2): In the middle of the battle scene, verse 3 gives the deeper theological reason why Nineveh must fall: "For the LORD restores the majesty of Jacob like the majesty of Israel". In the Bible Israel and Judah are often compared to a vine (Psalm 80:9–17, English 80:8–16; Isaiah 5:1–7). The Assyrian plunderers had trampled God's vineyard and "ruined their vine branches". God's judgement on evil is never separate from his plan of salvation for his children. He breaks the oppressor's power so that his own people's spiritual heritage and dignity can be restored.
  3. The colours and speed of the attack (vv. 4–5, English 2:3–4): With short, staccato phrases in the original Hebrew, Nahum creates the sound and motion of a charge: - Scarlet and red (v. 4): The soldiers – probably the attackers, though the Hebrew could also mean Nineveh's own warriors – wear scarlet clothing and red shields. Some interpreters think the Medes and Babylonians wore red to strike fear into their enemies, or to hide bloodstains during battle so that the enemy could not take heart when someone was wounded. - Flashing chariots (v. 5): The chariots with their gleaming steel fittings race through the streets and across the city's wide squares. What was once Nineveh's pride – its own swift chariots – is now turned against it, like torches and lightning darting to and fro in the night.
  4. Panic and the opening of the river gates (vv. 6–7, English 2:5–6): In verse 6 the Assyrian king (if he is the one meant – the Hebrew could also refer to the attacker) desperately tries to summon his nobles and elite officers, but in their panic "they stumble as they go" on the way to the city walls, and "the protective cover is set up". Then comes the most remarkable prophecy, in verse 7: "The gates of the rivers are opened, and the palace melts away." - The Babylonian chronicle of Nineveh's fall tells only of a three-month siege and the capture of the city. The later Greek historian Diodorus Siculus (drawing on older Greek sources), however, tells how heavy rains made the river burst its banks and how the floodwater brought down part of the city wall. Whether the sluices and water gates meant to protect the city gave way under the pressure or were manipulated by the attackers, we cannot know for sure. - If it happened that way, the floodwater literally made parts of Nineveh's mud-brick walls and foundations "melt away", so that the invading armies could storm into the city. However exactly it happened: what had been Nineveh's greatest natural protection (the rivers) became, in God's hand, the instrument of its downfall.
  5. From queen to slave (v. 8, English 2:7): "And it is fixed: she is stripped, she is carried away". The mighty city, which saw herself as an invincible queen and goddess (probably also a reference to the idol of Ishtar, Nineveh's patron goddess), is stripped and carried off into exile. Her slave women moan "like the sound of doves, beating on their breasts" – the soft, hopeless cooing of mourning doves. The roles are completely reversed: the city that carried nations away without mercy is now carried away herself.

Living It Out

At first glance Nahum 2:2–8 (English 2:1–7) looks like ancient war history, but the spiritual principles locked up in it speak powerfully to our inner life and our walk of faith today.

  • The fragility of false fortresses: Nineveh put its trust in enormous walls and a clever system of river gates. Today we also build our own "walls of Nineveh" to make ourselves feel safe and in control – our bank accounts, our careers, our intellect, our physical strength or our reputation. But verse 7 (English 6) shows how quickly earthly security can "melt away" when the floodwaters of life rise. True security is never found in the walls we build, but only in the Refuge (Nahum 1:7) who is God himself.
  • God's promise of restoration for the ruined vine: Look again at the beautiful promise in verse 3 (English 2): "For the LORD restores the majesty of Jacob like the majesty of Israel; for plunderers have plundered them and ruined their vine branches." Perhaps today you feel like that ruined vine. Perhaps hard seasons, hurts, your own mistakes or the attacks of life have plundered your joy, your gifts or your spiritual strength. God sees the broken branches. His plan is not only to remove whatever is ruining you, but to restore your spiritual dignity, your fruitfulness and your calling in Christ.
  • No spiritual stronghold is untouchable for God: Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 10:4 that the weapons of our warfare are powerful for God to tear down "strongholds". Sometimes there are spiritual strongholds – deep-rooted thought patterns, sins passed down through generations, bitterness or powerful temptations – that seem to us as high and invincible as Nineveh's walls. Nahum 2 reminds us that the "one who scatters" (v. 2, English 2:1) is mightier than any stronghold. When God opens the floodgates of his Spirit, the enemy's highest walls melt away like mud brick in a flood.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Which human "walls" or forms of false security do you tend to lean on when you feel uncertain or threatened? What does Nineveh's "melting palace" (v. 7, English 6) teach you about where your true anchor must lie?
  • Read verse 3 (English 2) again. In which area of your life do you feel that your "vine branches" have been plundered or ruined? How does it give you hope today to know that God is a God who restores the honour and fruitfulness of his children?
  • Is there a stubborn "stronghold" (in your own thought life, your family, or a situation around you) that seems humanly impossible to break through? How does this chapter encourage you to pray about it with new faith?

Prayer

Almighty God and heavenly Father, before you the highest walls of human pride and the strongest fortresses of this world are like clay melting in water. I thank you for your Word, which shows that you are the Lord of history – what you speak, you bring to pass, and no power can hold back your hand.

Lord, forgive me for the times I build my own "walls" of self-sufficiency, earthly security or human control and think they will protect me. Help me to lay down every false prop and to put my whole trust in you alone, the eternal Rock.

Today I hold on especially to your precious promise in Nahum 2:3 (English 2:2). You see every place in my life and in my heart where the plunderers of this life – disappointment, sin, fear and exhaustion – have damaged my vine branches. I ask you, Lord of the harvest: restore what is broken! Let the floodgates of your Holy Spirit open over my life. Let every spiritual stronghold of fear, doubt or bondage melt away before your face, and let my life blossom again and bear fruit to the glory of your name. I pray this in the mighty and restoring name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
