A Refuge in the Day of Distress · Session 2

The Lions' Den Plundered

Nahum 2:1–14

To get started

What is the best piece of news you have ever received – and how did you hear it (a call, a letter, someone at the door)?

Where we are in the story

Last week ended with God breaking the yoke from Judah's neck and pronouncing the grave of the Assyrian king (1:12–14). Chapter 2 opens with a messenger running over the mountains with news of peace (2:1; English 1:15), and then shows how Nineveh will fall: the attackers storm in, the river gates are opened, and the city's wealth drains away (2:2–11; English 2:1–10). Nahum probably wrote this decades before 612 BC, when Babylonians and Medes destroyed the city. The chapter ends with a taunt over the lions' den – the lion was the Assyrian kings' own royal symbol (2:12–14; English 2:11–13). (Our verse numbers are Hebrew: in English Bibles subtract one throughout this chapter, and 2:1 is 1:15.)

A look at the Hebrew

מְבַשֵּׂר

mevasser · “one who brings good news”

“Look, on the mountains the feet of one who brings good news, who announces peace!” (2:1; English 1:15). The Greek Old Testament renders this verb with euangelizomai, the root of the New Testament word “gospel”. The verse is almost the same as Isaiah 52:7, which Paul quotes in Romans 10:15 for the preaching of Christ, and the news is shalom: peace, wholeness, safety.

A look at the Hebrew

נָמוֹג

namog · “melts away”

“The gates of the rivers are opened, and the palace melts away.” (2:7; English 2:6). It is the same verb as in 1:5, where the hills melt away before the LORD. The water that was Nineveh's greatest protection becomes, in God's hand, the instrument of its downfall.

A look at the Hebrew

בּוּקָה וּמְבוּקָה וּמְבֻלָּקָה

buqah umevuqah umevullaqah · “Empty! Emptied! Laid waste!”

One of the most striking sound plays in the Bible (2:11; English 2:10): the syllables build from two to three to four, like water glugging out of a bottle or walls rumbling down. Everything Nineveh had hoarded from the nations ends in emptiness.

The big idea

The good news of peace for God's people means the end of the oppressor: the walls, rivers and treasures that Nineveh trusted melt away, the lions' den falls silent, and the LORD of hosts himself says to the proud, “Look, I am against you”.

Discussion · 1/7

The watchmen on Jerusalem's walls waited to see how a runner came over the hills. For Judah the news meant: “Keep your festivals, Judah, pay your vows!” What good news do you need to hear today – and how does the news of Christ's victory call you back to worship before every problem is solved?

Nahum 2:1

Discussion · 2/7

“Guard the rampart! Watch the road!” – the prophet mockingly dares Nineveh to put up its best defence. Where do you see people (or yourself) preparing feverishly against what God has decided?

Nahum 2:2

Discussion · 3/7

In the middle of the battle comes the reason: “For the LORD restores the majesty of Jacob like the majesty of Israel; for plunderers have plundered them and ruined their vine branches.” Where do you feel that your “vine branches” have been plundered? What hope does it give that God's judgement on evil serves his plan to restore?

Nahum 2:3

Discussion · 4/7

Nineveh trusted its huge walls and river system, yet “the palace melts away”. What are the “walls of Nineveh” we build for ourselves – money, career, intellect, reputation? What happens to them when the floodwaters of life rise?

Nahum 2:6–7

Discussion · 5/7

“Nineveh is like a pool of water” – wealth and people dammed up for decades, now draining away: “Empty! Emptied! Laid waste!” Compare Jesus' parable of the rich fool (Luke 12:15–21). Where are we tempted to build our own “lions' den” of possessions and status?

Nahum 2:9–11

Discussion · 6/7

“Where now is the lions' den … and no one made them afraid?” Assyria saw itself as the predator no one could stop. Then God speaks in the first person: “Look, I am against you – declares the LORD of hosts –”. How do James 4:6 and Romans 8:31 (“If God is for us, who is against us?”) help us hear this verse rightly?

Nahum 2:12–14

Discussion · 7/7

The chapter ends: “and the voice of your messengers will be heard no more” – the envoys who mocked the living God outside Jerusalem (2 Kings 18). Which intimidating voices (fear, accusation, old shame) are loudest in your ears? How can you trust the LORD of hosts to silence them?

Nahum 2:14

For the week ahead

  • Name one “wall of Nineveh” you lean on instead of God. Each day this week, tell him honestly what you would be afraid of if it fell away, and ask him to be your refuge instead (1:7).
  • Be a “messenger of good news” this week: bring a word of peace, hope or encouragement to one person who is under pressure – in person, by message or by a call.

Pray together

Begin by thanking God for the good news of peace in Christ (2:1). Then let each person name, aloud or silently, one intimidating voice they want God to silence, and pray for one another. End by praying that God would restore what has been plundered in the lives of people in the group.
