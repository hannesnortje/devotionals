Small-group kit · Session 1 / 3

A Refuge in the Day of Distress

Present ▶

The big ideaThe LORD who is slow to anger is also the Judge who will not let evil go on for ever – and that same God is good, a refuge for everyone who shelters in him and the One who breaks the yoke of the oppressed.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of a time you were caught out by a sudden storm, a power cut or a breakdown on the road. Where did you go to find shelter – and what made it feel safe?

Read together · Nahum 1:1–14

Have one reader take the hymn of 1:2–8 slowly and with weight; then two readers share 1:9–14 – one reads the words to Nineveh and the Assyrian king (vv. 9–11, 14), the other the comfort to Judah (vv. 12–13).

Where we are in the story

Nahum prophesied in the seventh century BC (roughly between 663 and 612 BC), when Assyria was the undisputed world power and Nineveh its seemingly invincible capital. About a century earlier Jonah had preached there, the city repented, and God spared it; but Nineveh slid back into cruelty, idolatry and bloodshed, carried northern Israel into exile in 722 BC and kept squeezing little Judah. Nahum's name means “comfort”: tonight we hear the LORD's majesty in a poem (1:2–8) and then his words turning in turn to Nineveh and to Judah (1:9–14).

A look at the Hebrew

  • El qanno' – אֵל קַנּוֹא “A jealous God”: The book opens with “A jealous God, one who takes vengeance, is the LORD” (1:2). This is not petty human envy but the fiery, protective love of the covenant God who will not stand by while his people are destroyed. Three times the verse says the LORD “takes vengeance” (noqem): not a loss of control, but the Judge of all the earth putting things right.
  • ma'oz – מָעוֹז “refuge”: The anchor verse of the book, which gives this series its title: “The LORD is good, a refuge in the day of distress; he knows those who shelter in him.” (1:7). A ma'oz is a mountain stronghold, an impregnable fortress, and “knows” speaks of intimate, personal care. The same word “distress” (tsarah) returns in 1:9: the distress in which God is a refuge is the distress he brings to an end.
  • qallota – קַלּוֹתָ “you weigh nothing”: God's sentence on the Assyrian king ends: “I will make your grave, for you weigh nothing.” (1:14). Qalal means to be light, weightless, of no account – much like Belshazzar in Daniel 5:27, weighed on the scales and found too light. The world thought Assyria a heavyweight; on God's scales its pride weighs nothing.

Discussion

  1. Nahum begins not with Nineveh but with a portrait of God: jealous, taking vengeance, “slow to anger and great in power”. Which of these words do you find hardest, and which do you find most comforting? Why? (Nahum 1:2–3)
  2. “The LORD is slow to anger” – the very words Jonah used of God (Jonah 4:2) when Nineveh was spared. God had given the city about a century. How do we tell God's patience apart from his absence when the wicked seem to prosper? (Nahum 1:3)
  3. Assyria boasted of horses, chariots and walls, but for the LORD “clouds are the dust of his feet”, and Bashan, Carmel and Lebanon wither at his word. How does this picture of God's power change the size of the problems that loom largest for you? (Nahum 1:3–6)
  4. Verse 7 does not promise that there will be no “day of distress” – it assumes such days will come. Where do you usually run first when the storm breaks: control, distraction, other people, or the Lord? What would sheltering in him look like this week? (Nahum 1:7–8)
  5. “What are you devising against the LORD?” The Assyrians thought they were simply fighting a small state called Judah. What does it mean that to touch God's people is to plot against God himself? (Nahum 1:9–11)
  6. To Judah God says: “I have afflicted you; I will afflict you no more.” Assyria had been the rod of his discipline (Isaiah 10:5), but the season was over. What “yoke” or “bonds” do you long for God to break – and how does it help to know that every hard season has an expiry date? (Nahum 1:12–13)
  7. The problems or people that intimidate us can look like “tangled thorns”, yet on God's scales they weigh nothing. What in your life are you giving more weight than God's promises? Share only what you are comfortable sharing. (Nahum 1:10–14)

For the week ahead

  • Write out 1:7 and put it where you will see it each day. When a moment of distress comes this week, read it aloud and name the specific thing you are bringing to your refuge.
  • Is there someone who has wronged you and whom you would like to pay back? Following Romans 12:19, give the case to God in prayer each day this week and ask him to free you from bitterness.

Pray together

Begin by thanking God together for who he is in 1:2–7: holy, patient, mighty and good. Then pray in pairs or threes for the “days of distress” each person is facing, and for the yokes people long to see broken, asking the LORD to be their refuge. Close by praying for those in the world today who live under oppression.

Notes for the leader

  • This first session opens the series. Take two minutes to introduce Nahum: his name means “comfort”, the series title comes from 1:7, and the whole book is a message of God's judgement on a cruel oppressor that brings comfort to the oppressed.
  • Some people will stumble over 1:2 (“jealous”, “vengeance” three times). Don't rush past it, and don't explain it away: follow day 1 – God's jealousy is protective covenant love, and his vengeance is not a fit of rage but the Judge restoring justice. If God did not deal with evil, he would not be good. Keep the balance of verse 3: slow to anger, yet he will surely not acquit the guilty.
  • Make the link to Jonah: the same Nineveh God spared after Jonah's preaching, with the same words “slow to anger” (Jonah 4:2). God's patience had a purpose and also a limit. If the group has done the Jonah series, ask what they remember.
  • The addressee changes without warning in 1:9–14 (Nineveh, then Judah, then the Assyrian king). Point this out before reading, or the passage is confusing; the read-aloud roles help.
  • The question about yokes and bonds can touch real pain – addiction, abuse, long illness, financial pressure. Don't push anyone to share, and offer to talk afterwards. Be careful not to promise that every hardship will end this week; the promise is that God sees and sets limits.
  • Day 2 runs on to 2:1 (English 1:15), the feet of one who brings good news; leave that verse for next week, where it opens the session.

Scripture Nahum 1:1–14

1An oracle concerning Nineveh. The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.

2A jealous God, one who takes vengeance, is the LORD;the LORD takes vengeance and is full of wrath.The LORD takes vengeance on his adversariesand holds it against his enemies.3The LORD is slow to anger and great in power,and the LORD will surely not acquit the guilty.In whirlwind and in storm is his way,and clouds are the dust of his feet.4He rebukes the sea and makes it dry,and all the rivers he dries up.Bashan and Carmel wither,and the blossom of Lebanon withers.5The mountains shake because of him,and the hills melt away;the earth rises up before his face,the world and all who live in it.6Before his fury who can stand?Who can rise up against his burning anger?His wrath pours out like fire,and the rocks are torn down by him.7The LORD is good,a refuge in the day of distress;he knows those who shelter in him.8But with a flood that passes overhe will make a full end of her place,and his enemies he will pursue into darkness.9What are you devising against the LORD?He is making a full end;distress will not rise up a second time.10For even if they are like tangled thornsand drunk as with their drink,they are devoured like dry stubble, completely.11From you has come outone who devises evil against the LORD,one who counsels what is worthless.12Thus says the LORD:Though they are at full strength and ever so many,even so they will be shorn and pass away.I have afflicted you;I will afflict you no more.13And now I will break his yoke from off youand tear your bonds apart.14But concerning you the LORD has commanded:Nothing more will be sown that bears your name.From the house of your gods I will cut offcarved image and cast image;I will make your grave,for you weigh nothing.
