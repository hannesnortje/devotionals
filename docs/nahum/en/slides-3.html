A Refuge in the Day of Distress · Session 3

Woe to the City of Bloodshed

Nahum 3:1–19

To get started

Think of a product, a place or a promise that looked wonderful in the advert but turned out very different in real life. What gave it away?

Where we are in the story

Chapter 2 showed how Nineveh would fall; chapter 3 shows why. It opens with the cry “Woe!” – both an indictment and a funeral lament – and exposes a city built on blood, deceit and occult seduction (3:1–7). Then Nahum points to No-Amon (Thebes in Egypt), which Assyria itself conquered in 663 BC: if that city with water all around her fell, so will Nineveh (3:8–13). The book ends with mocking calls to strengthen the walls, the image of locusts that fly away, and a lament over the king of Assyria – closing, like Jonah, with a question (3:14–19).

A look at the Hebrew

עִיר דָּמִים

'ir damim · “city of bloodshed”

Literally “city of bloods” – plural, bloody deeds. Verse 1 sums up Nineveh in four charges: “Woe to the city of bloodshed! All of it is lies, full of plunder; the prey never departs.” Its splendour had been bought with human blood, its treaties were deceit (kachash), and like a predator it was never satisfied.

A look at the Hebrew

מְנַחֲמִים

menachamim · “comforters”

“Where shall I seek comforters for you?” (3:7). The word comes from the same root as the prophet's name, Nahum (Nachum), “comfort”. Nahum brings rich comfort to oppressed Judah, but for cruel, unrepentant Nineveh no comforter can be found.

A look at the Hebrew

מָעוֹז

ma'oz · “refuge”

“You too will seek a refuge from the enemy.” (3:11). It is the same word as in 1:7: “The LORD is good, a refuge in the day of distress”. Nineveh looks for a refuge and finds none; the LORD alone is the refuge that does not fall.

The big idea

God sees through the charm of violence, lies and seduction and brings it to an end: Nineveh finds no comforter and no refuge, its allies fly away like locusts – but the LORD remains good, the refuge that never falls, and the Good Shepherd who never slumbers.

Discussion · 1/7

Read the four charges of verse 1. Which of them – violence, lies, plunder, never being satisfied – do you see most clearly in the systems of our world today? Where are we ourselves tempted to bend the truth or get ahead at others' expense?

Nahum 3:1

Discussion · 2/7

Nineveh is pictured as a prostitute, “lovely and charming, mistress of sorceries”, “who sells nations through her harlotries”. Day 5 says sin never knocks at the door in its true form. Which temptations look “lovely and charming” to you, and where do they lead in the end?

Nahum 3:4

Discussion · 3/7

“Who will grieve for her?” Nineveh had used everyone and had no one to comfort her when she fell. How do you treat people when you are in a position of strength? Who could you comfort this week, as the Holy Spirit, the true Comforter, comforts you?

Nahum 3:7

Discussion · 4/7

“Are you better than No-Amon”? Nineveh's own history proved that rivers and armies do not make a city untouchable. What “past examples” do we ignore because we think it could never happen to us?

Nahum 3:8–11

Discussion · 5/7

“Go into the clay and tread the mortar! Take hold of the brick mould!” Nineveh feverishly tried to repair its walls in its own strength instead of humbling itself before God. Where are you “baking bricks” to control a situation you really need to surrender to the Lord?

Nahum 3:14

Discussion · 6/7

Nineveh's merchants, guards and officials were like locusts – many in good times, gone “when the sun rises”. Have you experienced people or supports disappearing in a crisis? How does that help you rest in the Good Shepherd, who does not slumber or fly away (Psalm 121:4; John 10:11–12)?

Nahum 3:16–18

Discussion · 7/7

Jonah ends with God's question of grace over a repentant Nineveh (Jonah 4:11); Nahum ends with “for over whom has your evil not passed continually?” What do the two endings together teach us about God? And looking back over the whole series: which truth about God has comforted you most?

Nahum 3:19

For the week ahead

  • Ask the Holy Spirit each day this week to show you one place where you keep up a mask – of success, of having it all together, of religion – and bring it to God in honesty.
  • Choose one person who has been hurt by the harshness of the world and be a voice of God's comfort to them this week: a visit, a meal, a message or a prayer together.

Pray together

Begin with a few moments of silent confession, asking God to cleanse anything built on dishonesty or on wronging others. Then thank him together for the Good Shepherd, who took our “fracture” on himself so that through his wounds we are healed (Isaiah 53:5). Close by reading 1:7 aloud together as the summary of the whole series.
