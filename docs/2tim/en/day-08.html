Faithful to the End · Day 8 / 9

Proclaim the Word and Finish the Race

Scripture 2 Timothy 4:1–8

1I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus, who is going to judge the living and the dead, and by his appearing and his kingdom: 2proclaim the word; keep at it whether the time is right or not; reprove, rebuke, encourage, with all patience and teaching. 3For the time will come when people will not put up with sound teaching, but, following their own desires, will pile up teachers for themselves because their ears itch, 4and they will turn their ears away from the truth and wander off to myths. 5But you, be sober-minded in everything, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, carry out your service fully.

6For already I am being poured out as a drink offering, and the time for my departure is here. 7I have fought the good fight, I have run the race to the end, I have kept the faith. 8What remains is this: the crown of righteousness is stored up for me, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will give me on that day – and not to me only, but to all who have loved his appearing.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

These eight verses form the dramatic and emotional climax of Paul's whole apostolic ministry. At the end of chapter 3, Paul laid the unshakeable foundation that all Scripture is breathed out by God (theopneustos) and fully sufficient (3:16–17). Now, without any break (there were, after all, no chapter divisions in the original manuscript), he links that teaching about the nature of Scripture directly to the most solemn charge in the letter: because Scripture is God's living voice, Timothy must proclaim it.

Paul writes knowing that his earthly time has run out. In Rome death awaits him – according to early church tradition, by the executioner's sword. But before he leaves the stage of this world, he summons Timothy, as it were, into the heavenly courtroom – into the presence of God and Christ Jesus, the coming Judge and King – to take over from him the torch of proclaiming the Word without compromise.

Insights from the Greek

The Greek text opens a treasure chest of images from the courtroom, the altar, the harbour and the racetrack:

  • "Proclaim the word" (kēryxon ton logon – κήρυξον τὸν λόγον, v. 2): The verb kēryssō comes from kēryx – the imperial herald or town crier of the ancient world. A herald did not stand up in the town square to share his own personal philosophy or to chair a panel discussion; with a loud, authoritative voice he announced the King's decree. His only duty was absolute faithfulness to the message of the one who sent him.
  • "Whether the time is right or not" (eukairōs akairōs – εὐκαίρως ἀκαίρως, v. 2): He is to keep at it (epistēthi – "stand ready", like a sentry at his post) "whether the time is right or not". That means bringing the Word when the season is favourable and also when it is inconvenient, unwelcome or dangerous – whether it suits the preacher and whether the hearers welcome it or not.
  • "Because their ears itch" (knēthomenoi tēn akoēn – κνηθόμενοι τὴν ἀκοήν, v. 3): Why is this urgency needed? Because seasons will come when people within the visible church "will not put up with sound teaching" (ouk anexontai). The Greek knēthō literally describes an itchy spot that wants to be scratched or tickled. Instead of submitting their lives to God's Word, they "pile up teachers for themselves" (episōreusousin – to heap up in a pile) who will cater to their sinful desires and soothe their consciences without ever addressing their sin.
  • "Be sober-minded" and "carry out your service fully" (nēphe – νῆφε / plērophorēson – πληροφόρησον, v. 5): In the midst of the culture's spiritual drunkenness, Timothy is to be "sober-minded in everything" (nēphō – clear-headed, calm and focused) and to carry out his service fully (plērophoreō – to finish a task down to the last drop).
  • The drink offering and the departure (spendomai – σπένδομαι / kairos tēs analyseōs – καιρὸς τῆς ἀναλύσεως, v. 6): Here Paul gives us two of the most beautiful images for a believer's death in the whole Bible:
    1. The drink offering: "For already I am being poured out as a drink offering." In the Old Testament sacrificial service (Numbers 15:1–10) and in the ancient world, the drink offering was a cup of precious wine poured out with the sacrifice – according to later Jewish sources at the foot of the altar, among the Greeks often over the sacrifice itself. Together with the offering it was "a pleasing aroma to the Lord" (Numbers 15:10). Paul does not see his coming execution as a tragic waste of his life, but as the final offering of worship poured out on God's altar!
    2. Casting off the ropes: "… and the time for my departure is here." The noun analysis was used of a traveller pulling up his tent pegs to go home, and of a ship in harbour whose heavy mooring ropes are loosed from the quay so that it can sail out to the open sea. For a child of God, death is not a blind sinking into the dark, but the loosing of earthly ropes to sail into the eternal harbour.
  • The threefold look back and the crown (vv. 7–8): With three verbs in the Greek perfect tense (which marks a completed action with lasting results), Paul looks back on his life: "I have fought the good fight, I have run the race to the end, I have kept the faith" (ton kalon agōna ēgōnismai, ton dromon tetelēka, tēn pistin tetērēka). And then he looks ahead: in Rome an unjust court of Nero's empire will soon find him guilty, but in heaven "the righteous judge" is waiting with "the crown of righteousness" (stephanos – the victor's wreath). This crown is not meant for apostles only, but for "all who have loved his appearing" (tois ēgapēkosi tēn epiphaneian autou) – every believer who longs for Christ's return.

Core Theological Truths

  1. We live and serve before the eyes of the coming King. The strongest antidote to the fear of people and to compromise is knowing that one day we will give account to Jesus Christ, who at his appearing will judge the living and the dead.
  2. True ministry of the Word comforts the grieving and wakes up the comfortable. Preaching and bearing witness faithfully means that with the truth we reprove, rebuke and encourage with endless patience (makrothymia), whether the culture wants to hear it or not.
  3. Ear-tickling is the forerunner of spiritual ruin. As soon as people make their own desires the standard and turn their ears away from the truth, they fall prey to absurd myths.
  4. A life poured out for Christ ends in eternal triumph. The world sees an old man laying his head on the executioner's block; heaven sees a victor crossing the finish line to be crowned by the hand of his King.

Living It Out

This passage challenges us to the very marrow of our spiritual lives: How do we listen to the Word – and how will we finish our race?

First, look at the diagnosis in verses 3 and 4: people gather teachers "because their ears itch". In the first century, people had to travel far to find a false teacher who would soothe their conscience. Today, with the internet and social media, anyone can "pile up" thousands of voices on a screen within seconds, all saying exactly what they want to hear. If you want to keep living in a particular sin, or if you are only looking for a message that strokes your ego and promises you earthly prosperity without ever speaking of repentance, self-denial and the cross, you will find plenty of preachers to scratch your ears.

But a doctor who only tells you how well you look while a deadly tumour grows in your body does not love you! We should thank God every time the preaching of his Word reproves and rebukes us – when the Holy Spirit makes us uncomfortable about our sin – because that is the operating theatre where God saves our souls and makes them whole.

Second, look at Paul's testimony at the end of his road (vv. 6–8). Each of our own analysis – the day our mooring ropes on this earth are loosed – is drawing nearer. We do not know whether we have thirty years or thirty hours left. The most important question on your deathbed will not be how much money you made, how popular you were or how comfortable your life was. The only question that will matter then is whether you can say with Paul: I stayed in the arena, I ran my lane all the way to the white line, and I held on to the treasure of the faith!

And how do you manage to finish like that? Verse 8 gives the secret: by belonging to "all who have loved his appearing". When your heart is full of longing for the day you will see Jesus face to face, the cheap little crowns of this world fade – and you receive the strength to pour out your life today like a drink offering to his glory.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • When I read the Bible and listen to sermons, am I only looking for messages that "tickle my ears" and make me feel good – or do I welcome the Word also when it reproves and rebukes me?
  • Do I share the truth of the gospel with the people around me "whether the time is right or not", with patient love – or do I fall silent as soon as the opportunity feels uncomfortable?
  • If my earthly mooring ropes were loosed today, could I testify with confidence that I have poured out my life as a thank offering for Christ and that I am looking forward with joy to his appearing?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, living King and righteous Judge, before whose eyes my whole life lies open and bare:

I bow before you today in holy reverence. When I think of the day of your glorious appearing and your kingdom, the noise and the emptiness of this world fade away. Keep my heart from ever growing tired of the pure, sound teaching of your Word. Heal me of ears that itch and only want to be soothed; give me a hunger for the whole truth – even when your Word reproves me, breaks my pride and calls me to repentance.

Lord, make me spiritually sober and awake in a world intoxicated by myths and lies. Give me the courage to bear witness to your grace whether the time is right or not, and to carry out fully, to the last drop, the calling you have placed on my life.

I thank you for the victorious testimony of your servant Paul. Teach me not to hold my life back stingily for myself, but to pour it out every day on your altar like a fragrant drink offering of love and service. Give me the grace to fight the good fight, to run my race to the end and to keep the faith until one day the mooring ropes are loosed and I see your face. I love your appearing, O Lord – come soon!

In your mighty and royal name. Amen.
