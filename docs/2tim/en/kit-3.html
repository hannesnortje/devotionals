Small-group kit · Session 3 / 4

Remain in the Word

Present ▶

The big ideaIn difficult times an outward form of godliness is not enough; what keeps us standing is the God-breathed Scripture, learned from people whose lives we have seen up close.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Who taught you something important – not in a classroom, but by letting you watch how they lived? What did you pick up from them?

Read together · 2 Timothy 3:1–17

Use two readers: verses 1–9 (the warning – read the list at a steady pace and pause after “Turn away from such people!”) and verses 10–17 (the answer); then read verses 16–17 aloud together.

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 2 Paul told Timothy to treat the misled with gentleness and patience. Now he warns him not to be naive: the “last days” – the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return – bring difficult times, even inside the church. Tonight we read chapter 3: a portrait of religion that is only an outer shell, and Paul's answer – a life lived in the open and the Scriptures breathed out by God.

A look at the Greek

  • philautoi … philotheoi – φίλαυτοι … φιλόθεοι “lovers of themselves … lovers of God”: Paul frames the list with words built on *philos* (love): it begins with “lovers of themselves” and ends with “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God” (3:2, 4). The root of the decay is disordered love – when love of self pushes out love of God, relationships fall apart.
  • morphōsis eusebeias – μόρφωσις εὐσεβείας “outward form of godliness”: A *morphōsis* is an outward form or shell. These people are “having an outward form of godliness but denying its power” (3:5): the right words and the right meetings, but no *dynamis* – no power of the Spirit that changes the heart.
  • theopneustos – θεόπνευστος “God-breathed”: The word occurs only here in the New Testament (3:16): Scripture is breathed out by God. When Scripture speaks, God speaks – and so it is useful “for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness”.

Discussion

  1. Read the list in verses 2–4 again. Which of these marks do you recognise most in our culture today? Why might the list begin with “lovers of themselves”? (2 Timothy 3:1–4)
  2. Paul is describing people who are “having an outward form of godliness but denying its power”. What could that look like in a church – and, honestly, in my own life? (2 Timothy 3:5)
  3. “always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth” – how is it possible to take in many sermons, podcasts and books and still not change? (2 Timothy 3:7)
  4. Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses by imitating his miracles. Where do we meet imitations of true faith today, and how can we tell the difference? (2 Timothy 3:8–9)
  5. Timothy had closely followed Paul's life – including what happened in Lystra, probably his home town. Who has let you see their faith up close? And who is watching yours? (2 Timothy 3:10–11)
  6. “all who want to live in godliness in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.” Do we expect the Christian life to be comfortable? What has following Christ cost you? (2 Timothy 3:12)
  7. Scripture is useful “for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness”. Which of these four do you welcome easily – and which do you tend to avoid? (2 Timothy 3:16–17)

For the week ahead

  • Take one Bible passage each day this week and ask four questions: What does it teach me? Where does it reprove me? What does it want to set straight? How does it train me in righteousness?
  • Spend time this week with someone younger in faith – a child, a grandchild or a new believer – and tell them about one time when God carried you through something hard.

Pray together

Begin with a minute of silence, asking God to show you any area where you are content with an outward form. Then pray together: thank God for his God-breathed Word, for the parents, grandparents and mentors who passed it on to you, and pray for the children and young people in your lives.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers two devotionals (days 6 and 7). The list in verses 2–4 easily turns into complaining about “the world out there”; day 6 aims the sharpest question at “us who sit in the pews” – steer the group there.
  • Handle 3:6 (“weak women”, *gynaikaria*) with care. Following day 6, it is a judgment on these particular people who let themselves be taken in, not on women in general. The ones under judgment are the false teachers; the people they prey on are “weighed down with sins and driven by all kinds of desires” – a weakness any of us can have.
  • Our translation reads 3:16 predicatively: “All Scripture is God-breathed and useful”. Day 7 notes that the reading every God-breathed Scripture is also useful is grammatically possible; our translation follows the more common reading. Mention it if asked, but don't let it take over.
  • Verse 15 (“from childhood”) may be painful for those without a Christian upbringing, or for parents whose children have turned away. It is never too late to sow the Word – say so gently.
  • If time is short, skip question 4. Point ahead: next week is the last session – chapter 4, Paul's final charge and his last words. Consider ending it with a meal or a longer time of prayer.

Scripture 2 Timothy 3:1–17

1But understand this: in the last days difficult times will come. 2For people will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boastful, arrogant, blasphemers, disobedient to their parents, ungrateful, unholy, 3heartless, irreconcilable, slanderers, without self-restraint, brutal, haters of what is good, 4treacherous, reckless, puffed up, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, 5having an outward form of godliness but denying its power. Turn away from such people! 6For among them are those who worm their way into households and take captive weak women, who are weighed down with sins and driven by all kinds of desires, 7always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. 8Just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these also oppose the truth: people corrupted in mind, who have failed the test regarding the faith. 9But they will not get very far, for their folly will become plain to everyone, as happened with those two.

10You, however, have closely followed my teaching, my way of life, my purpose, my faith, my patience, my love, my endurance, 11my persecutions and my sufferings, the things that happened to me in Antioch, in Iconium and in Lystra. What persecutions I bore! And the Lord rescued me from all of them. 12Indeed, all who want to live in godliness in Christ Jesus will be persecuted. 13But evil people and impostors will go from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived. 14But you, remain in what you have learned and have become convinced of, knowing from whom you learned it, 15and that from childhood you have known the sacred writings, which can give you the wisdom that leads to salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. 16All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness, 17so that the man of God may be fully capable, equipped for every good work.
