Faithful to the End · Session 3

Remain in the Word

2 Timothy 3:1–17

To get started

Who taught you something important – not in a classroom, but by letting you watch how they lived? What did you pick up from them?

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 2 Paul told Timothy to treat the misled with gentleness and patience. Now he warns him not to be naive: the “last days” – the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return – bring difficult times, even inside the church. Tonight we read chapter 3: a portrait of religion that is only an outer shell, and Paul's answer – a life lived in the open and the Scriptures breathed out by God.

A look at the Greek

φίλαυτοι … φιλόθεοι

philautoi … philotheoi · “lovers of themselves … lovers of God”

Paul frames the list with words built on *philos* (love): it begins with “lovers of themselves” and ends with “lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God” (3:2, 4). The root of the decay is disordered love – when love of self pushes out love of God, relationships fall apart.

A look at the Greek

μόρφωσις εὐσεβείας

morphōsis eusebeias · “outward form of godliness”

A *morphōsis* is an outward form or shell. These people are “having an outward form of godliness but denying its power” (3:5): the right words and the right meetings, but no *dynamis* – no power of the Spirit that changes the heart.

A look at the Greek

θεόπνευστος

theopneustos · “God-breathed”

The word occurs only here in the New Testament (3:16): Scripture is breathed out by God. When Scripture speaks, God speaks – and so it is useful “for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness”.

The big idea

In difficult times an outward form of godliness is not enough; what keeps us standing is the God-breathed Scripture, learned from people whose lives we have seen up close.

Discussion · 1/7

Read the list in verses 2–4 again. Which of these marks do you recognise most in our culture today? Why might the list begin with “lovers of themselves”?

2 Timothy 3:1–4

Discussion · 2/7

Paul is describing people who are “having an outward form of godliness but denying its power”. What could that look like in a church – and, honestly, in my own life?

2 Timothy 3:5

Discussion · 3/7

“always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth” – how is it possible to take in many sermons, podcasts and books and still not change?

2 Timothy 3:7

Discussion · 4/7

Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses by imitating his miracles. Where do we meet imitations of true faith today, and how can we tell the difference?

2 Timothy 3:8–9

Discussion · 5/7

Timothy had closely followed Paul's life – including what happened in Lystra, probably his home town. Who has let you see their faith up close? And who is watching yours?

2 Timothy 3:10–11

Discussion · 6/7

“all who want to live in godliness in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.” Do we expect the Christian life to be comfortable? What has following Christ cost you?

2 Timothy 3:12

Discussion · 7/7

Scripture is useful “for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness”. Which of these four do you welcome easily – and which do you tend to avoid?

2 Timothy 3:16–17

For the week ahead

  • Take one Bible passage each day this week and ask four questions: What does it teach me? Where does it reprove me? What does it want to set straight? How does it train me in righteousness?
  • Spend time this week with someone younger in faith – a child, a grandchild or a new believer – and tell them about one time when God carried you through something hard.

Pray together

Begin with a minute of silence, asking God to show you any area where you are content with an outward form. Then pray together: thank God for his God-breathed Word, for the parents, grandparents and mentors who passed it on to you, and pray for the children and young people in your lives.
