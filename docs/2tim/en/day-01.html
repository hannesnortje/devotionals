Faithful to the End · Day 1 / 9

Rekindle the Gift of God

Scripture 2 Timothy 1:1–7

1Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, in keeping with the promise of the life that is in Christ Jesus, 2to Timothy, my beloved child: grace, mercy and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

3I am grateful to God, whom I serve with a clear conscience as my forefathers did, when I remember you without ceasing in my prayers, night and day. 4I long to see you – I remember your tears – so that I may be filled with joy, 5for I have been reminded of the sincere faith that is in you, a faith that lived first in your grandmother Lois and in your mother Eunice, and I am convinced that it lives in you as well. 6For this reason I remind you to rekindle the gift of God that is in you through the laying on of my hands. 7For God did not give us a spirit of cowardice, but of power and of love and of self-control.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Today we begin a nine-day journey through Paul's second letter to Timothy – a letter about faithfulness to the end, written by an apostle who can already see the end of his own race.

With the opening of 2 Timothy we enter a very different world from that of 1 Timothy. When Paul wrote his first letter (around AD 62–64), he was a free man, hoping to visit Timothy in Ephesus soon. But in the few years that followed, the political and spiritual climate of the Roman Empire changed dramatically. After the great fire of Rome broke out in AD 64, the emperor Nero put the blame on the Christians. Suddenly, confessing the name of Christ could cost you your life.

Paul was arrested again – possibly in Troas, where he left his cloak and books behind (cf. 4:13) – and taken to Rome. This time he was not in a comfortable rented house under house arrest, as at the end of Acts; he was in chains (1:16), treated like a criminal (2:9), and expecting his execution (4:6). Tradition places him in a cold, damp underground dungeon, the Mamertine Prison. This letter, probably written around AD 66–67, is Paul's swan song – his spiritual testament. And to whom does the old, battle-tested soldier write his last words? To his "beloved child" Timothy, whose tears (v. 4) Paul cannot forget, and who was bowed down in Ephesus under enormous pressure and intimidation.

Insights from the Greek

The Greek text lays bare the deep emotion and powerful theology of this opening:

  • "The promise of the life" (kat' epangelian zōēs – κατʼ ἐπαγγελίαν ζωῆς, v. 1): While the shadow of the executioner's sword hangs over Paul's neck and the smell of death fills his cell, he begins his letter by speaking of "the promise of the life that is in Christ Jesus". Nero could kill Paul's earthly body, but he could never touch the eternal life anchored in Christ.
  • "Sincere faith" (anypokritou pisteōs – ἀνυποκρίτου πίστεως, v. 5): Paul reminds the discouraged Timothy of "the sincere faith that is in you" – literally a faith without a mask, without hypocrisy. This faith had a precious family history: it lived first in his Jewish grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice (cf. Acts 16:1), from whom he came to know the sacred writings from childhood (2 Timothy 3:14–15). Yet a grandmother's and a mother's faith does not automatically make anyone a child of God; that is why Paul adds that this same faith now lives personally in Timothy: "it lives in you as well".
  • "Rekindle" (anazōpyrein to charisma tou theou – ἀναζωπυρεῖν τὸ χάρισμα τοῦ θεοῦ, v. 6): Here Paul uses a brilliant, vivid verb that occurs only once in the New Testament: anazōpyreō. It is made up of three parts: ana (again / up), zōos (alive) and pyr (fire). It calls up the picture of someone working a pair of bellows over smouldering coals until the grey ash blows away and the living flame leaps up again. Paul does not say that Timothy's gift (charisma) is dead, but that fear, tears and human opposition have let a thick layer of ash settle over his zeal. He must deliberately rake the coals open again and fan them into flame.
  • "A spirit of cowardice" (pneuma deilias – πνεῦμα δειλίας, v. 7): Why was Timothy's fire burning low? Because he was intimidated by persecution from outside and opposition from within. Paul declares: "For God did not give us a spirit of cowardice." The Greek word deilia does not mean natural human caution or holy reverence (phobos), but cowardice – the paralysing fear of a soldier who throws down his weapon in battle and shrinks back because he dreads pain or rejection. That cowardice never comes from the Holy Spirit!
  • "Of power and of love and of self-control" (dynameōs kai agapēs kai sōphronismou – δυνάμεως καὶ ἀγάπης καὶ σωφρονισμοῦ, v. 7): Against the paralysis of fear stands the threefold equipment of the Spirit that God has given every believer: 1. Dynamis (power): God's supernatural, inner ability and courage to stand firm when your own strength is spent. 2. Agapē (love): self-giving love for God and people, which sets you free from self-absorption and from the fear of what people can do to you (cf. 1 John 4:18). 3. Sōphronismos (self-control / a sound mind): this word, too, occurs only here in the New Testament. It means a disciplined, calm and clear-thinking mind that does not panic in a crisis or get carried away by emotion, but stays under the rule of Christ's truth.

Core Theological Truths

  1. The promise of eternal life triumphs over every earthly darkness. Even in a death cell a child of God can overflow with gratitude and peace, because our future is guaranteed by Christ.
  2. A spiritual heritage is a privilege that must be made your own. God powerfully uses the faithful prayers and teaching of parents and grandparents, but every generation must take up the torch of sincere faith for itself.
  3. Spiritual fire needs daily tending. Even the most gifted believer's zeal can be smothered under the ash of discouragement and fear if it is not deliberately fanned into flame.
  4. Fear is not overcome by willpower alone, but by the fullness of the Spirit. Where the Holy Spirit reigns, cowardly shrinking back is replaced by divine power, burning love and a calm, disciplined mind.

Living It Out

How often do we feel exactly like Timothy in Ephesus? You see the hostility of the culture towards biblical truth, you feel the pressure at work or in your family when you want to stand up for Christ, or you are simply emotionally drained after a season of tears and disappointment. Before you know it, a thick layer of grey ash has settled over your spiritual life. You still believe, but the flame of passion, boldness and joy has turned into a dull ember.

Today Paul comes and stands beside you with the bellows of the Word: Rekindle the gift! (Anazōpyrein!) A campfire dies by itself if you do nothing; it only keeps burning if you push the coals together, shake off the ash and add fresh wood. How do you do that spiritually? By refusing to sink into passivity, by getting back on your knees, by opening the Scriptures, and by starting again to serve with the gifts God has given you.

Look honestly, too, at the role of fear in your life (v. 7). We often have fine excuses for our silence: "I'm just an introvert", or "I don't want to make people uncomfortable", or "I'm waiting for a better opportunity." But Paul calls it by its name: when we shrink back from what God has called us to because we are afraid of people's reactions or of failure, that is deilia – a spirit of cowardice. And the wonderful news of verse 7 is that you do not have to be a slave to that fear, because it does not come from your Father!

When anxiety or intimidation knocks at your door today, remind yourself who already lives within you: the Spirit of power (for your weakness), the Spirit of love (to turn your focus away from yourself towards others) and the Spirit of self-control (who keeps your thoughts calm and fixed on Jesus in the middle of the storm).

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Who were my Lois and Eunice? Who sowed the seed of the gospel in my heart – and how am I living today so that I can pass on that same sincere faith to the next generation?
  • Is there ash on the coals? Is there a layer of "ash" – discouragement, hurt or sluggishness – dampening the spiritual fire and the gifts in my life right now, and what practical step must I take today to fan the flame again?
  • Where does fear paralyse me? In what specific situation am I letting a "spirit of cowardice" or panic paralyse me, instead of acting out of the power, love and sound mind the Holy Spirit has already given me?

Prayer

Heavenly Father, God of life and source of all grace, mercy and peace,

I thank you today for the precious gift of a sincere faith. Thank you for every spiritual parent, grandparent and mentor you have placed on my path to pray for me and to live out your truth before me. Help me to carry that torch with the same sincerity and faithfulness.

Lord, I confess to you that I often let the storms of life, my own tears and the pressure of this world make the fire within me dwindle. I confess that I shrink back so easily into cowardice, fear of people and worry when you call me to stand firm. Forgive me where I have left the gifts you entrusted to me lying under the ash of passivity.

Breathe again today with the breath of your Holy Spirit over the coals of my heart! Blow away the ash of discouragement and let my devotion to Jesus Christ blaze up brightly once more. Today, in the name of Jesus, I reject every spirit of fear and cowardice, and with gratitude I receive what you have given me: your power for my weakness, your burning love that drives out all fear, and a calm, disciplined mind that stands firm on your Word.

In the victorious name of Jesus Christ, our Lord. Amen.
