Faithful in God's Household · Day 10 / 16

Train Yourself for Godliness

Scripture 1 Timothy 4:6–10

6If you put these things before the brothers and sisters, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, nourished on the words of the faith and of the good teaching that you have followed. 7But have nothing to do with irreverent, old wives' myths. Rather, train yourself for godliness. 8For bodily training is of little value, but godliness is of value in every way, holding promise for the present life and for the life to come. 9This is a trustworthy saying and deserves full acceptance. 10For to this end we toil and are reviled, because we have set our hope on the living God, who is the Saviour of all people, especially of those who believe.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

In verses 1 to 5 Paul exposed the false asceticism of the false teachers – people who thought true holiness came from forbidding marriage and certain foods. Now he shows Timothy what genuine spiritual discipline looks like. Paul does not reject discipline as such; he rejects only misplaced, legalistic discipline that has no eternal value.

To drive his point home, Paul reaches for an image every resident of Ephesus would grasp at once: the Graeco-Roman gymnasion and the world of athletics. Ephesus was known for its sports facilities and for the regular games held in honour of Artemis. Young men trained for hours under strict coaches, pushing their bodies to the limit to win a fading laurel wreath. Paul takes this cultural obsession with physical training and turns it into a powerful picture of the Christian life.

Insights from the Greek

The Greek text unfolds rich images of nourishment, training and endurance:

  • "If you put these things before…" (hypotithemenos – ὑποτιθέμενος, v. 6): To be a "good servant" (kalos diakonos) of Christ Jesus, Timothy must put these truths before the brothers and sisters. The verb hypotithēmi literally means to set something carefully in front of someone (like a nourishing meal on a table) or to lay a firm foundation under someone's feet. A true spiritual leader does not bully or manipulate the flock as the false teachers did; he patiently serves them with the truth.
  • "Nourished" (entrephomenos – ἐντρεφόμενος, v. 6): This participle is in the present tense and describes an ongoing, daily process of nourishment. No athlete can perform without the right diet, and no servant of God can feed others spiritually while their own soul is starving. Timothy's menu consists of "the words of the faith" and "the good teaching that you have followed" (parēkolouthēkas – like someone following step by step in a teacher's footsteps).
  • "Irreverent, old wives' myths" (bebēlous kai graōdeis mythous – βεβήλους καὶ γραώδεις μύθους, v. 7): Over against the healthy diet of the Word stands spiritual junk food. Bebēlos describes what is profane, worldly and without any sacred value. Graōdēs ("old-womanish") was a common expression among Greek philosophers for superstitious, unprovable stories and pointless speculation. Timothy must firmly "have nothing to do with" such mythoi (paraiteomai – to refuse even to listen to them).
  • "Train yourself for godliness" (gymnaze seauton pros eusebeian – γύμναζε σεαυτὸν πρὸς εὐσέβειαν, v. 7): Here Paul uses the imperative of gymnazō, which gives us gymnasium and gymnastics. In the ancient world athletes trained naked (gymnos) so that no heavy clothing would hinder them, and they built their muscles with sweat and sacrifice. Paul is saying that godliness (eusebeia – a life of deep reverence and practical obedience towards God) never happens by accident or by itself. It requires deliberate, disciplined spiritual effort.
  • "Bodily training is of little value" (pros oligon estin ōphelimos – πρὸς ὀλίγον ἐστὶν ὠφέλιμος, v. 8): Paul does not say that caring for and exercising the body is useless or wrong. He says its value is limited (pros oligon), because its benefits apply only to this short earthly life and end at the grave. The value of godliness, by contrast, spans two worlds: it holds "promise for the present life and for the life to come".
  • "We toil and are reviled" (kopiōmen kai oneidizometha – κοπιῶμεν καὶ ὀνειδιζόμεθα, v. 10): For this eternal goal we toil (kopiaō – to work to the point of physical and emotional exhaustion) and are reviled (oneidizō – to endure insult and mockery; some ancient manuscripts read agōnizometha here, "we struggle as in an arena"). What gives a believer the strength to sweat like this and bear reproach? Our hope is set "on the living God".
  • "The Saviour of all people, especially of those who believe" (sōtēr pantōn anthrōpōn, malista pistōn – σωτὴρ πάντων ἀνθρώπων, μάλιστα πιστῶν, v. 10): In his common goodness God sustains, preserves and provides for all people every day with life and breath, and his offer of salvation is open to all (see 2:4). But in a very particular, eternal way (malista – especially, above all) he gives complete and final salvation to those who believe in Christ.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Healthy ministry flows from healthy personal nourishment. You cannot pour from an empty cup. Before you can put God's truth before others, your own heart must be filled with Scripture every day.
  2. Grace does not rule out spiritual effort; it fuels it. We are saved by grace alone, yet that very grace calls us to train in holiness, prayer and obedience with the dedication of a top athlete.
  3. An eternal perspective sets our priorities. It is folly to invest all our time and energy in caring for a temporary earthly tent while our immortal soul is neglected.
  4. Our hope in the living God is the fuel of perseverance. When our eyes are fixed on the promises of the living God, earthly exhaustion and human mockery become light to carry.

Living It Out

We live in a culture obsessed with the physical body, wellness and outward appearance. Billions are spent every year on gym memberships, strict diets, vitamins, running shoes and cosmetics, trying to halt the ageing process. There is nothing wrong with being good stewards of our bodies – Paul does grant that bodily training has some value, for a short while.

The tragedy is the imbalance. How many Christians would never miss their morning run or their gym session, and count every calorie, while their Bibles lie closed under a layer of dust week after week? We build muscles for a body that will return to dust, while our spiritual muscles waste away for the eternity that lies ahead.

Look at your spiritual diet, too (vv. 6–7). What do you feed your mind every day? If you scroll through social media for hours, stuffing yourself with political quarrels, conspiracy theories, worldly entertainment and the modern "old wives' myths" of our day, don't be surprised if your faith feels weak and your spirit anxious.

To train yourself for godliness (gymnazō) means building deliberate spiritual habits – even on days when you don't "feel like it". An athlete doesn't train only when inspired; he trains according to a fixed discipline. In the same way, spiritual fitness requires us to lift the heavy weights every day: meditating on the Word, persevering in prayer, self-denial, forgiveness and service. The reward is beyond compare: peace and character in this life, and indescribable glory in the life to come.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • If I compare the time and energy I spend on my body, my appearance and earthly comfort with the time I give to spiritual training (Bible study, prayer and service), what picture does that paint of my real priorities?
  • What do I "feed" my soul during the week – the words of the faith, or the empty calories of worldly entertainment, news obsession and pointless arguments?
  • Which spiritual discipline in my life has slackened, and is asking today for a new, deliberate decision to start "training" again?

Prayer

Living God, my Creator, my Saviour and my eternal hope, I come to you today aware of how easily my priorities slip. Forgive me, Lord, that I so often give my best time, my sharpest thinking and most of my energy to things that matter only for this short, passing life, while I neglect the training of my soul.

Lord Jesus, awaken in me a deep, unquenchable hunger for the words of the faith and for good teaching. Guard my mind and my eyes from the empty, irreverent stories and distractions of this world that steal my spiritual strength. Feed me daily with the living bread of your Word, so that I may grow strong and be a blessing to others.

Holy Spirit, give me the discipline of a spiritual athlete. Help me to throw off every weight and the sin that so easily entangles me, and to train with perseverance in true godliness. When the road grows steep, when I am worn out by the work, or when people revile me for my faith, keep my eyes fixed on your promises – for today and for the eternity ahead. In the name of Jesus Christ, the pioneer and perfecter of our faith. Amen.
