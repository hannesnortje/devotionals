Prayer for All, Worship in Order

1 Timothy 2:1–15

To get started

Who is a public figure – a politician, a celebrity, a CEO – you find hard to pray for? (No need to say why.)

Where we are in the story

After warning against false teaching, Paul turns to the life of the church gathered for worship. First he urges prayer for all people, including rulers, because there is one God and one mediator. Then he speaks to men and women about how they pray, dress, learn and serve – some of the most discussed verses in the New Testament.

A look at the Greek

μεσίτης

mesitēs · “mediator”

A mediator stands between two estranged parties to reconcile them. There is “one mediator between God and humankind, the man Christ Jesus” (2:5) – no other bridge is needed or possible.

A look at the Greek

ἀντίλυτρον

antilytron · “ransom”

Christ “gave himself as a ransom for all” (2:6). The prefix anti- means “in the place of”: our freedom was bought at the cost of his own life.

A look at the Greek

ἡσυχία

hēsychia · “quietness”

The same root as the “peaceful and quiet life” Paul wants for all believers (2:2). In 2:11–12 it describes a peaceful, teachable spirit – not a humiliating silence.

The big idea

God “wants all people to be saved”, so the church's first task is prayer for all – and its worship should reflect reconciled hearts, holy lives and God's good order.
