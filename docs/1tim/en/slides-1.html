Faithful in God's Household · Session 1

Guard the Gospel

1 Timothy 1:1–20

To get started

Think of something precious someone once entrusted to you – a key, a child, a secret. How did it change the way you behaved?

Where we are in the story

Paul has left Timothy behind in Ephesus, a busy port city full of temples and ideas, to deal with people who were teaching “a different doctrine”. This letter is Paul's instruction for how the church should live as God's household. Tonight we read chapter 1: the danger of false teaching, Paul's own story of grace, and a charge to Timothy to stay in the fight.

A look at the Greek

τέλος τῆς παραγγελίας

telos tēs parangelias · “the goal of our instruction”

The goal of all teaching is not winning debates but “love from a pure heart, from a good conscience and from a sincere faith” (1:5). Teaching that breeds pride and quarrels has missed its goal.

A look at the Greek

πιστὸς ὁ λόγος

pistos ho logos · “this is a trustworthy saying”

The first of five “trustworthy sayings” in the Pastoral Letters (1:15). It anchors the church in a simple confession: “Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners.”

A look at the Greek

στρατεία

strateia · “warfare”

Timothy is to “wage the good warfare” (1:18) – a long military campaign, not a single battle. Faith and a good conscience are his equipment; without them faith suffers shipwreck.

The big idea

Sound teaching is known by its fruit – love, a good conscience and sincere faith – and at its heart stands the gospel of grace for sinners like Paul and like us.

Discussion · 1/7

What were the false teachers in Ephesus busy with, according to verses 3–7? What might “myths and endless genealogies” look like in church life today?

1 Timothy 1:3–7

Discussion · 2/7

Paul says the goal of instruction is “love from a pure heart, from a good conscience and from a sincere faith”. How can we tell whether teaching we hear is “sound”?

1 Timothy 1:5

Discussion · 3/7

Paul describes himself as “a blasphemer, a persecutor and a violent man”. Why do you think he tells his own story at this point in the letter?

1 Timothy 1:13

Discussion · 4/7

Paul says “of them I am the first” – present tense, not past. What does this tell us about how a mature Christian sees himself or herself?

1 Timothy 1:15

Discussion · 5/7

Paul's testimony ends in worship (v. 17). When did thinking about God's grace last lead you to praise?

1 Timothy 1:17

Discussion · 6/7

“Some have pushed this conscience aside and have made shipwreck of their faith.” How can ignoring our conscience slowly wreck our faith?

1 Timothy 1:19

Discussion · 7/7

Where is your conscience “flashing a warning light” at the moment? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

For the week ahead

  • Write down your own short “testimony of grace” (three or four sentences, as Paul does in verses 12–16) and share it with one person this week.
  • Ask God each evening this week: “Is there anything I am pushing aside in my conscience?” – and act on one thing he shows you.

Pray together

Begin by reading 1:17 aloud together as praise. Then pray in pairs: thank God for his mercy to each of you, and pray for people you know who have “made shipwreck” of their faith, that they would be restored.
