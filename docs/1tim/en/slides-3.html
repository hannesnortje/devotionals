Faithful in God's Household · Session 3

Leaders for God's Household

1 Timothy 3:1–16

To get started

If you had to hire someone to look after your house and family while you were away for a year, what would matter most to you?

Where we are in the story

The crisis in Ephesus was at heart a leadership crisis. In chapter 3 Paul sets out what overseers and deacons must be like – almost all of it about character, very little about skills. Then he explains why: the church is the household of the living God, and he ends with an early Christian hymn about Christ.

A look at the Greek

ἀνεπίλημπτος

anepilēmptos · “above reproach”

The heading over the whole list (3:2): a picture from wrestling – someone with nothing an opponent can grab hold of. Not sinless, but without obvious footholds for accusation.

A look at the Greek

δοκιμάζω

dokimazō · “be tested”

Of deacons Paul says: “let them also be tested first” (3:10) – the word for testing precious metals in fire. Character is proved over time, before a public task is given.

A look at the Greek

εὐσέβεια

eusebeia · “godliness”

The letter's key word. “great is the mystery of godliness” (3:16): true godliness is not produced by willpower, it flows from wonder at who Christ is and what he has done.

The big idea

The church is God's household and “a pillar and foundation of the truth”, so its leaders are chosen for tested character more than talent – and all godliness flows from the mystery of Christ.
