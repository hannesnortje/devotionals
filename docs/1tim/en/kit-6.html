Small-group kit · Session 6 / 6

Real Wealth, Real Life

Present ▶

The big ideaTrue gain is godliness with contentment; so flee the love of money, pursue Christlike character, fight the good fight of the faith – and guard the gospel entrusted to you.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

If your house were on fire and everyone were safe, what one thing would you go back for – and why?

Read together · 1 Timothy 6:1–21

Let one reader take verses 1–10, a second verses 11–16, and a third verses 17–21. Read verses 15–16 together as a group, like a confession of faith.

Where we are in the story

In the last chapter Paul gathers up the letter. He contrasts teachers who see godliness as “a means of gain” with the real gain of contentment, calls Timothy a “man of God” with four commands – flee, pursue, fight, take hold – breaks out into praise of the King of kings, and ends with one urgent plea: guard what has been entrusted to you.

A look at the Greek

  • autarkeia – αὐτάρκεια “contentment”: In Stoic philosophy, the pride of needing nothing. Paul fills it with new content: being satisfied with what God gives, because he is enough (6:6–8).
  • philargyria – φιλαργυρία “love of money”: Not money itself, but the love of it is “a root of all kinds of evil” (6:10). It quietly draws people away from the faith and leaves them “pierced … with many pains”.
  • parathēkē – τὴν παραθήκην φύλαξον “guard what has been entrusted to you”: A deposit handed over for safekeeping: the keeper may not change it or keep it for himself, but must protect it and pass it on intact (6:20). That is how Paul sees the gospel.

Discussion

  1. Verses 1 and 2 speak to slaves in the Roman world. We live in a different world, but most of us work under someone's authority. What does it look like to work in a way that brings honour, not shame, to “the name of God”? (1 Timothy 6:1–2)
  2. Some people think “that godliness is a means of gain”. Where do we see this today – and where in ourselves? (1 Timothy 6:5)
  3. “But godliness with contentment is great gain.” What makes contentment so hard in our culture? What helps you practise it? (1 Timothy 6:6–8)
  4. “For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.” How can you recognise when love of money is growing in your own heart? (1 Timothy 6:10)
  5. Flee, pursue, fight, take hold (6:11–12). Which of the four commands is most urgent for you at the moment? (1 Timothy 6:11–12)
  6. Paul tells the rich not to set their hope on “the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy”. Compared with most of the world, many of us are rich. What would “rich in good works” look like for us? (1 Timothy 6:17–19)
  7. Looking back over the six sessions: what has God shown you through 1 Timothy, and what will you take with you?

For the week ahead

  • Do a simple “contentment check” this week: each evening, write down three things God has given you that day, and one thing you can give away.
  • Choose one concrete act of generosity – time, money or hospitality – and do it before the week is over, quietly.

Pray together

Begin by reading 6:15–16 aloud together as praise. Then pray for each other to “take hold of the life that is truly life”, and thank God for what the group has received through this study of 1 Timothy.

Notes for the leader

  • This is the last session. Leave enough time for question 7 and for prayer; you may drop one of the earlier questions to make room.
  • Verses 1–2 on slavery need care. Explain briefly, as Day 15 does, the Roman context and Paul's concern for the gospel's reputation, and don't suggest that the Bible endorses slavery; then move to today's workplaces.
  • Money is a personal topic. Avoid comparing incomes, and don't put anyone on the spot; let people share what they are comfortable with.
  • Point out the contrast between the false teachers' “gain” (6:5) and true “gain” (6:6) – the group often discovers it themselves if you ask what word appears twice.
  • Consider ending with a short celebration or meal together. If your group wants to continue, the letter of 2 Timothy is a natural next step.

Scripture 1 Timothy 6:1–21

1Let all who are under the yoke as slaves regard their own masters as worthy of all honour, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be blasphemed. 2Those who have believing masters must not look down on them because they are brothers; rather, they must serve them all the better, because those who benefit from their good service are believers and beloved. Teach and urge these things.

3If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and with the teaching that accords with godliness, 4he is puffed up and understands nothing. He has an unhealthy craving for controversies and quarrels about words, which produce envy, strife, slander, evil suspicions, 5and constant friction among people who are corrupted in mind and deprived of the truth, who think that godliness is a means of gain. 6But godliness with contentment is great gain. 7For we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of it. 8But if we have food and clothing, with these we will be content. 9But those who want to be rich fall into temptation, into a trap, and into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. 10For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Some, by craving it, have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pains.

11But you, man of God, flee from these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness. 12Fight the good fight of the faith. Take hold of the eternal life to which you were called and about which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses. 13I charge you before God, who gives life to all things, and before Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession, 14to keep the commandment spotless and above reproach until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ,

15which he will display at the proper time –he who is the blessed and only Sovereign,the King of kings and Lord of lords,16who alone has immortality,who dwells in unapproachable light,whom no one has ever seen or can see.To him be honour and eternal dominion. Amen.

17As for the rich in this present age, instruct them not to be arrogant, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy. 18They are to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share, 19so storing up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of the life that is truly life.

20O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you. Turn away from irreverent, empty chatter and the contradictions of what is falsely called knowledge, 21which some have professed and so have swerved from the faith. Grace be with you all.
