Flee, Pursue, Fight – and Guard What Has Been Entrusted

Scripture 1 Timothy 6:11–21

11But you, man of God, flee from these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness. 12Fight the good fight of the faith. Take hold of the eternal life to which you were called and about which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses. 13I charge you before God, who gives life to all things, and before Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession, 14to keep the commandment spotless and above reproach until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ,

15which he will display at the proper time –he who is the blessed and only Sovereign,the King of kings and Lord of lords,16who alone has immortality,who dwells in unapproachable light,whom no one has ever seen or can see.To him be honour and eternal dominion. Amen.

17As for the rich in this present age, instruct them not to be arrogant, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy. 18They are to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share, 19so storing up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of the life that is truly life.

20O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you. Turn away from irreverent, empty chatter and the contradictions of what is falsely called knowledge, 21which some have professed and so have swerved from the faith. Grace be with you all.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With these stirring closing verses, Paul reaches the summit of his first letter to Timothy. Having described in verses 3 to 10 the tragic fate of the false teachers, swallowed up by the love of money and quarrels about words, he draws a sharp dividing line in verse 11: "But you, man of God …"

This form of address – man of God (anthrōpe theou) – is used as a title for a person in the New Testament only here and in 2 Timothy 3:17. In the Old Testament, "man of God" was the highest title of honour for God's prophetic messengers such as Moses, Samuel, Elijah, Elisha and David. By addressing the young, often intimidated Timothy in this way, Paul reminds him that he does not belong to people's opinions or to the culture of Ephesus. He is God's own possession and stands in the noble line of prophets who held their ground alone against a faithless generation.

Insights from the Greek

Here the Greek text unfolds a powerful symphony of charges, worship and stewardship:

  • The fourfold charge (vv. 11–12): Paul fires four dynamic verbs at Timothy that sum up the rhythm of a faithful Christian life:
    • "Flee" (pheuge – φεῦγε): The present tense means to keep running away from the sins of greed, strife and false teaching. Spiritual courage knows when to run from temptation (see Joseph in Genesis 39:12).
    • "Pursue" (diōke – δίωκε): A hunting term meaning to stay tirelessly on the trail until you catch up with your quarry. While fleeing sin, you are to actively hunt down six Christlike qualities: righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance (hypomonē – staying on your feet under a heavy load) and gentleness (praypathia).
    • "Fight" (agōnizou – ἀγωνίζου): In 1:18 Paul used a military image ("wage the good warfare"); here he chooses an athletic term from the Greek games (from which we get the word agony). It demands every ounce of strength and stamina in the arena of faith.
    • "Take hold of" (epilabou – ἐπιλαβοῦ): To grasp eternal life with both hands – not to try to earn salvation, but to live practically and wholeheartedly out of the reality of the eternal life to which God has already called you and about which you made the good confession.
  • The good confession and the King of kings (vv. 13–16): When Timothy is tempted to give in under pressure, Paul points him to Christ Jesus, who stood before the Roman governor Pontius Pilate. With his own life at stake, Jesus did not flinch but fearlessly testified to his kingship and to the truth (John 18:36–37). The thought of Christ's "appearing" (epiphaneia) makes Paul burst out once more into a majestic doxology. In contrast to the Roman emperors, worshipped in Ephesus as "lords" and "gods", Paul declares that God alone is the Dynastēs ("Sovereign"), that he "alone has immortality" (athanasia) and "dwells in unapproachable light" (phōs aprositon) – a blinding holiness and glory so pure that no sinful eye can look upon it.
  • The life that is truly life, for the rich (vv. 17–19): Ephesus was an immensely wealthy commercial city, and there were well-to-do believers in the church. Notice Paul's balance: he does not order them to throw away their possessions and become poor; he even affirms that God richly provides us with everything "to enjoy" (eis apolausin). But he warns against the two great diseases of wealth: arrogance (hypsēlophronein – looking down on others because you are rich) and false security (setting your hope on the adēlotēs – the fickle uncertainty – of money). Instead of being rich in bank accounts, they are to be "rich in good works", "generous" (eumetadotous) and "ready to share" (koinōnikous). In this way they "take hold of the life that is truly life" (tēs ontōs zōēs).
  • "Guard what has been entrusted to you" (tēn parathēkēn phylaxon – τὴν παραθήκην φύλαξον, v. 20): Paul closes the letter with an emotional cry: "O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you." In ancient law, a parathēkē was a precious treasure or deposit handed to a trusted person for safekeeping. The keeper had no right to alter it or claim it for himself; he had to protect it with his life and return it intact. This deposit is the pure gospel of Jesus Christ. Timothy is to turn his back on "what is falsely called knowledge" (pseudōnymos gnōsis – the early seeds of Gnosticism, which boasted of secret intellectual knowledge). Those who ran after this sham wisdom "swerved from the faith" (ēstochēsan – an archer's term meaning to miss the target completely).

Core Theological Truths

  1. Holiness is a double movement. No believer stays spiritually healthy by knowing only what he is against (flee), without living passionately for what he is called to (pursue).
  2. God's sovereign majesty is the anchor of our courage. When we grasp that we serve the King of kings who dwells in unapproachable light, earthly "Pilates" and human threats lose their grip on our hearts.
  3. Generosity turns passing money into eternal treasure. Earthly wealth is shifting sand when you build your hope on it, but it becomes a firm foundation for eternity when it is used to bless others.
  4. We are guardians, not designers, of the truth. The gospel is a holy parathēkē, a treasure entrusted to us. Our highest calling is to carry the torch of faith unstained until our King appears.

Living It Out

At the end of our journey through 1 Timothy, Paul leaves us with a programme for life that directs every day of our walk with the Lord.

First, ask yourself: What am I running away from – and what am I running towards? Many Christians define their faith only negatively – by all the things they do not do. But if you only try to flee from sin without actively filling your heart with the pursuit of righteousness, love, endurance and gentleness, you will burn out spiritually. What are you sweating for in the arena today (agōnizou)?

Second, look at Paul's wise counsel about our possessions and finances (vv. 17–19). Compared with the rest of world history, most of us who have a regular income, a home and a car today belong to "the rich in this present age". God does not begrudge us the joy of his blessings – after all, he richly provides us with everything "to enjoy"! But the temptation is always to tie our peace of mind and our identity to our savings, our pensions and our assets. A single economic crash or a single medical diagnosis shows how quickly "the uncertainty of riches" can evaporate. How do you break the power of materialism over your soul? By giving deliberately, generously and willingly. Every time you open your hand to support God's work and people in need, you move your treasure from a temporary earthly bank to an imperishable heavenly account – and taste here and now what "the life that is truly life" feels like.

Finally, Paul's last appeal rings personally in each of our ears. Put your own name into verse 20: "O [your name], guard what has been entrusted to you!" For two thousand years believers before us – often at the cost of their own blood – have guarded the pure gospel of grace so that it could be in our hands today. Now it is our shift on the wall. Do not let the "falsely called knowledge" of our modern spirit of the age lead you away from the cross of Jesus Christ. Hold on to what has been entrusted to you until the King of kings appears!

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Am I as purposeful and passionate in pursuing righteousness, love, endurance and gentleness as I am in fleeing from worldly temptations?
  • Where does my deepest financial security lie today: in the shifting security of my earthly assets, or in God – as I use my blessings with an open, generous hand to store up a good foundation for the future?
  • At the end of this journey through 1 Timothy: which specific part of what has been entrusted to me – in my personal conscience, my household or my involvement in the local church – is the Lord calling me to guard with new seriousness from today on?

Prayer

Blessed and only Sovereign, King of kings and Lord of lords! You alone have immortality and dwell in unapproachable light – before you I bow today in deep worship and wonder.

I thank you from the bottom of my heart for the journey on which you have led me through this letter to Timothy. Thank you that your Word has searched my conscience, renewed my mind and fixed my eyes afresh on the glory of Jesus Christ, our only Mediator and Saviour.

Lord, give me the grace as a child of God to flee from everything that defiles my soul, and to pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance and gentleness with unquenchable zeal. When the fight of faith grows fierce, remind me of Jesus, who made the good confession before Pilate, and make me steadfast and unafraid.

Free me from the delusion of setting my hope on earthly goods or uncertain riches. As I gratefully enjoy what you provide from your Father's hand, make me rich in good works, generous and ready to share, so that I take hold of the life that is truly life with both hands.

And now, Lord, help me by your Holy Spirit to guard the precious treasure of the gospel you have entrusted to me, spotless and above reproach, until the wonderful day when Jesus Christ appears in glory. To you be all honour and eternal dominion, for ever and ever. Amen.
