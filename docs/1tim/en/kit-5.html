Small-group kit · Session 5 / 6

The Church as a Family

Present ▶

The big ideaGod's household is a family: we treat each other as fathers, mothers, brothers and sisters, care concretely for those who are truly alone, and honour and correct leaders fairly and without favouritism.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Who was a “spiritual father” or “spiritual mother” to you – someone outside your own family who shaped your faith? What did they do?

Read together · 1 Timothy 5:1–25

The chapter is long. Read it in three sections with different readers: verses 1–8, verses 9–16, verses 17–25.

Where we are in the story

Paul has called the church “the household of God” (3:15). In chapter 5 he shows what that means in everyday life: how to speak to older and younger people, how to look after widows who have no one, and how to treat elders – with honour, but also with fair correction. It is the most practical chapter of the letter, and some of it is far from our world; the principles behind it are not.

A look at the Greek

  • ontōs chēra – τὰς ὄντως χήρας “truly widows”: Paul uses this phrase three times (5:3, 5, 16). The church's help is meant first for those who really have no one else – families are to take responsibility for their own first (5:4, 8).
  • parakalei – μὴ ἐπιπλήξῃς, ἀλλὰ παρακάλει “do not rebuke … harshly, but encourage”: Even necessary correction is to happen in a family tone: an older man as a father, younger men as brothers (5:1). The word for “encourage” comes from the same root as the Holy Spirit's name, the Paraclete.
  • diplē timē – διπλῆς τιμῆς “double honour”: Elders who lead well and labour in preaching and teaching deserve respect and also practical support (5:17–18) – and when they sin, they are not exempt from correction (5:19–20).

Discussion

  1. How should Timothy speak to older men, younger men, older women and younger women? What changes if we really treat the church as a family? (1 Timothy 5:1–2)
  2. “Honour widows who are truly widows.” Who are the people in our church and town today who are “truly alone”? How would we know? (1 Timothy 5:3)
  3. Paul is very sharp in verse 8: whoever does not provide for his own relatives “has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever”. Why does care for our own families weigh so heavily? (1 Timothy 5:8)
  4. The widows on the list were known for hospitality, washing the feet of the saints and helping the afflicted (5:10). What does this list tell us about what God values in a life? (1 Timothy 5:10)
  5. Elders are “worthy of double honour”, yet accusations need two or three witnesses, and sin is rebuked openly (5:17–20). How can a church honour its leaders and still hold them accountable? (1 Timothy 5:17–20)
  6. “Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands.” Why is patience so important before trusting people with leadership? Have you seen what happens without it? (1 Timothy 5:22)
  7. Paul tells Timothy to look after his health (5:23). What does it say about God that such a practical remark stands in the middle of instructions for church leaders? (1 Timothy 5:23)

For the week ahead

  • Think of one older person and one younger person in the church you could treat this week as a “father/mother” or “brother/sister” – with a call, a visit or a meal.
  • Find out together whether there is someone in your church or neighbourhood who is “truly alone”, and agree on one concrete step of help.

Pray together

Pray for people in the church who are alone, ill or grieving, by name where appropriate. Then pray for the elders and leaders of your church: for wisdom, protection and faithfulness.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers three devotionals (Days 12–14). The chapter is long; don't try to discuss every verse. Questions 1, 2 and 5 carry the main line.
  • The rules about younger widows (5:11–15) can sound harsh or strange today. Explain briefly that the “list” was a church office with lifelong support and a vow of service (Day 13), and don't force the group into a single application.
  • Group members may hold different convictions about church structures, elders and the roles of women and men. Let the text speak, follow the line of the devotionals, and keep the conversation respectful; don't settle every disagreement tonight.
  • Verse 5:23 (wine) may provoke jokes or touch real struggles with alcohol. Keep the focus on Paul's care for Timothy's health, as Day 14 does, and be sensitive to anyone for whom alcohol is painful.
  • Question 3 can touch painful family situations (neglect, broken relationships, caring for parents). Allow silence, and offer to talk afterwards.

Scripture 1 Timothy 5:1–25

1Do not rebuke an older man harshly, but encourage him as you would a father, younger men as brothers, 2older women as mothers, younger women as sisters, in all purity.

3Honour widows who are truly widows. 4But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let them first learn to show godliness towards their own household and to repay their parents, for this is pleasing before God. 5The one who is truly a widow and left all alone has set her hope on God and continues in petitions and prayers night and day. 6But the one who lives for pleasure is dead while she lives. 7Instruct them in these things too, so that they may be above reproach. 8But if anyone does not provide for his own relatives, and especially for members of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever. 9Let a widow be put on the list if she is not less than sixty years old, the wife of one husband, 10and well known for good works: if she has brought up children, if she has shown hospitality, if she has washed the feet of the saints, if she has helped the afflicted, if she has devoted herself to every good work. 11But refuse younger widows; for when their desires draw them away from Christ, they want to marry, 12and so incur condemnation for having set aside their first faithfulness. 13Besides that, they learn to be idle, going round from house to house; and not only idle, but also gossips and busybodies, saying what they should not. 14So I want younger widows to marry, to have children, to manage their households, and to give the adversary no occasion for slander. 15For some have already turned aside to follow Satan. 16If any believing woman has widows in her family, let her help them, and let the church not be burdened, so that it may help those who are truly widows.

17Let the elders who lead well be considered worthy of double honour, especially those who labour in preaching and teaching. 18For the Scripture says, "You shall not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain," and, "The worker deserves his wages." 19Do not accept an accusation against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses. 20Those who persist in sin, rebuke in the presence of all, so that the rest may also stand in fear. 21I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen angels to keep these rules without prejudice, doing nothing out of favouritism. 22Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands, and do not take part in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure. 23No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent illnesses. 24The sins of some people are obvious, going ahead of them to judgement, but the sins of others follow after them. 25So also good works are obvious, and even those that are not cannot remain hidden.
