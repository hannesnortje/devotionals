Small-group kit · Session 3 / 6

Leaders for God's Household

Present ▶

The big ideaThe church is God's household and “a pillar and foundation of the truth”, so its leaders are chosen for tested character more than talent – and all godliness flows from the mystery of Christ.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

If you had to hire someone to look after your house and family while you were away for a year, what would matter most to you?

Read together · 1 Timothy 3:1–16

One reader for verses 1–7 (overseers), another for verses 8–13 (deacons), a third for verses 14–15; then the whole group reads the hymn in verse 16 together, line by line.

Where we are in the story

The crisis in Ephesus was at heart a leadership crisis. In chapter 3 Paul sets out what overseers and deacons must be like – almost all of it about character, very little about skills. Then he explains why: the church is the household of the living God, and he ends with an early Christian hymn about Christ.

A look at the Greek

  • anepilēmptos – ἀνεπίλημπτος “above reproach”: The heading over the whole list (3:2): a picture from wrestling – someone with nothing an opponent can grab hold of. Not sinless, but without obvious footholds for accusation.
  • dokimazō – δοκιμάζω “be tested”: Of deacons Paul says: “let them also be tested first” (3:10) – the word for testing precious metals in fire. Character is proved over time, before a public task is given.
  • eusebeia – εὐσέβεια “godliness”: The letter's key word. “great is the mystery of godliness” (3:16): true godliness is not produced by willpower, it flows from wonder at who Christ is and what he has done.

Discussion

  1. Read through the qualities in verses 2–7. How many are about skills, and how many about character? What does that tell us? (1 Timothy 3:2–7)
  2. Why is the way someone manages his own household a test for leading “God's church”? (1 Timothy 3:4–5)
  3. Why should a leader “have a good reputation with outsiders”? How do colleagues and neighbours see our church – and us? (1 Timothy 3:7)
  4. Deacons are to be “not double-tongued”. Why is consistency in our words so important in a church family? (1 Timothy 3:8)
  5. The church is “the household of God … a pillar and foundation of the truth”. Do we treat our local church more like a household or like a service provider? (1 Timothy 3:15)
  6. Read the hymn in verse 16 slowly. Which line speaks to you most today, and why? (1 Timothy 3:16)
  7. These qualities are for leaders, but they describe every mature Christian. Which one would you like God to grow in you?

For the week ahead

  • Pray this week for the leaders of your church by name, using one quality from verses 2–13 for each of them.
  • Take on one quiet, unseen task of service in your church or home this week, without telling anyone.

Pray together

Pray for the overseers, elders and deacons of your church. Then read the hymn of verse 16 aloud once more and turn each line into a short sentence of praise.

Notes for the leader

  • This session covers three devotionals (days 6–8). Choose four or five questions; question 6 on the hymn makes a good ending.
  • Be careful that the evening doesn't become a critique of current church leaders. Keep the focus on what God values and on our own character.
  • Phrases like “husband of one wife” (3:2, 12) and “women” (3:11 – deacons' wives or women deacons; the day 7 devotional leaves both options open) can lead to long debates. Acknowledge the questions and return to the main point: tested character.
  • Some in the group may feel disqualified because of divorce, family struggles or past failures. Remind them of last week's theme: grace for the worst of sinners.
  • Verse 16 reads “He was revealed in the flesh”; older Bibles read “God”. Our translation follows the Greek text used for it (SBLGNT); the day 8 devotional explains the difference. Either way, the hymn is clearly about Christ.

Scripture 1 Timothy 3:1–16

1This is a trustworthy saying: if anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a good work. 2The overseer, then, must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, 3not a drunkard, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money, 4one who manages his own household well and keeps his children in submission, with all dignity – 5for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he take care of God's church? – 6not a recent convert, so that he does not become conceited and fall into the condemnation of the devil. 7He must also have a good reputation with outsiders, so that he does not fall into disgrace and into the devil's trap. 8Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not given to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain, 9holding the mystery of the faith with a clear conscience. 10And let them also be tested first; then let them serve, if they prove blameless. 11Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober-minded, faithful in all things. 12Deacons must each be the husband of one wife, managing their children and their own households well. 13For those who have served well gain a good standing for themselves and great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

14I am writing these things to you, though I hope to come to you soon, 15so that, if I am delayed, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, a pillar and foundation of the truth. 16And beyond all question, great is the mystery of godliness:

He was revealed in the flesh,vindicated by the Spirit,seen by angels,proclaimed among the nations,believed in throughout the world,taken up in glory.
