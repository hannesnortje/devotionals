Who Is a God Like You? · Session 6

What the LORD Seeks

Micah 6:1–16

To get started

Think of a time someone gave you a big present when what you really wanted was an apology or simply their time. How did it feel?

Where we are in the story

With chapter 6 the last major section of Micah begins, and once again we are led into a courtroom. This time the LORD calls the mountains and the age-old hills as witnesses in his case against his people – yet he speaks not like an angry judge but like a wounded Father: “My people, what have I done to you?” Then the LORD's measuring line is carried from the hills into Jerusalem's market squares, where it finds false scales, violence and lies.

A look at the Hebrew

וְאַהֲבַת חֶסֶד

ahavat chesed · “to love faithful love”

Many English Bibles say “to love kindness” or “mercy”, but literally the text says to love chesed: loyal covenant love, faithfulness, mercy and undeserved grace. It is the very word Micah uses for God himself at the end of the book (7:18, 20) – God asks us to love what he himself delights in.

A look at the Hebrew

וְהַצְנֵעַ לֶכֶת

hatsnea' lekhet · “to walk humbly”

The rare verb tsana' occurs in the Old Testament only here and in Proverbs 11:2. It means walking with God humbly, attentively, without show and with a listening heart – not ahead of him in haste, not lagging behind, but with him.

A look at the Hebrew

חֻקּוֹת עָמְרִי

chuqqot 'omri · “the statutes of Omri”

The word chuqqot normally means the holy statutes of the LORD. With cutting irony, verse 16 says Judah keeps the “statutes” of Omri and Ahab instead – the unwritten rules of greed, abuse of power and religious compromise that brought prosperity to a few and ruin to the nation.

The big idea

The LORD who redeemed his people cannot be bought with offerings; he has told us what is good – to do justice, to love faithful love and to walk humbly with our God – and he looks for it in the market, at the workbench and in our weighing of others.

Discussion · 1/7

Why do you think the LORD calls the mountains and hills as witnesses? And how is the tone of verse 3 different from what you would expect in a court case: “My people, what have I done to you? How have I wearied you? Answer me!”

Micah 6:1–3

Discussion · 2/7

In verses 4–5 the LORD answers the charge of “wearying” them by recalling Egypt, Balaam's blessing and the road from Shittim to Gilgal. Why does remembering what God has done drive out spiritual weariness? What is a “Shittim to Gilgal” moment in your own story?

Micah 6:4–5

Discussion · 3/7

The worshipper in verses 6–7 keeps raising the price – calves, thousands of rams, rivers of oil, even “Shall I give my firstborn for my rebellion”. What picture of God lies behind these questions? In what modern ways do we try to “negotiate” with God?

Micah 6:6–7

Discussion · 4/7

“He has told you, O man, what is good.” Look at the three parts of verse 8. Why do they belong together, and what happens to each when one of the others is missing?

Micah 6:8

Discussion · 5/7

Verse 8 asks us not only to show faithful love but to love it – the same chesed God delights in (7:18). What is the difference between doing kind things out of duty and loving faithful love?

Micah 6:8

Discussion · 6/7

“Can I count anyone pure with wicked scales and with a bag of deceitful weights?” Where do we handle “scales” today – at work, in money matters, or in the way we weigh other people's faults against our own?

Micah 6:10–12

Discussion · 7/7

Verses 14–15 describe eating without being satisfied and sowing without reaping. Where do you see this restless emptiness around you – or in yourself? What does verse 9 mean when it calls us to “Hear the rod, and the one who appointed it!”?

Micah 6:9–15

For the week ahead

  • Choose the one part of 6:8 that is most neglected in your life right now and take one concrete step in it this week – an honest correction, an act of faithful love, or ten quiet minutes walking with God.
  • Check one “scale” in your life this week (your timesheet, an invoice, a promise, a judgement about someone) and make it honest.

Pray together

Begin by thanking God for the “righteous acts” he has done in your own lives. Then pray 6:8 for one another, each person naming the part they find hardest, and close by praying for honesty and justice in the places where group members work.
