Who Is a God Like You? · Day 13 / 14

Light in the Darkness: Waiting for the LORD When Everything Crumbles

Scripture Micah 7:1–7

1Woe is me! For I have becomelike one gathering summer fruit,like one gleaning after the grape harvest:there is no cluster to eat,no early fig that my soul longs for.2The faithful one has perished from the land,and there is no one upright among people.They all lie in wait for blood;each hunts his brother with a net.3Their hands are on evil, to do it well:the official asks, and the judge – for a payment;and the great man speaks out the craving of his soul;and so they weave it together.4The best of them is like a briar,the most upright worse than a thorn hedge.The day of your watchmen, your punishment, has come;now their confusion will be.5Do not trust a friend,do not rely on a close companion;from her who lies in your embraceguard the doors of your mouth.6For son treats father with contempt,daughter rises up against her mother,daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law;a man's enemies are the people of his own house.7But as for me, I will watch for the LORD;I will wait for the God of my salvation;my God will hear me.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With chapter 7 we enter the last, deeply personal movement of the book of Micah. After the prophet has exposed Jerusalem's dishonest trade and corrupt leadership in chapter 6, we now hear in verses 1 to 6 a heart-rending personal lament. Micah walks, as it were, like a hungry traveller late in the summer through the vineyards and fig trees of Judah, looking for just one juicy cluster of grapes or one early fig – just one fruit of true righteousness and covenant faithfulness. But the orchards have been stripped bare.

What Micah sees is a society that has collapsed at every level. In public life (vv. 2–4) the official, the judge and the influential rich man work hand in hand to twist justice. Even more shocking, this rot has penetrated the most intimate family circles (vv. 5–6): friends betray one another, spouses cannot trust each other, and children rebel against their parents.

And then, in the middle of this suffocating moral night in which every human pillar is collapsing, one of the most beautiful declarations of faith in the whole Bible rings out in verse 7: "But as for me, I will watch for the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation; my God will hear me."

Insights from the Hebrew

  • The early fig (bikkurah – בִּכּוּרָה): In verse 1 Micah longs for a bikkurah – the first ripe fig, which appeared on the trees in early June and was considered a great delicacy in ancient Israel (cf. Isaiah 28:4; Hosea 9:10). By the end of the summer harvest in August/September all these choice fruits had long since been picked. It is Micah's picture of a spiritually exhausted nation in which moral integrity has become as rare as a summer fig in autumn.
  • The faithful one (chasid – חָסִיד): In verse 2 Micah mourns: "The faithful one has perished from the land." The word chasid is derived directly from chesed – the loyal, merciful covenant love the Lord asked of his people in Micah 6:8! Because the people refused to love chesed, there is no chasid (no person of grace and faithfulness) left to be found in the streets.
  • "They weave it together" (waye'abbetuha – וַיְעַבְּתוּהָ): At the end of verse 3 Micah uses the rare verb 'avat, which means to plait or knot ropes or branches tightly together. The official (sar), the judge (shofet) and the powerful businessman (gadol) form a tightly woven syndicate of corruption in which one hand washes the other, so that the ordinary citizen has no chance of justice.
  • Briar (chedeq – חֵדֶק) and thorn hedge (mesukah – מְסוּכָה): Farmers in Israel often planted dense hedges of thorn bushes around their vineyards to keep out wild animals. But in verse 4 Micah says that even the "best" leaders of Judah have become like a briar: instead of protecting people, they tear and wound everyone who comes near them for help.
  • Watching (tsafah – צָפָה) and waiting in hope (yachal – יָחַל):* Look at the two powerful verbs with which Micah describes his position in verse 7. Tsafah is the word for a watchman standing high on the city wall, shading his eyes with his hand and peering intently at the horizon for the coming of the king. Yachal*** means to wait with patient, eager expectation. This is not passive resignation but active, wakeful faith.

Core Theological Truths

  1. When a society rejects God, trust between people crumbles. Micah 7:2–6 shows that our horizontal relationships (in the state, in the market and in the family) are held together by our shared reverence for God. When the fear of the LORD disappears, every relationship becomes a power struggle in which even family members exploit one another for their own gain.
  2. Jesus Christ knew the loneliness of Micah 7:6 personally and foretold it for his followers. In Matthew 10:35–36 (and Luke 12:53) Jesus quotes Micah 7:6 almost word for word to warn his disciples that faithfulness to him can sometimes bring division right into a person's own family. When you choose to stand for God's light in a dark time, it may even provoke resistance from those closest to you.
  3. True faith shines brightest when every outward support falls away. Notice the turn between verse 6 and verse 7. Micah does not say, "Because everything around me is collapsing, I might as well give up." He says: "But as for me …" (wa'ani – וַאֲנִי). When human institutions, leaders and even friends disappoint, the believer is driven back to the only Person who never fails: "the God of my salvation" (Elohei yish'i – אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׁעִי).
  4. God's ear is always open to the lonely righteous person. In Micah 3:4 we read that the LORD hides his face when corrupt oppressors cry to him. But for the child of God who waits on him in the midst of a crooked generation, the sure promise of verse 7 stands: "my God will hear me."

Living It Out

We live today in a world that often feels just like Micah 7:1–6. When we read the news or look at the decline of integrity in politics, business and society at large, it often feels as if we are walking through a stripped vineyard where truth and faithfulness have become scarce. Sometimes, though, the hurt comes much closer to our own front door: a trusted friend who wounds you behind your back, tension and estrangement within your own family, or a thorny workplace where people wound one another to get to the top.

The great danger in such times is that we become cynical, bitter and hardened. When we see how the official, the judge and the great man weave their own interests together, or when we are let down by people close to us, we want to lock our hearts away behind thick walls.

In verse 7 Micah shows us an entirely different way out: the holy turn of the head. Instead of fixing your eyes on the briars of human disappointment around you, lift your eyes like a watchman on the wall (tsafah): "But as for me, I will watch for the LORD."

  • If the orchards around you feel bare today and you receive little chesed (loyal love) from others, the Lord asks that you be the early fig (bikkurah) and the chasid (person of grace) in your surroundings. Don't wait for others to change before you live out integrity and gentleness.
  • If you are going through a season in which people you counted on have let you down, let that pain drive you deeper into the arms of "the God of my salvation". People are fragile reeds; even the best person can prick like a thorn when you build your ultimate happiness on them. But the Lord will never betray you, and his ear is wide open to your voice today.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • As Micah walks through the "vineyard" of Judah, he searches in vain for the fruit of chesed (vv. 1–2). When weary people deal with you today, do they find in you the refreshing "early fig" of integrity and grace, or the sharp "thorns" of defensiveness and selfishness (v. 4)?
  • Has disappointment with leaders, institutions or even people close to you made your heart cynical and distrustful lately? How does verse 7 call you to move from bitterness to hopeful expectation?
  • Where in your life are you currently sitting in the tension of verses 5–6 (pain or misunderstanding within close relationships or family), where you need to hold on to the comfort that Jesus himself understands this pain and stands beside you?
  • What does it mean for you, practically, to "watch" (tsafah) for the Lord today like a watchman on the wall, in the middle of a situation that has not yet been resolved?

Prayer

Lord, God of my salvation and my only unshakeable Rock, when the shadows around me grow long and human faithfulness vanishes like mist before the sun, today I deliberately turn my eyes away from the brokenness and look up to you! Keep my heart from growing cold, cynical or bitter when I experience disappointment, dishonesty or broken relationships.

Lord, where I myself have sometimes been like a briar that pricked others with sharp words or impatience, change me by your Spirit into a fruitful tree that hands out your faithful love in a hungry world. Today I lay every sore place in my friendships and in my family at your feet. Where human supports have wavered, I thank you that your faithfulness reaches to the clouds.

Today I climb up to the watchtower of faith: I watch for you, I wait with quiet hope for you to act, and I rest in the sure knowledge: my God will hear me! In the name of Jesus Christ, my faithful Friend and Redeemer, Amen.
