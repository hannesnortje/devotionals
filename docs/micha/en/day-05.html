Who Is a God Like You? · Day 5 / 14

Leaders Who Twist Justice and Prophets Who Prophesy for Money

Scripture Micah 3:1–7

1And I said:Hear, please, you heads of Jacoband rulers of the house of Israel!Is it not for you to know justice?2You who hate good and love evil,who tear their skin from off themand their flesh from off their bones;3who eat the flesh of my peopleand strip their skin from off them,break their bones in piecesand chop them up like meat for the pot,like flesh in the cauldron.4Then they will cry out to the LORD,but he will not answer them;he will hide his face from them at that time,because they have made their deeds evil.5Thus says the LORD concerning the prophetswho lead my people astray,who, when they have something to bite with their teeth,call out "Peace!",but whoever puts nothing into their mouths,against him they sanctify war:6Therefore it will be night for you, without vision,and darkness for you, without divination.The sun will go down on the prophets,and the day will grow dark over them.7The seers will be ashamedand the diviners disgraced;they will all cover their lips,for there is no answer from God.

Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With the words "Hear, please" (shim'u na – שִׁמְעוּ נָא) Micah 3 begins a new series of oracles. In chapter 1 Micah addressed the nation as a whole; in chapter 2 he confronted the wealthy land barons who pulled the strings behind the scenes. Now the prophet walks straight into the highest seats of power in Jerusalem and addresses the two pillars that were meant to carry society: the civil leaders and judges (vv. 1–4) and the religious prophets (vv. 5–7).

In ancient Israel the court at the city gate was the last refuge of the widow, the orphan and the impoverished smallholder. The heads and rulers had been appointed by God to be shepherds of the people. But in Micah's day the shepherds had become butchers. Worse still: while the political leaders were draining the people financially and physically, the official prophets – who were supposed to be the nation's conscience – hired out their voices to the highest bidder. As long as they were well paid, they pronounced religious blessing over a thoroughly corrupt system.

Insights from the Hebrew

  • To "know" justice (lada'at et hammishpat – לָדַעַת אֶת הַמִּשְׁפָּט): In verse 1 Micah asks: "Is it not for you to know justice (mishpat)?" In Hebrew the verb yada' (know) means far more than intellectual knowledge of legal clauses. It speaks of an intimate, lived relationship and practical commitment. The leaders could probably recite the laws of Moses by heart, but because they hated good and loved evil (v. 2), they had completely rejected the heart of God's justice.
  • The shocking butcher metaphor (vv. 2–3): Micah does not shy away from raw, shocking imagery. To expose the leaders' economic exploitation, he describes them as cannibals and butchers. First they tear and strip off the skin (gazal – גָּזַל, v. 2; pashat – פָּשַׁט, v. 3), then they tear the flesh from the bones, break the bones (patsach – פָּצַח) as if to suck out the last bit of marrow, and chop the pieces up for the pot and the cauldron (sir – סִיר and qallachat – קַלָּחַת). Why does the Spirit of God use such gruesome language? Because corrupt leaders like to hide their deeds behind tidy bookkeeping, tax laws and court orders. God rips away the veil and shows what injustice looks like in heaven's eyes: it literally devours people's lives and dignity.
  • Prophets who "bite with their teeth" (hannoshekhim – הַנֹּשְׁכִים, from nashakh – נָשַׁךְ): In verse 5 the LORD says that the false prophets call out "Peace!" (shalom) "when they have something to bite with their teeth". Elsewhere in the Old Testament the verb nashakh is used for the venomous bite of a snake (Numbers 21:6), and from the same root comes neshekh – usury, cruel interest that bites a person to death financially. These prophets are spiritual mercenaries. If a rich man fills their hands with silver or food, they guarantee him God's favour (shalom). But whoever puts nothing into their mouths, "against him they sanctify war" (weqiddeshu 'alaw milchamah – וְקִדְּשׁוּ עָלָיו מִלְחָמָה) – they abuse holy, religious language to destroy that person's name and to present him as accursed!
  • Covering the lips (we'atu 'al safam – וְעָטוּ עַל שָׂפָם): In verse 7 the shamed seers must cover their upper lip or moustache (safam). According to Leviticus 13:45 this was the prescribed gesture of a leper (who had to cry out, "Unclean! Unclean!"), and according to Ezekiel 24:17 and 22 a sign of deep mourning. Those who claimed to be God's mouthpiece must now cover their own mouths in shame.

Core Theological Truths

  1. Leadership and influence are a sacred stewardship, never a privilege for self-service. Whether in the state, in business, in the church or in the family – God gives authority so that the strong can protect the weak and help them flourish. When leaders use their position to put meat in their own pot at the expense of those in their care, they betray God, who speaks of "my people" (v. 3).
  2. How we treat people determines whether God listens to our prayers. Verse 4 contains one of the most serious warnings in all of Scripture: "Then they will cry out to the LORD, but he will not answer them; he will hide his face from them at that time." God does not let himself be manipulated by pious prayers while our hands are busy harming our neighbour (see Proverbs 21:13; Isaiah 1:15; 1 Peter 3:7).
  3. When spiritual truth is traded for human favour, the light goes out. The false prophets squandered their calling for comfort and approval. The just penalty is spiritual blindness – a "night … without vision" in which the sun goes down on their discernment (v. 6). If you refuse to speak God's truth when it costs you something, you eventually lose the ability to hear his voice when you need it most.

Living It Out

It is easy to read Micah 3 and immediately think of corrupt politicians or greedy television preachers in our own day. But the word of God is a two-edged sword that always aims at our own heart first before it points outward.

First, verses 1 to 4 ask each of us: How am I using the authority and influence entrusted to me? You don't have to be a president or a judge to be a "head" or "ruler" in your own small world. Perhaps you manage a team at work, have employees, lead a ministry, or are the head of a household. Do you use the people around you as "ingredients" for your own comfort, your own standing and your own emotional "cooking pot"? Or do you use your strength to build up their dignity and lighten their loads? And if your prayer life feels dry right now and the heavens seem made of bronze, ask the Holy Spirit to search your relationships: is there someone towards whom you have acted harshly, unjustly or exploitatively?

Second, verses 5 to 7 prick our own tendency towards "transactional love and truth". See how subtly human nature works: we are often very friendly, generous with compliments and full of shalom towards people who can put something into our mouths – people who can advance our career, raise our social standing or flatter our ego. But how do we treat the person who can give us absolutely nothing back, or the one who challenges our selfishness? Do we then quietly declare "war" on them in our attitude – through coldness, gossip or exclusion? True children of God speak the truth and show love without a price tag attached.

Questions for Personal Reflection

  • Do you know God's justice (mishpat) only as a theological concept in your head, or does it become visible in the way you speak to and deal with the people under your authority or in service roles every day?
  • Is there any injustice, bitterness or exploitation in your relationships that is standing like a wall between you and God's face in your prayer life right now (v. 4)?
  • Be honest about your motives in relationships: do you treat people who can give you advantage, recognition or popularity (who put something into your mouth) better than those who have nothing to offer you?
  • Have you ever kept silent about an important spiritual or moral truth because you were afraid it would cost you financial advantage, friendship or people's approval?

Prayer

Holy and righteous Lord, Protector of the weak and Judge of all the earth, today I bow in deep awe before your word. Forgive me for the times I have known your truth with my mind but have been selfish and harsh towards others in my daily conduct. Keep me from ever using people to serve my own ambition or comfort while forgetting that every person I touch is precious in your eyes.

Lord, search my relationships today. Wherever there is injustice, lovelessness or neglect in my life that causes you to hide your face from my prayers, show it to me, so that I can put it right before you and before my neighbour.

Deliver me from a mercenary heart that hands out peace and kindness only when I can get something out of it. Never let the sun of your Spirit and your word go down over my heart, but make me a pure, selfless channel of your grace and truth. In Jesus' name, Amen.
