Small-group kit · Session 6 / 7

What the LORD Seeks

Present ▶

The big ideaThe LORD who redeemed his people cannot be bought with offerings; he has told us what is good – to do justice, to love faithful love and to walk humbly with our God – and he looks for it in the market, at the workbench and in our weighing of others.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Think of a time someone gave you a big present when what you really wanted was an apology or simply their time. How did it feel?

Read together · Micah 6:1–16

Use four voices: a narrator for 6:1–2, the LORD for 6:3–5, a worried worshipper for 6:6–7, the prophet for 6:8; then let the LORD's voice return for 6:9–16.

Where we are in the story

With chapter 6 the last major section of Micah begins, and once again we are led into a courtroom. This time the LORD calls the mountains and the age-old hills as witnesses in his case against his people – yet he speaks not like an angry judge but like a wounded Father: “My people, what have I done to you?” Then the LORD's measuring line is carried from the hills into Jerusalem's market squares, where it finds false scales, violence and lies.

A look at the Hebrew

  • ahavat chesed – וְאַהֲבַת חֶסֶד “to love faithful love”: Many English Bibles say “to love kindness” or “mercy”, but literally the text says to love chesed: loyal covenant love, faithfulness, mercy and undeserved grace. It is the very word Micah uses for God himself at the end of the book (7:18, 20) – God asks us to love what he himself delights in.
  • hatsnea' lekhet – וְהַצְנֵעַ לֶכֶת “to walk humbly”: The rare verb tsana' occurs in the Old Testament only here and in Proverbs 11:2. It means walking with God humbly, attentively, without show and with a listening heart – not ahead of him in haste, not lagging behind, but with him.
  • chuqqot 'omri – חֻקּוֹת עָמְרִי “the statutes of Omri”: The word chuqqot normally means the holy statutes of the LORD. With cutting irony, verse 16 says Judah keeps the “statutes” of Omri and Ahab instead – the unwritten rules of greed, abuse of power and religious compromise that brought prosperity to a few and ruin to the nation.

Discussion

  1. Why do you think the LORD calls the mountains and hills as witnesses? And how is the tone of verse 3 different from what you would expect in a court case: “My people, what have I done to you? How have I wearied you? Answer me!” (Micah 6:1–3)
  2. In verses 4–5 the LORD answers the charge of “wearying” them by recalling Egypt, Balaam's blessing and the road from Shittim to Gilgal. Why does remembering what God has done drive out spiritual weariness? What is a “Shittim to Gilgal” moment in your own story? (Micah 6:4–5)
  3. The worshipper in verses 6–7 keeps raising the price – calves, thousands of rams, rivers of oil, even “Shall I give my firstborn for my rebellion”. What picture of God lies behind these questions? In what modern ways do we try to “negotiate” with God? (Micah 6:6–7)
  4. “He has told you, O man, what is good.” Look at the three parts of verse 8. Why do they belong together, and what happens to each when one of the others is missing? (Micah 6:8)
  5. Verse 8 asks us not only to show faithful love but to love it – the same chesed God delights in (7:18). What is the difference between doing kind things out of duty and loving faithful love? (Micah 6:8)
  6. “Can I count anyone pure with wicked scales and with a bag of deceitful weights?” Where do we handle “scales” today – at work, in money matters, or in the way we weigh other people's faults against our own? (Micah 6:10–12)
  7. Verses 14–15 describe eating without being satisfied and sowing without reaping. Where do you see this restless emptiness around you – or in yourself? What does verse 9 mean when it calls us to “Hear the rod, and the one who appointed it!”? (Micah 6:9–15)

For the week ahead

  • Choose the one part of 6:8 that is most neglected in your life right now and take one concrete step in it this week – an honest correction, an act of faithful love, or ten quiet minutes walking with God.
  • Check one “scale” in your life this week (your timesheet, an invoice, a promise, a judgement about someone) and make it honest.

Pray together

Begin by thanking God for the “righteous acts” he has done in your own lives. Then pray 6:8 for one another, each person naming the part they find hardest, and close by praying for honesty and justice in the places where group members work.

Notes for the leader

  • Micah 6:8 is so well known that people may answer from memory rather than from the text. Slow the group down to look at the context: the verse answers the bargaining of verses 6–7 and follows the reminder of grace in verses 4–5.
  • Make sure grace comes first: before God says how his people should live, he reminds them how he redeemed them. Day 11 also notes that the desperate question of verse 7 is answered in Christ, whom the Father gave for our rebellion.
  • Verse 7 mentions child sacrifice (which even King Ahaz practised, 2 Kings 16:3). Name it briefly as the extreme of “buying” God's favour; don't dwell on it.
  • The questions on verses 10–16 can feel accusing for those in business or in debt. Keep it as self-examination, not judgement of others, and offer to talk privately with anyone weighed down by a work situation.
  • Verse 9 should not be used to say that every hardship someone suffers is God's punishment. Day 12 calls the rod a gracious warning that calls us back; let people ask what God may be teaching them rather than telling them.
  • Two rich passages in one evening: if time is short, use questions 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7.

Scripture Micah 6:1–16

1Hear, please, what the LORD says:Get up, plead the case before the mountains,and let the hills hear your voice!2Hear, you mountains, the case of the LORD,you enduring ones, foundations of the earth!For the LORD has a case against his people,and with Israel he will argue it.3My people, what have I done to you?How have I wearied you? Answer me!4For I brought you up from the land of Egypt,and from the house of slaves I redeemed you;and I sent before youMoses, Aaron and Miriam.5My people, rememberwhat Balak king of Moab planned,and what Balaam son of Beor answered him –from Shittim to Gilgal –so that you may know the righteous acts of the LORD.6With what shall I come before the LORD,and bow down before God on high?Shall I come before him with burnt offerings,with calves a year old?7Will the LORD be pleased with thousands of rams,with ten thousand rivers of oil?Shall I give my firstborn for my rebellion,the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?8He has told you, O man, what is good,and what the LORD seeks from you:only to do justice,to love faithful love,and to walk humbly with your God.9The voice of the LORD calls out to the city –and it is wisdom to see your name:Hear the rod, and the one who appointed it!10Are there still in the house of the wickedtreasures of wickedness,and the short measure that is cursed?11Can I count anyone pure with wicked scalesand with a bag of deceitful weights?12Her rich men are full of violence,her inhabitants speak falsehood,and their tongue is deceit in their mouth.13So I too have begun to strike you,to make you desolate because of your sins.14You will eat, but not be satisfied,and the hunger will be inside you;you will put things aside, but not save them,and what you save I will give to the sword.15You will sow, but not reap;you will tread olives, but not anoint yourself with oil,and grapes for new wine, but not drink wine.16The statutes of Omri are kept,and all the practices of the house of Ahab,and you walk in their counsels;so I will make you a desolation,and her inhabitants something to hiss at;and you will bear the reproach of my people.
