Who Is a God Like You? · Session 2

Greed and the Breaker

Micah 2:1–13

To get started

Which advert, slogan or jingle from your childhood can you still recite – and what did it promise you?

Where we are in the story

In chapter 1 Micah exposed Judah's relationship with God: idols and false securities. Now he turns to how they treat their neighbours. In Israel every family's land was an inheritance, a gift from the LORD that guaranteed its freedom and bread (Leviticus 25:23; 1 Kings 21:3). But in the eighth century BC a wealthy elite used debts, bribed courts and sheer power to take the smallholders' fields. Tonight we read the woe over the land-grabbers (vv. 1–5), Micah's clash with the preachers who want him silenced (vv. 6–11), and a sudden promise: the LORD gathers his flock and the Breaker goes before them (vv. 12–13).

A look at the Hebrew

חָמַד

chamad · “covet”

“They covet fields and seize them” (2:2). Chamad is the word of the Tenth Commandment (Exodus 20:17). What begins as a quiet desire on the bed at night ends at daybreak in gazal – seizing by force.

A look at the Hebrew

חָשַׁב

chashav · “devise”

The oppressors lie on their beds and devise wickedness (2:1). The LORD answers with the same verb: “Look, against this family I am devising evil” (2:3). The schemers run headlong into the one who plans the history of the world.

A look at the Hebrew

הַפֹּרֵץ

haporets · “the one who breaks open”

From parats, to break a gap in a wall. The flock is shut in like sheep in a fold, but “The one who breaks open goes up before them” (2:13), and their king and the LORD lead the way out. The devotional sees its full meaning in Christ, the Good Shepherd (John 10).

The big idea

God sees the schemes we devise at night and the power we use against the weak, he will not be silenced by soothing preachers – and yet he himself goes ahead of his people as the one who breaks open the way.

Discussion · 1/7

“Woe to those who devise wickedness and work evil on their beds!” What do your thoughts usually turn over in the quiet hours of the night? Why does Micah start with the bed and not with the deed?

Micah 2:1

Discussion · 2/7

“Because it is in the power of their hand” – the word for power (el) is the same as the word for God, so one can also hear it as: their hand is a god to them. Where do you have power or leverage over others (work, money, home), and how do you use it?

Micah 2:1–2

Discussion · 3/7

For the greedy it is about “fields” and “houses”; God sees “a man and his house, a person and his inheritance”. Where are people today treated as things in the name of profit – and where are we part of it?

Micah 2:2–5

Discussion · 4/7

The opponents say: “One should not preach about such things; disgrace will not overtake us.” Why do we so easily prefer comfortable voices – the preacher of “wine and strong drink” – to the truth that heals?

Micah 2:6–11

Discussion · 5/7

“Do my words not do good to the one who walks uprightly?” How do you react inwardly when a passage of Scripture or a friend puts a finger on a sore spot in your life?

Micah 2:7

Discussion · 6/7

“From her little children you take away my splendour for ever.” How does injustice against families rob the next generation? Who are the vulnerable people around you whose dignity needs protecting?

Micah 2:8–10

Discussion · 7/7

“The one who breaks open goes up before them.” What wall of fear, failure or impossibility are you facing right now – and what would it mean to follow the King who goes ahead of you?

Micah 2:12–13

For the week ahead

  • Look at one area where the power is in your hand (as an employer, parent, customer or neighbour) and do one concrete thing this week that protects the dignity of someone weaker.
  • Before going to sleep each night, give your thoughts of the day to God in a short prayer – especially any envy or resentment – instead of turning them over in bed.

Pray together

Pray together: ask God to cleanse the secret rooms of our thoughts and to make us content with the inheritance he has given us in Christ. Pray for people in your area who are losing home, land or work. Then thank Jesus, the Breaker, for going ahead of each person into the situation they named.
