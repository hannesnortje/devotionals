Who Is a God Like You? · Session 4

Swords into Ploughshares

Micah 4:1–14

To get started

Have you ever turned something broken or useless into something useful – an old tool, a piece of furniture, a pair of worn-out jeans? What did you make of it?

Where we are in the story

Chapter 3 ended with a heap of ruins: because of Judah's corrupt leaders, Zion would be ploughed like a field and the temple mount overgrown with scrub. Chapter 4 suddenly opens with “And it will come to pass in the last days” – a vision found almost word for word in Isaiah 2:2–4. Then three scenes (“On that day”, “Now”, “And now”) lead along the hard road of exile to Babylon, and the chapter ends in 4:14 (English 5:1) with Jerusalem under siege and her judge struck on the cheek.

A look at the Hebrew

וְנָהֲרוּ

wenaharu · “will stream”

The verb comes from nahar, “river”: the peoples will flow like a mighty river (4:1). But water flows downhill – here the nations flow uphill, up the mountain of the LORD. The pull of God's grace is stronger than the gravity of human selfishness and division.

A look at the Hebrew

הַצֹּלֵעָה

hatstsole'ah · “the lame”

The verb tsala' is used of Jacob limping after he wrestled with God (Genesis 32:31, Hebrew 32:32). A human leader builds a nation from the strongest; the LORD says, “I will make the lame a remnant” (4:7). Weakness is not a disqualification but the place where his power is seen.

A look at the Hebrew

חוּלִי וָגֹחִי

chuli wagochi · “writhe and push”

In 4:9–10 Jerusalem screams in terror, and Micah compares her pain to that of a woman giving birth (kayyoledah). Death throes end in a grave; birth pains, however fierce, bring forth new life – “There you will be rescued; there the LORD will redeem you” (4:10).

The big idea

Judgement is not God's last word: the mountain left in ruins becomes the mountain the nations stream to, and on the hard road through exile the LORD gathers the lame, turns his people's pain into birth pains and rules even over the plans of their enemies.

Discussion · 1/7

At the end of chapter 3 the temple mount lies in ruins; in 4:1 the same mountain “will stand firm as the highest of the mountains”. What does this sudden turn tell us about God's judgement and his purposes?

Micah 4:1

Discussion · 2/7

The nations say: “Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob, so that he may teach us his ways and we may walk in his paths.” What draws them – force or longing? Do you come to God's word with that kind of hunger?

Micah 4:2

Discussion · 3/7

Notice the order in verses 2–3: first instruction goes out from Zion and the LORD judges, and only then are swords beaten into ploughshares. Why can't lasting peace come before that? What “swords and spears” (sarcasm, old grudges, harsh words) might God want to reforge in your relationships?

Micah 4:2–3

Discussion · 4/7

Each person sits under his vine and fig tree, “and no one will make them afraid” – while in 2:1–2 the powerful seized the small farmers' fields. Verse 5 then says: “but we will walk in the name of the LORD our God for ever and ever.” Where does the culture around you invite you to walk in the name of other “gods”?

Micah 4:4–5

Discussion · 5/7

The LORD gathers the lame, the outcast and “the one on whom I brought evil”, and then says: “I will make the lame a remnant.” Why would God build his future with people like that? What does this mean for someone who feels limping or second-rate?

Micah 4:6–7

Discussion · 6/7

“Is there no king in you? Has your counsellor perished, that pain has seized you like a woman giving birth?” The nations gather to destroy Zion, “But they do not know the plans of the LORD”. Looking back, can you name a crisis that turned out to be birth pains rather than death throes?

Micah 4:9–12

Discussion · 7/7

The chapter ends with a siege: “with the staff they strike the judge of Israel on the cheek” (4:14; English 5:1). When our own leaders, plans and strength are humiliated like this, where can hope still come from? (Leave the answer open for next week.)

Micah 4:14

For the week ahead

  • Choose one “sword” in your speech or attitude towards a particular person and turn it into a “ploughshare” this week: one honest, kind word or act that builds up instead of cutting down.
  • Read 4:1–5 each morning this week and pray for one place in the world (or one relationship) where there is war, asking that the word of the LORD would bring peace there.

Pray together

Thank God together that his judgement never has the last word. Let each person pray, silently or aloud, for one area where they feel “lame” or under pressure, and ask the LORD to gather and use it. Close by praying verse 5 together as a commitment: we will walk in the name of the LORD our God.
