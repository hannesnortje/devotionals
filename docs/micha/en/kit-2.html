Small-group kit · Session 2 / 7

Greed and the Breaker

Present ▶

The big ideaGod sees the schemes we devise at night and the power we use against the weak, he will not be silenced by soothing preachers – and yet he himself goes ahead of his people as the one who breaks open the way.

Timing

  • 10 min Welcome & opener
  • 10 min Read the passage
  • 10 min Background & original language
  • 40 min Discussion
  • 15 min Application & prayer

To get started

Which advert, slogan or jingle from your childhood can you still recite – and what did it promise you?

Read together · Micah 2:1–13

Read in three parts: a narrator for Micah's words, a small group for the voices of his opponents (“Do not preach!”, v. 6; “Is the Spirit of the LORD impatient?”, v. 7), and a third reader for the promise in verses 12–13.

Where we are in the story

In chapter 1 Micah exposed Judah's relationship with God: idols and false securities. Now he turns to how they treat their neighbours. In Israel every family's land was an inheritance, a gift from the LORD that guaranteed its freedom and bread (Leviticus 25:23; 1 Kings 21:3). But in the eighth century BC a wealthy elite used debts, bribed courts and sheer power to take the smallholders' fields. Tonight we read the woe over the land-grabbers (vv. 1–5), Micah's clash with the preachers who want him silenced (vv. 6–11), and a sudden promise: the LORD gathers his flock and the Breaker goes before them (vv. 12–13).

A look at the Hebrew

  • chamad – חָמַד “covet”: “They covet fields and seize them” (2:2). Chamad is the word of the Tenth Commandment (Exodus 20:17). What begins as a quiet desire on the bed at night ends at daybreak in gazal – seizing by force.
  • chashav – חָשַׁב “devise”: The oppressors lie on their beds and devise wickedness (2:1). The LORD answers with the same verb: “Look, against this family I am devising evil” (2:3). The schemers run headlong into the one who plans the history of the world.
  • haporets – הַפֹּרֵץ “the one who breaks open”: From parats, to break a gap in a wall. The flock is shut in like sheep in a fold, but “The one who breaks open goes up before them” (2:13), and their king and the LORD lead the way out. The devotional sees its full meaning in Christ, the Good Shepherd (John 10).

Discussion

  1. “Woe to those who devise wickedness and work evil on their beds!” What do your thoughts usually turn over in the quiet hours of the night? Why does Micah start with the bed and not with the deed? (Micah 2:1)
  2. “Because it is in the power of their hand” – the word for power (el) is the same as the word for God, so one can also hear it as: their hand is a god to them. Where do you have power or leverage over others (work, money, home), and how do you use it? (Micah 2:1–2)
  3. For the greedy it is about “fields” and “houses”; God sees “a man and his house, a person and his inheritance”. Where are people today treated as things in the name of profit – and where are we part of it? (Micah 2:2–5)
  4. The opponents say: “One should not preach about such things; disgrace will not overtake us.” Why do we so easily prefer comfortable voices – the preacher of “wine and strong drink” – to the truth that heals? (Micah 2:6–11)
  5. “Do my words not do good to the one who walks uprightly?” How do you react inwardly when a passage of Scripture or a friend puts a finger on a sore spot in your life? (Micah 2:7)
  6. “From her little children you take away my splendour for ever.” How does injustice against families rob the next generation? Who are the vulnerable people around you whose dignity needs protecting? (Micah 2:8–10)
  7. “The one who breaks open goes up before them.” What wall of fear, failure or impossibility are you facing right now – and what would it mean to follow the King who goes ahead of you? (Micah 2:12–13)

For the week ahead

  • Look at one area where the power is in your hand (as an employer, parent, customer or neighbour) and do one concrete thing this week that protects the dignity of someone weaker.
  • Before going to sleep each night, give your thoughts of the day to God in a short prayer – especially any envy or resentment – instead of turning them over in bed.

Pray together

Pray together: ask God to cleanse the secret rooms of our thoughts and to make us content with the inheritance he has given us in Christ. Pray for people in your area who are losing home, land or work. Then thank Jesus, the Breaker, for going ahead of each person into the situation they named.

Notes for the leader

  • Verses 1–5 are about land, but avoid turning the evening into a debate on politics or economics. Keep the focus on the heart (coveting) and on how each of us uses power.
  • The money question can be sensitive: some in the group may be in debt or be landlords, employers or tenants. Speak about attitudes, not about anyone's situation.
  • Verses 6–11 are hard to follow. Explain briefly that “preach” translates nataf, literally “to drip” – the opponents mock Micah's words as dripping on and on (day 4).
  • Scholars read 2:12–13 in different ways (promise, the false prophets' words, or judgement). The devotional reads it as a promise fulfilled in Christ; you don't need to settle the debate in the group.
  • Leave enough time for the last question: after two heavy passages the group needs the hope of verses 12–13.

Scripture Micah 2:1–13

1Woe to those who devise wickednessand work evil on their beds!In the light of morning they carry it out,because it is in the power of their hand.2They covet fields and seize them,and houses, and take them away;they oppress a man and his house,a person and his inheritance.3Therefore thus says the LORD:Look, against this family I am devising evil,from which you cannot remove your necks;and you will not walk with heads held high,for it will be a time of evil.4On that day they will take up a taunt-song against youand wail a bitter lament:"It is done!" one says."We are utterly ruined!He exchanges the portion of my people.How he takes it away from me!To the renegade he divides up our fields."5Therefore you will have no oneto cast the measuring line by lotin the assembly of the LORD.6"Do not preach!" – so they preach."One should not preach about such things;disgrace will not overtake us."7Should this be said, house of Jacob:"Is the Spirit of the LORD impatient?Are these his deeds?"Do my words not do goodto the one who walks uprightly?8But lately my people has risen up as an enemy.From off the garment you strip the cloakfrom those who pass by in trust,like men coming home from war.9The women of my people you drive out,each from the house she delights in;from her little children you take awaymy splendour for ever.10Get up and go!For this is not the resting place;because of uncleanness it brings destruction,a grievous destruction.11If a man came walking in wind and falsehoodand lied: "I will preach to you about wine and strong drink!" –he would be the preacher for this people.12I will surely gather all of you, Jacob;I will surely assemble the remnant of Israel.I will put them together like sheep in a fold,like a flock in the midst of its pasture;they will be noisy with people.13The one who breaks open goes up before them;they break through, pass through the gateand go out by it.Their king passes on before them,and the LORD at their head.
