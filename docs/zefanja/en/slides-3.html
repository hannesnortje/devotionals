He Rejoices over You · Session 3

In Your Midst

Zephaniah 3:1–20

To get started

Think of someone whose face lights up when they see you – a grandparent, a friend, a child, a dog at the door. What does it do to you to be welcomed like that?

Where we are in the story

At the end of chapter 2 Judah would have cheered at Nineveh's downfall. Now Zephaniah pronounces a new “Woe” over a rebellious, defiled, oppressing city without naming it – and only in verses 2 and 4 does it become clear that he means Jerusalem herself (3:1–5). The fire of God's judgment (3:6–8) is not the last word but a furnace that refines: God promises a pure lip for the peoples, a reversal of Babel (3:9–10), and a humble remnant that shelters in his name (3:11–13). The book ends with one of the most tender love songs in Scripture (3:14–20), with 3:17, the verse that gives the series its title.

A look at the Hebrew

מוּסָר

musar · “discipline”

“She has not listened to the voice; she has not accepted discipline.” (3:2). Musar is loving correction – not punishment meant to destroy, but a father's training (compare Hebrews 12:5–11). Verse 2 traces decline in four steps: not listening, not accepting discipline, not trusting, not drawing near. When we stop listening and push away his correction, trust and prayer dry up.

A look at the Hebrew

שָׂפָה בְרוּרָה

safah berurah · “a pure lip”

“For then I will change the lip of the peoples into a pure lip” (3:9). Safah means “lip”, but also “speech, language”. At Babel the whole earth had one safah, and there the LORD confused their safah (Genesis 11:1, 7, 9). Here God reverses Babel: people from every nation, even from beyond the rivers of Cush, will call on his name and serve him “shoulder to shoulder” (shekhem 'echad, literally “with one shoulder”, like oxen under one yoke). The New Testament fulfilment begins at Pentecost (Acts 2).

A look at the Hebrew

יַחֲרִישׁ בְּאַהֲבָתוֹ

yacharish be'ahavato · “he is silent in his love”

The phrase in 3:17 is hard. Literally it says “he is silent (keeps still) in his love”, and that is how we translate it; some read “he quiets you by his love”, and the Septuagint has “he will renew you”. Day 5 pictures a mother who grows still over a crying child in wordless tenderness, and a God who is silent about our former sins and never brings them up again – he has taken away the judgments (3:15).

The big idea

God does what we cannot do for ourselves: he exposes our deafness, refines us in the fire of his judgment, gives us a pure lip and a humble heart – and then, with the judgments taken away, the King in our midst, a warrior who saves, rejoices over us with a song of joy.

Discussion · 1/7

The listeners expected another woe on Nineveh – and then heard: “to her God she has not drawn near.” Why is it so much easier to see the sins of “out there” than our own spiritual deafness? Of the four steps in verse 2 – listening, accepting discipline, trusting, drawing near – where do you feel the most resistance at the moment?

Zephaniah 3:1–2

Discussion · 2/7

Compare verse 5 with verse 7: “Morning by morning he brings his justice to light” – but “they rose early and made all their deeds corrupt.” What does the way you begin your mornings – your first thoughts, first loyalty, first energy – show about who is king in your life?

Zephaniah 3:5–7

Discussion · 3/7

At Babel God confused humanity's “lip”; here he promises to change it “into a pure lip”, so that all “call on the name of the LORD and serve him shoulder to shoulder.” What did the words that crossed your lips this past week say about your heart? Where do you need God to give you a pure lip?

Zephaniah 3:9–10

Discussion · 4/7

“I will leave in your midst a people poor and lowly, and they will shelter in the name of the LORD.” This is not about being downtrodden but about being healed of self-exaltation. Are you building your identity on looking strong and independent – or have you found the freedom of sheltering in his name?

Zephaniah 3:11–13

Discussion · 5/7

Watch the words “in her midst” and “in your midst” through the chapter: roaring lions (3:3), the righteous LORD (3:5), the proud removed (3:11), the poor left (3:12), the King (3:15), the warrior who saves (3:17). Why can a city that was under heavy judgment now sing? Look at the two reasons in verse 15: “The LORD has taken away the judgments against you” and “The King of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst”.

Zephaniah 3:14–15

Discussion · 6/7

Many believers picture God with a frown – loving them because he has to, but always a little disappointed. Verse 17 says: “He beams with gladness over you; he is silent in his love; he rejoices over you with a song of joy.” Which of these phrases is hardest for you to believe about yourself? What keeps you from accepting his joy over you?

Zephaniah 3:16–17

Discussion · 7/7

God gathers those who grieve far from the feast, saves “the lame” and assembles “the outcast”, and makes them “a praise and a name” where they were put to shame (3:18–19). Looking back over the whole book – from “I will sweep away, sweep away everything” (1:2) to “he rejoices over you” (3:17) – what has God shown you in this series? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

Zephaniah 3:18–20

For the week ahead

  • Each morning this week, before you check your phone, read 3:17 aloud with your own name in it, and give God your first thought of the day (3:5).
  • Think of someone who is “limping” or has fallen behind – far from the church's feasts, discouraged or ashamed. Contact them this week with a message, a visit or an invitation, as a small sign of the God who gathers the lame and the outcast.

Pray together

Begin with thanks for the whole journey from the dark day of chapter 1 to the song of chapter 3. Let a moment of silence give room to lay down pride and old shame at the foot of the cross. Then let anyone who wishes name, in a sentence, where their hands are hanging limp (3:16), and let the group pray for one another. Close by reading 3:17 aloud together over one another as a blessing.
