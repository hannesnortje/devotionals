He Rejoices over You · Session 2

Seek the LORD

Zephaniah 2:1–15

To get started

As a child, where was your favourite hiding place – and who were you hiding from?

Where we are in the story

After the storm clouds of chapter 1, Zephaniah changes his tone from accusation to an urgent plea: “Seek the LORD” (2:1–3). Then he walks a full circle of the compass around Judah: west to the Philistine coastal cities (2:4–7), east to Moab and Ammon, Israel's relatives through Lot (2:8–11), far south to Cush, the land along the Nile beyond Egypt (2:12), and north to Assyria and its capital Nineveh (2:13–15), which fell in 612 BC and was never rebuilt. The message: the LORD is King over the whole world, and no corner of the earth lies outside his jurisdiction.

A look at the Hebrew

הִתְקוֹשְׁשׁוּ וָקוֹשּׁוּ

hitqosheshu waqoshu · “gather yourselves together, yes, gather”

The verb qoshesh is used elsewhere for gathering stubble or straw (Exodus 5:7, 12) – literally something like “gather yourselves up like straw”, an idiom for “Pull yourselves together! Come to your senses!” Straight after it comes “like chaff the day passes by” (2:2): the opportunity for grace blows past as fast as chaff in the wind.

A look at the Hebrew

תִּסָּתְרוּ

tissateru · “you will be hidden”

“perhaps you will be hidden on the day of the anger of the LORD” (2:3). The verb is satar, “to hide” – a different root from tsafan, from which Zephaniah's name comes (“The LORD has hidden”), but very close in meaning: a quiet play on the prophet's name. The “perhaps” does not make God fickle; it shows how serious the moment is.

A look at the Hebrew

אֲנִי וְאַפְסִי עוֹד

ani we'afsi 'od · “I am, and there is none besides me”

Nineveh's motto (2:15) echoes the words with which God describes himself in Isaiah (compare Isaiah 45:5–6: “I am the LORD, and there is no other”); Isaiah 47:8, 10 puts the same words in the mouth of Babylon. Nineveh speaks like one who takes God's place – and ends as a lair for wild beasts.

The big idea

God's warnings are a gracious invitation: he calls a numb people to seek him, righteousness and humility while there is time, and he shows that every proud power that says “I am, and there is none besides me” will fall – so our security lies not in high walls but in whom we are hidden.

Discussion · 1/7

Zephaniah calls Judah a “nation without longing”. The word can mean a nation that no longer longs for God, or one that no longer turns pale at its own sin. Where do we see in our culture – and in ourselves – a conscience that has grown numb (“everyone does it”)?

Zephaniah 2:1–2

Discussion · 2/7

“Seek the LORD, all you humble of the land”. Zephaniah tells people who are already humble to “seek humility”. Why is humility a daily choice rather than a certificate on the wall? Which of the three – seeking the LORD, righteousness or humility – is most neglected in your routine at the moment?

Zephaniah 2:3

Discussion · 3/7

“perhaps you will be hidden on the day of the anger of the LORD.” How do you hear that “perhaps”? What does it mean for the believer that “your life is hidden with Christ in God” (Colossians 3:3)?

Zephaniah 2:3

Discussion · 4/7

The rich harbour cities of Philistia looked unshakeable, yet they become pastures for sheep, and “For the LORD their God will care for them and turn back their captivity.” The verb for “care for” (paqad) is the same one used for “punish” in chapter 1. Which “fortresses” in your life look too big to overcome – and how does it change things to know that the Lord cares for you?

Zephaniah 2:4–7

Discussion · 5/7

“I have heard the reproach of Moab and the revilings of the Ammonites”. God takes the mockery of his people personally and swears by his own life (2:9). Where have you been belittled or treated unfairly? What would it mean to lay down the burden of revenge and leave your case in his hands?

Zephaniah 2:8–10

Discussion · 6/7

In the middle of the judgment oracles: “and they will bow down to him, each from its place, all the coastlands of the nations.” Long before the New Testament, Zephaniah sees worship spreading to the most distant nations in their own lands (compare John 4:21–24). How does that widen our view of what God is doing in the world?

Zephaniah 2:11

Discussion · 7/7

Nineveh said in her heart: “I am, and there is none besides me.” We would never say that in church, but we can live it when our finances, health and career go well. Where might you be living with a quiet “Nineveh attitude” – “I've got this under control; I don't need anyone”?

Zephaniah 2:13–15

For the week ahead

  • Choose one of the three commands of 2:3 – seek the LORD, seek righteousness, seek humility – and take one concrete step each day this week (for example: ten minutes with God before the phone, putting right a small dishonesty, letting someone else be right).
  • Where you have been treated unfairly, write the words “I have heard” (2:8) on a card and hand the matter to God in prayer each time it comes back to mind. Where you recognise a “Nineveh attitude”, ask someone for help with something this week.

Pray together

Thank God that his warnings come from a heart of rescue. Let each one pray a sentence of confession for a numb or proud heart, and then pray together the three commands of 2:3, asking God to make you a people who seek him, righteousness and humility. Close by praying for those in the group who feel mocked or overwhelmed, and thank the Lord that in Christ they are hidden and cared for.
