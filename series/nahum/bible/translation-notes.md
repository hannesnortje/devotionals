# Nahum – translation notes (for review)

Base text: Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible); where the WLC has a Ketiv/Qere, the Qere is translated (as in Micah):
- 1:3 Ketiv *wegadol*, Qere *ugdol* "and great (in power)" – same sense.
- 2:1 Ketiv *la'avor*, Qere *la'avar* "to pass through" – same sense.
- 2:6 Ketiv *bahalikhotam* (pl.), Qere *bahalikhatam* (sg.) "in their going".
- 3:3 Ketiv *yikkashlu* "they will stumble", Qere *wekhashlu* "and they stumble".

Versification: Hebrew in all three languages. Hebrew 2:1 = English 1:15; Hebrew 2:2–14 = English 2:1–13. The EN file marks this at 2:1 and 2:2; every file states it in the `versification` field.
Aim: close to the Hebrew, natural to read; the book's key words kept the same every time, and the same as in Micah and Jonah where the Hebrew word is the same.
Layout: 1:1 (superscription) is prose; everything from 1:2 to 3:19 is poetry (`> ` lines), split into blocks at the breaks (1:2–8 hymn; 1:9–11; 1:12–14; 2:1; 2:2–11; 2:12–14; 3:1–7; 3:8–13; 3:14–17; 3:18–19).

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **1:7 *ma'oz beyom tsarah* – and the series title.** Our text: DE „Gut ist der HERR, eine Zuflucht am Tag der Not; er kennt die, die sich bei ihm bergen.“ EN "The LORD is good, a refuge in the day of distress; he knows those who shelter in him." AF "Die HERE is goed, 'n toevlug op die dag van nood; Hy ken dié wat by Hom skuiling soek."
   - *ma'oz* means a strong place, a fortress (root *'azaz* "be strong"), but was early linked with *'uz* "take refuge"; "refuge / Zuflucht / toevlug" fits both and is the title word. Alternatives: "stronghold / Bollwerk, Burg / vesting, sterkte". Keeping "refuge" in 3:11 ("you too will seek a refuge from the enemy") gives the book's sharpest contrast: the LORD *is* the refuge (1:7), Nineveh will look for one and find none (3:11). Because *ma'oz* = "refuge", *chasah* is "shelter in / sich bergen bei / skuiling soek by" (not "take refuge", to avoid using the word twice).
   - *tsarah*: Jonah 2:3 has "Not / distress / nood". Kept the same here. **The title must adjust in EN and AF if you keep that:** DE „Eine Zuflucht am Tag der Not“ fits unchanged; EN "A Refuge in the Day of Distress" (or keep "Trouble" and use "trouble" in 1:7, 9 – then EN differs from Jonah); AF "'n Toevlug op die Dag van Nood" (the current title "in die Dag van Benoudheid" is the 1933/53 wording of this verse). Recommendation: adjust EN and AF titles to "Distress" / "Nood".
   - AF "op die dag" (modern) rather than "in die dag".
2. **1:2 *el qanno' wenoqem*.** Our text: "A jealous God, one who takes vengeance, is the LORD" / „Ein eifersüchtiger Gott, der Vergeltung übt, ist der HERR“ / "'n Jaloerse God wat wraak neem, is die HERE". Hebrew word order kept (the verse opens with *El* – the first letter of the acrostic). *noqem* three times, kept the same each time.
   - *qanno'*: EN "jealous" (chosen) or "zealous"; DE „eifersüchtig“ (chosen; Luther has „eifernd“), „eifernd“, „leidenschaftlich“; AF "jaloers" (chosen), "ywerig" (sounds like "diligent" today), "een wat sy eer beskerm" (1983, paraphrase). The word means God's exclusive, passionate claim on what is his (Exod 20:5; 34:14), not envy – the devotional can explain.
   - *noqem*: EN "takes vengeance" (chosen, = Micah 5:14 "execute vengeance") or "avenging / avenges"; DE „übt Vergeltung“ (chosen, = Micah 5:14 „übe ich Vergeltung“; avoids Luther's „Rächer“) or „rächend / rächt“; AF "neem wraak" (chosen) or "wrekend / wreker" (1933/53).
   - *ba'al chemah* literally "lord/master of wrath" → "full of wrath / voller Grimm / vol grimmigheid". Literal alternative "a master of wrath / ein Herr des Grimms" (in DE and AF "Herr/Heer" could be confused with the divine name).
   - *wenoter hu le'oyvav* "and he keeps (it) for his enemies" (wrath implied): "holds it against / trägt es nach / dra dit na".
3. **1:2–8 – partial alphabetic acrostic (aleph to kaph).** Line openings: א *El* (2), ב *besufah* (3b), ג *go'er* (4), [ד missing – 4b repeats *umlal*; many read *dalelu* "they languish"], ה *harim* (5), ו *wattissa'* (5b), ז *za'mo* (6), ח *chamato* (6b), ט *tov* (7), י *weyodea'* (7b), [כ *kalah* (8b)]. Not imitated in the translation; the line breaks follow the acrostic lines, so the devotional (day 1) can point to it. MT 1:4 *umlal* kept (no emendation).
4. **1:3 *erekh appayim … wenaqqeh lo yenaqqeh*.** "slow to anger / langsam zum Zorn / lankmoedig" exactly as Jonah 4:2 (and Exod 34:6). *Naqqeh lo yenaqqeh* (Exod 34:7) literally "acquit he will not acquit": "will surely not acquit the guilty / spricht gewiss nicht frei, wer schuldig ist / spreek die skuldige sekerlik nie vry nie". Alternative: "will not leave unpunished / lässt nicht ungestraft / laat nie ongestraf nie". Note: the second YHWH of 1:3 belongs to the first half (the ב line begins with *besufah*).
5. **1:10 – very obscure.** *Ki 'ad sirim sevukhim ukhesov'am sevu'im ukkelu keqash yavesh male'* – sound play (*sirim / sevukhim / sov'am / sevu'im*). Literal: "For even if they are like tangled thorns and drunk as with their drink, they are devoured like dry stubble, completely." *'ad* is read as "even if"; *male'* "full(y)" taken as "completely" (or "fully dry"). Many commentators call the text corrupt; options are all guesses.
6. **1:11–2:1 – who is addressed?** 1:11 fem. "from you" = Nineveh; 1:12–13 fem. = Judah ("I have afflicted you"); 1:14 masc. = the king of Assyria; 2:1 = Judah. Translated without adding names. Alternative: add headings or names in brackets (not recommended in the Bible text; the devotionals for days 1–2 can explain).
7. **1:12 *im shelemim wekhen rabbim wekhen nagozzu we'avar*.** "Though they are at full strength and ever so many, even so they will be shorn and pass away." *Shelemim* "whole, intact" (or "allies"); *nagozzu* "shorn" (like sheep, or "cut down"); *we'avar* is singular ("and he/it passes") – smoothed to "pass away". Some read *'avar* with what follows ("He passes; I have afflicted you…").
8. **1:14 *asim qivrekha ki qallota*.** "I will make your grave, for you weigh nothing / denn du wiegst nichts / want jy weeg niks". *Qalal* "be light, of no account, despised" – the opposite of *kavod* "heavy, glory" which runs through the book (2:10 wealth "heavy", 3:3 "heavy mass", 3:10 "honoured men", 3:15 *hitkabbed* "make yourself heavy/many"). Alternative: "for you are despised / denn du bist verachtet / want jy is veragtelik" (smoother, loses the word play). Also possible: "I will make (the house of your gods) your grave".
9. **2:1 *mevasser … mashmia' shalom*.** Echo of Isa 52:7 (used in Rom 10:15). "one who brings good news, who announces peace / eines Freudenboten, der Frieden ausruft / een wat goeie nuus bring, wat vrede aankondig". *Beliyya'al* "the worthless one" (as 1:11); alternative "Belial" (transliteration, the later name of Satan; 2 Cor 6:15) or DE „der Ruchlose“, AF "die verdorwene".
10. **1:5 *wattissa' ha'arets*.** *Nasa'* (Micah "bear / tragen / dra") is here intransitive: the earth "rises up / hebt sich / rys op" (heaves) before him. Same verb, different sense – kept natural; the link to Micah 7:9, 18 is not forced.
11. **2:2 *'alah mefits*.** "One who scatters / Ein Zerstreuer / 'n Verstrooier" (MT *mefits*, from *puts*). Many read *mappets* "a hammer, shatterer" (Jer 51:20). *Natsor metsurah* etc. are infinitives used as commands (ironic call to Nineveh to defend itself).
12. **2:3 *shav YHWH et ge'on Ya'aqov*.** "the LORD restores the majesty of Jacob" (*shuv* "turn back, restore"). Alternative "turns away the pride of Jacob" (judgment). *Ge'on*: "majesty / Hoheit / majesteit" or "pride / Stolz / trots".
13. **2:4–8 – the battle scene, translated literally:**
    - 2:4 "his warriors" – of the attacker (the "scatterer") or of Nineveh; *be'esh peladot* (only here) "fire of steel" (or, reading *lappidot*, "fire of torches"); *habberoshim hor'alu* "the cypresses are brandished" = spears of cypress wood (Greek: "the horsemen", *parashim*).
    - 2:6 *yizkor addirav* "he remembers (= summons) his nobles"; who? (Nineveh's king, or the attacker). *Hassokhekh* "the covering" – a siege shelter of the attackers or a defensive screen.
    - 2:7 "gates of the rivers" – sluices or city gates by the Tigris/Khosr; *namog* "melts away" (as 1:5 the hills; or "collapses").
    - 2:8 ***wehutsav* (huzzab)** – hophal of *natsav* "it is fixed / determined" (chosen: "And it is fixed: she is stripped, she is carried away"). Options: (a) "it is decreed" (chosen); (b) a name or title, "Huzzab" (KJV: a queen or the city); (c) "the beauty / the lady" (emended); (d) "the (statue of the) goddess is set up / brought out"; (e) reading with 2:7 "the palace melts and is set up (?)". *Gulletah* "she is stripped / uncovered" (or "led into exile", same root as *golah* 3:10); *ho'alatah* "she is carried away (up)". *Menahagot* "moan" (or "lead [her]").
    - 2:9 *mimei hi* "all her days" (literally "from the days of her"); Greek reads "her waters" ("Nineveh is like a pool whose waters run away").
    - 2:11 *buqah umevuqah umevullaqah* – sound play "Empty! Emptied! Laid waste!" / „Leere! Entleerung! Verheerung!“ / "Leeg! Uitgeledig! Verwoes!"; *qibbetsu pa'rur* (also Joel 2:6) literally "gather a glow": "grow pale / werden fahl / word bleek" (or "flush").
14. **3:8 No-Amon.** = Thebes in Upper Egypt (city of the god Amun), taken by Assyria in 663 BC. Kept as "No-Amon" in all three. Alternative: "Thebes / Theben / Tebe" (clearer, but not the Hebrew). *Ye'orim* "the Niles" = arms/channels of the Nile; *yam* "sea" = the Nile itself. 3:9 *be'ezratekh* "your helpers" (2nd fem.) – kept MT, although the verse speaks of No-Amon in the 3rd person; Greek has "her helpers".
15. **3:11 *tehi na'alamah*.** "you will be hidden away / wirst verborgen sein / sal weggesteek wees"; or "you will faint / be in a stupor" (cf. drunkenness), or "be forgotten".
16. **3:15–17 – the locusts.** *Yeleq* "hopper" (the young, wingless locust; AF "voetganger" is the exact word); *arbeh* "locust"; *gov govai* "a swarm of locusts". 3:15 *tokhlekh kayyeleq* "it (the sword) will devour you like the hopper (devours)" – or "as one devours hoppers". *Hitkabbed / hitkabbedi* (masc./fem.) "make yourself many" (literally "heavy", see 8). 3:16 *pashat* "sheds its skin" (or "raids / spreads out").
17. **3:17–18 – obscure words.** *Minnezarayikh* (only here) "your guards" (or "princes", from *nezer* "crown"); *tafsarayikh* "your officials" (Akkadian *tupsarru* "scribe"; also Jer 51:27); *gederot* "walls" (stone fences). "And its place is not known – where are they?" (*ayyam*, last word, may belong to 3:18). 3:18 *yishkenu* "lie down to rest" (many read *yashenu* "sleep" as a parallel to "slumber"); *nafoshu* "are scattered" (from *push*; or *nafotsu*). "Shepherds" = the rulers – kept with the shepherd root as in Micah.
18. **3:19 *ein kehah leshivrekha*.** "There is no easing for your fracture" (*kehah* only here, "dimming, relief"); *nachlah makkatekha* "your wound is grievous / schwer / ernstig" (or "incurable"). The book ends with a question, like Jonah (4:11) – the devotional may compare.
19. **1:1 *massa'*.** "oracle / Ausspruch / godspraak" (chosen). Alternative: "burden / Last / las" (literal; KJV, Luther). *Chazon* "vision / Vision / visioen".
20. **AF names.** Ninevé as in Jona; Elkosiet, Kus, Libiërs, Assirië in common Afrikaans spelling. Please check.

## 4. Style

- Speech of people inside the poetry uses quotation marks: „…“ (DE), "…" (EN, AF) – 2:9 ("Stop! Stop!"), 3:7. God's speech is not put in quotation marks.
- German uses "du" for the single addressee (Nineveh, Judah, the king of Assyria), "ihr" for plural (1:9; 2:10 "Raubt").
- The many short commands (2:2; 3:14) and exclamations without verbs (2:11; 3:2–3) are kept short, as in Hebrew; no verbs are added.
- Dashes mark the formula "– declares the LORD of hosts –" (EN, DE) as in Micah; AF uses commas ("sê die HERE van die leërskare").
- The EN verse-number notes are in italics at the end of 2:1 and 2:2, as Micah 4:14 and 5:1.
