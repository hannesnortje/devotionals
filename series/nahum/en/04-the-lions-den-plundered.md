## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

(A note on verse numbers: today's passage is 2:9–14 in the Hebrew text, which we follow; English Bibles number it 2:8–13. In this chapter, subtract one from our verse number to find it in an English Bible.)

Yesterday, in the first half of chapter 2, we saw the invading armies storm into the city, the gates of the rivers opened and the palace melting away (2:7; English 2:6). Now, in verses 9 to 14 (English 8–13), Nahum takes us deeper into the falling city to watch the total collapse of its economy, its army and its national pride. The passage reaches a dramatic climax in one of the most powerful images in the Old Testament: the **lions' den** – *"the lions' den"* – that God himself silences.

For centuries the Assyrian kings used the lion as their chief royal symbol. Excavations at Nineveh (especially in the palaces of Sennacherib and Ashurbanipal) have uncovered many large stone reliefs showing Assyrian kings hunting and overpowering lions, and identifying themselves with the strength and ruthlessness of a predator. In their royal titles Assyrian kings compared themselves to lions – Ashurnasirpal II, for example, lists "lion" among his titles of self-praise. Nahum seizes this very national symbol and turns it against them with shocking poetic justice.

**The burst dam and the endless plunder (vv. 9–10; English 8–9):** Nahum compares Nineveh to *"a pool of water"* – a great reservoir. Through decades of conquest, tribute and trade, the wealth and people of the ancient world had flowed into Nineveh and been dammed up there. But now that the walls are broken, the reservoir is draining! Its inhabitants and mercenaries pour out of the city in blind panic like water through a burst dam wall. The commanders shout in desperation: *"Stop! Stop!"* (Hold your positions!) – *"but no one turns back."* In verse 10 (English 9) the attackers (the Medes and Babylonians) are urged to plunder the treasuries. For more than a century Assyria had robbed and hoarded the silver and gold of other nations – including the treasures of the temple and palace in Jerusalem (2 Kings 18:14–16). Now its own bottomless treasuries are carried off in a single day.

**The question history has answered (v. 12; English 11):** When Nineveh fell in 612 BC, the destruction was so complete that the city never again became a capital; apart from small later settlements on the site, it decayed into mounds of ruins. When the Greek general Xenophon marched past the ruins of Nineveh with his army in 401 BC (a little over 200 years later), he apparently did not even recognise them as the mighty capital of Assyria – he does not even give its name! The question *"Where now is the lions' den"* has literally been answered by history.

### Insights from the Hebrew

- **"Like a pool of water" (*kivrekhat mayim* – כִבְרֵכַת מַיִם):** A *berekhah* is a large reservoir – a picture of the wealth and people that had pooled in Nineveh for centuries and are now draining away.
- **"Stop! Stop!" (*'imdu 'amodu* – עִמְדוּ עֲמֹדוּ):** Two commands in quick succession, like the cry of a commander trying to halt his fleeing troops.
- **The poetry of total devastation (*buqah umevuqah umevullaqah* – בּוּקָה וּמְבוּקָה וּמְבֻלָּקָה):** Verse 11 (English 10) contains one of the most brilliant sound plays (alliteration and assonance) in the whole Bible. Our translation tries to imitate it: *"Empty! Emptied! Laid waste!"*
  - The syllables build (from two, to three, to four), and the hard sounds in Hebrew sound like water glugging out of a bottle, or the rumble of walls collapsing. It paints a city completely shaken out, stripped and laid waste.
  - Look at what fear does physically to the once proud warriors: *"The heart melts, the knees knock, trembling is in all loins, and all their faces grow pale."* Their hearts melt (despair), their knees knock together, their loins (*motnayim* – מָתְנַיִם, the seat of physical strength in Hebrew thought) convulse with spasms of anguish, and their faces grow pale (or flush with mortal terror). The terrorists of the ancient world are now overwhelmed by terror themselves.
- **Four words for lions (vv. 12–13; English 11–12):** In just two verses Nahum uses four different Hebrew words for lions: *aryeh* (אַרְיֵה) for the full-grown male lion, *kefirim* (כְּפִרִים) for the strong young hunting lions, *lavi* (לָבִיא) for the lioness, and *gur* (גּוּר) for the lion's cub.
- **"The LORD of hosts" (*YHWH Tseva'ot* – יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת):** The LORD who commands all the powers of heaven and earth.
- **"Your messengers" (*mal'akhekhe* – מַלְאָכֵכֵה):** The envoys of the Assyrian king – the very voices that had spread fear throughout the ancient world (see below).

### Core Theological Truths

1. **The taunt over the lions' den (vv. 12–13; English 11–12).** With cutting irony Nahum asks in verse 12: *"Where now is the lions' den"*? The image describes the Assyrian state down to the finest detail: its kings and generals hunted the earth like predators, "strangled" and "tore" nations, and brought the prey back to their "den" in Nineveh to feed their wives, princes and citizens in luxury – *"and no one made them afraid."* But the God of Israel puts an end to the predator no one could stop.
2. **The most terrifying words in Scripture (v. 14; English 13).** The chapter ends with a divine indictment that should stop anyone in their tracks: **"Look, I am against you – declares the LORD of hosts."** Up to this point in chapter 2 Nahum has described the fall of the city; now God himself takes the floor in the first person (*"I"*). The mighty lion of Assyria thought he stood above the law, but now he stands before the Creator of the universe – the *LORD of hosts*, who commands all the powers of heaven and earth.
3. **The fourfold sentence God carries out in person:**
   - **The war machine is destroyed:** *"I will burn her chariots in smoke."* Their military technology goes up in flames.
   - **The next generation of leaders falls:** *"The sword will devour your young lions"* – the elite soldiers and princes.
   - **The exploitation ends:** *"I will cut off your prey from the earth."* Their economic imperialism and plundering are stopped for good.
   - **The arrogant voice is silenced:** *"The voice of your messengers will be heard no more."* For generations Assyrian envoys (like the notorious Rabshakeh before the walls of Jerusalem in 2 Kings 18) travelled from city to city reading out threatening letters, demanding tribute and mocking the living God. God says: those intimidating voices will never be heard again!

## Living It Out

Nahum 2:9–14 (English 2:8–13) confronts us with the great contrast between the kingdoms of this world (the "lions' den" mentality) and the kingdom of God. It holds deep lessons for the attitude of our hearts and for our faith today:

**Guard against the "lions' den" mentality of pride and greed.** Nineveh's whole existence was built on the principle of *grab, hoard and dominate at the expense of others* (vv. 10, 13; English 9, 12). We may not be ancient emperors, but we live in a culture that encourages us to build our own "lions' dens" – to pile up wealth, status and possessions as our main source of identity, often without caring who gets hurt along the way. But verses 9–11 (English 8–10) show where everything hoarded outside God's will ends: *buqah umevuqah umevullaqah* – *"Empty! Emptied! Laid waste!"* In Luke 12:15–21 Jesus warns us against the fool who fills barns with treasure for himself but is not rich towards God. True wealth is not found in what we "catch" for ourselves, but in what we receive by grace and give away as a blessing to others.

**The awe of "I am against you" – and the wonder of "God is for us".** There is no more frightening place in the universe than to hear, because of pride and an unrepentant heart, the LORD of hosts say: *"Look, I am against you"* (James 4:6: "God sets himself against the proud, but gives grace to the humble"). On the other hand, for the child of God who flees to Christ in humility, the exact opposite is true – the truth of Romans 8:31: **"If God is for us, who is against us?"** When we lay our pride down at the cross, the Judge of Nahum 2:14 (English 2:13) becomes our Protector and Advocate.

**God silences the voice of the accuser.** Look again at the last promise of verse 14 (English 13): *"and the voice of your messengers will be heard no more."* In our spiritual lives the enemy (who in 1 Peter 5:8 is also compared to a roaring lion) often sends his "messengers" into our thoughts – voices of intimidation, fear, condemnation, despair and doubt, which, like the Assyrian envoys, shout at you that you are defeated and that God cannot help you. Nahum reminds us that the Lion of the tribe of Judah (Revelation 5:5) is mightier than the roaring lion of darkness. God has the power to silence every intimidating voice of the enemy in your life for good!

### Questions for Personal Reflection

1. Nineveh was like *"a pool of water"* (v. 9, English 8) and a treasury full of gold and silver (v. 10, English 9), but when the test came, everything drained away in a moment and left emptiness (*buqah*) behind. Are there earthly things in which you are seeking your emotional security right now that could leave you empty in the end?
2. Where in your own heart do you sometimes notice subtle signs of pride or selfishness – the desire to protect your own "lions' den" of comfort and recognition? How do James 4:6 and Nahum 2:14 (English 2:13) call you back today to true humility before the Lord?
3. Which "intimidating messengers" (thoughts of fear, old accusations or threatening circumstances) are loudest in your ears right now? How can you trust the *LORD of hosts* today to silence those voices within you?

## Prayer

*LORD of hosts, almighty King, before whom no earthly power and no proud lion can stand, I come before your throne today in deep humility. Your word in Nahum reminds me afresh how empty and passing the wealth, power and fame of this world are. Everything built up by human pride and greed ends in emptiness and ruin.*

*Father, search my heart today. Keep me from trying to build my own kingdom or "lions' den" at the expense of others or apart from you. Wherever even a trace of pride, selfishness or trust in earthly treasure has crept into my life, I confess it to you. I never want to stand where you must oppose me; I choose to humble myself under your mighty hand, knowing that through Jesus Christ you are for me and not against me.*

*Lord, thank you for the powerful promise in verse 14 (English 13) that you silence the voice of the enemy's messengers. Every voice of fear, intimidation, discouragement and accusation that shouts at my mind today I bring under the authority of the Lion of the tribe of Judah. Speak your peace over my thoughts, strengthen my knees when they want to knock, and fill my heart with unshakeable courage in your presence. In the name of Jesus Christ, my King and Victor. Amen.*
