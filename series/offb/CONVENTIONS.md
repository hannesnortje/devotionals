## Revelation specifics
- Days 1–9 come from Hannes's Gemini docs (process as usual). Days 10–31 have no Drive doc: they are written by Claude following `WRITING-BRIEF.md` in the repo root, Afrikaans first, then DE/EN translated from the AF.
- Interpretive position (Hannes, 6 Nov, refined the same day): **for everyone, always.** Revelation was written to the seven churches in Asia; what it meant for them (Rome, emperor worship, persecution, pressure to compromise, wealth and complacency), it means for the church in every age. Each day therefore first explains what the vision meant for the first readers, then draws the same message for us today. No timeline, no decoding of symbols into later or modern events. The visions are not consecutive stages but different angles on the same reality between Christ's first and second coming. Still future: Christ's return, the last judgement and the new creation (19:11–22:21). The thousand years (20:1–6) are symbolic: Christ reigns now and his people with him; Satan is bound so that he cannot stop the gospel among the nations. Babylon, the beasts, 666 = the powers that opposed God in John's day (Rome) and oppose him in every age. 144,000 and the great multitude = the whole people of God. Other readings (futurist, dispensational, preterist) in one fair sentence where they matter, without polemics.
- Genre (Hannes, 6 Nov): Revelation is **apocalyptic** – *apokalypsis* (1:1) = unveiling. It pulls back the curtain and shows what is really happening behind the scenes: behind Rome's power the throne in heaven (ch. 4), behind the emperor cult the dragon (12–13), behind the churches' suffering the Lamb who has already conquered (5). The question is never "when?" but "who really reigns, and whose side am I on?". Main picture: "agter die skerms / hinter den Kulissen / behind the curtain". The "conspiracy theory" comparison is used at most **once, as a contrast** (day 10, only if it reads naturally): it may look like a conspiracy story – a hidden power struggle, the official version ("Caesar is lord") not the true one – but it is the opposite: God openly unveils the truth, and it leads to worship, endurance and faithful witness, not to fear and suspicion.
- "The Old Testament in the New Testament" (Hannes, 6 Nov): Revelation has hardly any formal quotation but is woven from Old Testament images and phrases (Exodus, Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah, Zechariah, the Psalms). Every day shows where its images come from in the OT and what they meant there (e.g. the plagues of Egypt behind the trumpets and bowls, Daniel 7 behind the beasts, Ezekiel 1 and Isaiah 6 behind the throne room, Ezekiel 40–48 and Isaiah 65 behind the new Jerusalem) – this is the main key to the symbols, together with the first-century setting. Numbers: say "hundreds of allusions" / "about two thirds of its verses echo the OT" (Swete 1906: 278 of 404 verses; counts range from about 250 to over 600 depending on criteria) – never a precise count as fact; no formal quotation formula ("as it is written"). Revelation is the OT re-read through the Lamb: it takes up the images and shows where they were going (5:5–6 is the hermeneutical key). OT texts quoted from our own Bible files where they exist (jona, micha, nahum, habakuk, zefanja), otherwise own short renderings.
- The whole Bible behind the scenes (Hannes, 6 Nov): Revelation unveils how the whole biblical story works behind the scenes, from Genesis to the new creation. Each day shows where the passage stands in that story (creation – fall – exodus – kingdom – temple – exile/Babylon – Christ – church – new creation). Day 31 closes the circle with Genesis 1–3 (tree of life, river, God dwelling with people, no more curse, 22:1–5).
- The heavenly council (Hannes, 6 Nov): as in Job 1–2 – Satan, "the accuser", before God; what happens on earth is understood from the hidden scene, and Satan can do nothing without God's permission – Revelation opens the curtain for the whole church and history. OT line: Job 1–2, 1 Kings 22:19–22, Isaiah 6, Zechariah 3, Daniel 7. Day 10 (ch. 4) introduces this line; day 18 (ch. 12) shows "the accuser" of Job and Zechariah thrown down (12:10–11, by the blood of the Lamb). Job quoted in our own short rendering from the Hebrew.
- Hannes's four key motifs (6 Nov) – weave them through the days that carry them:
  1. **Hearing, then seeing** – John hears one thing and sees another, and the seeing interprets the hearing: 1:10–12 (voice → lampstands and Son of Man); 5:5–6 (hears the Lion of Judah → sees a Lamb standing as though slain – God's power looks like the cross; key to the book); 7:4 / 7:9 (hears 144,000 of Israel → sees a countless multitude from every nation – Abraham's promise fulfilled); 17:1–3 (prostitute on many waters → woman on a beast in the desert); 21:9–10 (the bride → a city). Introduce in day 10; carry in days 11, 13, 23, 29.
  2. **Johannine dualism – in or out, no neutral ground**: two cities (Babylon / Jerusalem), two women (prostitute / bride), two thrones, two worships, "those who dwell on the earth" vs those who belong to the Lamb; the beast imitates the Lamb (13:3 "as though slain", 13:11 "horns like a lamb"). Link to Laodicea's lukewarmness (3:15–16). Days 19, 23, 24, 29.
  3. **Symmetry of the marks**: God's people sealed on the forehead (7:3), the name of the Lamb and his Father (14:1), "his name on their foreheads" (22:4) – the dragon's people marked on the right hand or forehead (13:16–17). OT: Ezekiel 9:4; many interpreters hear Deuteronomy 6:8 (Shema: hand and forehead) – the mark is the counterfeit of belonging to God: what you think and what you do. No one is unmarked; everyone belongs to someone. Days 13, 19, 20, 30.
  4. **Prostitution with the whore of prosperity, on this old heaven and earth**: Babylon (17–18) as the economic seduction of every age; the merchants' lament and cargo list ending in "bodies and souls of human beings" (18:11–13); Laodicea "I am rich" (3:17) ↔ Babylon "I sit as a queen" (18:7); "Come out of her, my people" (18:4); all belongs to the old heaven and earth that pass away (21:1). Days 23, 24, 28, linking back to day 9.
- **The new exodus** (Hannes's question, 6 Nov): the exodus is one of the great lines of the book – not in the seals (their background is Zech 1:8–11; 6:1–8, Ezek 14:21, Lev 26, Mark 13), but from ch. 7 on: the seal on the forehead before judgement as the Passover blood (7:3; Exod 12:13, 23); the trumpets (8–9) and bowls (16) as the plagues of Egypt, and like Pharaoh "they did not repent" (9:20–21; 16:9, 11); the Lamb as the Passover lamb who ransoms a people and makes them "a kingdom, priests" (5:9–10; 1:6; Exod 19:6); the woman carried on eagle's wings into the wilderness (12:14; Exod 19:4); the song of Moses and of the Lamb by the sea of glass (15:2–4; Exod 15); "Come out of her, my people" (18:4). It is an exodus out of Babylon – out of the system of this age – not out of creation: the goal is the new creation where God dwells with people (21:1–3), as Israel's exodus led into the promised land. Days 13, 14–15, 18, 21–22, 24, 28.
- **How to use all of the above – lenses, not a checklist** (Hannes: "the worldview, not the details per se"; 6 Nov: "good flowing devotionals, not devotionals that say everything but come across clumsy"; his aim is to show that Revelation is devotional for all Christians at all times):
  - Each day has **one central picture** from its own passage and reads like Hannes's approved days (warm, clear, one picture, one concrete step). Flow beats completeness.
  - The movement "first the seven churches, then us" shapes every day naturally – without announcing it.
  - Use **at most one or two** of the threads above per day, and only where the passage itself raises them; the assigned days are suggestions, not obligations. One or two OT links per day, not lists.
  - Never repeat the framing as slogans ("as we have seen, Revelation is the OT in the NT / behind the curtain …"). The big threads are shown at key moments (days 10, 11, 18, 28–31), not stated daily.
  - Pastoral tone for the hard themes: "in or out" = a call to faithfulness ("whose are you?"), never "us against them" or judging others; the marks = belonging, never fear (no chips, barcodes, dates); Babylon = seduction of prosperity *and* violence and idolatry (18:24) – no sermon about money.
  - The conspiracy-theory contrast: at most one sentence in day 10, and only if it reads naturally – otherwise leave it out.
