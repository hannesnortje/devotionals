# Revelation – translation notes (for review)

Base text: **SBL Greek New Testament** (SBLGNT), see §3. The text follows SBLGNT throughout; where SBLGNT differs from the older Bibles in a way readers will notice, this is listed in §2.
Aim: close to the Greek, natural to read, and each key word rendered the same way every time it occurs, in all three languages. Revelation's Greek is rough and simple (long chains of "and", sudden changes of tense, grammar that breaks the rules on purpose, e.g. 1:4 "from *he who is*"). The translation keeps that simplicity and does not polish it into church language. Key words shared with Hebrews, 1 and 2 Timothy use the renderings of `series/hebr`, `series/1tim` and `series/2tim` translation-notes §1 (DECISIONS.md). Divine names: "Herr / Lord / Here" in normal case, since there is no YHWH in the NT, also in OT allusions.
OT allusions and quotations are translated as Revelation's Greek has them, not adjusted to the Hebrew or the Septuagint, but with wording close enough to the OT that the allusion stays recognisable (§1a, Hannes: "Revelation is the Old Testament in the New Testament"). Hymns and songs (1:5b–6; 4:8; 4:11; 5:9–10; 5:12; 5:13; 7:10; 7:12; 7:15–17; 11:15, 17–18; 12:10–12; 15:3–4; 16:5–6, 7; 18:2–3, 4–8, 10, 14, 16–17a, 19–20, 21–24; 19:1–2, 3, 4, 5, 6–8; 21:3–4) and OT poetry (1:7; 2:27 Ps 2:9) are set as `> ` lines, like the poetic quotations in Hebrews. Where a verse starts in a hymn and goes on in prose (18:17b), the prose continues without a new verse marker.
Each church's angel is addressed in the singular: *du / you / jy*. Where Christ turns to the church members in the plural (2:10b, 13, 23–25), the text switches to *ihr / you / julle*, as the Greek does.
The translation was done in **three parts** – part 1 = chapters 1–7, part 2 = 8–14, part 3 = 15–22 – and **merged** on 2026-11-06: `offb-{de,en,af}.md` now hold the whole book. At the merge the three sets of notes were combined into this file (one key-word table in §1, one OT table in §1a, one list in §2 whose items keep their part as a prefix: 1.x = ch. 1–7, 2.x = ch. 8–14, 3.x = ch. 15–22), and every recurring word or phrase was made identical across the parts (§1b).
Verse count checked against the base text (script, SBLGNT `source/Rev-source.txt`, verse numbers in sequence): chapter 1 = 20, 2 = 29, 3 = 22, 4 = 11, 5 = 14, 6 = 17, 7 = 17, 8 = 13, 9 = 21, 10 = 11, 11 = 19, 12 = 18, 13 = 18, 14 = 20, 15 = 8, 16 = 21, 17 = 18, 18 = 24, 19 = 21, 20 = 15, 21 = 27, 22 = 21 – **22 chapters, 405 verses** in all three languages. 12:18 is a verse of its own, as in SBLGNT (§2 2.2).

## 1a. OT background

Hannes: "Revelation is the Old Testament in the New Testament." Revelation hardly ever quotes the OT with a formula, but it alludes to it in almost every verse. **Rule for all three parts:** when an expression clearly alludes to an OT text, keep the wording close enough to that text that the allusion stays recognisable. The translation still follows Revelation's Greek (see the introduction), but where the Greek allows a choice, choose the OT wording. Where the OT passage is in our own Bible files (`series/{jona,micha,nahum,habakuk,zefanja}/bible`), use the same key words as there; those are marked **own**. Other OT books have no own translation yet. There, use the plain wording of the Hebrew (or the Septuagint, where Revelation follows it), and keep it consistent within Revelation.

## 1b. Aligned across the three parts (merge, 2026-11-06)

All recurring words and phrases were compared across chapters 1–22 in all three languages: the phrases asked for in the merge brief, and a script over the SBLGNT text that lists every Greek word sequence of four or more words that occurs in more than one verse (about 270 groups), with each group's verses read side by side in DE, EN and AF. Where the Greek is the same, the rendering is now the same. Changes made:

1. 14:8 *porneia* (Babylon) "Unzucht / sexual immorality / ontug" → "Hurerei / harlotry / hoerery", = 18:3 (same phrase "the wine of the fury of her …"), 17:2, 4; 18:9; 19:2 (part 3's Nahum family, §2 3.1).
2. 14:10 DE *potērion* "Kelch" → "Becher", = 16:19; 17:4; 18:6.
3. 14:10, 19 EN "God's fury" → "the fury of God", = 15:1, 7; 16:1 (and DE/AF "des Grimms Gottes / van die woede van God").
4. 19:17 AF *mesouranēma* "in die middel van die hemel" → "hoog in die hemel", = 8:13; 14:6.
5. 9:14 Euphrates: DE "am großen Fluss Euphrat" → "am großen Strom, dem Euphrat,"; EN "the great river Euphrates" → "the great river, the Euphrates", = 16:12 (AF was already identical).
6. DE *potamos* "Fluss / Flüsse" → "Strom / Ströme" in 8:10; 12:15, 16, = 9:14; 16:4, 12; 22:1, 2.
7. 13:8 DE "von Grundlegung der Welt an" → "seit Grundlegung der Welt", = 17:8 (EN/AF were identical).
8. 17:8 EN "whose names have not been written" → "whose name has not been written", = 13:8 (Greek singular in both).
9. 21:27 EN "the Lamb's book of life" → "the book of life of the Lamb", = 13:8 ("book of life" now identical in 3:5; 13:8; 17:8; 20:12, 15; 21:27).
10. 15:1 DE *sēmeion* "ein anderes Zeichen am Himmel" → "im Himmel", = 12:1, 3.
11. 14:1 DE "auf ihre Stirnen geschrieben" → "an ihren Stirnen geschrieben", = 7:3; 22:4.
12. EN "the Lord God the Almighty" → "the Lord God, the Almighty" in 15:3; 16:7; 19:6; 21:22, and "God the Almighty" → "God, the Almighty" in 16:14; 19:15, = 1:8; 4:8; 11:17.
13. AF vocative "Here God, Almagtige" → "Here God, die Almagtige" in 15:3; 16:7, = 11:17.
14. 15:7 AF "wat lewe tot in alle ewigheid" → "wat tot in alle ewigheid lewe", = 4:9, 10; 10:6.
15. 19:15 AF "met 'n ysterstaf soos 'n herder oppas" → "soos 'n herder met 'n ysterstaf oppas", = 2:27; 12:5.
16. 19:11 AF "en kyk, 'n wit perd" → "en daar was 'n wit perd", = 6:2 and the §4 rule for *kai idou* before a vision object (EN already "there was").

## 2. Choices you should check

Numbered by part: 1.x = chapters 1–7, 2.x = chapters 8–14, 3.x = chapters 15–22. References "§2 1.4" etc. point here.

### Chapters 1–7

1.1 ***Nikaō* (2:7–3:21; 5:5; 6:2): "siegen / conquer / oorwin".** The verb occurs 17 times, for the believers, for Christ (3:21; 5:5) and for the beast that "conquers" the saints (11:7; 13:7). It needs one rendering that works with and without an object.
   - (a) **siegen / conquer / oorwin** (my recommendation): DE "Wer siegt, dem …", "hat gesiegt" (5:5), "siegend und um zu siegen" (6:2), later "besiegen" with an object (11:7). It is the military word of the Greek, and it avoids Luther's "Wer überwindet".
   - (b) überwinden / overcome / oorwin (Luther, KJV/NKJV, AF 1933): softer, more inward, and less natural in 5:5 and 6:2 ("hat überwunden, die Buchrolle zu öffnen").
   - (c) EN "be victorious" (NIV). AF "seëvier" would also work.
   The phrasing "Wer siegt, dem werde ich …" also reproduces the Greek's awkward double object (*tō nikōnti dōsō autō*).
1.2 ***Thlipsis* "Bedrängnis / affliction / verdrukking", also 7:14.** This is carried over from Hebrews (10:33). In Revelation it gives 1:9; 2:9, 10, 22 and 7:14 "die große Bedrängnis / the great affliction / die groot verdrukking". The same phrase without the article is used in 2:22 for Jezebel's followers ("große Bedrängnis"). The option is EN "tribulation" throughout ("the great tribulation", ESV/KJV/NIV). That is more familiar, but it is a technical term for many readers, and it would break the link with 1:9 ("your brother who shares with you in the affliction"). I recommend keeping "affliction". (The word does not occur again after 7:14.) In 7:14, *hoi erchomenoi* is present tense: "die … kommen / who are coming / wat … kom", not "who have come".
1.3 **SBLGNT readings that readers will notice.** I followed SBLGNT in each case:
   - 1:5 *lysanti* "befreit / freed / losgemaak". The Textus Receptus (TR; Luther, KJV, AF 1933) has *lousanti* "gewaschen / washed / gewas".
   - 1:6 and 5:10 *basileian* "ein Königreich (, Priester) / a kingdom (, priests) / 'n koninkryk (, priesters)". TR has "kings and priests".
   - 5:9 no "us" after "purchased". I supplied "Menschen / people / mense" (the Greek has no object), and 5:10 continues with "them". TR has "redeemed *us* … made *us* … *we* shall reign".
   - 5:10 *basileuousin* "sie herrschen / they reign / hulle regeer" is present. Many manuscripts have the future "they will reign".
   - 6:1, 3, 5, 7 *Erchou* "Komm! / Come! / Kom!". TR has "Come and see". Without "and see", the call is probably addressed to the riders, not to John.
   - 6:17 "ihres Zorns / their wrath / hulle toorn" (God and the Lamb). TR has "his".
   - 1:8 and 1:11: TR adds "the beginning and the end" (1:8) and "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last" (1:11). 4:11 SBLGNT "unser Herr und Gott / our Lord and God" (TR "O Lord"). 5:14 TR adds "twenty-four" and "him who lives for ever and ever".
   For ch. 8–14 see §2 2.2, for ch. 15–22 see 3.3.
1.4 ***Biblion*: "Buchrolle / scroll / boekrol" or "Buch / book / boek".** The same Greek word is used for the sealed scroll (5:1–9), the sky "rolled up like a scroll" (6:14), the little scroll that is eaten (10), and "this book" (1:11; 22:7–19). I used **scroll** wherever the object is handled as a scroll (sealed, opened, rolled, eaten: 5; 6:14; 10) and **book** in 1:11, 20:12 and ch. 22, where the whole writing is meant. "The book of life" (3:5; 13:8; 20:12, 15; 21:27) stays "book" whichever Greek word is used. The alternative is one word everywhere: DE "Buchrolle" works in every place, EN "scroll" mostly does (22:7 "the words of the prophecy of this scroll"). Part 3 used "book" throughout ch. 22 (22:7, 9, 10, 18, 19), as recommended here; the choice is still open for your review.
1.5 **3:10 *tērēsō ek tēs hōras tou peirasmou*.** Translated "werde ich dich bewahren vor der Stunde der Prüfung / I will keep you from the hour of testing / sal Ek jou bewaar uit die uur van beproewing". *Ek* means "out of". Readings:
   - (a) kept *from* the hour, i.e. spared it (often used for a rapture before the tribulation)
   - (b) kept safe *through* it, brought out of it (cf. John 17:15, the same *tērein ek*)
   DE "vor" and EN "from" lean slightly toward (a). AF "uit" keeps the Greek preposition. A neutral alternative is DE "bewahren aus", EN "keep you out of", which is less natural. The play on words "you kept my word – I will keep you" is kept in all three languages. *Ton logon tēs hypomonēs mou* is literal: "das Wort von meiner Ausdauer / the word of my endurance / die woord van my volharding" (my call to endure, or the word about Christ's own endurance).
1.6 ***Porneia* (2:14, 20–21; 9:21; 14–19): "Unzucht / sexual immorality / ontug", *pornē* "Hure / prostitute / hoer".** In 2:14 and 2:20 the sexual sense and the OT image of idolatry as "prostitution" (Num 25; Hos) are both present. "Sexual immorality" keeps the literal sense. "Prostitution" (DE "Hurerei", AF "hoerery") would keep the image that dominates ch. 17–18 ("die große Hure / the great prostitute / die groot hoer"). I recommend the table renderings, and *pornē* = Hure / prostitute / hoer for part 3. The alternative is one word family throughout (DE "Hurerei / Hure", EN "prostitution / prostitute", AF "hoerery / hoer"). **Update from part 3 (see 3.1):** where Babylon is meant (14:8; 17:2, 4; 18:3, 9; 19:2), Nahum's family "Hurerei / harlotry / hoerery" is now used; the literal uses here, in 9:21 and in the vice lists (21:8; 22:15) keep "Unzucht / sexual immorality / ontug".
1.7 **1:4 *apo ho ōn kai ho ēn kai ho erchomenos*.** The Greek breaks grammar on purpose: after "from" it keeps the nominative, as if it were a fixed name of God, and "who was" uses *ēn*, a verb with an article. None of the three languages can show this. I translated it as a normal phrase and noted it here (and 1:5 "the faithful witness" also stays nominative). The devotional can explain it. *Ho erchomenos* is "der kommt / who is coming / wat kom", not "who is to come", so that the link with "Sieh, er kommt / Look, he is coming" in 1:7 stays visible.
1.8 **1:13 *homoion huion anthrōpou*.** No article, as in Dan 7:13: "einer, der aussah wie ein Menschensohn / one like a son of man / iemand soos 'n mensseun". The title "der Menschensohn / the Son of Man / die Seun van die mens" (Gospels, with the article) would read the Gospels into the vision. I recommend lower case and the indefinite article. The same applies in 14:14.
1.9 **1:10 *en pneumati* and "the seven spirits".**
   - 1:10; 4:2: "im Geist / in the Spirit / in die Gees", capitalised: the Holy Spirit as the one who brings the vision (cf. 2:7 "what the Spirit says"). The alternative is "in spirit / in a trance" (lower case), a state of John's own mind.
   - "The seven spirits" (1:4; 3:1; 4:5; 5:6) is literal and lower case. Readings include the sevenfold Holy Spirit (Isa 11:2; Zech 4:2, 10), which fits 1:4, where they stand between the Father and Jesus Christ, or seven angels of the presence. The alternative is "der siebenfältige Geist / the sevenfold Spirit / die sewevoudige Gees", which decides the question. I recommend the literal wording.
1.10 **3:14 *hē archē tēs ktiseōs tou theou*.** "Der Anfang der Schöpfung Gottes / the beginning of God's creation / die begin van die skepping van God". *Archē* can mean
    - (a) origin, source (the one through whom creation began; cf. Col 1:15–18)
    - (b) ruler (NIV "the ruler of God's creation")
    - (c) first created thing (the Arian reading)
    "Beginning" keeps all three open. Your devotional can say which you take.
1.11 **Words for parts 2–3** (used as recommended here throughout ch. 8–22):
    - *salpinx* "Trompete / trumpet / trompet" (already used in 1:10; 4:1). The alternatives are Luther's "Posaune" (a trombone in modern German) and AF 1933 "basuin". I recommend "Trompete / trompet".
    - *thērion* "Tier / beast / dier". The alternative is DE "Bestie", which is more modern and makes the beast distinct from the "wild animals" of 6:8, but sounds like a tabloid. I recommend "Tier".
    - *thymos* "Grimm / fury / woede", kept apart from *orgē* "Zorn / wrath / toorn" (EN departs from Hebrews "anger", see §1).
1.12 **Smaller points in ch. 1–7, translated literally:**
    - 2:23 and 6:8 *thanatos* = "Seuche / pestilence / pes". In 2:23 *apoktenō en thanatō* is literally "kill with death"; in 6:8 "sword, famine, death, wild beasts" follows Ezek 14:21, where the Septuagint uses *thanatos* for "pestilence". The literal alternative is "Tod / death / dood".
    - 2:23 *nephrous kai kardias* "Nieren und Herzen / minds and hearts / niere en harte". EN uses the idiom, because "kidneys" would be misunderstood; DE and AF keep the Greek image.
    - 2:22 *klinē* "Bett / bed / bed", not "Krankenbett / sickbed": the bed of her adultery becomes the bed of her punishment.
    - 2:27 Ps 2:9 *poimanei* "weiden / shepherd / soos 'n herder oppas", not "rule" (NIV/ESV): the same verb as 7:17, where the Lamb shepherds his people. AF has no single verb that is natural in both places; "oppas soos 'n herder" is a compromise. The alternative is "regieren / rule / regeer" in 2:27 only.
    - 2:13 *tēn pistin mou* "den Glauben an mich / your faith in me / die geloof in My" (objective genitive); literally "my faith".
    - 3:16 *emesai* "ausspeien / vomit / uitbraak" is literal.
    - 3:20 *deipnēsō* "Mahl halten / eat / eet" (the evening meal).
    - 5:8 *kithara* "Leier / lyre / lier", not "Harfe / harp / harp".
    - 6:6 *choinix* "Maß / quart / maat" (about one litre, a day's ration); *dēnarion* "Denar / denarius / denarius" (a day's wage).
    - 6:8 *chlōros* "fahlgrün / pale green / vaalgroen", the colour of a corpse.
    - 6:10 *despotēs* "Gebieter / Master / Heerser" (see §1).
    - 6:11 *heōs plērōthōsin* "bis … vollzählig wären / until … were complete in number / totdat … voltallig is"; some manuscripts read "until they had completed (their course)".
    - 7:4 "aus jedem Stamm der Israeliten / of the people of Israel / van die Israeliete" for *huiōn Israēl*, as in 2:14.

### Chapters 8–14

2.1 ***Charagma* (13:16–17; 14:9, 11; 16:2; 19:20; 20:4): DE "Kennzeichen"** (part 3 followed it). §1 proposed "Malzeichen", but that is Luther's (and Elberfelder's) word and sounds archaic. "Zeichen" alone would merge it with *sēmeion* "Zeichen", which in 12:1–3 and 13:13–14 is a different word and needs its own rendering. Options:
   - (a) **Kennzeichen** (my recommendation): modern, plain; the mark identifies who belongs to the beast (the counterpart of the seal on the foreheads, 7:3; 14:1).
   - (b) Malzeichen (Luther): familiar, but old church language. If (a) is chosen, "Malzeichen" could go into `avoid_words`.
   - (c) "Prägung" or "Stempel": closer to the Greek (*charagma* was the word for the imperial stamp on coins and documents), but odd on a hand or forehead.
   EN "mark" and AF "merk" are unproblematic.

2.2 **SBLGNT readings that readers will notice.** I followed SBLGNT in each case:
   - 8:13 "einen Adler / an eagle / 'n arend". TR (KJV, Luther 1912, AF 1933) has "an angel".
   - 9:13 "aus den Hörnern / from the horns / uit die horings" of the altar. Many manuscripts and TR have "the *four* horns".
   - 11:17 "der ist und der war / who is and who was / wat is en wat was" without "and who is coming". TR adds it; the old manuscripts do not have it. From 11:17 on, God is no longer "coming": he has come (also 16:5).
   - 12:8 "er war nicht stark genug / it was not strong enough / hy was nie sterk genoeg nie" (singular). TR has the plural ("they did not prevail").
   - **12:18 "Und er stellte sich auf den Sand des Meeres / And it took its stand on the sand of the sea / En hy het op die sand van die see gaan staan."** SBLGNT reads *estathē* "he (the dragon) stood". TR has *estathēn* "I (John) stood" and joins it to 13:1 (KJV 13:1 "And I stood upon the sand of the sea"). So in the KJV tradition chapter 12 has 17 verses. With SBLGNT, the dragon takes his stand on the shore and calls the beast up out of the sea (13:1).
   - 13:10 "wenn jemand mit dem Schwert getötet werden soll, wird er mit dem Schwert getötet / if anyone is to be killed with the sword, with the sword he will be killed / as iemand met die swaard gedood moet word, word hy met die swaard gedood". This is a call to accept suffering (Jer 15:2), not a warning to persecutors. TR (KJV, AF 1933) has "he that killeth with the sword must be killed with the sword". TR also reads "he that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity" in the first half.
   - 13:17 "das Kennzeichen: den Namen des Tieres oder die Zahl seines Namens / the mark: the name … or the number" (the name and number *are* the mark). TR has "the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number".
   - 13:18 666. Some early manuscripts (P115, C) have 616; SBLGNT reads 666.
   - 14:1 "seinen Namen und den Namen seines Vaters / his name and his Father's name". TR has only "his Father's name".
   - 14:5 ends with "sie sind makellos / they are without blemish". TR adds "before the throne of God".
   Smaller ones nobody will notice (11:12 "they heard", some manuscripts "I heard"; 13:7a, missing in some manuscripts but in SBLGNT) are translated without comment.

2.3 **10:6 *chronos ouketi estai*: "keine Frist mehr / no more delay / geen uitstel meer".** *Chronos* usually means "time". The KJV/Luther 1912 rendering "that there should be time no longer" was often read as "time itself will end". Readings:
   - (a) **no more delay** (my recommendation): the answer to the martyrs' "How long?" (6:10–11, "rest a little longer"); fits 10:7 ("but in the days of the seventh angel … the mystery of God is fulfilled") and Dan 12:7, the oath behind the verse.
   - (b) "es wird keine Zeit mehr sein / there will be no more time / daar sal geen tyd meer wees nie": literal, but suggests the end of time as such.
   In 10:7 *etelesthē* is past tense ("was fulfilled"), the prophetic perfect: in God's plan it is as good as done. I translated "vollendet ist / is fulfilled / voltooi is".

2.4 **13:8 "from the foundation of the world" – kept ambiguous.** The Greek order is "written … in the book of life of the Lamb slaughtered from the foundation of the world". The phrase can go with
   - (a) "slaughtered" (the Lamb slaughtered from the foundation of the world; cf. 1 Pet 1:19–20), or
   - (b) "written" (names not written from the foundation of the world; 17:8 says exactly this, without the Lamb).
   I kept the Greek order so that both readings stay open: "im Buch des Lebens des geschlachteten Lammes seit Grundlegung der Welt / in the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb from the foundation of the world / in die boek van die lewe van die geslagte Lam van die grondlegging van die wêreld af". If you prefer one reading, the sentence can be rearranged. (DE "seit Grundlegung der Welt" aligned with 17:8 at the merge; formerly "von Grundlegung der Welt an".)

2.5 **14:4 *parthenoi* "jungfräulich / virgins / maagde" and "not defiled with women".** Translated literally. Readings:
   - (a) the holy-war image: soldiers of Israel kept apart from women before battle (Deut 23:10–15; 1 Sam 21:5–6). The 144,000 are the Lamb's army (7:4–8), and "defiled" is the language of faithfulness to God, as Israel is "the virgin" and the "firstfruits" (Jer 2:2–3; 2 Cor 11:2). This is the reading that fits your "144,000 = the whole people of God".
   - (b) literal celibacy, which would then exclude married believers and women.
   I recommend the literal wording and explaining (a) in the devotional. "Jungfräulich" (adjective) avoids DE "Jungfrauen" (feminine) for a masculine group.

2.6 **Other choices with options:**
   - 13:18 *arithmos anthrōpou* "die Zahl eines Menschen / the number of a human being / die getal van 'n mens". It can mean (a) the number of a particular man (gematria: Nero Caesar in Hebrew letters = 666, and 616 in a Latin spelling, which would explain the variant), or (b) a human number, i.e. one people can calculate, or the number of humankind without God (six, short of seven). The wording keeps both open; your devotional can name the Nero link as the first-century key without "decoding" it for today.
   - 14:12 *tēn pistin Iēsou* "den Glauben an Jesus / faith in Jesus / die geloof in Jesus" (objective genitive), as 2:13 "den Glauben an mich". The alternative is "die Treue Jesu / the faithfulness of Jesus" (subjective: keeping Jesus' own faithfulness). 12:17 *tēn martyrian Iēsou* "das Zeugnis Jesu / the testimony of Jesus" stays open (as in 1:2, 9).
   - 11:8 *pneumatikōs* "geistlich / spiritually / geestelik". The city is called Sodom and Egypt by the Spirit (prophetically), not literally. The alternative is "im übertragenen Sinn / symbolically / simbolies", which is clearer but loses the link with the Spirit. "Ihr Leichnam / their corpse / hulle lyk" is singular in 11:8–9a, as in the Greek (one dead body on the street, one witness), and plural in 9b.
   - 14:14–16, the one "like a son of man" on the cloud. In 1:13 the same phrase is Christ; here an angel calls to him (14:15), so some take him to be an angel. AF had to decide the pronoun: I used "Hy / Hom" (capitalised), following 1:13. The alternative is lower case "hy", which leaves it open. DE/EN are not affected.
   - 11:3 *dōsō tois dysin martysin mou, kai prophēteusousin*, literally "I will give to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy" (the object is missing). I translated "ich werde meinen zwei Zeugen gewähren, dass sie … prophezeien / I will grant my two witnesses that they prophesy / Ek sal aan my twee getuies gee dat hulle … profeteer". The alternative is to supply "Vollmacht / authority / gesag" (as 11:6).
   - 11:15 *egeneto hē basileia tou kosmou tou kyriou hēmōn kai tou christou autou* "Das Königreich der Welt ist unserem Herrn und seinem Christus zuteilgeworden / The kingdom of the world has become our Lord's and his Christ's / Die koninkryk van die wêreld het ons Here s'n en sy Christus s'n geword". One kingdom (singular; TR "the kingdoms"), and the Greek simply says it now *belongs* to the Lord. EN avoids the common "has become the kingdom of our Lord", which repeats "kingdom" where the Greek does not.
   - 12:5 *hērpasthē* "emporgerissen / snatched up / weggeruk", not DE "entrückt", which is heard as the "rapture" of later end-time schemes. The verse looks back to Christ's ascension.

2.7 **Smaller points, translated literally:**
   - 8:3 *hina dōsei tais proseuchais* "damit er es mit den Gebeten … gebe / so that he would put it with the prayers / sodat hy dit saam met die gebede … kon sit": the incense is added to the prayers.
   - 8:9 *ta echonta psychas* "die Leben hatten / that had life / wat lewe gehad het" – *psychē* here is "life", not "soul".
   - 8:13 *henos aetou* "einen Adler" (literally "one eagle"); *mesouranēma* "hoch am Himmel / in midheaven / hoog in die hemel" (the zenith, where everyone can see and hear it).
   - 9:7 *ta homoiōmata* "die Gestalten / the shapes / die gestaltes"; the repeated "wie / like / soos" of 9:7–10 is kept.
   - 9:16 *dismyriades myriadōn* = 200 million, in the pattern of 5:11 ("zweimal zehntausendmal Zehntausende").
   - 9:17 *hyakinthinos* "hyazinthblau / hyacinth blue / hiasintblou" (dark blue, the colour of the smoke); *theiōdēs* "schwefelgelb / sulphur yellow / swaelgeel", matching fire – smoke – sulphur at the end of the verse.
   - 9:20 *hina mē proskynēsousin* "um nicht mehr … anzubeten / so as to stop worshipping / om op te hou om … te aanbid".
   - 11:13 *onomata anthrōpōn chiliades hepta*, literally "names of people, seven thousand" (a Hebrew way of counting persons). Translated "siebentausend Menschen / seven thousand people / seweduisend mense". 1 Kgs 19:18 (the seven thousand of Elijah's time) is behind it.
   - 11:18 *diaphtheirai tous diaphtheirontas* "die verdirbst, die die Erde verderben / destroy those who destroy / verderf wat … verderf": the wordplay is kept.
   - 12:2 *krazei* "schreit / cries out / skreeu". DE and AF depart from §1 (*krazō* "rufen / uitroep" elsewhere) because the cry is the cry of labour, and Mic 4:9–10 (**own**) has "schreien / skreeu".
   - 12:11 *dia to haima* "durch das Blut / by the blood / deur die bloed". Strictly *dia* + accusative is "because of, on the basis of" the blood: they conquered because the Lamb's blood had already won.
   - 12:12 *hoi en autois skēnountes* "die ihr in ihnen euer Zelt habt / you whose tent is in them / julle wat jul tent daarin het", and 13:6 "die im Himmel ihr Zelt haben / those whose tent is in heaven / dié wat hulle tent in die hemel het" – *skēnoō* as §1 (7:15; 21:3).
   - 13:3 *ethaumasthē holē hē gē opisō tou thēriou* "die ganze Erde lief staunend dem Tier nach / the whole earth followed the beast in amazement / die hele aarde het die dier verwonderd agternagegaan" – amazement that turns into following.
   - 13:5 *poiēsai* "zu wirken / to act / om te werk" – the Greek has no object.
   - 13:14 and 13:15: the Greek shifts between neuter and masculine for the beast (13:14 *hos echei*, as if it were a person). Not reproduced.
   - 14:6 *tous kathēmenous epi tēs gēs*, literally "those who sit on the earth" (not the usual *katoikountas* "who live"). Translated "die auf der Erde ansässig sind / those who are settled on the earth / dié wat op die aarde gevestig is".
   - 14:10 *kekerasmenou akratou*, literally "mixed unmixed": wine was normally diluted; this one is "unverdünnt eingeschenkt / poured undiluted / onverdun ingeskink".
   - 14:13 *ap' arti* "von jetzt an / from now on / van nou af" is placed with "die in the Lord". It can also begin the Spirit's answer ("From now on, yes, says the Spirit"). *Ta erga autōn akolouthei met' autōn* "ihre Werke gehen mit ihnen / their works go with them / hulle werke gaan saam met hulle" (not Luther's "folgen ihnen nach").
   - 14:15 *exēranthē* "is dried": translated "dürr und reif / dry and ripe / droog en ryp" (grain that has dried is ready to harvest). "Ripe" is supplied.
   - 14:16, 19 *ebalen to drepanon* "warf seine Sichel / swung his sickle / het sy sekel … geswaai"; DE keeps "warf", the same verb as "warf die Trauben in die Kelter" (*ebalen*).
   - 14:19 *tēn lēnon … ton megan*: "great" is masculine after a feminine noun, another irregularity of the Greek. Not reproduced.
   - 14:20 1600 stadia, about 300 km (roughly the length of the land of Israel); "Stadien / stadia / stadies" kept, with no conversion in the text.

### Chapters 15–22

3.1 ***Pornē / porneia* in ch. 17–19, aligned with Nahum 3:4.** Our Nahum has "die vielen Hurereien der Hure … Meisterin der Zaubereien / the many harlotries of the prostitute … mistress of sorceries / die vele hoererye van die hoer … meesteres van towerye". Revelation 17–18 uses Nahum's picture (a city as a prostitute and sorceress who sells the nations) together with Isa 23 and Jer 51. I used:
   - *pornē* Hure / prostitute / hoer (as §2 1.6 proposed);
   - *porneia / porneuō* where Babylon is meant (17:2, 4; 18:3, 9; 19:2) **Hurerei / Hurerei treiben, harlotry / commit harlotry, hoerery / hoerery pleeg**, i.e. Nahum's word family instead of §1 "Unzucht / sexual immorality / ontug";
   - *pharmakeia* (18:23) Zauberei / sorcery / towery (Nahum);
   - *pornoi* in the vice lists (21:8; 22:15) kept "die Unzüchtigen / the sexually immoral / die ontugtiges", because there the literal sense is meant, as in 2:14, 20–21 and 9:21.
   Options: (a) **as above** (my recommendation): the OT picture stays visible, and "the wine of her harlotry" makes sense as a metaphor; (b) §1 throughout ("Unzucht / sexual immorality / ontug", and only *pornē* = Hure), i.e. one word for *porneia* everywhere, but "the kings of the earth committed sexual immorality with her" reads as if it were literal; (c) Nahum's family everywhere, also in 2:14–21 and the vice lists. **At the merge** 14:8 (part 2), "the wine of the fury of her *porneia*", the same phrase as 18:3, was given the same word ("Hurerei / harlotry / hoerery"; §1b). DE "Hurerei treiben" and AF "hoerery pleeg" come close to Luther / 1933 here; the key word of our own Nahum wins (§4). EN "harlotry" is a little old-fashioned; "prostitution / prostitute themselves" would be the modern alternative, but then Nahum EN would need changing too.

3.2 **20:4–5 *ezēsan* "sie lebten / they lived / hulle het geleef".** The aorist can be (a) ingressive, "**came to life**" (most modern versions; often read as a bodily resurrection at the start of a literal millennium), or (b) constative, "**lived**", leaving it open what kind of life it is (in your reading: the souls of the faithful dead live and reign with Christ now, 20:4 "I saw the souls"). I chose (b) in all three languages, also in 20:5 "the rest of the dead did not live", because it is the plainest rendering and does not decide the question either way. Part 1 rendered the same verb for Christ in 2:8 "wieder lebendig wurde / came to life / weer lewend geword" – there the context (dead → alive) forces it. If you prefer (a), 20:4 becomes "wurden lebendig / came to life / het lewend geword" and 20:5 "wurden nicht lebendig". 20:5b "Das ist die erste Auferstehung" stays literal.

3.3 **SBLGNT readings that readers will notice** (followed in each case):
   - 15:3 "König der Zeitalter / King of the ages / Koning van die eeue" (TR "King of saints", many manuscripts "King of the nations").
   - 15:6 *linon* "Leinen / linen / linne"; some old manuscripts read *lithon* "stone".
   - 16:5 no "O Lord" (TR "Thou art righteous, O Lord, which art …"), and "who is and who was" without "who is coming" (as 11:17).
   - 16:17 "aus dem Tempel, vom Thron her" (TR "of heaven").
   - 18:3 *peptōkan* "**sind … gefallen / have fallen / het … geval**". Most later manuscripts and the older Bibles read *pepōkan* "have drunk". SBLGNT chooses "fallen" (it repeats "fallen, fallen" from 18:2); readers will notice.
   - 19:1 "die Rettung und die Herrlichkeit und die Kraft" (TR adds "and the honour" and "unto the Lord our God").
   - 19:6 "der Herr, Gott, der Allmächtige" without "our" (TR "the Lord God omnipotent").
   - 19:13 *bebammenon* "in Blut getaucht / dipped in blood / in bloed gedoop" (other manuscripts "sprinkled").
   - 20:9 "Feuer kam vom Himmel herab" without "from God".
   - 21:3 *laoi* "seine **Völker** / his peoples / sy volke" plural (TR "his people") and no "and be their God".
   - 21:4 "Das Frühere ist vergangen" without "for".
   - 22:14 "die ihre Gewänder waschen / who wash their robes / wat hulle klede was" (TR "that do his commandments"), as you asked.
   - 22:19 "vom Baum des Lebens / in the tree of life / van die boom van die lewe" (TR "out of the book of life").
   - 22:21 "mit allen / with all / met almal" (TR "with you all", other manuscripts "with all the saints"), no final "Amen".

3.4 **20:4 *krima edothē autois* "das Gericht wurde ihnen gegeben / judgement was given to them / die oordeel is aan hulle gegee".** Literal, with the divine passive kept (§1 *edothē*). It can mean (a) they received authority to judge (thrones; 1 Cor 6:2), or (b) judgement was given *in their favour* (Dan 7:22, "judgement was given for the saints of the Most High" – the verse John is echoing). The literal wording keeps both. Who "sat on them" is not said either; it is left open.

3.5 **18:20 *ekrinen ho theos to krima hymōn ex autēs*.** Literally "God has judged your judgement from her". Readings: (a) God has decided your case against her, i.e. given you justice (Deut 32:43; 6:10 "How long … will you not judge and avenge our blood?"); (b) God has passed on her the sentence she passed on you (the talion of 18:6). I translated (a): "Gott hat euren Rechtsstreit an ihr entschieden / God has judged your case against her / God het julle saak teen haar beslis". Alternative close to (b): "Gott hat an ihr das Urteil vollstreckt, das sie über euch gefällt hat / God has imposed on her the sentence she passed on you".

3.6 **22:18–19 rendered plainly**, with the Greek word play kept: "hinzufügt … wird Gott ihm hinzufügen / adds … God will add / iets daarby voeg … sal God by hom voeg"; "wegnimmt … wird Gott wegnehmen / takes away … God will take away / wegneem … sal God wegneem". It echoes Deut 4:2 (a covenant warning against changing God's word), not a rule about copying a manuscript. "Seinen Anteil … am Baum des Lebens / his share in the tree of life / sy deel van die boom van die lewe" (SBLGNT, 3.3).

3.7 **22:14 *exousia* "Anrecht / the right / reg"** departs from §1 "Vollmacht / authority / gesag" (see §1). Literally "that their authority may be over the tree of life". The alternative is literal "Vollmacht über den Baum des Lebens / authority over the tree of life / gesag oor die boom van die lewe". *Plynontes* is present: "die … waschen / who wash / wat … was" – an ongoing washing, as 7:14 "washed … made white in the blood of the Lamb".

3.8 **17:8, 11 the beast "war und ist nicht und wird da sein" (*kai parestai*).** A deliberate parody of God's name "der ist und der war und der kommt" (1:4, 8; 4:8). DE "da sein wird", EN "will be present", AF "weer daar sal wees" (AF adds "weer", because "daar sal wees" alone sounds unfinished). Keep it literal so that the devotional can show the parody; the alternative "wiederkommen / come again / weer kom" makes it sound like Christ's coming. 17:10 *dei meinai* "darf … nur kurze Zeit bleiben / must remain only a little while / moet … net 'n kort tydjie bly". The seven kings (17:9–11) are translated literally, without naming emperors.

3.9 **Smaller points, translated literally:**
   - 15:2 *tous nikōntas ek tou thēriou* "die über das Tier … siegen / those who conquer the beast / dié wat die dier … oorwin" (present participle, as "Wer siegt" in ch. 2–3). *Hestōtas epi tēn thalassan* "am / beside / langs" the sea of glass, as Israel stood by the sea (Exod 14–15); "on" is also possible.
   - 16:6 *axioi eisin* "Sie haben es verdient / They deserve it / Hulle verdien dit". The same word as "Würdig bist du / Worthy are you" (4:11; 5:9), used here with bitter irony; the literal "Sie sind es wert / They are worthy" would be misunderstood. §1 had proposed this.
   - 16:6 *exechean* "ausgegossen / poured out / uitgestort" – the same verb as the bowls (talion: they poured out blood, God pours out the bowls). DE "Blut ausgießen" is a little unusual, but it keeps the link.
   - 16:13–16 the three spirits are neuter plural with a singular verb (*synēgagen*): "sie versammelten sie / they gathered them / hulle het hulle versamel". 16:15 (Christ's interruption) is set between dashes, as the Greek does.
   - 16:21 *talantiaia* "so schwer wie ein Talent / as heavy as a talent / so swaar soos 'n talent" (≈ 26–36 kg); "zentnerschwer / hundred-pound" would be Luther / NIV.
   - 17:5 *mystērion* "ein Name … , ein Geheimnis: Babylon …" – "Geheimnis" may be part of the name (KJV "MYSTERY, BABYLON") or say that the name is symbolic. The comma keeps both.
   - 18:2 *phylakē* "Gefängnis / prison / gevangenis" (×3), the same word as 20:7 "aus seinem Gefängnis / from his prison". "Haunt / lair" (ESV/NIV) is the alternative.
   - 18:13 *sōmatōn, kai psychas anthrōpōn* "Leiber – und Menschenseelen / bodies – and human souls / liggame – en mensesiele". "Bodies" was a trade word for slaves; John adds "and human souls" at the end of the list as a protest. A translation "slaves" would hide that; the dash marks it.
   - 18:22 *kitharōdoi* "Leierspieler / lyre players / lierspelers" (as 5:8; 14:2; 15:2 "Leier / lyre / lier").
   - 19:10; 22:9 *Hora mē* "Tu das nicht! / Do not do that! / Moenie!".
   - 19:15 *poimanei* "weiden / shepherd / soos 'n herder oppas", as 2:27 and 7:17 (1.12).
   - 19:16 *mēros* "Schenkel / thigh / dy".
   - 20:2 the dragon is in the nominative after an accusative (*ton drakonta, ho ophis*), the same irregularity as 1:5; translated as a normal sentence.
   - 20:13 "der Tod und das Totenreich / death and Hades / die dood en die doderyk" in lower case (EN/AF), as §1 decided (capital only in 6:8).
   - 21:8 *apistoi* "die Untreuen / the faithless / die ontroues" (§1).
   - 21:16 "zwölftausend Stadien / twelve thousand stadia / twaalfduisend stadies" and 21:17 "hundertvierundvierzig Ellen" are kept as numbers (12 × 1000; 12 × 12), not converted.
   - 22:2 punctuation as SBLGNT: the river flows "mitten auf ihrer Straße / through the middle of the city's street / in die middel van haar straat" (22:1–2a one sentence); then "on either side of the river the tree of life". *Karpous dōdeka* "zwölf Früchte / twelve kinds of fruit / twaalf vrugte".
   - 22:6–7 and 22:12–16: the speaker changes from the angel to Christ without notice. I kept 22:6–7 in one quotation (as the Greek runs on) and 22:10–16 in one quotation; the devotionals can explain the change of voice.
   - 22:16 *to genos Dauid* "der Nachkomme Davids / the descendant of David / die nakomeling van Dawid" (5:5 "die Wurzel Davids"). *Hymin* plural: "euch / you / julle".

3.10 **Merge with part 2.** The points this part listed for the merge (14:8 = 18:3; *potērion* 14:10; *mesouranēma* AF; *potamos* 9:14; *plateia* 11:8; *lēnos*; "from the foundation of the world" 13:8 = 17:8; 13:18 vs 17:9; *charagma* DE; the AF pronoun for *dier*) were all dealt with; see §1b. ## 4. Style

- Modern, natural German, English and Afrikaans; nothing copied from Luther (1912/1984/2017), Elberfelder, ESV/NIV/KJV/NKJV, or AF 1933/53 or 1983. Where a key word or a very literal phrase brings a sentence close to a traditional one (e.g. "Heilig, heilig, heilig"), the Greek wins.
- **Revelation's roughness is kept.** The "and" chains (*kai … kai*) stay, also at the start of sentences ("Und ich sah / And I saw / En ek het gesien"). The sudden switches between past and present tense in the visions (4:5 "gehen aus / come"; 5:5, 9; 6:16; 7:10) are kept. The book's grammatical irregularities (1:4–5; 2:13 *Antipas*; 3:12 *hē katabainousa*) are translated as normal sentences and noted in §2 1.7. Long periodic sentences are not created where the Greek is paratactic.
- *Idou* = "sieh / look / kyk", not "siehe / behold". In "kai eidon kai idou" before a vision object (6:2, 5, 8) EN has "And I looked, and there was …" and AF "En ek het gekyk, en daar was …", to avoid "and I saw, and look".
- **Address.** Each church's angel is *du / you / jy*. Plural *ihr / you / julle* only where the Greek switches (2:10b, 13 "bei euch", 23–25). In the hymns God and the Lamb are addressed DE/EN *du / you*, AF *U*, as in Hebrews. John's "Mein Herr / My lord / My heer" to an elder (7:14) is followed by AF "u" in lower case (a human). AF capitalises subject and object pronouns for God and Christ (*Hy, Hom, Ek, My*), and possessives are lower case (*sy, u, my*), as in Hebrews and 2 Timothy. The "voice like a trumpet" (4:1) is Christ's (1:10–12), so AF "Ek".
- **Quotation marks.** Short direct speech is in quotation marks („…“ DE, "…" EN/AF), including Christ's words in 1:17–20. The seven letters (2:1–3:22) are dictated texts introduced by "schreib: / write: / skryf:" and stand without quotation marks; quoted words inside them follow a colon (3:17 "Denn du sagst: Ich bin reich …"). Speech that runs on into hymn lines (7:14b–17) follows a colon without quotation marks, as in Hebrews.
- **Hymns** are set as `> ` lines (verse numbers inside the lines where a verse begins within the hymn). The doxology lists keep each Greek word's rendering from §1 (*doxa* Herrlichkeit, *timē* Ehre, *dynamis* Kraft, *kratos* Macht, *ischys* Stärke, *eulogia* Lob, *eucharistia* Dank). The verbless doxologies take DE "sei", EN "be", AF "kom … toe"; 7:10 (a statement) "gehört / belongs / behoort".
- *Laleō* = "sprechen / speak / praat" (1:12; 4:1), as in Hebrews. *Kēryssō* "verkünden / proclaim / verkondig" (5:2).
- Numbers are written as words: DE "hundertvierundvierzigtausend", "zwölftausend"; EN "a hundred and forty-four thousand"; AF "honderd vier-en-veertigduisend", "twaalfduisend", "vier-en-twintig".
- Book name and chapter/verse: Offenbarung 1,1 (DE), Revelation 1:1 (EN), Openbaring 1:1 (AF).
