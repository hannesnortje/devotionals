## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

"And I saw a beast coming up out of the sea". The dragon, defeated and furious, has taken its stand on the sand of the sea (12:18), and now we see whom it is waiting for. Every Jewish reader knew where this picture came from. Daniel saw four beasts come up out of the sea – a lion, a bear, a leopard and a terrifying fourth beast with ten horns – and they were four empires that oppressed God's people (Daniel 7:2–8, 17). John's beast is Daniel's fourth beast, with the features of the first three in one body: *"And the beast that I saw was like a leopard, and its feet were like a bear's, and its mouth like a lion's mouth."* Notice the order. Daniel looks forward – lion, bear, leopard – but John looks back from his own time and names them in reverse. On the usual interpretation they were Babylon, Medo-Persia and Greece, and the beast of John's day is the sum of them all: the speed of the leopard, the crushing strength of the bear, the devouring mouth of the lion. Rome inherited them all. The lion's mouth is also a counterfeit of the Lion of Judah (5:5), who turns out to be a slaughtered Lamb; this lion only devours. It is godless world power in its full form.

For the churches in Asia the beast had a face. Rome came across the sea, and its power was visible everywhere. "And the dragon gave it its power and its throne and great authority." The second beast comes "up out of the earth", out of their own surroundings: according to many interpreters the local elite – priests and city leaders who promoted the imperial cult. Pergamum already had a temple to Roma and the emperor in the time of Augustus, and Ephesus built a great temple to the emperors under Domitian. Whoever wanted to take part in the life of the city – its festivals, the guilds of traders and craftsmen, the market – faced the same choice again and again: incense for the emperor, or exclusion. All this "so that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark".

*"And it was given a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies"* – words straight from Daniel 7:8, 25, where the little horn speaks against the Most High. On the beast's heads are *"names of blasphemy"*. For the first readers these were the emperors' titles on the coins in their hands: *Divus* (divine), *Augustus* (the revered one), *divi filius* (son of a god); according to Suetonius, Domitian wanted to be addressed as *Dominus et Deus*, Lord and God. The blasphemy is directed *"against God, to blaspheme his name and his tent"* – propaganda that takes God's place: the emperor as saviour, lord and bringer of peace. But here too it says "was given": the mouth and the forty-two months are permitted and limited by God.

The most shocking thing about the chapter, however, is how much the beast resembles the Lamb. One of its heads "seemed slaughtered to death", and its death wound is healed – just as the Lamb is "as though slaughtered" (5:6). It "came to life" (v. 14), the very words Christ uses of himself (2:8). (Many interpreters also hear the rumour that spread after the emperor Nero's death in AD 68, that he would return.) As we saw yesterday with Daniel 2, we can also understand it like this: the beast is struck, but rises again in new forms. Rome's full power has passed, but the iron power lives on in the toes. Every time the world thinks a kingdom of the beast is over, it rises again somewhere else. The second beast "had two horns like a lamb, and it spoke like a dragon." And the crowd cries, "Who is like the beast, and who can make war against it?" – a counterfeit of Israel's song: Who is a God like you? (Exodus 15:11; Micah 7:18). Evil creates nothing new. It imitates.

### Insights from the Greek

- **Ten horns, ten diadems (*kerata deka … deka diadēmata* – κέρατα δέκα … δέκα διαδήματα, v. 1):** The beast has the same seven heads and ten horns as the dragon (12:3): it is the dragon's image in history. John explains it himself later: the seven heads are *"seven mountains"*, Rome on its seven hills, and also seven kings; the ten horns are *"ten kings"* who receive authority together with the beast (17:9, 12) – the divided powers of the toes in Daniel 2. Notice the shift: on the dragon the diadems sit on its heads (12:3), on the beast on its horns. The dragon rules behind the curtain; the beast rules openly, through the kings of the earth.
- **The false prophet (*allo thērion* – ἄλλο θηρίον, v. 11; cf. 16:13; 19:20):** The second beast later receives its own name: *"the false prophet"* (16:13). It does not rule itself; it makes people worship the first beast (v. 12). It had *"two horns like a lamb"*, but it speaks like the dragon – like the serpent in the garden. The Lamb has *"seven horns"* (5:6), full power; this beast only two – a borrowed, limited power that only looks like the Lamb. And over against God's two witnesses, who are true prophets (11:3–4), stands this one false prophet. Its signs are counterfeits of those of the true prophets. *"Fire come down from heaven"* imitates Elijah on Carmel (1 Kings 18:38) and the two witnesses, from whose mouth fire comes (11:5). And *"to give breath to the image of the beast"* is a counterfeit of the Spirit: God breathed the breath of life into Adam (Genesis 2:7), and in 11:11 *"a breath of life from God"* entered the dead witnesses. In Asia Minor speaking statues and temple tricks were known; in the second century Lucian describes a "false prophet" with a talking snake god. The test remains the same as with Moses: a sign that draws people away from the true God is false, however impressive it may be (Deuteronomy 13:1–3).
- **Mark (*charagma* – χάραγμα, vv. 16–17):** The word for a stamp or impression – also for the emperor's stamp on coins and documents. Many of the coins in the hand of a trader in Thyatira bore the emperor's image and titles. According to verse 17 the mark is "the name of the beast or the number of its name": it says whom you belong to.
- **As though slaughtered (*hōs esphagmenēn* – ὡς ἐσφαγμένην, v. 3):** The same expression as for the Lamb in 5:6 (*hōs esphagmenon*). John wants us to notice the likeness – and the difference. The Lamb's wounds are for others; the beast's healing is for itself.
- **Calculate (*psēphisatō* – ψηφισάτω, v. 18):** From *psēphos*, the pebble used for counting. "Here is wisdom", says John. It is the wisdom of discernment (cf. 17:9), not of code-breakers.
- **The number of a human being (*arithmos anthrōpou* – ἀριθμὸς ἀνθρώπου, v. 18):** "And its number is six hundred and sixty-six" (so the SBLGNT; a few early manuscripts have 616). One well-known reading: the words "Nero Caesar" in Hebrew letters add up to 666, and in a Latin spelling to 616. Others read it as "a human number": six falling short three times of seven, the number of fullness – humanity putting itself in God's place.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **Evil is a counterfeit of the Lamb.** The beast has a wound, a resurrection, a throne, worshippers and a "prophet". So the danger for the church is not first of all the open enemy, but the convincing copy: power that looks like salvation, a leader who sounds like a lamb. The test is not how impressive something is, but whether it is like the Lamb – or speaks like the dragon. Revelation shows three faces of the same power that serves the dragon: the first beast, political and military power; the second beast, the propaganda and religion that make that power look holy and use the market as a weapon (v. 17); and later the prostitute, Babylon, the seduction of prosperity (chapters 17–18). And the danger was not only outside the church. In Pergamum some followed the teaching of Balaam (2:14), in Thyatira "Jezebel" taught that it was not so bad to eat along at the guild's temple meal (2:20), and Laodicea said, *"I am rich"* (3:17). The voice of the second beast can speak inside the church too – still today, wherever the church blesses the power of the day or preaches prosperity as the proof of God's favour.

2. **Power that demands worship is always the beast.** The image that speaks and has everyone killed who will not worship it comes from Daniel 3, where Nebuchadnezzar's golden statue had to be worshipped. The three friends refused, even if God should not rescue them (Daniel 3:17–18). Rome was the beast of John's day; every age has its own systems – political, economic, ideological – that lay claim to people's whole heart.

3. **No one is unmarked – everyone belongs to someone.** The mark on the right hand or the forehead is the counterpart of God's seal (7:3), which recalls Ezekiel 9:4. Many interpreters also hear the Shema: God's words are to be a sign on your hand and between your eyes (Deuteronomy 6:8). The forehead stands for what you think, the hand for what you do. And God's seal came first: his servants were sealed before the beast ever appeared.

4. **The saints conquer by endurance, not by fear.** "Here is the endurance and the faith of the saints." The beast may make war and even conquer, but only "for forty-two months" – God's measured time. And whoever belongs to the Lamb has their name in "the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb" (v. 8). Notice the two kinds of victory. The beast is given power *"to make war against the saints and to conquer them"* (v. 7) – it conquers by killing. But in 12:11 and 15:2 it is the saints who conquer – by staying faithful even to death. Verse 10 takes up words from Jeremiah 15:2 and calls not for resistance with the beast's weapons but for endurance: whoever faces captivity or the sword accepts it from God's hand. The Lamb's army does not fight like the beast.

## Living It Out

The mark of the beast is not something you can receive by accident, or a technology you need to live in fear of. The believers in Asia knew exactly what it was about: the small pinch of incense, the guild meal at the temple, the silence that would save your business. The mark did not arrive in a day; it grew through a thousand small choices of the hand and the forehead.

We know the same pressure. At work you can only "buy and sell" if you go along with a little dishonesty. Among friends you have to keep quiet about certain things to fit in. Advertising tells you every day who you must be and what you must have. That is not loud persecution, but quiet worship of power, money and status.

So the question of Revelation 13 is not "when?" but "whose are you?" And the gospel's answer is not, "Watch out, or you will be marked!" It is: you are already sealed. Your name is written in the book of the Lamb who was slaughtered for you. You can say no to the beast because you have said yes to him.

Ask yourself two questions tonight: Where is the beast pulling at my hand – at something I do? And where at my forehead – at something I think? Bring both to God with the words: I belong to the Lamb.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- Where are you drawn by a convincing "counterfeit" – something that looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon (v. 11)?
- In which area of your life do you feel the pressure that you can only "buy or sell" if you play along (v. 17)?
- What would someone who had watched your week say: whom do your hand and your forehead belong to (v. 16)?
- How do your fears change when you know that your name is in the book of life of the slaughtered Lamb (v. 8)?

*Lamb of God, you alone are worthy to be worshipped. The power of this world seems great, and it cries: Who is like the beast? But I confess: Who is a God like you? You were slaughtered for me, and my name is written in your book of life.*

*Forgive me for the small compromises with which I try to fit in, and for the thoughts and deeds with which I honour the idols of my time. Forgive me when I try to stand with one foot with you and the other with the beast.*

*Thank you that you sealed me before I knew anything of this struggle. Give me the wisdom to recognise the counterfeits, and the endurance and faith of the saints. Let my hand and my forehead belong to you today. In the name of the Lamb, Amen.*
