## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Today we reach the seventh and last city on the ancient postal route, in the Lycus valley: **Laodicea** (near modern Denizli in Turkey). Laodicea was the richest and most self-sufficient city in the region. When a devastating earthquake struck the valley in AD 60, Laodicea, according to the Roman historian Tacitus (*Annals* 14.27), rose again from the ruins from its own resources, without any help from Rome.

The city was famous for three great industries – and in verses 17 and 18 Jesus uses all three to expose its spiritual poverty:

1. **Financial wealth and banking:** Laodicea was an important centre for money-changing, a city of banks and riches.
2. **Textiles and clothing:** According to Strabo the region was famous for sheep with soft, glossy black wool, from which expensive garments were woven.
3. **A medical school and eye-salve:** Near Laodicea there was a well-known medical school, and many interpreters link the city with "Phrygian powder" – an eye remedy (*kollourion* – κολλούριον) mentioned by ancient writers.

Yet this immensely wealthy city had one great weakness: **its water supply**. About ten kilometres to the north, across the valley, lay **Hierapolis** (modern Pamukkale), famous for its healing, mineral-rich **hot** springs. About sixteen kilometres to the east lay **Colossae**, known for its cold, refreshing water. Laodicea itself had no good springs and had to bring its water through a stone pipeline from springs to the south; the remains of the pipes are still thickly coated with lime deposits today. Many interpreters think that by the time the water reached Laodicea it was no longer hot enough to heal, nor cold enough to quench thirst. It was **lukewarm** and full of lime – water you would want to spit out. We cannot say for certain that Jesus had this very water in mind, but the picture would not have been strange to the first readers.

### Insights from the Greek

Let us look at some key words of the Greek text:

- ***Ho Amēn* – ὁ Ἀμήν (the Amen) and *archē* – ἀρχή (beginning / origin):** In verse 14 Jesus calls himself "the Amen" (cf. Isaiah 65:16 – the God of the Amen, the God of truth). He is the final, unshakeable guarantee of every promise of God. When he calls himself "the beginning of God's creation" (cf. Colossians 1:15–18 – a letter that, according to Colossians 4:16, was also to be read in Laodicea!), *archē* does not mean that Jesus was the first thing created, but that he is the **active Origin, Source and Ruler** of the whole creation.
- ***Chliaros* – χλιαρός (lukewarm) and *emeō* – ἐμέω (vomit):** The word *chliaros* occurs only here in the New Testament and means lukewarm. The reaction to it is *emeō* – which literally means to **vomit, to throw up**.
- ***Zēleuō* – ζηλεύω (be zealous):** In verse 19 this word comes from *zēlos* – ζῆλος (zeal), which is often linked with the word for "hot / boiling" (*zestos* – ζεστός) in verse 15. Jesus is saying, as it were: *"Stop being lukewarm; start boiling with zeal again, and repent!"*

### Core Theological Truths

This gripping letter opens up four important theological truths:

1. **Spiritual uselessness disgusts the Lord (vv. 15–16):** For centuries people have thought that "hot" means spiritually on fire and "cold" means far from God. But if we read it against the background of Hierapolis (hot, healing baths) and Colossae (cold, refreshing drinking water), as many interpreters do today, then **both hot and cold are positive and useful!** A church that is spiritually "hot" brings healing to broken people; a church that is "cold" brings refreshment to a thirsty world. But Laodicea had become like its city water: lukewarm, fruitless and useless. It healed no one and quenched no one's thirst.
2. **Material prosperity often breeds spiritual self-deception (v. 17):** Notice the sharp contrast between Smyrna (Day 4) and Laodicea. Smyrna thought itself desperately poor, but Jesus says: "but you are rich" (2:9). Laodicea boasts: "I am rich, I have become wealthy, and I need nothing." In the Greek the pronoun *sy* – σύ ("you") is emphatic, and the article stands before "wretched" (*ho talaipōros* – ὁ ταλαίπωρος): "you do not know that it is **you** who are wretched" – literally: the wretched one – "and pitiable and poor and blind and naked." Their greatest problem was not only their spiritual bankruptcy but their **blindness** to it. When our bank accounts are full and life is comfortable, we easily forget how desperately we need God.
3. **True spiritual wealth is only "bought" from Christ's hand of grace (v. 18):** How does a beggar who has nothing buy anything? As Isaiah 55:1 invites: "Come, buy without silver and without payment!" Jesus gently mocks Laodicea's three great industries:
   - Instead of the world's gold, seek **gold refined by fire** – tested, genuine faith that will endure into eternity (1 Peter 1:7).
   - Instead of Laodicea's showy black woollen garments, put on the **white clothes** of Christ's imputed righteousness to cover your guilt and shame.
   - Instead of Phrygian eye powder, let the Holy Spirit anoint your eyes with heavenly **salve**, so that you can see yourself and God truly again.
4. **Discipline and the knock at the door flow from Christ's unquenchable love (vv. 19–20):** After speaking the hardest words of all seven letters, Jesus opens his heart: "All those I love, I reprove and discipline" (*phileō* – φιλέω – tender, friendly affection). The most shocking picture in all of Revelation 1–3 is verse 20: the Head of the church stands **outside** the door of his own church! They were so busy inside with their meetings, their wealth and their religious programmes that they had not even noticed that Jesus was shut out. And yet he does not break the door down; he stands patiently and knocks. To the individual ("if *anyone* hears my voice") who opens, he promises to eat with him (*deipneō* – δειπνέω, from *deipnon*, the main evening meal, at which guests sat at table for hours in deep fellowship) – and one day to sit with him on his throne (v. 21).

## Living It Out

If there is one letter that strikes home in the modern Western church and our own comfortable lives, it is the letter to Laodicea. We have insurance for everything, medical cover, catering, entertainment on demand and comfortable homes. And without our noticing, our culture's attitude to life whispers its way into our spiritual life: *"I can manage on my own. I need nothing."*

We pray only when a crisis hits that we cannot solve with a credit card or a doctor. We are not against God, but neither do we burn with passion for him. We are moderate, decent, balanced – and spiritually **lukewarm**. We adapt so seamlessly to the temperature around us that we quench no one's spiritual thirst and lead no one to healing at the cross.

But today, listen beyond the sharp diagnosis to the wonder of verse 20. However long you have relied on your own strength and prosperity, and even if you have pushed Jesus to the outer edge of your daily decisions – **he has not walked away.** He stands today at the door of your heart and your life, and he knocks through his Word and through the conviction of his Spirit.

He is not after your money or your impressive CV; he wants to come in to you and sit at your table. To open the door means letting go of your self-sufficiency, honestly admitting your spiritual poverty, and giving him once more the place of honour at the head of your life's table.

The seven letters end with a throne: the one who conquered sits with his Father on his throne (v. 21). Tomorrow another door opens for John – "a door stood open in heaven" (4:1) – and he is allowed to see who really sits on that throne.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- Is your life at the moment like Hierapolis's hot water (bringing comfort and healing) and Colossae's cold water (quenching spiritual thirst) for the people around you, or has your faith adjusted to the lukewarm temperature of your surroundings?
- In which area of your life have material security, knowledge or success perhaps made you feel without realising it: *"I need nothing"*, so that your daily dependence in prayer has dried up?
- In which particular room of your life (your finances, your marriage, the way you spend your time or your secret thoughts) is Jesus still standing outside, knocking to be let in?

*Lord Jesus, you are the Amen, the faithful and true Witness, on whose word I can build my whole eternity. Today I bow before you in deep humility. Forgive me my lukewarmness. Forgive me for the times I grew so comfortable in my earthly blessings and my own abilities that I thought I could handle life on my own. Without you I am poor, blind and naked.*

*I come to you today with empty hands, to buy from you without money and without price: refine my faith like gold, clothe me in the white clothes of your righteousness, and anoint my eyes with your Holy Spirit so that I may see you and myself rightly again. Thank you that you love me too much to leave me in my lukewarm state.*

*I hear your voice and your knock at my door today, Lord. I open every room of my heart wide to you. Come in, be the Lord of my house, and sit at table with me. Let my heart boil again with zeal and love for you alone. Amen.*
