## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

About 55 kilometres north of Ephesus, along the coast of the Aegean Sea, lies the next stop on the postal route: **Smyrna** (today the city of Izmir in Turkey). Smyrna was proud of its splendid architecture on the slopes of Mount Pagos; the public buildings that ringed the hill were sometimes compared to a crown. It was a wealthy harbour city – and for Christians one of the most dangerous places on earth to live.

Smyrna was fiercely loyal to Rome. As early as 195 BC it built one of the very first temples in honour of the goddess *Roma*, and in AD 26 it beat ten other cities for the coveted right to build a temple to the emperor Tiberius. In Smyrna, emperor worship was not merely a religious ritual; it was civic pride. A pinch of incense on the altar before the emperor's image and the confession *Kyrios Kaisar* ("Caesar is Lord") counted as proof of loyalty to the city and the empire. Whoever refused became suspect and could lose his place in the trade guild, his work and his customers. (The well-known certificates proving that someone had sacrificed come only from the persecution under the emperor Decius in AD 250; in John's day the pressure was more social and economic – but no less real.)

The name **Smyrna** is the Greek word for **myrrh** – a precious, fragrant resin used for perfume and for embalming the dead (and given to Jesus both at his birth and at his burial, Matthew 2:11; John 19:39). Myrrh is a familiar picture: it releases its sweetest, richest fragrance when it is **crushed and pounded**. The church in Smyrna lived up to its name: a crushed church giving off a lovely fragrance for Christ. (It is also one of only two churches, together with Philadelphia, to which Jesus speaks not a single word of rebuke or criticism!)

### Insights from the Greek

Let us look at the powerful Greek terms in this short but profound letter:

- **"Poverty" (*ptōcheia* – πτωχεία, v. 9):** Greek has two words for poverty. The first is *penia* (πενία): you have just enough to live on and no luxuries. But Jesus uses *ptōcheia* here – **extreme, beggarly poverty**, having absolutely nothing. Because the Christians refused to worship the emperor, their businesses were boycotted, their property plundered and their work taken away.
- **"Affliction" (*thlipsis* – θλῖψις, vv. 9–10):** The word means pressure: being pressed together or crushed under a massive weight (picture olives in an olive press).
- **"Crown" (*stephanos* – στέφανος, v. 10):** When Jesus promises *"the crown of life"* in verse 10, he does not use *diadēma* (διάδημα, the royal crown of a ruler) but *stephanos* – the laurel wreath handed to an athlete who had successfully finished a gruelling race. Smyrna was known for its athletic games, and the believers would have grasped the picture at once.

### Core Theological Truths

This passage reveals four priceless truths for believers who suffer:

1. **The risen Christ knows the road of suffering and death first-hand (v. 8):** To a church facing death, Jesus introduces himself as the one *"who was dead and came to life"*. He does not shout orders from a safe distance; he himself died the worst of deaths and walked out on the other side of the grave. Many interpreters also hear an echo of Smyrna's own history: the old city was destroyed around 600 BC and only rebuilt about three centuries later – a city that had, as it were, "died and lived again". Jesus shows that he is the true Resurrection and the Life.
2. **Heaven defines wealth radically differently from the world (v. 9):** In the eyes of Smyrna's citizens the Christians were pitiful beggars (*ptōcheia*). But Jesus inserts four words that change everything: ***"but you are rich"***. Why? Because they had treasure in heaven that no emperor or mob could steal (cf. James 2:5). Later we will see Laodicea, which thinks it is rich while it is spiritually bankrupt; Smyrna has nothing on earth but possesses everything in Christ. Even when members of the local Jewish community (who were exempt from sacrificing to the gods and the emperor) probably denounced the Christians to the Roman authorities to protect themselves, Jesus declares that true spiritual identity lies in faith, not in bloodline.
3. **God sets a firm limit on the length and purpose of our suffering (v. 10):** Jesus does not hide the truth – it will get harder before it gets better. The devil is behind the persecution, tempting them to compromise, but God uses the very same fire to test and refine their faith (*"so that you may be tested"*). Notice the words **"for ten days"**. In the Bible (as in Daniel 1:12–14, where Daniel and his friends were tested for ten days) "ten days" points to an **intense but strictly limited and measured period**. The devil is on a leash; God holds the stopwatch.
4. **Eternal life disarms the fear of physical death (vv. 10–11):** Jesus' command is not *"Be successful"* but *"Be faithful to the point of death"* (that is: even if it costs you your life). The first death (physical death) can strike the body, but the one who conquers will never be harmed by the **second death** (Revelation 20:14 – eternal separation from God in the lake of fire). About sixty years after this letter was written (if Revelation was written around AD 95, as most assume), Polycarp, the aged bishop of Smyrna – according to Irenaeus a disciple of John – was executed in the city's stadium. When the proconsul pressed him to curse Christ, Polycarp, according to the account of his martyrdom, replied: *"Eighty-six years I have served him, and he has never done me any wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King who saved me?"*

## Living It Out

We live in a culture that tries to avoid suffering at all costs and treats prosperity as the only proof of God's blessing. When hardship, financial loss, rejection or illness comes our way, our first reaction is often: *"What have I done wrong? Why has God abandoned me?"*

Smyrna smashes this false theology. This church had done **nothing** wrong – on the contrary, they were so faithful to Jesus that they lost everything! Sometimes God allows us to go through the winepress of *thlipsis* (affliction), not because he is angry with us, but because he is releasing the precious myrrh-fragrance of Christ's character in our lives.

Perhaps you are going through a "ten-day" season of pressure today. Maybe you are being sidelined at work because you refuse to join in the lying or to excuse corruption. Maybe you feel shut out by friends or family because you stand by biblical values. Or maybe you are walking through a dark valley of loss where you feel you have come to the very end of your strength (*ptōcheia*).

Hear the voice today of the One who himself went through death and lives again: **"Do not fear anything you are about to suffer."** Your pain is not blind or endless. It has been measured out by your Father's hand ("ten days"), and at the end of this race the victor's crown (*stephanos*) of life is waiting for you. What you give up for Jesus' sake today makes you rich for eternity.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- By what do you currently measure whether you are "rich" and blessed – your bank balance, comfort and people's approval, or your spiritual wealth in Christ?
- Where in your life are you being tempted right now to burn a "pinch of incense" to the world's idols (keeping your faith quiet or bending your values) just to avoid conflict, mockery or loss?
- How does it change your view of your present trial to know that God has set a fixed time limit ("ten days") on your suffering and that the enemy does not have the last word?

## Prayer

*Lord Jesus, you who are the First and the Last, who drank the bitter cup of death and rose in victory, I come to take refuge in you today. Thank you that you see every tear, every bit of pressure and every loss in my life. Forgive me that I so often measure my worth, and your love for me, by outward comfort and earthly prosperity.*

*When life crushes me like myrrh, give me the grace not to become bitter and rebellious, but to release the sweet fragrance of your presence. Today I pray especially for my brothers and sisters around the world who sit in prison for your name's sake, who lose their livelihood and endure persecution – strengthen them with your supernatural courage.*

*And in my own "ten days" of trial, take away my fear. Make me faithful, not only when it is easy, but to the very end, with my eyes fixed on the crown of life. Amen.*
