## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Yesterday we saw the new Jerusalem: a city without a temple, because God and the Lamb themselves are its temple. Today the angel leads John into the middle of that city, and what he sees there is not another building but a garden: "a river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing out from the throne of God and of the Lamb", and on either side of it the tree of life.

Every reader who knows the Bible knows where we last heard these words. In its very first chapters a river went out from Eden to water the garden, and in the middle of the garden stood the tree of life (Genesis 2:9–10). After humanity's rebellion God cursed the ground (Genesis 3:17), and cherubim with a flaming sword guarded the way to the tree of life (Genesis 3:24). From that day on humankind was an exile from the garden. The whole Bible tells of God's way of opening the road back. In Revelation 22 the circle closes: what was shut in Genesis now stands open – and it is greater than the beginning. The garden has become a city, and the tree stands in the middle of its street.

John borrows his images from the prophets as well. Ezekiel saw water flowing out from under the threshold of the temple, deeper and deeper, until it became a river that made the Dead Sea fresh; on both banks grew trees that bore fruit every month, and their leaves were for healing (Ezekiel 47:1–12). Zechariah saw living water flowing out from Jerusalem and promised that there would never again be a curse (Zechariah 14:8, 11). The first readers had heard this promise before: to Ephesus Jesus had said, "To the one who conquers I will give to eat from the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God" (2:7). What was promised to one church at the beginning of the book is given to the whole people of God at its end.

### Insights from the Greek

- **Tree (*xylon* – ξύλον, v. 2):** The word really means "wood" and can also be used for a tree. The same word is used in the New Testament for the cross (Acts 5:30; 1 Peter 2:24), and Paul quotes Deuteronomy 21:23: cursed is everyone who hangs on a *xylon* (Galatians 3:13). Many interpreters hear the connection here: because Christ bore the curse on the wood, the tree of life stands open again.
- **No longer any curse (*pan katathema ouk estai eti* – πᾶν κατάθεμα οὐκ ἔσται ἔτι, v. 3):** Literally "every accursed thing will be no more". The curse of Genesis 3, which lay on the ground, on human work and on human relationships, is gone – as Zechariah 14:11 promised.
- **They will serve him (*latreusousin autō* – λατρεύσουσιν αὐτῷ, v. 3):** The word for priestly service in the sanctuary. In the city without a temple all God's servants are priests, and their whole life is worship.
- **They will see his face (*opsontai to prosōpon autou* – ὄψονται τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ, v. 4):** God told Moses that no one can see his face and live (Exodus 33:20). The psalmists longed for it (Psalm 17:15; 42:2). Now the deepest longing of the Old Testament is fulfilled. Notice too: "the throne of God and of the Lamb", but then *his* servants, *his* face, *his* name – the Father and the Lamb are one.
- **For the healing of the nations (*eis therapeian tōn ethnōn* – εἰς θεραπείαν τῶν ἐθνῶν, v. 2):** The same nations that were deceived by Babylon (18:23) are in the end not destroyed but healed.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **What was lost in Eden is restored by the Lamb – and more.** The river flows "from the throne of God and of the Lamb". The way back to the tree of life does not bypass the cross; it goes through it. That is why the last page of the Bible is not simply a return to the beginning: in Eden the tree was a promise; in the new Jerusalem it is fruit given every month.

2. **The name on the forehead: we belong to him.** Throughout the book everyone bears a mark: God's seal (7:3) or the mark of the beast (13:16), as we saw in chapter 13. But now only one mark remains: "his name will be on their foreheads". What was often hidden in this age is then visible for ever: they belong to him.

3. **To see God is the goal of our existence.** Heaven is not first of all a place of golden streets but of a Person. "They will see his face." All we know of God here is like a reflection in a dim mirror; then we shall see him face to face (1 Corinthians 13:12).

4. **The servants reign.** The book does not end with people who merely enjoy things passively: "they will reign for ever and ever" – the promise of Daniel 7:18, 27. What already began in 5:10, in witness and suffering, is completed here in glory.

## Living It Out

We still live east of Eden. The curse is visible everywhere: in work that is toilsome, in relationships that break, in bodies that grow ill and old, in an earth that groans. Revelation 22 does not pretend otherwise, but it tells the first readers – and us – where the story is going. The last word over your life is not the curse but the tree of life.

That future already casts its light on today. The leaves of the tree are "for the healing of the nations". Every time Christians bring peace where there was hatred, care for the sick, or forgive where it would be easier to blame, they show something of that healing before it has fully come. You do not have to save the world, but you can be a leaf of that tree for someone near you.

And then there is the name on the forehead. We all carry other names that try to define us: the name of our job, of our failures, of what others say about us. Some of those names are heavy to carry. But God's last word over his children is his own name. You belong to him, and one day that will be plain for all to see.

Tonight, read Genesis 3:22–24 and then Revelation 22:1–5 one after the other, and thank God for the open way.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- Where do you feel the weight of the curse today – in your work, your body, your relationships (v. 3)? What changes when you know it is not the last word?
- Do you really long to see God's face, or do you care more about the blessings than about him (v. 4)?
- Whose name do you bear in what you think and do – to whom does your heart belong (v. 4; cf. 13:16)?
- For whom can you be a leaf of healing this week (v. 2)?
