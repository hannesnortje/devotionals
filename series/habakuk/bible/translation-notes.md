# Habakkuk – translation notes (for review)

Base text: Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible); where the WLC has a Ketiv/Qere, the Qere is translated (as in Micah and Nahum):
- 3:14 Ketiv *perazo* (sg.), Qere *perazav* (pl.) "his warriors" – the only Ketiv/Qere in the WLC of Habakkuk.
- (3:4 *'uzzoh* "his power" – the WLC keeps the Leningrad Codex's unusual spelling with *he*; OSHB notes it differs from BHS on a Ketiv/Qere here. Same sense.)

Versification: Hebrew = English in all of Habakkuk (1:1–17; 2:1–20; 3:1–19; 56 verses). The `versification` field says so; no EN notes needed.
Aim: close to the Hebrew, natural to read; the book's key words kept the same every time, and the same as in Micah, Nahum and Jonah where the Hebrew word is the same.
Layout: prose = 1:1 (superscription), 2:2a ("Then the LORD answered me and said:"), 3:1 (heading of the prayer), 3:19b (subscription, after the poetry, as its own line). Everything else is poetry (`> ` lines), in blocks: 1:2–4 (first complaint); 1:5–11 (first answer); 1:12–17 (second complaint); 2:1; 2:2b–5 (second answer); 2:6–8, 2:9–11, 2:12–14, 2:15–17, 2:18–20 (five woes); 3:2; 3:3–7; 3:8–15; 3:16; 3:17–19. *Sela* (3:3, 9, 13) stands as its own line in italics where the Hebrew has it. Note for the build: 3:19b has no verse marker, so it is attached to 3:19 (shown as the last line of the verse).

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **2:4 – the key verse.** *Hinneh 'uppelah lo yashrah nafsho bo; wetsaddiq be'emunato yichyeh.* Our text: EN "Look, he is puffed up – his soul is not upright within him; but the righteous will live by his faithfulness." DE „Sieh, aufgeblasen ist er – nicht aufrichtig ist seine Seele in ihm; der Gerechte aber wird durch seine Treue leben.“ AF "Kyk, hy is opgeblaas – sy siel in hom is nie opreg nie; maar die regverdige sal lewe deur sy getrouheid."
   - ***'emunah*** comes from *'aman* "be firm, reliable" (Exod 17:12 Moses' hands were *'emunah* "steady"); in the Hebrew Bible it means steadfastness, reliability, faithfulness – a trust that holds on. The same root stands in 1:5 *lo ta'aminu* "you would not believe". Options: (a) "faithfulness / Treue / getrouheid" (chosen – the Hebrew sense; Einheitsübersetzung-type rendering); (b) "faith / Glauben / geloof" (Reformation and NT reading; but DE „durch seinen Glauben leben“ = Luther, AF "deur sy geloof lewe" = 1933/53); (c) "steadfast trust / sein Vertrauen, seine Beständigkeit / sy vaste vertroue" (explanatory; *batach* "trust" in 2:18 is a different word). Recommendation: (a) in the text; the devotional (day 4) explains that *'emunah* is faith that stays faithful, and shows how Paul and Hebrews read it.
   - **Whose faithfulness?** MT "his" (the righteous person's); LXX "by **my** faithfulness" (God's; *ek pisteōs mou*); Heb 10:38 SBLGNT "**my** righteous one will live by faith" (our Hebrews file: „mein Gerechter aber wird aus Glauben leben / but my righteous one will live by faith / maar my regverdige sal uit geloof lewe"); Rom 1:17 and Gal 3:11 "the righteous will live by faith" (no pronoun). We follow the MT; the devotional should quote Heb 10:38 from our Hebrews file word for word, and Rom 1:17 / Gal 3:11 in our own short rendering ("Der Gerechte wird aus Glauben leben / The righteous will live by faith / Die regverdige sal uit geloof lewe" – matching Heb 10:38).
   - ***'uppelah*** (pual of *'afal*, only here) "swollen, puffed up" (cf. *'ofel* "mound"; Num 14:44 "they presumed"). Many emend (e.g. *'ullefah* "faints", or "the wicked"); LXX "if he draws back" (*hypostelētai*, used in Heb 10:38 "if he draws back"). Kept MT. Subject unclear: the Chaldean (2:5) or anyone proud.
   - *Yichyeh* "will live" (survive the coming judgment, and more).
2. **2:2 *lema'an yaruts qore' vo* "so that whoever reads it may run".** Options: (a) literal (chosen) – a herald reads it and runs to spread it, or the reader runs (flees, acts); (b) "so that one can read it easily / at a glance (on the run)" (EN "so that a runner may read it"; DE „damit man es geläufig lesen kann“; AF "sodat 'n mens dit vlot kan lees"); (c) "so that the one who reads it may live/act by it". *Luchot* "tablets" (plural, article: "the tablets"; perhaps public notice boards). *Ba'er* "make plain" (Deut 27:8).
3. **3:17–18 and the series title.** Our text: DE „Mag auch der Feigenbaum nicht blühen … – so will ich doch im HERRN frohlocken, will jubeln über den Gott meines Heils.“ EN "Though the fig tree does not blossom … – yet I will exult in the LORD, I will rejoice in the God of my salvation." AF "Al bloei die vyeboom nie … – tog sal ek in die HERE juig, ek sal jubel oor die God van my redding."
   - Two verbs: *'alaz* "exult" (also 3:14 the enemy's *'alitsut* "exulting") and *gil* "rejoice, shout for joy" (also 1:15 the Chaldean "rejoices" over his catch). I gave *gil* the title word: jubeln / rejoice / jubel. So the title echoes the second half of 3:18, and the book's contrast is kept (the Chaldean rejoices in his net, the prophet in his God).
   - **Titles can stay:** „Dennoch will ich jubeln“ (= „so will ich doch … jubeln“), "Yet I Will Rejoice" (= "yet I will … rejoice"), "Tog sal ek jubel" (= "tog sal ek … jubel"). Two small points: (i) AF in series.yml is written "Tog sal ek **J**ubel" – should be "Tog sal ek jubel" (verb; or title case "Tog Sal Ek Jubel"); (ii) EN "Yet I will rejoice in the LORD" is the KJV wording of 3:18a – our 3:18 avoids it ("exult" first), the three-word title is common property and can stay. Alternative if you want the title word-for-word from the text: put *gil* first ("yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will exult in the God …") – not recommended, that is KJV order.
   - *Wa'ani* "but I" (emphatic) – rendered by "doch / yet / tog" after the concessive *ki* "though" of 3:17. Literal alternative: "But as for me, I will exult …" / „Ich aber will …“ / "Maar ek, ek wil …".
   - 3:17 *kichesh ma'aseh zayit* "the work of the olive deceives" → "fails / enttäuscht / faal". *Shedemot* "terraced fields" (EN), „Ackerland“, "akkers". *Gazar mimmikhlah tson* "the flock is cut off from the pen".
   - 3:18 *Elohei yish'i* "the God of my salvation" = Micah 7:7 exactly (DE „Gott meines Heils“, AF "God van my redding").
4. **2:5 *hayyayin boged* "wine is treacherous".** MT "wine"; the Qumran Habakkuk commentary (1QpHab) reads *hon* "wealth" ("wealth betrays the arrogant man"), which fits the greed of 2:5–6 better. Kept MT; option: "wealth / Reichtum / rykdom". *Lo yinweh* (only here) – "does not stay at home / bleibt nicht daheim / bly nie tuis nie" (from *naweh* "abode": the restless conqueror); or "will not endure / succeed", "will not rest". *Nafsho* "his throat/appetite" (the same *nefesh* as "his soul" in 2:4 – the wordplay is lost in translation; the devotional can point to it).
5. **2:3 *weyafeach laqqets* "it pants toward the end".** Literal (chosen): the vision "pants / keucht / hyg" toward its fulfilment, like a runner (picks up 2:2 "run"). Options: "hastens / eilt / haas hom" (common); or, from Ugaritic *yph* "witness", "it is a witness to the end / ein Zeuge für das Ende / 'n getuie vir die einde" (parallel to "will not lie"). *Lo yekhazzev* "will not deceive / lügt nicht / sal nie bedrieg nie". *Lo ye'acher* "will not delay" = our Heb 10:37 (säumen / delay / talm). Heb 10:37 (with LXX) makes the subject a person ("the one who is coming"); the Hebrew subject is the vision (fem. in DE: „sie“; EN/AF "it").
6. **2:16 *he'arel* "show your foreskin".** MT: niphal imperative of *'arel* "be uncircumcised" – "expose yourself as uncircumcised" (chosen, literal: "show your foreskin / zeige deine Vorhaut / wys jou voorhuid"). 1QpHab, LXX, Syriac read *hera'el* "stagger / taumle / steier" (letters swapped) – fits "drink" better. The verse turns 2:15 around: the one who made others drunk to see their nakedness is himself made drunk and exposed. *Qiqalon* (only here) "utter disgrace" (an intensified *qalon*; perhaps *qi' qalon* "shameful vomit" – option). *Kavod* "glory" against *qalon* "disgrace" (literally "heavy" against "light", as Nahum 1:14); "glory / Herrlichkeit / heerlikheid" kept the same as 2:14 – Babylon's "glory" is shamed, the LORD's "glory" fills the earth.
7. **3:2.** *Shama'ti shim'akha* "I have heard the report of you" (DE keeps the play: „ich habe gehört, was man von dir hört“; AF "ek het gehoor wat van U te hore is"). *Yare'ti … pa'olkha* "I fear … your work" (the *po'al* of 1:5). ***Beqerev shanim*** (2×) literally "in the midst of (the) years" (chosen) – meaning "in our time / in these years / within our lifetime" (DE alternative „in unseren Jahren“, AF "in ons tyd"). *Chayyehu* "bring it to life / rufe es ins Leben / laat dit lewe" (or "revive it", "keep it alive"). ***Berogez rachem tizkor*** "in turmoil remember compassion": *rogez* = agitation, turmoil (Job 3:26), here usually read as God's wrath; the same root *ragaz* returns in 3:7 and 3:16 (2×, "tremble"). Chosen: EN "turmoil", DE „Beben“ (keeps the link with „bebte / bebe“ 3:16), AF "onrus". Options: "wrath / Zorn / toorn" (traditional; AF 1933/53 "toorn … ontferming"). *Rachem* "compassion" as Micah 7:19.
8. **3:9 – very obscure.** *'Eryah te'or qashtekha shevu'ot mattot omer* – literally "nakedness, it is laid bare, your bow; oaths/sevens, rods, word". Our text: "Your bow was laid bare, utterly bare; sworn were the rods – a word." (DE „Entblößt, ganz bloß lag dein Bogen; geschworen waren die Ruten – ein Wort.“ AF "Kaal, heeltemal kaal was u boog; gesweer was die roedes – 'n woord.") *Mattot* "rods, shafts, arrows" (or "tribes") – kept as "rods" (Micah 6:9 *matteh* "rod"; again 3:14 "with his own rods"). Proposed meanings: "you took the bow out of its case; your arrows were sworn by your word" (God's arrows act on his oath); emended "you filled your quiver with arrows" (reading *sibba'ta* "you satisfied"); "the oaths to the tribes were a word" (covenant promises). *Neharot tevaqqa' arets* "you split the earth into rivers" (or "with rivers"). Recommendation: keep the literal line and let the devotional say that the verse is uncertain.
9. **3:19 *YHWH Adonai cheli*.** Unusual order (YHWH before Adonai; the MT points YHWH here as "Elohim"). Rendered with the Micah convention: "the LORD, the Lord, is my strength / der HERR, der Herr, ist meine Kraft / die HERE, die Here, is my krag". Note: AF 1933/53 also has "HERE, die Here, is my krag" – the convention makes this unavoidable. Alternative: "the LORD, my Lord" (Adonai literally "my Lord") – breaks the convention. ***Kā'ayyalot*** "like the does / hinds" (feminine): "like those of the deer / wie die der Hirschkühe / soos dié van die hindes" (sure-footed on steep rock; cf. Ps 18:34). AF "hindes" is modern but rare – alternative "soos dié van 'n ribbok" (a South African mountain antelope; idiomatic, not literal) or "takbokke" (1933/53 – avoid). ***Bamotai*** "my high places / meine Höhen / my hoogtes" as Micah (*bamot* = heights, also cult places; here simply heights). *Yadrikheni* "he lets me tread" – the same verb as 3:15 (God "trod" the sea). Subscription *lamnatseach binginotai* "For the choir director, on my stringed instruments" (as in Psalm headings; *binginotai* "my strings" – first person, unusual).
10. **1:4 *torah* and *mishpat*.** *Torah* = "Weisung / instruction / onderrig" as Micah 4:2; alternative here "law / Gesetz / wet" (the context – courts, justice – suggests the law). *Tafug* "grows numb / erstarrt / raak verdoof" (literally "grows cold, powerless"). *Mishpat* = "Recht / justice / reg" in 1:4 (2×) and 1:7 (Micah's word) – so the irony stands: justice "never comes forth" in Judah, and the Chaldean's "justice comes forth from himself". In 1:12 *lemishpat samto* I used "for judgment / zum Gericht / vir oordeel" (natural); option to keep "justice" there too ("you appointed him to execute justice / um Recht zu üben / om reg te laat geskied") and so tie all four together.
11. **1:5.** *Re'u vaggoyim* "See among the nations"; LXX reads *bogdim* "you scoffers" ("Look, you scoffers"), which Paul quotes in Acts 13:41. *Ki po'al po'el* "for (one) is working a work" – no subject in Hebrew; "I" supplied from 1:6 ("I am raising up"). Alternative: "for a work is being done in your days". *Lo ta'aminu* "you would not believe" – same root as *'emunah* 2:4.
12. **1:12 and 2:1.**
    - *Lo namut* "we will not die" (MT). Jewish tradition counts this among the "corrections of the scribes" (*tiqqunei sopherim*): the original was *lo tamut* "you do not die" (you, the eternal God). Kept MT; option "You do not die / Du stirbst nicht / U sterf nie".
    - *Tsur lehokhiach yesadto* "O Rock, you have established him to reprove". 2:1 *tokhachti* (same root) "my reproof": either "my complaint" (the reproof I brought against God), or "when I am reproved". Kept the same word in both verses so the link shows ("reprove / reproof", „Zurechtweisung“, "bestraf / bestraffing"); options for 2:1: "my complaint / meine Klage / my klagte". *Yedabber bi* "speak in/through me" (DE literal „in mir reden“; EN/AF "to me").
13. **1:9–11 – difficult lines.** 1:9 *megammat peneihem qadimah* (*megammah* only here) "the set of their faces is forward" – or "eastward" / "like the east wind" (*qadim*). 1:10 *vayyitsbor 'afar* "he heaps up dust" = siege ramps. 1:11 *az chalaf ruach vayya'avor we'ashem* "then he sweeps on like the wind and passes by, and becomes guilty" ("like" supplied; or "then the wind changes", or "then he sweeps by as a wind and passes – guilty"); 1QpHab *wayyasem* "and he sets (it up)". *Zu kocho le'lohō* "he whose strength is his god" (or "this strength of his is his god").
14. **1:17 *yariq chermo*** "keep emptying his net"; 1QpHab reads *charbo* "his sword" ("Will he keep drawing his sword?"). Kept MT. *Lo yachmol* "without sparing" (DE „ohne zu schonen“; *Erbarmen* is reserved for *rachamim* 3:2).
15. **2:6–20 – the five woes.** The taunt-song of "all these" (the nations). No quotation marks: the woes run through to 2:19 and mix with the LORD's own words (2:13, 16, 20), so marks would have to open and close several times. Option: put 2:6b–19 in quotation marks. 2:6 *'avtit* (only here) "pledges" (loan pledges; KJV "thick clay" read it as two words) – *makhbid* "loads himself heavy" (the *kavod* root). 2:7 *noshkhekha* "those who bite you" – also "your creditors" (*neshekh* = interest, "the bite"); kept the picture. 2:11 *kafis* (only here) "rafter / Sparren / balk".
16. **2:17 *yechitan*.** MT "(the destruction of the beasts, which) terrified them"; LXX, Syriac: "will terrify you" (*yechittekha*). Kept MT. "Violence to Lebanon" = felling its cedars (cf. Isa 14:8), and the destruction of its animals.
17. **3:3–15 – tense.** The Hebrew mixes short (archaic past) and long verb forms. Translated as past throughout ("God came from Teman"), as a hymn remembering the exodus and Sinai (3:2 "your work"), which is then hoped for again (3:16). Alternative: present or future ("God comes …", "God will come …"), seeing the theophany as the coming deliverance. *Eloah* (3:3) = "God". *Teman* (Edom) and *Paran* (Sinai desert) as in Deut 33:2.
18. **3:4–6 details.** *Qarnayim miyyado lo* literally "two horns/rays from his hand (are) his" – "rays broke from his hand" (*qeren* = horn and ray of light, as Exod 34:29). *Chevyon 'uzzoh* "the hiding place of his power" → "there his power was veiled". 3:5 *dever … reshef* "pestilence … plague" (Reshef was also a Canaanite plague-god; option "burning fever / Fieberglut / koorsgloed"). 3:6 *wayemoded* "and he measured" (polel of *madad*); option "and he shook (the earth)" (from *mot / mud*; LXX "shook"). *Halikhot 'olam lo* "the ancient ways are his" (or "his goings are as of old").
19. **3:13–14.** *Le-yesha' et meshichekha* "for the salvation of your anointed" (the king, or the people; *et* can also mean "with": "to save (your people) with your anointed"). AF "gesalfde" and EN "anointed" lower case (not read messianically in the text; the devotional can). *'Arot yesod 'ad tsawwar* "laying bare the foundation up to the neck" (house and body images mixed, as Hebrew). 3:14 *perazav* (Qere) "his warriors" – also "his villagers / his hordes / his leaders" (*perazon* Judg 5:7, 11).
20. **3:16 *asher anuach leyom tsarah*.** "Yet I will wait quietly for the day of distress" (chosen; *nuach* "rest") – the "day of distress" (Nahum 1:7) is the day that comes upon the invader. Options: "that I must wait quietly …" (resignation); "I will rest (= be at peace) in the day of distress" (DE „Doch ich will Ruhe halten bis zum Tag der Not“ keeps both). *La'alot le'am yegudennu* "to come up against the people who invade us" (*gud* "attack, raid").
21. **AF and DE names.** AF Chaldeërs, Kusan; DE Chaldäer, Kuschan (German-style transliteration as in Jonah/Micah). Musical notes: 3:1 *shigionot* (EN/AF English-style), *Schigjonot* (DE; only here and Ps 7:1 *shiggayon*; perhaps an agitated lament) – kept transliterated in italics, not translated; *Sela* (3:3, 9, 13) the same in all three. Please check.

## 4. Style

- Speech of people inside the poetry uses quotation marks: „…“ (DE), "…" (EN, AF) – 1:2 ("Violence!"), 2:19 ("Wake up!" "Awake!"). God's speech (1:5–11; 2:2b–5) is not put in quotation marks, as in Micah and Nahum. The taunt-song of the nations (2:6b–19) is not in quotation marks either (see §2.15).
- German uses "du" for God (Habakkuk's prayers), for the prophet (2:2–3) and for the oppressor (2:7–8, 10, 15–17); "ihr" for the group addressed in 1:5.
- Rhetorical questions are kept as questions (1:12, 13, 17; 2:6, 7, 13, 18, 19; 3:8). Short exclamations (2:20 *has* "Silence!") are kept short.
- *Sela* stands on its own line in italics where it falls in the Hebrew verse (mid-verse in 3:3 and 3:9, end of 3:13).
- The *hinneh* of 1:6; 2:4, 13, 19 is "Look / Sieh (1:6 plural: seht) / Kyk" as in Micah and Nahum.
