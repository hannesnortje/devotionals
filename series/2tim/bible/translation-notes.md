# 2 Timothy – translation notes (for review)

Base text: **SBL Greek New Testament** (SBLGNT), © 2010 Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Textual-variant markers were removed; the text follows SBLGNT throughout.
Aim: close to the Greek, natural to read, and each key word rendered the same way every time it occurs, in all three languages. Key words shared with 1 Timothy use exactly the renderings of `series/1tim/bible/translation-notes.md` §1 (decided by you, see DECISIONS.md). Divine names: "Herr / Lord / Here" in normal case, since there is no YHWH in the NT. Timothy is addressed as *du / you / jy*, and the closing greeting in 4:22b is plural (*euch / you all / julle*).
Poetry: the trustworthy saying in 2:11b–13 is set as hymn lines. The short doxology in 4:18b stays in the prose.
Verse count checked against the base text: chapter 1 = 18, 2 = 26, 3 = 17, 4 = 22 (83 verses) in all three languages.

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **1:12 *tēn parathēkēn mou* – whose deposit?** Literally "my deposit / my entrusted thing". I kept it open: "mein anvertrautes Gut / my entrusted deposit / my toevertroude goed". Readings:
   - (a) what *I* have entrusted to him (my life, my soul, my work – KJV, Luther 1912 "meine Beilage")
   - (b) what *he* has entrusted to me (the gospel – as in 1:14 and 1 Tim 6:20, where *parathēkē* is clearly what was entrusted to Timothy)

   1:14 is unambiguous ("das anvertraute Gut / what has been entrusted to you / wat aan jou toevertrou is"). If you choose (b), 1:12 could become "das mir anvertraute Gut / what he has entrusted to me / wat Hy aan my toevertrou het"; for (a) "was ich ihm anvertraut habe / what I have entrusted to him / wat ek aan Hom toevertrou het". Your Day 2 devotional should match. EN uses "deposit" in 1:12 only because "what has been entrusted" would already decide the question.
2. **2:11–13 the trustworthy saying.** Set as a hymn (four "if" lines). Points to note:
   - 2:12b *arnēsometha* is future ("if we *will* deny him"), unlike the other verbs; I kept the future.
   - 2:13 *apistoumen* can mean "be unfaithful" or "be faithless / not believe". I chose "untreu / faithless / ontrou", which keeps the wordplay with *pistos* ("treu / faithful / getrou").
   - Is 2:13 a warning (he remains faithful to his word, so he must deny) or comfort (his faithfulness outlasts ours)? The text doesn't say, and the dash before "denn sich selbst verleugnen kann er nicht / for he cannot deny himself / want Homself kan Hy nie verloën nie" keeps it open. Whether 2:11 "Das Wort ist zuverlässig" points back (to 2:8–10) or forward (to the hymn) is also open; the *gar* ("denn / for / want") in 2:11b supports forward.
3. **3:16 *pasa graphē theopneustos*** – predicative as you decided: "Alle Schrift ist von Gott eingegeben und nützlich … / All Scripture is God-breathed and useful … / Die hele Skrif is deur God ingegee en nuttig …". *Note only:* the attributive reading is "Every God-breathed Scripture is also useful …" (cf. Luther "Alle Schrift, von Gott eingegeben, ist nütze …"). *Pasa graphē* can also be "every Scripture (passage)" rather than "all Scripture"; I followed your wording.
4. **4:6 *spendomai*.** The verb means "to be poured out as a libation" (the wine poured over or beside a sacrifice, cf. Num 28:7; Phil 2:17). I translated "als Trankopfer ausgegossen / poured out as a drink offering / as 'n drankoffer uitgegiet". Present tense: "already being poured out" – Paul's death has begun. *Analysis* "Aufbruch / departure / vertrek" is the image of breaking camp or a ship casting off; "Abscheiden / Tod / heengaan" would be weaker.
5. **4:14 *apodōsei* – "will repay" or "may … repay"?** SBLGNT (with the oldest manuscripts) reads the future *apodōsei*: "der Herr wird ihm vergelten / the Lord will repay him / die Here sal hom vergeld" – a statement leaving judgment to God (echo of Ps 62:12; Prov 24:12). Later manuscripts read the optative *apodōē* ("möge vergelten / may the Lord repay" – KJV, Luther 1912 "Der Herr bezahle ihm"), which sounds like a curse. I followed SBLGNT. This contrasts with 4:16 *mē autois logistheiē* (optative: "möge es ihnen nicht angerechnet werden"). Same verb *apodidōmi* as in 4:8 ("give/award").
6. **2:15 *orthotomeō*.** Literally "to cut straight" (Prov 3:6; 11:5 LXX: to make straight paths). I used "geradlinig umgehen mit / handle accurately / reguit werk met". Options:
   - (a) handle/teach it correctly (most common)
   - (b) "cut a straight path" for the word – a road image
   - (c) "rightly divide" (KJV) – avoided, it is in `avoid_words`, and AF 1983 "reg hanteer" was avoided too.
7. **2:19 the two "seal" quotations.** The first is Num 16:5 LXX almost word for word ("The Lord knows those who are his" – Korah's rebellion). The second is no exact quotation: it combines Num 16:26 ("turn away from the tents of these wicked men"), Isa 26:13 LXX ("we name your name") and Sir 17:26 / Isa 52:11 ("turn away from wrongdoing"). Both are in quotation marks; a note in Day 5 could say the second is an allusion, not a verse.
8. **2:20–21 vessels.** 2:21 *apo toutōn* "von diesen / from these / van hulle" can mean the dishonourable vessels (= the false teachers) or the things in 2:16–18. I kept it open. *Skeuos* "Gefäß / vessel / voorwerp": AF "voorwerp (tot eer)" – Hannes's decision (2 Nov), matching his Day 5 title; "houer" was proposed.
9. **2:26 *eis to ekeinou thelēma*.** Literally "captured by him for *that one's* will". Readings:
   - captured by the devil to do the devil's will (most translations)
   - they escape from the devil, (now) captured by God (or the Lord's servant) for God's will

   I translated "für seinen Willen / for his will / vir sy wil" without deciding who "he" is.
10. **4:17 "out of the mouth of the lion".** Literal, as a picture of mortal danger (Ps 22:21; Dan 6). It is unclear whether it means Nero, the death sentence, Satan, or a real arena. I kept the image without explanation. 4:16 *prōtē apologia* = "erste Verteidigung / first defence / eerste verdediging" (a preliminary hearing in court).
11. **1:8 *to martyrion tou kyriou hēmōn*.** "das Zeugnis von unserem Herrn / the testimony about our Lord / die getuienis oor ons Here" (objective genitive: the witness to him). The alternative is "the testimony our Lord gave" (cf. 1 Tim 6:13).
12. **1:3 *ap' progonōn*.** "wie schon meine Vorfahren / as my forefathers did / soos my voorouers". Paul places his service of God in continuity with Israel's faith.
13. **3:6 *gynaikaria*.** Diminutive, contemptuous ("little women"). I used "schwache Frauen / weak women / swak vroue", following the description that comes after it. It is a judgment on these particular people, not on women in general; it may deserve a sentence in Day 6.
14. **4:13 *membranai*.** "Pergamente / parchments / perkamente": possibly notebooks or OT scrolls; kept literal.
15. **Names.** DE following Loccum: Lois, Eunike, Phygelus, Hermogenes, Onesiphorus, Hymenäus, Philetus, Jannes, Jambres, Antiochia, Ikonion, Lystra, Demas, Thessalonich, Kreszens, Galatien, Dalmatien, Tychikus, Troas, Karpus, Priska, Aquila, Erastus, Trophimus, Milet, Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Klaudia; "Provinz Asien". AF: Loïs, Eunike, Figelus, Hermogenes, Onesiforus, Himeneus, Filetus, Antiogië, Ikonium, Listra, Tessalonika, Kressens, Galasië, Dalmasië, Tigikus, Karpus, Akwila, Trofimus, Milete, Klaudia. EN: usual forms; Prisca (not Priscilla), as in the Greek.

## 3. Style

- Modern, natural German, English and Afrikaans; nothing copied from Luther, Elberfelder, ESV/NIV/KJV, or AF 1933/53 or 1983. Where a key word fixed in 1 Timothy brings a phrase close to a traditional one (e.g. 4:7 "den guten Kampf kämpfen / fight the good fight / die goeie stryd stry"), the key word wins.
- The `avoid_words` in series.yml (godsaligheid, ongeveinsde, lankmoedigheid, bewaar die pand, Gottseligkeit, gewisslich wahr, faithful saying, rightly dividing) don't appear.
- Paul's long sentence in 1:8–11 is kept as one sentence; the interruptions in 1:17–18 and 4:14, 16 are set with dashes.
- OT quotations (2:19) are in quotation marks.
- Book name and chapter/verse: 2. Timotheus 1,1 (DE), 2 Timothy 1:1 (EN), 2 Timoteus 1:1 (AF).
