# Zephaniah – translation notes (for review)

Base text: Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible); where the WLC has a Ketiv/Qere, the Qere is translated (as in Micah, Nahum and Habakkuk):
- 2:7 Ketiv *shevutam* "their fortunes", Qere *shevitam* "their captivity" → "turn back their captivity / ihre Gefangenschaft wenden / hulle gevangenskap omkeer" (see §2.9).
- 2:9 Ketiv *goy* "a nation", Qere *goyi* "my nation" → "the rest of my nation / was übrig ist von meiner Nation / die res van my nasie".
- These are the only two Ketiv/Qere in the WLC of Zephaniah. (OSHB also marks 3:10 *putsai* as an anomalous form, and 3:17 *beqirbekh* written without the usual dagesh – same sense.)

Versification: Hebrew = English in all of Zephaniah (1:1–18; 2:1–15; 3:1–20; 53 verses). The `versification` field says so; no EN notes needed.
Aim: close to the Hebrew, natural to read; the book's key words kept the same every time, and the same as in Jonah, Micah, Nahum and Habakkuk where the Hebrew word is the same.
Layout: prose = 1:1 (superscription). Everything else is poetry (`> ` lines), in blocks: 1:2–6 (the great sweeping away); 1:7–13 (the sacrifice; Jerusalem searched); 1:14–18 (the day of the LORD); 2:1–3 (call to seek the LORD); 2:4–7 (Philistia); 2:8–11 (Moab and Ammon); 2:12–15 (Cush, Assyria/Nineveh); 3:1–5 (woe on Jerusalem); 3:6–8; 3:9–13 (the pure lip; the poor remnant); 3:14–17 (song of Zion); 3:18–20 (gathering home).

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **3:17 – the series title.** *YHWH Elohayikh beqirbekh gibbor yoshia'; yasis 'alayikh besimchah, yacharish be'ahavato, yagil 'alayikh berinnah.* Our text: EN "The LORD your God is in your midst, a warrior who saves. He beams with gladness over you; he is silent in his love; he rejoices over you with a song of joy." DE „Der HERR, dein Gott, ist in deiner Mitte, ein Held, der rettet. Er strahlt vor Freude über dich, er schweigt in seiner Liebe, er jubelt über dich mit Freudengesang.“ AF "Die HERE jou God is in jou midde, 'n held wat red. Hy straal van blydskap oor jou, Hy swyg in sy liefde, Hy jubel oor jou met 'n vreugdelied."
   - **The title can stay unchanged in all three languages.** *Yagil 'alayikh* = "er jubelt über dich / he rejoices over you / Hy jubel oor jou" – word for word our text, and *gil* has the same rendering as in Habakkuk (1:15; 3:18 jubeln / rejoice / jubel). Tense: the verbs are imperfects; I used the present (a description of who God is "in your midst"), which matches the titles. Option: future ("he will rejoice over you …"); then the title still works as a summary.
   - ***Yacharish be'ahavato*** – the crux. MT literally "he is silent / keeps silent in his love" (hiphil of *charash* "be silent"). Options: (a) literal (chosen): a love too deep for words, or a love that no longer speaks of the sins (cf. "the LORD has taken away the judgments against you", 3:15); (b) "he quiets (you) by his love" (causative, no object in Hebrew); (c) LXX *kainiei se* "he will renew you in his love" (reading *yachadish*, *d* for *r*), followed by many modern versions; (d) *charash* II "plough, devise": "he plans (good) in his love". Recommendation: (a) in the text, the devotional (day 5) explains the options. Note: AF 1933/53 also has "swyg in sy liefde" – the literal sense allows little else.
   - ***Gibbor yoshia'*** "a warrior who saves" (DE „ein Held, der rettet“, AF "'n held wat red"). *Gibbor* = "warrior / Held / held" as in Nahum 2:4. The same word stands in 1:14: on the day of the LORD "bitterly the warrior cries out there" – the human warrior screams, the divine Warrior saves. Alternatives: "a mighty one who saves / ein starker Retter / 'n magtige Redder" (more traditional; loses the link with 1:14 if not used there too).
   - ***Yasis … besimchah***: *sus* (as Isa 62:5 "as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so your God will rejoice over you") needs a word different from *gil* (jubeln), *'alaz* (frohlocken) and *samach* (sich freuen, 3:14 "be glad"). Chosen: "beam with gladness / strahlen vor Freude / straal van blydskap" (the joy of the bridegroom; *simchah* keeps the link with 3:14 "be glad"). Options: EN "he takes delight in you with gladness" (but "delight" is *chafets* in Micah 7:18); DE „er freut sich über dich mit Freude“ (literal, repetitive); AF "Hy is opgetoë oor jou, vol blydskap".
   - ***Berinnah*** "with a song of joy / mit Freudengesang / met 'n vreugdelied": the same root as 3:14 *ranni* "sing for joy" – Zion is told to sing (3:14), and then hears that God sings over her (3:17). Avoided: KJV/NIV "with singing", Luther „mit Jauchzen“.
   - Quotation marks: I put 3:16b–17 in quotation marks (what "will be said to Jerusalem"); the 2nd person feminine runs on to 3:19, so the quotation could also end after 3:16 (then 3:17 is the prophet's own word). Please decide.
2. **2:3 – Hannes's day 2.** *Baqqeshu et-YHWH kol-'anwei ha'arets asher mishpato pa'alu; baqqeshu tsedeq, baqqeshu 'anawah; ulai tissateru beyom af YHWH.* Our text: EN "Seek the LORD, all you humble of the land, who have done his justice. Seek righteousness, seek humility; perhaps you will be hidden on the day of the anger of the LORD." DE „Sucht den HERRN, all ihr Demütigen des Landes, die ihr sein Recht getan habt! Sucht Gerechtigkeit, sucht Demut; vielleicht werdet ihr verborgen am Tag des Zorns des HERRN.“ AF "Soek die HERE, al julle nederiges van die land, julle wat sy reg gedoen het. Soek geregtigheid, soek nederigheid; miskien word julle weggesteek op die dag van die toorn van die HERE."
   - Threefold *baqqeshu* "seek" kept three times. *'Anwei ha'arets*: "of the land" (Judah; chosen) or "of the earth" (all the humble on earth; cf. 3:9–10). *Mishpato pa'alu* "who have done his justice" (= who keep his commandments; *mishpat* as everywhere "justice / Recht / reg"); option "who do what he commands / die ihr nach seinem Recht lebt / wat sy gebooie onderhou".
   - ***'Ulai*** "perhaps" kept – not a promise, but a hope (like Jonah 1:6; 3:9 "who knows"). ***Tissateru*** (niphal of *satar*) "you will be hidden" (chosen; passive: God hides them); options "you may find shelter / ihr könnt euch bergen / julle kan skuil" (Luther „bergen“; and *chasah* 3:12 already has „sich bergen / shelter / skuiling soek“). The name *Tsefanyah* means "the LORD has hidden / treasured" (*tsafan*, another root) – a play the devotional can mention, not visible in the text.
3. **1:14–16 – the *Dies irae*.** Our text keeps the Hebrew order and the sevenfold "day" of 1:15–16: "A day of rage is that day, a day of distress and anguish, a day of wreck and ruin, a day of darkness and deep gloom, a day of cloud and thick darkness, a day of ram's horn and battle cry …" (DE „Ein Tag des Wütens ist jener Tag, ein Tag der Not und der Bedrängnis, ein Tag des Untergangs und der Verheerung …“; AF "'n Dag van woede is daardie dag, 'n dag van nood en benoudheid, 'n dag van ondergang en verderf …").
   - ***'Evrah***: "rage / Wüten / woede" = Habakkuk 3:8 (carry-over; also 1:18 "the day of the rage of the LORD"). The Latin hymn *Dies irae, dies illa* comes from this verse (Vulgate). Option: "wrath / Zorn / toorn" (the classic "A day of wrath is that day"; but *af* already has „Zorn / toorn“ in 2:2–3, and EN "wrath" is *chemah* in Nahum/Habakkuk). Recommendation: keep "rage" and let the devotional quote the hymn.
   - *Shoah umeshoah* (a sound-play) "wreck and ruin" (EN keeps the sound); DE „Untergang und Verheerung“, AF "ondergang en verderf". *Tsarah umetsuqah* "distress and anguish" (*tsarah* as Nahum 1:7 "day of distress"; 1:17 *hatsereti* "I will bring distress" is the same root). *'Arafel* "thick darkness" (Sinai, Exod 20:21; Deut 4:11) – DE „Wolkendunkel“, AF "digte donkerte".
   - 1:14 *qol yom YHWH* "Listen! The day of the LORD!" (*qol* as an exclamation, "Hark!"); option "The sound of the day of the LORD is bitter; there the warrior cries out" (another division of the line). *Maher me'od* "hurrying very fast". *Shofar* "ram's horn / Widderhorn / ramshoring" (not "trumpet / Posaune / basuin").
4. **3:9 *safah berurah* "a pure lip".** *Ki az ehpokh el-'ammim safah berurah* literally "for then I will turn to the peoples a purified lip". Our text: "For then I will change the lip of the peoples into a pure lip" (DE „Denn dann verwandle ich die Lippe der Völker in eine reine Lippe“; AF "Want dan sal Ek die lip van die volke verander in 'n suiwer lip"). "Lip" kept (not "speech / Sprache / taal") so the link with Babel shows: Gen 11:1 "the whole earth had one *safah*", 11:7, 9 the LORD "confused their *safah*". *Hafakh* "turn, overturn" is also the verb of Sodom's overthrow (Gen 19:25) – here the overturning is grace. Options: "a pure speech / eine reine Sprache / 'n rein taal" (clearer, loses the Hebrew picture). *Shekhem 'echad* literally "with one shoulder" → "shoulder to shoulder / Schulter an Schulter / skouer aan skouer" (the same idiom in all three); option "with one accord / einmütig / eendragtig" (DE „einträchtig“ = Luther).
5. **1:3 *hammakhshelot et-haresha'im* "the stumbling blocks with the wicked".** Literal (chosen). Meanings: idols and cult objects that make people stumble (Ezek 14:3–7), swept away together with their worshippers; or "ruins". Many delete the phrase as a gloss (missing in LXX) or emend to *wehikhshalti* "I will make the wicked stumble". DE „die Stolpersteine samt den Frevlern“, AF "die struikelblokke saam met die goddelose".
6. **2:1–2 – obscure.** *Hitqosheshu waqoshu haggoy lo nikhsaf.* *Qoshesh* is the verb for gathering stubble/straw (Exod 5:7, 12; Num 15:32) – the nation is told to "gather itself" like straw, just before "like chaff the day passes by" (2:2). Our text: "Gather yourselves together, yes, gather, O nation without longing". ***Lo nikhsaf***: (a) "without longing" (chosen; *kasaf* niphal "long for", Gen 31:30; Ps 84:3) – a nation that no longer longs (for God); (b) "shameless / ohne Scham / skaamteloos" (from Aramaic *kesaf* "be pale, ashamed"; many modern versions); (c) "undesirable / not longed for" (passive). 2:2 *beterem ledet choq* literally "before a decree is born" (= before the decree takes effect); *kemots 'avar yom* "like chaff the day passes by" (or "the day passes like chaff"; LXX reads quite differently). *Beterem lo yavo'* has an odd extra "not" (twice) – translated without it, as usual. Recommendation: keep the literal lines; the devotional says the text is difficult.
7. **1:5 *malkam* – "their king" or Milkom.** MT *bemalkam* "by their king". Lucianic LXX, Syriac and Vulgate read *Milkom* (the god of Ammon, 1 Kgs 11:5, 33; Jer 49:1, 3). Chosen: MT "by their king / bei ihrem König / by hulle koning" – which probably means the god "king" (Molech/Milkom) anyway: they swear to the LORD *and* to "their king". Option: "by Milkom / beim Milkom / by Milkom". Recommendation: MT in the text, the devotional explains (divided loyalty).
8. **1:9 *haddoleg 'al-hammiftan* "who leaps over the threshold".** Literal (chosen). Readings: (a) a pagan custom of not stepping on the threshold (1 Sam 5:5, Dagon's priests); (b) *miftan* = the raised platform of a throne or temple: "who leaps onto the podium" (palace servants rushing to fill "their master's house" with plunder). *Beit adoneihem* "the house of their master" – the king, or their god(s) (plural form). DE „über die Schwelle springen“, AF "oor die drumpel spring".
9. **2:7 and 3:20 *shuv shevut*.** 2:7 Qere *shevitam* "their captivity"; 3:20 *shevuteikhem* (no Ketiv/Qere). I used one rendering for both: "turn back their/your captivity / ihre/eure Gefangenschaft wenden / hulle/julle gevangenskap omkeer". Option: "restore their/your fortunes / ihr/euer Geschick wenden / hulle/julle lot verander" (the idiom's wider sense; it is the Ketiv in 2:7). Please decide (applies to both verses).
10. **3:10 *'atarai bat-putsai* "my worshippers, the daughter of my dispersed ones".** Both words obscure: *'atar* "pray, entreat" (only here as a noun: "my suppliants / meine Beter / my aanbidders"); *bat putsai* "daughter of my scattered" (= the scattered people). Who brings the offering? (a) the dispersed Jews themselves bring it (chosen reading of the line; cf. 3:19–20); (b) the peoples of Cush bring God's scattered people as an offering (Isa 66:20); (c) some read *Put* (a people, with Cush, Nahum 3:9). Literal; the devotional can open the options. *Minchati* "my offering / meine Opfergabe / my offergawe".
11. **3:8 *leyom qumi le'ad*.** MT *le'ad* "for plunder" (as Gen 49:27; Isa 33:23) – "the day when I rise up for plunder" (chosen). LXX and Syriac read *le'ed* "as witness" ("the day I rise up as a witness"), followed by many. Option: "as witness / als Zeuge / as getuie". *Chakku li* "wait for me" – as Habakkuk 2:3 *chakkeh lo* "wait for it": waiting in hope, even here before judgment. *Mishpati* "my judgment" (DE „Gericht“, AF "oordeel", as Habakkuk 1:12).
12. **3:18 – obscure.** *Nugei mimmo'ed asafti mimmekh hayu mas'et 'aleha cherpah*: literally "those grieving from the appointed feast I have gathered; from you they were – a burden on her, a reproach." Chosen: literal ("Those who grieve far from the appointed feast I have gathered; they were from you – a burden on her, a reproach"). LXX joins the first words to 3:17: "… he rejoices over you with a song of joy, as on a day of festival" – then "I will gather your afflicted" (many modern versions). *Asafti* "I have gathered" – the same *asaf* that "swept away" in 1:2–3 now gathers (perfect: certain future; option "I will gather"). *Mo'ed* "appointed feast" (Habakkuk 2:3 *mo'ed* "appointed time").
13. **2:14 – obscure.** *Kol-chayto goy* "every beast of a nation" (odd; perhaps "every kind of beast", *goy* = swarm, cf. Joel 1:6); *qa'at* and *qippod* – uncertain birds/animals ("desert owl / Eule / uil" and "hedgehog / Igel / ystervark"; also "pelican", "bittern"); *qol yeshorer bachallon* "a voice will sing in the window" (an owl's call in the ruins); *chorev bassaf* "devastation on the doorstep" (LXX/Vulgate *'orev* "a raven on the threshold"); *ki arzah 'erah* "for he has laid bare the cedar work" – I used the passive "the cedar work is laid bare" because the subject is unclear (God or the enemy). Literal throughout.
14. **1:2–3 *asof asef* "I will sweep away, sweep away".** A double wordplay: *asof* (infinitive of *asaf* "gather, take away") + *asef* (hiphil of *suf* "make an end"). Our "sweep away / wegraffen / wegvee" gives the sense. The carry-over word for *asaf* is "gather / sammeln / versamel" (3:8, 18) – option: "I will gather up, gather up everything …" (keeps the link with 3:8, 18: God's "gathering" first for judgment, then for homecoming), but reads unclearly. 1:2–3 also play on *'adam* "man" and *'adamah* "ground" ("mankind … from the face of the ground").
15. **1:11–12 details.** *Hammakhtesh* "the Mortar" (a hollow district of Jerusalem; DE „Mörserviertel“, AF "die Vysel"). *Kol-'am kena'an* "all the people of Canaan" – "Canaanite" also means "trader" (Prov 31:24; Hos 12:8), so: the merchants; option "all the traders / das ganze Händlervolk / al die handelaars". *Nidmah* "destroyed" (or "silenced"). 1:12 *haqqof'im 'al-shimreihem* "who have thickened on their dregs" – wine left too long on its lees (Jer 48:11): complacent people (AF "op hulle moer verdik", DE „auf ihrer Hefe dick geworden“).
16. **1:18 *ki-khalah akh-nivhalah*.** "for a full end, yes, a terrifying end" (*kalah* "full end" as Nahum 1:8–9). *Nivhalah* "terrifying" (chosen) or "sudden" (DE „plötzlich“ = Luther); both senses exist. *Kol-ha'arets* "the whole earth" (1:2–3 is universal; 3:8 repeats the line) – option "the whole land".
17. **3:1 *mor'ah wenig'alah ha'ir hayyonah*.** *Mor'ah* "rebellious" (from *marah*, or "filthy" from *r'y*); *nig'alah* "defiled"; *hayyonah* "the oppressing" (*yanah*) – and also *yonah* "dove": the name of Jonah. A sound-play the devotional can mention (the city named like a dove oppresses). The city is not named until 3:14 (Jerusalem); after 2:13–15 (Nineveh) the reader first thinks of Nineveh.
18. **3:5, 7 – the morning.** 3:5 *babboqer babboqer* "morning by morning" the LORD brings his justice to light; 3:7 *hishkimu hishchitu* "they rose early and made all their deeds corrupt" – I kept "rose early" (literal; usually "they were eager") so the contrast shows. 3:7 *kol asher-paqadti 'aleha* "all that I had appointed for her" (punishment threatened), or "although I punished her"; LXX "from her eyes" for *me'onah* "her dwelling".
19. **2:15 and 3:15 details.** 2:15 *ani we'afsi 'od* "I am, and there is none besides me" (Isa 47:8, 10 Babylon; DE „Ich – und außer mir keine!“). *Yani'a yado* literally "shakes his hand" → "shakes his fist / schüttelt die Faust / skud sy vuis" (scorn; option "waves his hand"). 3:15 *mishpatayikh* "the judgments against you" (the sentences of 3:1–8); *lo tir'i ra' 'od* "you will fear evil no more" (some MSS "see evil").
20. **2:6 *nevot kerot ro'im*.** *Kerot*: "meadows" (chosen; *kar* "pasture", Isa 30:23), "cisterns/caves" (*karah* "dig"), or an echo of *Kerethim* "Cherethites" (2:5) – a pun in the Hebrew. Literal enough; no change needed.

## 4. Style

- Speech of people inside the poetry uses quotation marks: „…“ (DE), "…" (EN, AF) – 1:12 (the complacent), 2:15 (the city), 3:7 (God quoting himself), 3:16b–17 (what will be said to Jerusalem; see §2.1). God's speech is otherwise not put in quotation marks, as in Micah, Nahum and Habakkuk.
- German uses "du" for the single addressee (Canaan 2:5b; Jerusalem/Zion 3:7, 3:11–19) and "ihr" for groups (2:1–3; 2:5a; 2:12; 3:8; 3:20).
- The repeated words of the book are kept, even where English or German would vary them: "day" (1:7–2:3), "in your midst" (3:3–17), "desolation" (1:13–2:15), "remnant" (1:4; 2:7, 9; 3:13), "name" (1:4; 3:9–20), "gather / assemble" (3:8, 18–20).
- Short exclamations kept short (1:7 "Silence …!", 1:14 "Listen!", 3:14 imperatives).
