# Micha – translation notes (for review)

Base text: Masoretic Text, Westminster Leningrad Codex (Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible); where the WLC has a Ketiv/Qere, the Qere is translated (1:3, 1:8, 1:10, 3:2).
Versification: Hebrew in all three languages. Hebrew 4:14 = English 5:1; Hebrew 5:1–14 = English 5:2–15. The EN file marks this at 4:14 and 5:1; every file states it in the `versification` field.
Aim: close to the Hebrew, natural to read; the book's key words kept the same every time.
Layout: 1:1 (superscription) is prose; everything from 1:2 to 7:20 is poetry (`> ` lines), split into blocks at the oracle breaks.

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **6:8 – the most quoted verse.** Our text: DE „Er hat dir gesagt, Mensch, was gut ist und was der HERR bei dir sucht: nichts anderes, als Recht zu tun, Güte zu lieben und in Demut mit deinem Gott zu gehen.“ EN "He has told you, O man, what is good, and what the LORD seeks from you: only to do justice, to love faithful love, and to walk humbly with your God." AF "Hy het jou vertel, mens, wat goed is, en wat die HERE by jou soek: niks anders nie as om reg te doen, goedertierenheid lief te hê en nederig saam met jou God te wandel." Points to decide:
   - *higgid lekha*: "He has told you" (MT) or "It has been told you" (Greek/Latin tradition, also Luther). I follow the MT.
   - *doresh*: "sucht / seeks / soek" (literal; *darash* = seek, inquire) or "fordert / requires / vra / verlang".
   - *'asot mishpat*: "Recht tun / do justice / reg doen" (kept the same as *mishpat* in 3:1, 8, 9 and 7:9, so the leaders who should "know *mishpat*" in 3:1 stand behind the verse). Alternative: "gerecht handeln / act justly / regverdig optree".
   - *ahavat chesed*: "Güte lieben / love faithful love / goedertierenheid lief hê" (same word as 7:18, 20, so the devotional can show that God asks us to love what he himself delights in). Alternatives: DE „Treue lieben“, „Barmherzigkeit lieben“, „Gnade lieben“ (Jonah's DE word – see 6 below); EN "love kindness", "love loyalty", "love mercy"; AF "liefde vir trou hê", "barmhartigheid liefhê". EN "love faithful love" is literal but repetitive. **Decided (5 Nov): EN "to love faithful love" (the same word as 7:18, 20); DE/AF unchanged.**
   - *hatsnea' lekhet*: root *tsana'* means "modest, careful, attentive" (only here and Prov 11:2). Options: "humbly / in Demut / nederig" (chosen, traditional); "carefully, attentively / achtsam, behutsam / versigtig, aandagtig"; "wisely". Recommendation: keep "humbly" in the text, explain "attentively" in the devotional.
2. **5:1 *mots'otav miqqedem mimei 'olam*.** "his origins are from of old, from ancient days" (DE „sein Ursprung ist von der Vorzeit her, aus den Tagen der Urzeit“; AF „sy oorsprong is van ouds af, uit die dae van die oertyd“). *Yemei 'olam* in 7:14 clearly means "days of old", so I used the same. Alternative (messianic, cf. John 1; Matt 2:6): "from days of eternity / aus den Tagen der Ewigkeit / uit die dae van die ewigheid". *Mots'otav* literally "his goings-out" (same root as "will come out" in the line before); "origins" is the meaning. Please decide.
3. **5:4 *wehayah zeh shalom*.** Translated literally: "And this one will be peace / Und dieser wird Friede sein / En hierdie een sal vrede wees". Options: (a) "he will be (our) peace" (personal, messianic; cf. Eph 2:14); (b) "this will be (the) peace:" introducing what follows (the seven shepherds); (c) "this one will be the man of peace". The verse is split over days 9 and 10 (5:4a / 5:4b); the literal rendering serves both.
4. **1:10–15 – the place-name wordplays.** Translated literally (names kept, no puns imitated), so the devotional can explain them:
   - 1:10 **Gath** – *b'gat al taggidu* "in Gath (*gat*) do not tell (*taggidu*)" (cf. 2 Sam 1:20). *Bakho al tivku* "do not weep at all" – some read *be'akko* "in Acco". **Beth-leaphrah** "house of dust" – "roll yourself in the dust (*'afar*)"; Qere *hitpallashi* (fem. imperative), Ketiv "I have rolled".
   - 1:11 **Shaphir** "beautiful (town)" – goes into exile in nakedness and shame. **Zaanan** sounds like *yatsa'* "go out" – "has not come out" (dares not go out to fight). **Beth-ezel** "house beside/near" – it takes away "its standing place" (support): the neighbour gives no help. Text very uncertain.
   - 1:12 **Maroth** "bitterness" – "waits in anguish for good" (*chalah* = writhe / be sick; or "waits" from *yachal*). Yet evil (*ra'*) comes "from the LORD".
   - 1:13 **Lachish** – *rekhesh* "steed" sounds like *Lakhish*: "harness the steed" (to flee, or the city of chariots). "Beginning of sin for Daughter Zion" – perhaps because horses and chariots stood for trust in military power (cf. 5:9).
   - 1:14 **Moresheth-gath** (Micah's home) sounds like *me'orasah* "betrothed": "parting gifts" (*shilluchim* = dowry for a bride who leaves home). **Achzib** – *akhzav* "deceptive" (a brook that dries up): "the houses of Achzib will be a deceit".
   - 1:15 **Mareshah** sounds like *yoresh* "the one who takes possession" (the conqueror). **Adullam** – where David hid (1 Sam 22:1): "the glory of Israel" (the nobility, or the king) will have to hide in a cave again.
   - 1:16 *nesher* "vulture" (bald-headed), not "eagle". Alternative: "eagle / Adler / arend".
   Alternative for the whole passage: imitate the puns in the text (e.g. EN "In Dust-town roll in the dust"). Not recommended for a Bible text; better in the devotional.
5. **2:12–13 – promise or threat?** Translated literally as a promise (God gathers the remnant; "the one who breaks open", *haporets*, goes before them; "their king … the LORD at their head"). Readings: (a) promise of return from exile (most interpreters; Christian reading: the Messiah as "breaker"); (b) the false prophets' empty promise continued from 2:11; (c) judgment: God crowds them into a fold (besieged Jerusalem) and they break out into exile. *Botsrah* = "fold" (or the city Bozrah). *Haporets* capitalised in AF ("die Deurbreker") because the line names the LORD; DE „der Durchbrecher“, EN "the one who breaks open" are neutral. Please decide whether AF stays capitalised.
6. **chesed across the series.** Jonah DE uses „Gnade“ for *chesed*; here DE uses „Güte“ because „Gnade lieben“ (6:8) sounds odd and 7:18–20 pairs *chesed* with *emet* (DE „Treue“). EN "faithful love" and AF "goedertierenheid" are the same as in Jonah. Options: DE „Güte“ (chosen), „Gnade“ (as Jonah), „Treue“ (then *emet* 7:20 = „Wahrhaftigkeit / Beständigkeit“). EN *emet* 7:20: "truth" (chosen, to keep it apart from "faithful love"); alternative "faithfulness".
7. **7:18 *nose' 'awon ve'over 'al pesha'*.** "who bears away guilt and passes over rebellion" (DE „der Schuld wegträgt und an der Auflehnung vorübergeht“; AF „wat skuld wegdra en by opstand verbygaan“). *Nasa'* = lift, carry; the same verb as 7:9 "the rage of the LORD I will bear" and 6:16 "bear the reproach" – so the believer bears God's anger while God bears away guilt. Alternatives: "forgives iniquity / Schuld vergibt / ongeregtigheid vergewe" (smoother, loses the picture). *Mi El kamokha* "Who is a God like you?" plays on the name Micah (*Mikhayah* "who is like YHWH?").
8. **Adonai YHWH (1:2).** Jonah has no example. Chosen: "der Herr, der HERR / the Lord, the LORD / die Here, die HERE". Alternatives: EN "the Lord GOD" (YHWH in small caps as GOD, common in English Bibles); DE „Gott, der HERR“; AF „die Here my God“ is not literal. 4:13 *adon* "Lord of all the earth": AF "Heer" to keep it apart from Adonai "Here".
9. **2:6 *nataf* "drip".** Prophesying is called "dripping" (words dripping like rain). Rendered "predigen / preach / preek" every time (2:6, 11); 2:11 *maṭṭif* "preacher". Literal "drip / träufeln / drup" could go in the devotional. The last line *lo yissag kelimmot* is unclear: "disgrace will not overtake us" (from *nasag*) or "reproaches do not cease" (from *sug*).
10. **2:4; 2:8–10; 6:9–10, 14; 7:11–12 – textual difficulties, translated literally:**
    - 2:4 *leshovev* "to the renegade/apostate" (the conqueror or the land-grabbers); some read *leshovenu* "to our captors".
    - 2:8 *shuvei milchamah* "like men coming home from war" (or "averse to war", i.e. peaceful).
    - 2:10 "it brings destruction" – subject is the land (fem.) or "uncleanness".
    - 6:9 MT *yir'eh shemekha* "(wisdom) sees your name"; many read *yir'at* "it is wisdom to fear your name". *Shim'u matteh* "Hear the rod" (or "Hear, O tribe" – *matteh* = rod and tribe). Very uncertain.
    - 6:10 MT *ha'ish* (read *hayesh*) "Are there still …"; "short measure" = the shrunken ephah.
    - 6:14 *yeshach* (only here) "hunger/emptiness" – meaning guessed from context.
    - 7:4 "The day of your watchmen" = the day the prophets (watchmen) announced.
    - 7:11 *yirchaq choq* "the boundary will be pushed far out" (or "the decree will be far off").
    - 7:12 *matsor* = poetic name for Egypt (also "fortress"); "the River" = Euphrates.
11. **4:6 *wa'asher hare'oti* "and the one on whom I brought evil".** Kept with "evil" (*ra'*) so it links with 1:12 and 2:3 ("evil has come down from the LORD", "I am devising evil"). Alternative: "whom I have afflicted / die ich hart behandelt habe / wie Ek swaargekry laat het".
12. **4:13 *wehacharamti* "I will devote".** MT 1st person ("I"); Greek and Syriac read 2nd person fem. ("you will devote"). Kept MT. *Cherem* = devote to God (by destruction).
13. **4:14 *titgodedi bat gedud*.** "gather your troops, daughter of troops" – or "gash yourself (in mourning), daughter of a raiding band". "The judge of Israel" = the king (a pun on *shofet* / *shevet* "staff"?).
14. **5:5 *ra'u … baḥerev*.** "They will shepherd the land of Assyria with the sword" (*ra'ah*, kept the shepherd root, ironic); some read *ra'a'* "break, shatter". *Bifetacheiha* "at its gates" or "with the drawn sword".
15. **5:2 "she who is in labour"** – keep the link with 4:9–10 (Daughter Zion in labour); Christian reading also sees Isa 7:14. Translated without interpretation.
16. **3:8 *et ruach YHWH*.** "with the Spirit of the LORD" may be a gloss (it breaks the metre), but it is in the MT and is kept.
17. **7:2 *chasid*.** Derived from *chesed*; "the faithful one / der Treue / die getroue". Alternative: "the godly / der Fromme / die vrome".
18. **7:15 *ar'ennu* "I will show him".** God speaks; many emend to "show us". Kept MT.
19. **Names (AF).** Moreset, Saänan, Bet-Haësel, Lakis, Agsib, Maresa, Sittim, Basan, Agab, Assirië follow common Afrikaans spelling; please check you are happy with "Miga" in the title and "Sion".

## 4. Style

- Speech of people inside the poetry uses quotation marks: „…“ (DE), "…" (EN, AF) – 2:4, 6, 7, 11; 3:5, 11; 4:2, 11; 7:10. God's speech is not put in quotation marks (as in prophetic books generally).
- German uses "du" where one person, city or "my people" is addressed (4:8–14; 5:1, 8–13; 6:3–5, 8, 13–15; 7:5, 8–11), "ihr" for plural (1:2; 2:3, 8–10; 3:1, 9).
- *Wehayah* ("and it will be") at 4:1 and 5:9 is kept as "Und es wird geschehen / And it will come to pass / En dit sal gebeur" because it marks a new oracle.
