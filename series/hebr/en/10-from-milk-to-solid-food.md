## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

At the end of yesterday's passage (Hebrews 5:10) the author was just about to open up the fascinating and profound subject of Jesus' high priesthood "according to the order of Melchizedek". But suddenly he slams on the brakes! Before he can share this rich theological treasure with his readers, he realizes there is a major obstacle – not in the subject itself, but in the spiritual condition of his hearers. With this we enter the third great **warning passage** of the letter (Hebrews 5:11–6:12).

These Jewish Christians had been believers for some time ("by this time"), but under the pressure of persecution and the temptation to slip back into the visible shadows of Judaism, their spiritual growth had stalled. Worse still, they had gone backwards. In Greco-Roman philosophical teaching (among the Stoics, for example, and in Philo of Alexandria) the picture of milk for beginners and solid food for the advanced was a familiar teaching image. The author uses it masterfully to shake his congregation out of their spiritual apathy.

### Insights from the Greek

Let us look at the Greek terms and the theological structure of this passage:

- **The diagnosis: spiritual deafness and sluggishness (*nōthroi* – νωθροί, v. 11):** The author has much to say about Melchizedek and Christ's priesthood, but it is hard to explain "since you have become sluggish in hearing". The Greek word for sluggish is *nōthroi* (according to an old explanation, made up of *nē* = not and *ōtheō* = to push or move). It describes someone who has become dull, lazy, listless or numb. Notice the verb "have become" (*gegonate* – γεγόνατε): they were not always like this! Their spiritual hearing had gradually grown dull because they had stopped actively listening and obeying. In Hebrews 6:12 the same word (*nōthroi*) appears again as the opposite of eager faith and patience.
- **Grown-ups who have become babies again: milk instead of solid food (*stoicheia* – στοιχεῖα and *nēpios* – νήπιος, vv. 12–13):** By now these believers should have been mature teachers of others. Instead they had sunk back to a level where someone has to teach them all over again "the first basics of the words of God" (*ta stoicheia tēs archēs* – τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς – literally the ABC, the elementary building blocks). They had become like a nursing infant again (*nēpios* – according to the old explanation of the word, "one who cannot yet speak", a baby), able to live only on milk (*gala* – γάλα) and choking on solid food (*sterea trophē* – στερεὰ τροφή). There is nothing wrong with a newborn baby drinking milk (see 1 Peter 2:2); it is natural and healthy. But when an adult is still living on a bottle after years and cannot chew solid food, it is a sign of a serious developmental problem. That is why they are "unacquainted with the word of righteousness" – they do not yet grasp the deep implications of Christ's finished work of justification and his high-priestly ministry.
- **The mark of spiritual maturity (*aisthētēria gegymnasmena* – αἰσθητήρια γεγυμνασμένα, v. 14):** Who are the spiritually mature (*teleioi* – τέλειοι) who can digest solid food? Verse 14 gives a brilliant definition of true Christian maturity. It is not about how many Bible verses you know by heart or how many theological debates you can win. It is about people "whose faculties have been trained by practice to distinguish good from evil" (*aisthētēria* – powers of perception; *gegymnasmena* – from *gymnazō* – γυμνάζω, from which we get "gymnastics" and "gymnasium"). Spiritual discernment is not learned overnight in a classroom; it is built up like muscle in a gym through *hexis* (ἕξις – "practice", repeated daily habit). Every time you hear the word and put it into practice by choosing the good and rejecting evil (and false teaching), your spiritual muscles grow stronger.
- **Beyond the foundation to the finished house (*teleiotēta* – τελειότητα, Hebrews 6:1–3):** In 6:1 the author switches his picture from a growing child to the building of a house. We are to "leave behind the elementary word about Christ" (*aphentes* – ἀφέντες) and "be carried on to maturity" (*pherōmetha* – φερώμεθα; *teleiotēta* – maturity). Of course this does not mean rejecting or forgetting the foundation! No builder tears down his own foundation – but no sensible builder spends his whole life pouring the same foundation over and over without ever putting up walls and a roof. The author names six "foundation stones" in three pairs that were typically part of early instruction for Jewish converts (and that would to some extent have been familiar even to a devout Jew under the old covenant):
  - **The personal turnaround:** *repentance from dead works* (turning away from sin as well as from useless legalistic attempts to save oneself) and *faith in God*.
  - **The shared rites:** *teaching about washings* (plural: *baptismōn* – βαπτισμῶν – which may include the distinction between the many Jewish purification washings, John's baptism and Christian baptism) and *laying on of hands* (for blessing, receiving the Spirit and setting apart for service).
  - **The future hope:** *the resurrection of the dead* and *eternal judgment*. In verse 3 the author ends with a humble acknowledgement of God's sovereignty: "And this we will do, if indeed God permits" – for only by God's grace can a heart that has grown sluggish be raised up to maturity again.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **Spiritual sluggishness is a condition we drift into, not one we are born with.** The readers had *become* sluggish; their hearing had grown dull because they had stopped listening and obeying.
2. **Growth is the normal life of a believer.** Milk is good for babies, but someone who can take only milk after years has a developmental problem. God calls his children to become "teachers" of others.
3. **Maturity is trained discernment.** True maturity is measured not by knowledge but by faculties trained by practice to distinguish good from evil.
4. **The foundation stays, but we build on it.** Repentance, faith, washings, laying on of hands, resurrection and judgment are the indispensable foundation – but God wants to build a house on it.
5. **Growth is God's gift of grace.** "If indeed God permits" – only by his grace can a sluggish heart be carried on to maturity again.

## Living It Out

In the spiritual life there is no such thing as "neutral gear". Walking with Christ is like driving up a mountain or rowing against a strong current: the moment you stop moving forward, you start sliding back. This passage exposes one of the greatest dangers for believers who have walked with the Lord for many years: **spiritual boredom and stagnation**.

How easily we settle for the "milk" and the "foundation stones". We enjoy simple, uplifting thoughts about God's love and forgiveness (which are wonderful and true!), but we shy away from the "solid food" that challenges us to think more deeply about God's character, to crucify our own idols, to handle suffering maturely and to invest in discipling others. For years we attend worship services and read devotionals, yet remain perpetual "spiritual consumers" instead of becoming spiritual providers and teachers for the next generation.

Look at the author's secret of spiritual growth in verse 14: **the spiritual gym of daily practice.** Why do some Christians struggle to discern God's will or to stand firm when a crisis or temptation strikes? Because their spiritual faculties have not been *trained* (*gegymnasmena*) in the small decisions of every day. You do not become mature simply by reading more books; you become mature when you obediently put into practice the truth you already know today. Every time you choose to forgive, to speak the truth when it costs you, or to lean on Christ's righteousness instead of your own "dead works", you exercise your spiritual muscles and build walls on the foundation of your faith.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- **Consumer or provider?** Verse 12 says: "For though by this time you ought to be teachers …" Considering how long you have known the Lord, are you already able to nourish and guide younger believers, your family or friends spiritually – or are you still living off other people's spiritual energy?
- **The state of your spiritual ears:** Where might you have become *nōthros* (sluggish or dull) in hearing because God's word asked you for a change that felt too uncomfortable?
- **Your daily "gym" (*gymnazō*):** Which specific spiritual habits are you practising at the moment to keep your discernment between good and evil, truth and lies, sharp in a confusing world?

## Prayer

*Heavenly Father, I bow before you today with an open and teachable heart. Forgive me for the times I have grown spiritually lazy and sluggish in listening to your voice. Forgive me where I have been content to stand still in the same place and to look only for spiritual milk that makes me feel comfortable, while you are calling me to mature devotion and service.*

*Lord Jesus, thank you for the unshakeable foundation of my faith – that you have saved me from dead works and given me the sure hope of the resurrection. But I do not want to stay sitting at the foundation. By the power of your Holy Spirit I want to go on to maturity. I want to understand and live out the depth and richness of your word of righteousness.*

*Train my spiritual faculties today in the gym of everyday life. Give me sharp discernment to distinguish good from evil in every choice, and make me able to teach and encourage others around me in your truth with wisdom and love. Amen.*
