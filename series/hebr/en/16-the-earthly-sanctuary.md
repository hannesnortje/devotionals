## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

At the end of chapter 8 the author declared that with the coming of the new covenant God "has made the first one old" and that it "is close to disappearing". Now, in Hebrews 9:1–10, he takes us on a reverent guided tour of the Old Testament tabernacle in the wilderness (Exodus 25–40). For his Jewish readers the architecture, the gold and the rituals of the sanctuary were the highest symbol of God's presence on earth. The author does not look down on these things; on the contrary, he describes their splendour with great respect. But he shows that the very design and daily running of the tabernacle carried a built-in message from the Holy Spirit: **"No entry – wait for the Redeemer!"**

The author deliberately focuses on the original wilderness tabernacle of Moses (not on the later temples of Solomon or Herod), because it was the original blueprint. When a priest stepped through the first curtain, he entered the front tent, which is called "the Holy Place". On his left (the south side) stood the seven-branched golden lampstand (*menorah*) that gave light; on his right (the north side) was the table with the twelve loaves, which continually represented the twelve tribes of Israel before God's face.

Further in, screened off by a thick, heavy second curtain, was the innermost, cube-shaped room: the Most Holy Place. According to verse 4 it "had a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant". In Exodus 30:6 the incense altar physically stood just *in front of* the curtain in the Holy Place, but theologically and in its function it belonged inseparably to the Most Holy Place: the fragrant smoke was meant to pass through the curtain and fill the Most Holy Place, and on the great Day of Atonement the high priest had to carry the censer right into the Most Holy Place (Leviticus 16:12–13). Inside (or right beside) the ark, "covered on all sides with gold", were three memorials of Israel's history:

- **"A golden jar holding the manna"** (Exodus 16:33) – a reminder of God's faithful provision and of the people's ungrateful grumbling about his bread.
- **"Aaron's staff that had budded"** (Numbers 17:8) – a reminder of God's choice of the priesthood in the midst of the people's rebellion against his authority.
- **"The tablets of the covenant"** (Deuteronomy 10:5) – God's law, which the people had broken. Above the ark were "the cherubim of glory, overshadowing the atonement cover" (v. 5) – the very place where the blood was sprinkled to cover the verdict of the broken law inside the ark!

### Insights from the Greek

- **An earthly sanctuary (*hagion kosmikon* – ἅγιον κοσμικόν, v. 1):** The first covenant had "regulations for worship" (*dikaiōmata latreias* – δικαιώματα λατρείας) "and an earthly sanctuary". The Greek word *kosmikon* does not mean "sinful" or "worldly-minded" here, but **earthly, material, part of this created, physical cosmos** (in contrast to the heavenly sanctuary of 8:2 and 9:11).
- **The Holy Place (*Hagia* – Ἅγια) and the loaves set out (*hē prothesis tōn artōn* – ἡ πρόθεσις τῶν ἄρτων, v. 2):** In the first tent were "the lampstand and the table and the loaves set out; this is called the Holy Place". Literally the Greek speaks of the setting out of the loaves.
- **The second curtain (*katapetasma* – καταπέτασμα) and the Most Holy Place (*Hagia Hagiōn* – Ἅγια Ἁγίων, v. 3):** "Behind the second curtain was a tent called the Most Holy Place."
- **Altar of incense (*thymiatērion* – θυμιατήριον, v. 4):** The word can mean the altar of incense, but in the Greek Old Testament also a censer. The author says the inner tent "had" it – it belonged to it (see above).
- **Atonement cover (*hilastērion* – ἱλαστήριον, v. 5):** The golden lid of the ark, in Hebrew *kapporet* – the place where the high priest sprinkled the blood on the Day of Atonement.
- **Sins committed in ignorance (*agnoēmatōn* – ἀγνοημάτων, v. 7):** The ordinary priests went into the first tent continually to tend the lamps, burn incense and replace the bread – and the ordinary people were not even allowed in there! But "into the second only the high priest goes, once a year, and not without blood": only *one man*, only on one day of the year (*Yom Kippur*, the great Day of Atonement), and only with blood, to make atonement for himself and for "the sins the people have committed in ignorance". *Agnoēmata* are sins committed out of human weakness and error, in contrast to defiant, arrogant rebellion, for which there was no sacrifice under the law (see Numbers 15:30).
- **A symbol (*parabolē* – παραβολὴ, v. 9):** What did the Holy Spirit teach through this strict architectural separation? Verse 8 gives the answer: "the way into the sanctuary has not yet been revealed while the first tent still has standing". The whole earthly tabernacle system with its heavy curtains cried out that a barrier still stood between a holy God and sinful people. It is "a symbol for the present time" (*parabolē* – from which we get our word "parable": an object lesson or illustration).
- **The conscience (*kata syneidēsin* – κατὰ συνείδησιν, v. 9):** And where did the great shortcoming of all those animal sacrifices and gifts lie? Verse 9 goes to the heart of it: they "cannot make the worshipper perfect with respect to conscience". The Greek word for conscience, *syneidēsis* (literally "knowing together with oneself"), refers to our inner moral awareness that knows we stand guilty before a holy God.
- **Regulations for the flesh (*dikaiōmata sarkos* – δικαιώματα σαρκὸς) and washings (*baptismois* – βαπτισμοῖς, v. 10):** The Old Testament rules about "food and drink and various washings" were only "regulations for the flesh". They could cleanse your *outside* (your body) ceremonially, so that you were allowed back into the camp of Israel, but they could never reach your *inside* to wash the stain of guilt from your soul!
- **The time of setting things right (*kairou diorthōseōs* – καιροῦ διορθώσεως, v. 10):** These were only temporary measures, "imposed until the time of setting things right". The word *diorthōsis* was used, among other things, for setting a broken bone or for the radical reform of a broken system. With the coming of Christ that great *diorthōsis* – the new order that sets everything straight – has finally dawned!

### Core Theological Truths

1. **God is holy, and sinful people do not have access to him in themselves.** The whole design of the tabernacle – two tents, a thick curtain, one man, once a year, never without blood – made this truth visible.
2. **The tabernacle was a lesson from the Holy Spirit.** As long as the first tent still had standing, the way into God's presence had not yet been revealed. The old order itself pointed ahead to the one who would open the way.
3. **Outward religion cannot cleanse the conscience.** Sacrifices, gifts, food laws and washings touch only the flesh; the guilt of the heart remains untouched.
4. **Even under the old covenant God provided in grace.** Between the holy presence of God and the law the people had broken lay the atonement cover with the blood – a pointer to Christ and to the time of setting things right that has dawned in him.

## Living It Out

Hebrews 9:1–10 forces us to look honestly at one of the deepest human problems that every person on earth struggles with: **a guilty and restless conscience (*syneidēsis*).**

Think of how people today (and even we as believers) try to silence a nagging conscience. We may no longer have a physical tabernacle with bulls and goats, but we create our own "regulations for the flesh" and "first-tent rituals" (vv. 9–10). When we feel guilty about our shortcomings, we try to make up for it with outward religious activity: we give a little more money, we do charitable work, we make new resolutions, or we punish ourselves with days of self-reproach.

But just as soap and water can only wash the mud off your hands and can never erase a lie or a selfish act from your past, no outward religious effort can ever cleanse your conscience before God. You keep feeling as if a thick "curtain" hangs between you and the Father, and that you must keep a safe distance in the outer court.

This passage prepares our hearts to embrace the glory of Christ (which we will read about tomorrow in vv. 11–14) with tears of gratitude:

- **Admit the bankruptcy of outward religion:** Stop trying to buy your soul's peace with outward duties that touch only the "flesh". Admit that no human repetition of good works can erase the guilt of the past.
- **See how God already provided for grace under the old covenant:** Look again at verses 4 and 5. Inside the ark of the covenant lay the reminders of human sin (the stone tablets of the law the people had broken, Aaron's staff as a sign against the rebellion against his priesthood, the jar of manna recalling the grumbling about the bread). Had God's holy eyes looked directly on that sin in the ark, the people would have been consumed. But *between* the eyes of the cherubim and the law in the ark lay the **atonement cover (*hilastērion*)** with the blood on it! In Christ the Father no longer looks at the archive of your failures; he looks at you through the atonement cover of his Son's blood!

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- **Are you still living in front of a closed curtain?** Does your relationship with God sometimes still feel like the old tabernacle – as if he were hidden far away behind thick curtains and you could only communicate with him from a distance through dutiful rituals?
- **How do you deal with a restless conscience?** When your conscience (*syneidēsis*) accuses you of a fault or sin, which outward "washings" or efforts of your own do you often use to make yourself feel better, instead of going to the atonement cover of Christ?
- **The time of setting things right (*diorthōsis*):** Which area of your spiritual thinking has grown "crooked" through legalism and needs the Lord to set it straight today through the truth of the new covenant?

## Prayer

*Holy and triune God, with deep reverence I reflect today on the beauty and holiness of your ancient tabernacle. Every piece of gold, every curtain and every sacrifice bore witness to your spotless purity and showed us how serious my sin is in your eyes.*

*Lord, I confess today that no outward religious ritual, no good resolution and no effort of my own hands can ever make my conscience fully clean before you. Like the objects inside the ark of the covenant, my own history often testifies to broken commandments and ingratitude. If you were to judge me by my own works, I could never stand before you.*

*But I praise you that the time of setting things right has dawned in Jesus Christ! Thank you that the shadows have given way to the full sunlight of your grace, and that Jesus himself has become my atonement cover, covering all my transgressions before your holy eyes. Set my heart free today from all dead, formal religion, and prepare me to understand and receive the full power of Christ's precious blood for my conscience. Amen.*
