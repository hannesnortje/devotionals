## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Today we begin a journey of twenty-eight days through the letter to the Hebrews, in which we will explore the unrivalled greatness of Christ and the earnest call to persevere.

The letter to the Hebrews is one of the most masterful theological documents in the New Testament. Unlike Paul's letters, it does not open with a traditional greeting or with the name of its author. It launches straight in, like a powerful sermon speaking directly to the heart of a persecuted and discouraged congregation. The first readers were Jewish Christians (perhaps in or near Rome, cf. 13:24) who were under enormous social and physical pressure. While Judaism enjoyed Roman protection as a recognised religion (what was later called a *religio licita*), Christianity was increasingly seen as a suspect new sect. These believers were therefore tempted to return to the safe, visible rituals of the Old Testament temple service, the priesthood and the law of Moses.

The author's purpose is simple but vital: to draw back the curtain of heaven and show them that **Jesus Christ is infinitely better, greater and more exalted** than anything the old order could offer. To turn back to the shadows when full sunlight has already dawned would be spiritual suicide. In the Greek text, verses 1 to 4 form a single, breathtaking, poetic sentence that sets out the majesty of Christ in seven colossal truths.

### Insights from the Greek

Let us dig into the riches of the original Greek terms in this opening:

- **"In many parts and in many ways" (*polymerōs kai polytropōs* – πολυμερῶς καὶ πολυτρόπως, v. 1):** The author opens with a beautiful Greek alliteration: in the past God spoke *polymerōs* ("in many parts", "many times") and *polytropōs* ("in many ways") – through dreams, visions, burning bushes, laws and prophecies. No single Old Testament prophet had the whole picture; each held one piece of the puzzle. But *"at the end of these days"* (the messianic age that dawned with the coming of Jesus) God no longer speaks merely through messengers. He speaks *in Son* (the Greek literally has *en huiō* – ἐν υἱῷ, without the article).
- **"The worlds" (*aiōnas* – αἰῶνας, v. 2):** Christ has been appointed *"heir of everything"*. Everything that exists flows from him and moves back towards him. More than that, it was through him that God *"made the worlds"*. The Greek word here, *aiōnas*, does not mean only the physical globe (*kosmos* – κόσμος), but the "ages": time, space, matter and history itself.
- **"The radiance" (*apaugasma* – ἀπαύγασμα, v. 3):** *Apaugasma* means the radiance or reflection of light. Just as you cannot separate the sun from its rays, and just as we can only see and experience the sun through the rays that reach the earth, so Christ is the visible radiance of God's invisible glory. He does not merely reflect God's light as the moon reflects the sun; he radiates God's own glory.
- **"The very stamp of his being" (*charaktēr tēs hypostaseōs* – χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως, v. 3):** The word for "stamp" is *charaktēr* (from which we get our word "character"). In the ancient world it referred to the exact impression a signet ring makes in warm wax, or the image struck on a coin. The impression in the wax shows every finest line and detail of the original seal. Christ is the exact expression of God's being (*hypostasis*: his deepest reality and nature).
- **"He carries all things" (*pherōn* – φέρων, v. 3):** Jesus *"carries all things by the powerful word"*. The Greek participle *pherōn* is not a passive word, like the mythological Atlas balancing a dead weight on his shoulders. It is a dynamic, active word: to carry something forward towards a destination.
- **"Purification" (*katharismon* – καθαρισμόν, v. 3):** From the cosmic creation the author suddenly moves to our personal salvation: *"When he himself had made purification for sins, he took his seat at the right hand of the Majesty on high."* In the Old Testament tabernacle and temple there were splendid furnishings: the altar of burnt offering, the lampstand, the table with the bread of the Presence. But there was **no chair**. Why? Because the human priests' work was never finished. Day after day they had to stand and offer sacrifices. But when Jesus had offered his own blood, he *sat down*.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **God spoke step by step, and Christ is his final Word.** Jesus does not merely bring a message from God; **he is God's final, perfect Message.**
2. **Christ is heir and creator of the universe.** The one who hung on a Roman cross is the same one who created the galaxies and the ages of history.
3. **Christ reveals God completely.** He radiates God's glory because he himself is God. To look at Jesus' compassion, holiness and truth is to look straight into the Father's heart.
4. **Christ sustains everything and steers it to its goal.** He does not only hold the atoms of the universe together at this moment so that it does not collapse into chaos; he is actively steering the whole of history towards God's ultimate purpose.
5. **The purification is finished, and the King is seated.** The work of redemption is complete once and for all, and he now sits in the place of highest authority, at the right hand of the Majesty.

## Living It Out

When life becomes overwhelming and our plans fall apart, our spiritual sight often narrows to tunnel vision. Like the first readers of Hebrews, we are tempted to fall back on what we can see, feel and control: our own efforts, our religious duties, or worldly security. We grow tired of trusting an invisible God.

Hebrews 1:1–4 heals our spiritual short-sightedness by lifting our eyes to the greatness of Christ. If Jesus really created the *aiōnas* (the ages and the universe) and is *actively carrying* everything at this very moment by the powerful word, then your life is not out of control. The uncertainty about your future, your family, your health or your finances is not too heavy for the one who sustains galaxies by his mere word.

What is more, you no longer need to "scurry around" spiritually like an Old Testament priest trying to win God's favour. Jesus is *seated*. When guilt or old failures accuse you and tell you that you must first do "enough" for God again before you may come to him, this passage reminds you that the purification of your sins has already been completed by him himself. We rest in his finished work.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- **Where are you looking for a "new word"?** Do you often look for signs, feelings or human opinions to find peace, while God has already given his final, clearest Word in his Son and in Scripture?
- **Which part of your life feels as if it is falling apart today?** How does your anxiety change when you realise that Christ not only created the universe, but is actively *carrying* (*pherōn*) your life by the powerful word?
- **Are you still standing, or are you seated with Christ?** Are you still trying to pay for your sins through your own performance or religious dutifulness – or do you truly rest in the fact that Jesus has already sat down after making your purification?

## Prayer

*Heavenly Father, today I bow before you in deep wonder. Thank you that you did not leave me in the dark to guess who you are or what your heart is like. Thank you that at the end of these days you have spoken to me through your Son, Jesus Christ.*

*Lord Jesus, forgive me that I so often make you small in my thoughts while I make my problems and fears so big. You are the radiance of God's glory and the very stamp of his being. Through you the universe and the ages were made, and by the power of your one word you are carrying all things at this moment – including my life, my breath and my future.*

*Thank you that on the cross you yourself made full purification for my sins, and that you are now seated at the right hand of the Majesty. Teach my soul today to stop toiling to earn your favour, and help me to become still and rest in your finished work. Open my eyes on this journey through Hebrews to see you greater, brighter and mightier than ever before. Amen.*
