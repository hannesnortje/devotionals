## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With chapter 8 we enter the theological climax of the letter to the Hebrews (chapters 8 to 10). In chapter 7 the author showed us that the *person* of our high priest (according to the order of Melchizedek) is infinitely higher than the Levitical priests. Now, in chapter 8, he shifts the focus to the **work and ministry** of this high priest: he ministers in a **better sanctuary** (vv. 1–5) and is the mediator of a **better covenant** enacted on **better promises** (vv. 6–13).

For first-century Jewish Christians it was an enormous emotional struggle to be cut off from the magnificent temple in Jerusalem. Their non-Christian Jewish relatives and neighbours probably mocked them and asked: *"Where is your temple? Where is your visible priest, your altar? You have nothing!"* The author answers this charge by drawing back the veils of the visible world and showing that the earthly temple was never the real thing – it was only a temporary architectural sketch of the true, heavenly reality where Jesus now ministers.

To prove it, the author quotes Exodus 25:40, where God warned Moses on Mount Sinai to build everything exactly "according to the pattern" that was shown him on the mountain (v. 5). Then he gives the longest single Old Testament quotation in the whole New Testament: **Jeremiah 31:31–34** (vv. 8–12). He quotes it as the Greek translation (the Septuagint) has it, and at one point it differs from the Hebrew: where the Hebrew of Jeremiah 31:32 says "although I was their husband (or master)", the Greek reads "and I disregarded them" (v. 9).

Verse 13 closes with a prophetic remark: what is becoming old and growing aged "is close to disappearing". If the letter was written before AD 70, as many think, the temple in Jerusalem with its sacrifices was indeed "close to disappearing" at the time – and in AD 70 it finally disappeared.

### Insights from the Greek

- **The main point (*kephalaion* – κεφάλαιον, v. 1):** The author begins with the word *kephalaion*: the main point, the crowning point or core of the whole argument, not merely a summary. What is this core? "We have such a high priest, who took his seat at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens." While the earthly priests had to stand every day because their work was never finished (see Hebrews 10:11), our high priest has sat down.
- **Minister of the true tent (*leitourgos* – λειτουργὸς; *tēs skēnēs tēs alēthinēs* – τῆς σκηνῆς τῆς ἀληθινῆς, v. 2):** Verse 2 calls him "a minister in the sanctuary and in the true tent, which the Lord pitched, not a human being". A *leitourgos* is a public servant who acts in the sanctuary on behalf of the people. The word *skēnē* means "tent" – it is the word for the tabernacle of Moses. In biblical Greek the opposite of "true" (*alēthinos*) here is not "false" or "lying", but *shadowy, provisional*. The tabernacle that Moses set up in the wilderness was good and commanded by God, but it was a temporary replica; the heavenly sanctuary that the Lord himself pitched is the eternal, original reality.
- **Copy, shadow and pattern (*hypodeigmati kai skia* – ὑποδείγματι καὶ σκιᾷ; *typon* – τύπον, v. 5):** On earth Jesus would not even be a priest (v. 4); after all, he was born of the tribe of Judah, not of Levi (7:14). But his ministry does not take place on earth! The Levitical priests only serve "a copy and shadow of the heavenly things" – a *hypodeigma* is a copy or sketch. Moses had to build everything "according to the pattern" (*typos* – the blueprint, pattern or stamp) shown to him on the mountain. Just as an architect's scale model, or a shadow on the ground, shows that a massive, solid building stands in the sunlight, so the whole Old Testament tent worship pointed ahead to Christ's ministry in heaven.
- **Mediator of a better covenant (*mesitēs* – μεσίτης; *nenomothetētai* – νενομοθέτηται, v. 6):** Jesus' ministry is so far superior because he is "mediator of a better covenant, which has been enacted on better promises". A *mesitēs* is the person who stands between two parties to ratify a peace and an agreement. The verb *nenomothetētai* ("has been enacted") is a word of lawgiving: the covenant has been established with full legal authority on better promises.
- **Faultless? (*amemptos* – ἄμεμπτος; *memphomenos autous* – μεμφόμενος αὐτοὺς, vv. 7–8):** Why was a new covenant needed? Verses 7 and 8 are crystal clear: if the first covenant (the Mosaic covenant at Sinai) had been "faultless" and could really save and change people, God would never have promised a second. And where did the fault lie? Not with God or his holy law, but with broken, sinful people! Look at verse 8: "For he finds fault with **them** when he says." The old covenant demanded: *"Do this and you will live"* (see Leviticus 18:5), but it gave people no inner power to do it.
- **A new covenant (*diathēkēn kainēn* – διαθήκην καινήν, vv. 8 and 13):** For "new" the author uses *kainos* here. It is often distinguished from *neos*: not only new in time, but **new in kind** – fresh and of a completely different quality. (The distinction is not strict; in 12:24 Hebrews also calls the new covenant *nea*.) Why are the promises of the new covenant so infinitely better than those of Sinai? Because God no longer says *"If you …"*, but **"I will!"**
- **For (*hoti* – ὅτι), merciful (*hileōs* – ἵλεως) and no more (*ou mē mnēsthō eti* – οὐ μὴ μνησθῶ ἔτι, v. 12):** Notice the little word "For" (*hoti*) at the start of verse 12! Why are inner change and intimate fellowship with God possible? *Because* God promises: "For I will be merciful toward their unrighteous deeds, and I will remember their sins no more." *Hileōs* means reconciled and gracious. In the Greek there is a double negative, *ou mē mnēsthō eti*: I will **never, ever again** call them to mind against them!
- **Made old (*pepalaiōken* – πεπαλαίωκεν, v. 13):** By speaking of a "new" covenant, God "has made the first one old" (*pepalaiōken* – worn out and obsolete).

### Core Theological Truths

Here are the four great promises of the new covenant (vv. 10–12):

1. **Inner transformation instead of outward tablets of stone (v. 10a).** "I will put my laws into their minds, and I will write them on their hearts." At Sinai the law was engraved from the outside on cold stone, and it could only accuse people. In the new covenant the Holy Spirit engraves God's will from the inside on the soft tablets of our hearts and renews our desires, so that we *want* to do what God asks.
2. **An intimate covenant relationship (v. 10b).** "I will be their God, and they shall be my people." What was broken by Israel's unfaithfulness is restored by Christ so that it can never be broken.
3. **Direct, personal knowledge of God (v. 11).** "For they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest." Under the old covenant ordinary people depended on go-betweens – prophets, priests and kings – to know the Lord at a distance, and most members of the nation were unregenerate. In the new covenant every true child of the covenant – from the youngest believer to the oldest – knows the Lord personally and directly through the Spirit.
4. **The foundation of everything: complete and final forgiveness (v. 12).** God will be merciful toward their unrighteous deeds and will remember their sins no more. This is the ground on which all the other promises rest.

## Living It Out

Hebrews 8 touches the deepest nerve of our daily walk with God: **Are you living today under the old covenant of outward pressure, or under the new covenant of inner grace?**

Even though we are New Testament believers, our thoughts often slip back to Sinai without our noticing. We see God's commandments as a cold list of rules outside ourselves ("on tablets of stone"), and then we try, with clenched jaws and sheer willpower, to force ourselves to be good. And every time we stumble, we think God is sitting there with a notebook, digging up all our past failures to punish us. The result is that we keep God at a safe distance, full of guilt and spiritual weariness.

Look today with new eyes at the **better promises** of which Jesus is the mediator for you:

- **From tablets of stone to your heart (v. 10):** Christian holiness is not a matter of trying harder out of fear of punishment; it is the supernatural work of the Spirit, who writes God's love and will from the inside on your mind and heart. When you are struggling with a particular sin or attitude, you do not have to tackle it in your own strength – you may ask the author of the new covenant to engrave his truth more deeply on your heart and to change your desires from within.
- **From an archive of guilt to a clean page (v. 12):** Our human memory (and the accuser) loves to dig up old sins we confessed long ago, like ghosts from the past. But verse 12 says that God chooses **never, ever again** to remember your confessed sins! This does not mean that the all-knowing God suffers memory loss; it means that he promises never again to use your sin against you or to treat you according to your past, because Jesus has paid the debt in full in the true heavenly sanctuary.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- **Shadow or reality?** Do you sometimes look for your spiritual security in outward forms, rituals and human approval ("shadows"), instead of lifting your eyes to Christ, who now sits as your high priest in the true heavenly sanctuary?
- **Tablets of stone or tablets of the heart?** Do you currently experience God's will for your life as a heavy, outward burden you must try to keep – or do you let the Holy Spirit write God's truth from within on your mind and your love?
- **Do you really believe verse 12?** Is there a particular sin or failure from your past that you have already confessed to the Lord, yet for which you still accuse yourself? What does it mean for your peace today when the King of the universe says: "I will remember their sins no more"?

## Prayer

*Gracious and faithful Father, I praise you that today I do not have to stand at the foot of a smoking mountain of law and judgment, but that through my high priest, Jesus Christ, I may share in a new and infinitely better covenant.*

*Lord Jesus, thank you that you do not minister in an earthly shadow-temple, but sit in the true heavenly tent at the right hand of the Majesty as the mediator of better promises. Forgive me for all the times I still try to live as if I were under the old covenant – when I try to make myself acceptable to you through my own outward strength and willpower.*

*Father, fulfil your precious promise within me today: by your Holy Spirit write your words and your will deep in my mind and on the soft tablets of my heart, so that it becomes my joy to obey you. And thank you for the overwhelming wonder of verse 12 – that because of the blood of Jesus you are merciful toward my unrighteous deeds and will never, ever again remember my sins. Let me live today in the freedom and intimacy of this truth, as someone who knows you personally. Amen.*
