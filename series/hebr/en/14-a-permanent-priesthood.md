## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

With Hebrews 7:11–28 we reach the breathtaking summit of the section on our great high priest in chapters 4 to 7. Having set out the historical picture of Melchizedek in the first ten verses of chapter 7, the author now applies it directly to the person and work of Jesus Christ.

For a first-century Jew the law of Moses (*Torah*) and the Levitical temple service were two inseparable pillars: the priesthood administered the law, and the law regulated the priesthood. Here the author dares to set off a radical theological earthquake: he shows that the old covenant was never meant to be permanent. Why? Because from the start it was unable to solve fully the deepest human problem – a defiled conscience and separation from God.

The author begins with a sharp, logical question: "Now if perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood", why did David, centuries after the law was given, prophesy in Psalm 110:4 that *another* priest would arise according to the order of Melchizedek? What is more, "it is clear that our Lord has arisen from Judah" (v. 14), the royal tribe in connection with which Moses said nothing about priests.

Under the old covenant there was an endless relay race of priests. According to the Jewish historian Josephus there were eighty-three high priests from Aaron to the destruction of the temple in AD 70. Why so many? Because death kept catching up with them! Just when you had built up a relationship of trust with a wise, compassionate priest, he died, and you had to start again with his successor.

### Insights from the Greek

- **Perfection (*teleiōsis* – τελείωσις, v. 11):** *Teleiōsis* is perfect reconciliation and unhindered access to God. The Levitical priesthood could not bring it, "for the law made nothing perfect" (v. 19).
- **A fleshly commandment versus an indestructible life (*entolēs sarkinēs* – ἐντολῆς σαρκίνης; *zōēs akatalytou* – ζωῆς ἀκαταλύτου, v. 16):** Jesus has become a priest "not according to a law of a fleshly commandment" – outward rules about physical descent and family trees – "but according to the power of an indestructible life". His resurrection from the dead proved that nothing can dissolve or destroy his life.
- **Setting aside (*athetēsis* – ἀθέτησις, v. 18):** That is why in verse 18 "a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness". *Athetēsis* is a legal term for annulling or setting aside an old regulation. The law was not bad, but it was like a signpost: a signpost can show you where the destination is, but it has no power to get you there.
- **Draw near to God (*engizomen tō theō* – ἐγγίζομεν τῷ θεῷ, v. 19):** In its place "a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God". Under the old order the people had to keep their distance; under the new order we are invited to come near!
- **Guarantor (*engyos* – ἔγγυος, v. 22):** No Levitical priest was ever appointed with a divine oath; they simply inherited the office by birth. But Christ was appointed by the Father with a solemn sworn oath (Psalm 110:4). And precisely for that reason Jesus has become "the guarantor of a better covenant" (v. 22). The word *engyos* occurs only here in the whole New Testament. It is a stronger word than "mediator" (*mesitēs* – μεσίτης, 8:6). A mediator negotiates between two parties, but an *engyos* (guarantor) is someone who puts his own life, property and honour at stake to guarantee personally that all the terms of the agreement will be kept. If we should fail, the guarantor pays! Christ does not only guarantee God's promises to us; on the cross he also settled our whole debt to God.
- **A permanent priesthood (*aparabaton* – ἀπαράβατον, v. 24):** Jesus remains for ever, and therefore he "holds his priesthood permanently". *Aparabatos* literally means "inviolable" or "non-transferable" – his office will never pass to anyone else; he never retires.
- **Completely (*eis to panteles* – εἰς τὸ παντελὲς) and intercede (*entynchanein* – ἐντυγχάνειν, v. 25):** Then comes the golden crown jewel of chapter 7: "That is why he is able to save completely those who approach God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them." The phrase *eis to panteles* has a double shade in Greek: it means **completely, utterly** (to the deepest corner of our brokenness) and **for all time** (to the very end of time). Why is his salvation so perfect? Because his death on the cross is followed by his unceasing intercession (*entynchanein*) at the Father's right hand!
- **The perfect high priest (*hosios, akakos, amiantos* – ὅσιος, ἄκακος, ἀμίαντος, v. 26):** The author closes the chapter with a hymn of praise to Christ's character. He is *holy* (*hosios* – inwardly devoted to God), *innocent* (*akakos* – free of any evil or malice towards people), *undefiled* (*amiantos* – free of any ritual or moral defilement), and in his glorified state "separated from sinners, and become higher than the heavens".
- **Once for all (*ephapax* – ἐφάπαξ, v. 27):** Unlike Aaron's descendants, who had to stand and offer sacrifices day after day, Jesus had no sin of his own. And when he offered a sacrifice, he did not bring the blood of goats or calves – **he offered up himself!** Notice the mighty little word in verse 27: "for this he did once for all when he offered up himself." *Ephapax* is one of the key words of Hebrews (7:27; 9:12; 10:10): it describes something so final, perfect and conclusively done that it never needs to be repeated.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **The old covenant was never meant to be permanent.** The law and the Levitical priesthood could not bring perfection; that is why God himself announced another priest according to the order of Melchizedek.
2. **Christ's priesthood rests on his resurrection life and on God's oath.** Not descent, but "the power of an indestructible life" and the Father's sworn oath make him priest.
3. **Christ is our guarantor and our intercessor.** He guarantees the better covenant with his own life, and he always lives to intercede for us; that is why he can save completely.
4. **His sacrifice is once for all.** The Son, "made perfect for ever" (v. 28), offered up himself. Nothing can be added to his finished work.

## Living It Out

How does Hebrews 7:11–28 change the way you handle your faith and your failures today?

First, it sets us free from the **slavery of religious "rule-keeping"**. Just like the first readers, we so easily fall back on a "fleshly commandment" (v. 16) – the idea that our spiritual security depends on how well we keep a little list of outward rules. But rules have never had the power to change a human heart or bring you closer to God; they only expose your weakness (vv. 18–19). Real change and intimate nearness to God flow from "the power of an indestructible life" – the resurrection life of Jesus, who lives in you by his Spirit.

Second, rest today in the wonder of Jesus as your **guarantor (*engyos*)** and your **intercessor**. Perhaps you feel today that your grip on God is very weak. You look at your own fickleness and wonder: *How will I ever make it to the end?* Hear the gospel of verses 22 and 25 today: your perseverance does not depend on your ability to keep the covenant standing in your own strength; Jesus has personally signed as *guarantor* for you!

What is more, he did not just save you on the cross two thousand years ago and then leave you to fend for yourself. He lives **today** – at this very second – to intercede for you (*entynchanein*). As the nineteenth-century Scottish preacher Robert Murray M'Cheyne once said: *"If I could hear Christ praying for me in the next room, I would not fear a million enemies. Yet distance makes no difference. He is praying for me."* Because he prays for you, he is able to save you **completely** (*eis to panteles*) – from every sin, through every temptation, all the way into eternal glory.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- **Rules or resurrection power?** Are you currently trying to keep your spiritual life going through outward laws and duties (a "fleshly commandment") – or do you live each day from the inner power of Christ's indestructible life?
- **The reach of his salvation (*eis to panteles*):** Is there a particular besetting sin, a deep-rooted fear or a broken part of your life that you secretly think even Jesus cannot really save or heal? What does verse 25 say to that unbelief?
- **The once-for-all sacrifice (*ephapax*):** If Jesus' sacrifice on the cross really was enough *once for all* to take away your sins, why do you still sometimes punish yourself with old feelings of guilt, as if you had to add something to his sacrifice yourself?
