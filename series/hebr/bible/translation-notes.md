# Hebrews – translation notes (for review)

Base text: **SBL Greek New Testament** (SBLGNT), © 2010 Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Textual-variant markers were removed, and the text follows SBLGNT throughout. This includes places where SBLGNT differs from the older Bibles; those are listed in §2 when they matter.
Aim: close to the Greek, natural to read, and each key word rendered the same way every time it occurs, in all three languages. Key words shared with 1 and 2 Timothy use the renderings of `series/1tim` and `series/2tim` translation-notes §1 (DECISIONS.md, 4 Nov). Divine names: "Herr / Lord / Here" in normal case, since there is no YHWH in the NT, also in OT quotations. Hebrews addresses its readers in the plural throughout: *ihr / you / julle*.
OT quotations are translated as the Greek of Hebrews has them (mostly Septuagint), not adjusted to the Hebrew (DECISIONS.md). Poetic quotations (Psalms, prophets, also 2 Sam 7:14 because it is paired with Ps 2:7) are set as `> ` lines. Prose quotations and short repeated fragments (4:4 Gen 2:2; 4:5) stay in the running text in quotation marks.
The translation is done in three parts: part 1 = chapters 1–5 (this part), part 2 = 6–10, part 3 = 11–13. Parts 2 and 3 append their chapters to the three Bible files and their own items to §2.
Verse count checked against the base text, part 1: chapter 1 = 14, 2 = 18, 3 = 19, 4 = 16, 5 = 14 (81 verses) in all three languages.

## 2. Choices you should check

### Chapters 1–5

> **Decided by Hannes (4 Nov 2026): "by the grace of God" (*chariti theou*, majority reading, NA28) – the only deliberate departure from SBLGNT in this translation. Text: "damit er durch Gottes Gnade für jeden den Tod schmeckte / so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone / sodat Hy deur die genade van God vir elkeen die dood sou smaak".**

1. **2:9 *chōris theou* "ohne Gott / apart from God / sonder God".** Note first: SBLGNT does **not** read *chariti theou* here. It is one of the few editions that prints the minority reading χωρὶς θεοῦ ("apart from God"; a few manuscripts and patristic writers known to Origen and Jerome). Most manuscripts and NA28 read χάριτι θεοῦ, "by the grace of God" (Luther, 1933/53, 1983, ESV, NIV). I followed the base text as everywhere else: "damit er ohne Gott für jeden den Tod schmeckte / so that apart from God he might taste death for everyone / sodat Hy sonder God vir elkeen die dood sou smaak". Readings of *chōris theou*:
   - abandoned by God in his death (cf. Mark 15:34)
   - for everyone/everything *except* God (picking up 2:8 "nothing that is not subject to him")
   - in his divine nature not affected by death (a later Christological reading)

   If you prefer the majority reading, the alternative wording is "damit er durch Gottes Gnade für jeden den Tod schmeckte / so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone / sodat Hy deur die genade van God vir elkeen die dood sou smaak". That would be our first deliberate departure from SBLGNT, and it should go into DECISIONS.md. Your Day 4 devotional (2:5–9) should match whichever you choose.
2. **1:8 *ho thronos sou ho theos*.** "Dein Thron, o Gott, … / Your throne, O God, … / U troon, o God, …". *Ho theos* read as a vocative addresses the Son as God, which is the reading of most translations and fits the contrast with the angels in 1:7. The grammatically possible alternative is "Gott ist dein Thron in alle Ewigkeit / God is your throne for ever and ever / God is u troon tot in alle ewigheid". 1:9 "darum hat dich Gott, dein Gott, gesalbt" also allows *ho theos* (1st) to be a vocative ("therefore, O God, your God has anointed you"). I kept the usual reading there. SBLGNT reads "your kingdom" (σου) at the end of 1:8, not "his kingdom".
3. **5:7 *eisakoustheis apo tēs eulabeias*.** "erhört worden wegen seiner Ehrfurcht / heard because of his reverence / verhoor weens sy eerbied". *Eulabeia* is the same word as in 12:28 (and the verb in 11:7): reverent awe, godly fear. Options:
   - (a) heard *because of* his reverence – his submission ("not my will"), as translated
   - (b) heard *and freed from* his fear/anguish (*apo* = "from"; Luther 1912 "darum, dass er Gott in Ehren hatte" is (a); some read (b))

   "Retten *aus* dem Tod / save *out of* death / red *uit* die dood" is literal (*ek thanatou*): he was not spared dying, but brought out of death. *Prosenegkas* "dargebracht / offered up / aangebied" is the sacrificial verb of 5:1, 3. I kept it for the prayers, so that the link is visible.
4. **3:6 and 3:14 – the conditionals.** Translated literally, with no theology added:
   - 3:6 "Sein Haus sind wir, wenn wir an der Zuversicht und am Stolz auf die Hoffnung festhalten / we are his house, if we hold fast to … / sy huis is ons, as ons vashou aan …" (*ean*)
   - 3:14 "Denn wir sind Teilhaber an Christus geworden – wenn wir nur … festhalten / for we have become partners of Christ – if indeed we hold … / ons het deelgenote van Christus geword – as ons maar … vas behou" (*eanper*, "if indeed")

   The perfect tense "have become" stands next to the condition, and I left that tension standing. SBLGNT omits "firm to the end" (μέχρι τέλους βεβαίαν) in 3:6, which older Bibles have there. It does occur in 3:14. *Hypostasis* in 3:14 is rendered "Gewissheit / assurance / sekerheid". The alternative is "Grundlage / foundation / grondslag", or "Wesen" as in 1:3 (the same word: "the beginning of our [new] being"). Part 3 will need the same choice in 11:1.
5. **2:16 *epilambanetai*.** Literally "take hold of", rendered "ergreifen / take hold of / vasgryp". The same verb is used in 8:9, where God takes the fathers by the hand. Options:
   - (a) help, come to the aid of (most translations; Luther 2017 "nimmt sich … an")
   - (b) take on the nature of (older reading, KJV "took on him the nature of" – reads incarnation into it)

   The literal rendering keeps (a) open and avoids (b).
6. **4:12–13 the word and God.** 4:12 "lebendig und wirksam / living and effective / lewend en werksaam" (*energēs*). "Scheidung von Seele und Geist, von Gelenken und Mark" is an image of penetration to the innermost point, not a teaching on the parts of a human being; I translated the image without explaining it. In 4:13 *enōpion autou* ("before him/it") can refer to the word or to God:
   - DE "vor ihm" keeps both open (Wort is neuter)
   - EN "before him" and AF "voor Hom" (capital) decide for God

   The last clause *pros hon hēmin ho logos* has a play on *logos*: "dem wir Rechenschaft (*logos*) geben müssen / to whom we must give account / aan wie ons rekenskap moet gee". The alternative is "about whom we are speaking", which is weaker. Should EN read "before it" to keep the ambiguity?
7. **1:2 (and 11:3) *tous aiōnas*.** Literally "the ages". 1 Tim 1:17 has "König der Zeitalter / King of the ages / Koning van die eeue". In 1:2 I rendered "die Welten / the worlds / die wêrelde", because Hebrews means the created universe, which is spatial and temporal (cf. 11:3, where the *aiōnes* are framed and visible things come from them). The literal alternative is "durch ihn hat er auch die Zeitalter geschaffen / through him he also made the ages / deur Hom het Hy ook die eeue gemaak". This departs from the carry-over rule, so please confirm.
8. **1:6 *hotan de palin eisagagē*.** "Wenn er aber den Erstgeborenen wiederum in die Welt einführt / when he again brings the firstborn into the world / wanneer Hy die Eersgeborene weer in die wêreld inbring". The word order joins "again" to the verb, which points to the return of Christ, or to his enthronement. The alternative takes *palin* as introducing another quotation ("And again, when he brings …"), which points to the incarnation. DE "wiederum" keeps both readings. EN and AF follow the word order. The quotation itself is Deut 32:43 LXX (cf. Ps 97:7), which is not in the Hebrew of Deut 32.
9. **1:7 *pneumata*.** "Winde / winds / winde" (Ps 104:4: God makes his messengers winds, his servants fire). The same word in 1:14 is "Geister / spirits / geeste". The alternative in 1:7 is "zu Geistern / spirits / geeste", which keeps the link to 1:14 but loses the image of transience that Hebrews contrasts with the Son's permanence (1:10–12).
10. **2:6–8 Psalm 8.**
    - *anthrōpos / huios anthrōpou* "der Mensch / ein Menschensohn"; EN "a human being / a son of man"; AF "die mens / 'n seun van die mens". This keeps the possible link to Jesus without capitalising.
    - *brachy ti* "ein wenig / a little / 'n bietjie" can be degree or time ("for a little while", which many read in 2:9 because of "now … not yet").
    - In 2:8b "him" can be humankind or Jesus. AF lower case ("aan hom") keeps it open until Jesus is named in 2:9.
    - SBLGNT omits "and set him over the works of your hands" in 2:7, which is in the Psalm and in older Bibles.
11. **2:10 *pollous huious* "viele Söhne / many sons / baie seuns".** Kept as "sons" because of the link to the Son (1:2, 5) and to sonship (12:5–8). The alternative is "viele Kinder / many children / baie kinders" (inclusive, but it loses the link). *Adelphoi* is "Geschwister / brothers and sisters / broers en susters" as in 2 Timothy, including in the Psalm quotation in 2:12. *Ex henos* (2:11) "von einem / from one / uit een", lower case, because it can be God, Adam or Abraham.
12. **4:8 *Iēsous* = Joshua.** In Greek the name is the same as "Jesus". The translation has "Josua / Joshua / Josua" because the context is the entry into Canaan. A devotional can point out the wordplay.
13. **4:2 *synkekerasmenous*.** SBLGNT reads the plural ("they were not united by faith with those who heard"). The older Bibles read the singular ("the word was not mixed with faith in those who heard"). I added "wirklich / truly / werklik" ("those who truly heard"), since the point is hearing that obeys (Joshua and Caleb). Delete it if you want it stricter.
14. **3:7–11 Psalm 95.**
    - Hebrews inserts *dio* ("therefore") before "I was indignant", so the forty years go with "saw my works" (3:9–10). In 3:17 they go with God's indignation. I kept the Greek line break.
    - The base text has no "me" after "tested" in 3:9. It was added ("mich / me / My") because the sentence needs an object.
    - 3:15 is joined to 3:16 ("Wenn es heißt: … , wer waren denn …"); it can also be joined to 3:13–14.
    - "Auflehnung / rebellion / opstand" and "Versuchung / testing / beproewing" stand for the place names Meribah and Massah, as in the Septuagint.
15. **1:5; 5:5 Ps 2:7 *gegennēka*.** DE "gezeugt", AF "verwek", EN "fathered" (instead of the older "begotten"). Alternatives: "Vater geworden / become your Father / jou Vader geword", which is interpretive.
16. **1:2 *en huiō*.** No article: "in one who is Son". Rendered "durch den Sohn / through the Son / deur die Seun", parallel to "durch die Propheten" in 1:1. *Polymerōs kai polytropōs* is rendered "Viele Male und auf viele verschiedene Weisen / In many parts and in many ways / Herhaaldelik en op allerlei maniere", which deliberately avoids Luther's "vielfach und auf vielerlei Weise" and AF 1983's "baie kere en op baie maniere". 1:3 SBLGNT reads "durch das machtvolle Wort" without "his" (the word of power), and "selbst" (*di' hautou*) for the purification.


### Chapters 6–10

17. **6:4–6 "impossible … to renew again to repentance".** Translated literally, as one sentence with five aorist participles: "enlightened … tasted … become partners … tasted … **and fallen away**". *Parapesontas* is a participle in the same series, not an "if" clause (KJV "if they shall fall away" and AF 1933 "en afgeval het" differ here; ours keeps the participle). The sentence leaves three things open, and I did not decide them:
    - **Who cannot renew?** *Adynaton … anakainizein* has no subject: impossible for us (the teachers, 6:1–3 "let us…"), for anyone, or for God (cf. 6:18, where "impossible for God to lie" uses the same *adynaton*).
    - **The last participles** *anastaurountas … kai paradeigmatizontas* (present tense) can be:
      - (a) causal: "*because* they crucify the Son of God again", which makes the impossibility absolute
      - (b) temporal: "*as long as* they go on crucifying him", which makes it last only while they persist (some modern commentators)

      EN keeps both with participles ("crucifying … and holding him up"). DE ("sie, die … wieder kreuzigen") and AF ("hulle wat … weer kruisig") use a relative clause, which also keeps both.
    - ***Anastauroō*** is normally "crucify" (*ana* = up); Hebrews' context and the Greek fathers read "crucify *again*". I wrote "wieder kreuzigen / crucify again / weer kruisig". The stricter alternative is "kreuzigen / crucify / kruisig".

    Who are the people described? The text describes real experience (enlightenment, the Spirit, the powers of the age to come), but 6:9 says "we are convinced of better things in your case". Your Day 11 devotional (6:4–12) should keep whichever reading you hold. The translation itself does not take a position. Same verb *phōtizō* in 10:32 "nach eurer Erleuchtung / after you had been enlightened / nadat julle verlig is". This links the two warning passages.

18. **9:15–17 *diathēkē* – covenant or will?** One Greek word, two meanings: "covenant" (the OT sense, 9:15, 18, 20) and "last will and testament" (the ordinary Greek sense). I used **Testament / will / testament in 9:16–17 only** and Bund / covenant / verbond everywhere else, because "the death of the one who made it", "valid only at death" and "never in force while he lives" describe a will most naturally. 9:15 "inheritance" prepares the switch. The play on words cannot be kept in any of the three languages. Options:
    - (a) **as translated** (most modern translations): Hebrews moves from covenant to will and back again, to show why a death was needed
    - (b) **covenant throughout** (e.g. Lane, Hahn): the "one who makes the covenant" stands for the sacrificial victims, whose death ratifies it (cf. Gen 15; Exod 24 in 9:18–20). The wording would then be "Denn wo ein Bund ist, muss der Tod dessen beigebracht werden, der den Bund schließt; denn ein Bund wird über Toten rechtskräftig … / For where there is a covenant, the death of the one who makes the covenant must be brought in; for a covenant is made valid over the dead … / Want waar daar 'n verbond is, moet die dood van die een wat die verbond sluit, ingebring word; want 'n verbond word oor dooies bekragtig …"

    The verb *diatithemai* ("make a covenant", 8:10; 10:16) is the same as *ho diathemenos* ("the one who made it", 9:16, 17). A devotional or the kit can point this out. *Epi nekrois* (9:17) is literally "over/upon dead (people/bodies)"; I rendered "erst mit dem Tod / only at death / eers by die dood".

19. **7:3 "without father, without mother, without genealogy".** Translated literally. Readings:
    - (a) **argument from the silence of Scripture**: Gen 14 records no parents, birth or death for Melchizedek, so "in the text" he "remains a priest"; "made like (*aphōmoiōmenos*) the Son of God" makes the Son the original and Melchizedek the copy
    - (b) **a heavenly figure or an appearance of Christ** (some fathers; Qumran 11QMelch has a heavenly Melchizedek)

    The translation does not decide. AF "aan die Seun van God gelyk gemaak" is literal. "Für immer / for all time / vir altyd" (*eis to diēnekes*) is deliberately different from "in Ewigkeit / for ever / vir ewig" (*eis ton aiōna*, used of Christ, 7:17, 24, 28). Related choices:
    - 7:24 *aparabatos*: "bleibend / permanently / blywend". The alternative is "das nicht auf einen anderen übergeht / that does not pass to another / wat nie na 'n ander oorgaan nie" (no successor)
    - 7:25 *eis to panteles*: "völlig / completely / ten volle". The alternative is temporal: "für immer / for all time / vir altyd"

20. **8:8–12 Jeremiah 31 (LXX 38) quotation.** Translated as Hebrews has it, and it differs from the Hebrew in places:
    - 8:8 "einen neuen Bund vollenden / bring about / voltrek" (*syntelesō*; LXX *diathēsomai* "make")
    - 8:9 "und ich habe mich nicht mehr um sie gekümmert / and I disregarded them / en Ek het My nie meer oor hulle bekommer nie" (LXX; the Hebrew has "although I was their husband/master")
    - 8:9 "als ich … ergriff / took hold of / vasgegryp" is *epilambanomai*, the verb of 2:16, kept the same on purpose

    SBLGNT readings:
    - 8:8 *memphomenos autous* "er tadelt sie / he finds fault with them / Hy berispe hulle": the fault lies with the people. A variant (*autois*) allows "finding fault [with the covenant], he says to them".
    - 8:11 *politēn* "Mitbürger / fellow citizen / medeburger" (older Bibles: "neighbour")
    - 8:12 SBLGNT omits "and their lawless deeds", which older Bibles have and which does occur in 10:17
    - 8:13 *pepalaiōken* "für alt erklärt / made old / oud verklaar": the same verb as 1:11 "wear out like a garment"
    - **10:16–17**: Hebrews quotes again, shortened ("with them"; hearts and minds reversed). The Greek has no "then he says" before 10:17. I kept 10:16–17 as one quotation after "nachdem er gesagt hat / after saying / nadat Hy gesê het". The alternative takes "says the Lord" as the main verb: "after saying 'This is the covenant … after those days', the Lord says: 'I will put my laws …'".

21. **9:28 *chōris hamartias*.** Literal: "zum zweiten Mal, ohne Sünde, … erscheinen / will appear a second time, apart from sin / 'n tweede keer, sonder sonde, verskyn". The same phrase is in 4:15 ("yet without sin"). Readings:
    - (a) **not to deal with sin** again: the sin offering is done (most translations, e.g. "not to deal with sin")
    - (b) **without sin** (sinless, as 4:15)

    *Eis sōtērian* ("zur Rettung / for salvation / tot redding") can go with "will appear" or with "who wait for him". I kept the Greek order. In the same passage there are three different verbs: 9:24 *emphanisthēnai* "erscheinen / appear / verskyn" (before God now), 9:26 *pephanerōtai* "offenbart worden / revealed / geopenbaar" (once, in the past), 9:28 *ophthēsetai* "erscheinen / appear / verskyn" (a second time). "Die Sünden vieler zu tragen / to bear the sins of many / om die sondes van baie te dra" echoes Isa 53:12.

22. **10:5–7 Ps 40 LXX "a body you have prepared for me".** The Hebrew (Ps 40:7) has "ears you have dug (opened) for me". Most Septuagint manuscripts and Hebrews have *sōma*, "a body". Per DECISIONS.md the translation follows Hebrews. The argument depends on this word: 10:10 "die Darbringung des Leibes Jesu Christi / the offering of the body of Jesus Christ / die offergawe van die liggaam van Jesus Christus". A devotional may note the difference.
    - *Peri hamartias* "Sündopfer / sin offerings / sondoffers" (literally "[offerings] for sin")
    - *kephalis bibliou* "Buchrolle / scroll of the book / boekrol"
    - AF: Christ addresses God, so "U" (convention §3)
    - 10:9 *anairei* "beseitigt / takes away / skaf af" and *stēsē* "in Kraft setzen / establish / laat staan"

23. **10:26–31.**
    - **10:26** *hekousiōs hamartanontōn hēmōn*: "wenn wir vorsätzlich sündigen / if we sin deliberately / as ons doelbewus sondig". The present tense suggests a continuing state ("if we go on sinning deliberately", as many translations have it), and the background is Num 15:22–31, where unintentional sins (cf. 9:7 "aus Unwissenheit") are set against sins "with a high hand". I kept the plain "sin deliberately" so as not to decide between a persistent state and a single act. Avoided: Luther "mutwillig", AF 1933 "moedswillig", AF 1983 "opsetlik aanhou sondig".
    - **10:27** *pyros zēlos* (Isa 26:11 LXX) is literally "zeal/jealousy of fire": "der Eifer eines Feuers / the fury of a fire / die ywer van 'n vuur".
    - **10:29** "das Blut des Bundes, durch das er geheiligt wurde / … by which he was made holy / … waardeur hy geheilig is": "he" is grammatically the apostate. This sits uneasily with some theologies (cf. 6:4–6), and some read "he" as Christ, who was consecrated by his own blood (cf. John 17:19). Lower case in all three languages keeps the grammatical reading. *Koinon* "für unrein gehalten / regarded as unclean / as onrein beskou" deliberately links to 9:13 (the defiled).
    - **10:30** Deut 32:35, 36. "Der Herr wird sein Volk richten / will judge / sal … oordeel": in Deut 32:36 the sense is "vindicate (do justice for) his people". Hebrews uses it as a warning. The wording of 10:30a is closer to Rom 12:19 than to the LXX.

24. **10:37–38 Hab 2:3–4 LXX.**
    - "Eine kleine, ganz kleine Weile / a very little while / 'n klein, klein rukkie" comes from Isa 26:20 LXX.
    - Hebrews adds the article: "der Kommende / the one who is coming / Hy wat kom" (AF capital: the coming one is read as Christ).
    - It reverses the two lines of Hab 2:4.
    - SBLGNT reads "**mein** Gerechter / **my** righteous one / **my** regverdige" (*ho dikaios mou*). LXX has "the righteous will live by my faithfulness", the Hebrew "by his faithfulness", and Rom 1:17 and Gal 3:11 have neither "my".
    - *Ek pisteōs* can go with "will live" or with "righteous one" ("the one who is righteous by faith"). I kept the order of the Greek.
    - To avoid copying Luther and AF 1933/1983, *hypostellō* is "sich zurückziehen / draw back / (jou) terugtrek", not "zurückweichen / shrink back / terugdeins".
    - 10:39 *eis peripoiēsin psychēs*: "zum Gewinn der Seele / to the preserving of the soul / tot behoud van die siel". "Rettung / salvation / redding" is reserved for *sōtēria*.

25. **6:1 *ton tēs archēs tou Christou logon*.** Literally "the word of the beginning of Christ": "das Anfangswort über Christus / the elementary word about Christ / die eerste woord oor Christus". The genitive can also be "the beginning teaching *of* Christ (his own)". In 6:2 *baptismōn* is plural, "Waschungen / washings / wassings", as in 9:10. The alternative is "Taufen / baptisms / dope", or "teaching about baptisms" (Christian baptism contrasted with Jewish washings). SBLGNT reads *didachēn* (accusative), so the list stands in apposition to "foundation".

26. **6:17 *emesiteusen horkō*.** The verb belongs to *mesitēs* "Mittler / mediator / Middelaar" (8:6; 9:15). Literally "he mediated with an oath", i.e. he stepped in as guarantor between himself and the heirs. Rendered "hat sich mit einem Eid verbürgt / guaranteed it with an oath / Hom met 'n eed borg gestel". The link with "mediator" is lost. A devotional can mention it.

27. **9:4 "golden altar of incense" in the Most Holy Place.** *Thymiatērion* can be "Räucheraltar / altar of incense / reukofferaltaar" or "Räucherpfanne / censer / wierookpan" (LXX usage). In Exod 30:6 the incense altar stands *in front of* the curtain. Hebrews says the inner tent "had" (*echousa*) it, which can mean "belonged to" (cf. 1 Kings 6:22). I kept "Räucheraltar" and did not harmonise. 9:2 "die ausgelegten Brote / the loaves set out / die uitgestalde brode" deliberately avoids Luther's "Schaubrote" and AF "toonbrode".

28. **9:8–11.**
    - 9:8 "solange das erste Zelt noch Bestand hat / while the first tent still has standing / solank die eerste tent nog staande bly": "the first tent" can be the outer compartment (9:6) or the whole earthly sanctuary, i.e. the old order.
    - 9:9 "für die gegenwärtige Zeit / for the present time / vir die huidige tyd": the time then present (the old order) or the readers' own time.
    - 9:11 SBLGNT reads "der Güter, die **gekommen sind** / the good things that **have come** / die goeie dinge wat **gekom het**" (*genomenōn*). Older Bibles read "the good things to come" (*mellontōn*), as in 10:1.
    - 9:11–12 is one long sentence. DE/AF begin a new sentence at "Durch das größere … Zelt / Deur die groter … tent". In EN it stays one sentence.

29. **9:14 "durch den ewigen Geist / through the eternal Spirit / deur die ewige Gees".** No article in Greek (*dia pneumatos aiōniou*). Capitalised as the Holy Spirit (most translations). The alternative is "durch einen ewigen Geist / through an eternal spirit" (his own divine spirit). The verse ends with an exclamation mark in all three languages: it is the climax of a "how much more" argument, and the Greek is a statement, not a question.

30. **10:12 *eis to diēnekes*.** It stands between "one sacrifice for sins" and "sat down". It can go with "offered … for all time" or with "sat down for all time" (SBLGNT has no comma). The translation reads with "offered" in all three languages, which matches 10:14 "for all time made perfect". 10:14 *tous hagiazomenous* (present) is the key rendering "die geheiligt werden / those who are made holy / dié wat geheilig word". The progressive "who are being made holy" is possible.

31. **10:20 "that is, his flesh".** It can refer to "the curtain" (his flesh is the curtain through which the way leads, cf. the curtain torn at his death) or to "the way". I kept the Greek order. *Prosphatos* "neu / new / nuwe" (literally "freshly made") differs from *kainos* "new" (8:8, 13; 9:15). The three languages use the same word for both.

32. **10:34 SBLGNT readings.** "mit den Gefangenen / with the prisoners / met die gevangenes" (*tois desmiois*). Older Bibles read "with my bonds" (*tois desmois mou*), which was used to argue that Paul wrote the letter (cf. DECISIONS.md on authorship). *Echein heautous* "dass ihr selbst … habt / that you yourselves have / dat julle self … het" can also be read "that you have yourselves as a better possession". 9:19 SBLGNT has "das Blut der Kälber / of the calves / van die kalwers" without "and goats". 7:21 SBLGNT ends the Psalm quotation at "for ever", without "according to the order of Melchizedek".

33. **Smaller items.**
    - 6:10 "die Heiligen / the saints / die heiliges" for fellow believers.
    - 6:14 Gen 22:17 Hebrew idiom ("blessing I will bless") is rendered "reich segnen … reich vermehren / surely bless … surely multiply / sekerlik ryklik seën … ryklik vermeerder".
    - 7:5, 10 "Lenden / body / lende": EN uses "body" because "loins" is archaic.
    - 7:14 *anatetalken* "aufgegangen / arisen / opgekom" keeps the image of a rising star or sprouting branch (Num 24:17; Jer 23:5).
    - 9:1, 18 "covenant" is not in the Greek ("the first"). It is supplied as the sense, as in most translations.
    - 10:2 *syneidēsin hamartiōn* "kein Gewissen mehr wegen Sünden / no longer have any sins on their conscience / geen gewete meer oor sondes nie" keeps the key word *Gewissen*. The alternative is "kein Bewusstsein von Sünden / consciousness of sins".
    - 10:24 *katanoōmen* is the same verb as 3:1 ("betrachtet genau / consider closely / rig julle aandag op"). Here it is "aufeinander achten / consider one another / op mekaar ag gee". *Paroxysmos* (literally "provocation", in Acts 15:39 a sharp quarrel) is rendered "anspornen / spur on / aanspoor".
    - 10:25 "den Tag / the Day / die Dag" (capital in EN/AF for the Day of the Lord).

### Chapters 11–13

34. **11:1 *hypostasis* and *elenchos*.** "Der Glaube aber ist die Gewissheit dessen, was man hofft, der Beweis für Dinge, die man nicht sieht / Now faith is the assurance of what is hoped for, the proof of things that are not seen / Die geloof is die sekerheid van wat gehoop word, die bewys van dinge wat nie gesien word nie".
   - *Hypostasis* is "Gewissheit / assurance / sekerheid", the same as in 3:14 (§2.4), so that the two verses stay linked.
   - Alternatives: "Grundlage / foundation / grondslag" (faith as what the hoped-for things stand on), or "Wirklichkeit / reality / werklikheid" (cf. 1:3 "Wesen": faith gives the hoped-for things substance now). If you change 11:1, change 3:14 too.
   - *Elenchos* means proof or evidence (objective), or being convinced (subjective). "Beweis / proof / bewys" is objective. The alternative is "Überzeugtsein / conviction / oortuiging", but note that Elberfelder has "Überführtsein" and ESV has "conviction".
   - 11:1 is a description of what faith does, not a full definition.
35. **11:3 *tous aiōnas* and *mē ek phainomenōn*.** "die Welten / the worlds / die wêrelde", the same as 1:2 (§2.7). *Katērtisthai* is "geformt / fashioned / gevorm" (the same verb as 13:21 "ausrüsten / equip / toerus"). *Rhēmati theou* has no article: "durch Gottes Wort / by God's word / deur God se woord". The negation can be read in two ways:
   - (a) "what is seen did *not* come from visible things" (as translated)
   - (b) "what is seen came from things that do *not* appear" (creation out of the invisible)

   The meaning is almost the same either way.
36. **11:11 Who is the subject: Abraham or Sarah?** SBLGNT prints the dative καὶ αὐτῇ Σάρρᾳ, and *dynamis eis katabolēn spermatos* is literally "power for the deposit of seed", which is said of the father. So the subject is Abraham, continuing from 11:8–10, and 11:12 "von einem / from one man / uit een man … so gut wie tot / as good as dead" fits. I translated this: "Durch Glauben empfing er – auch zusammen mit Sara selbst – die Kraft, Nachkommen zu zeugen … / By faith he received power to father offspring – together with Sarah herself – … / Deur geloof het hy – saam met Sara self – krag ontvang om nageslag te verwek …". Options:
   - (a) Abraham the subject, with Sarah included (SBLGNT, as translated)
   - (b) Sarah the subject: "Durch Glauben empfing auch Sara selbst Kraft, schwanger zu werden / By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive / Deur geloof het Sara self ook krag ontvang om swanger te word". This is the nominative αὐτὴ Σάρρα of many editions; some manuscripts add στεῖρα "the barren one" (Luther, 1933/53, 1983, ESV)

   If you choose (b), it would depart from the base text. Your devotional on 11:8–19 should match whichever you choose.
37. **12:1–2 the race.**
   - *Archēgos kai teleiōtēs*: "Wegbereiter und Vollender des Glaubens / the pioneer of faith and the one who brings it to perfection / baanbreker en voltooier van die geloof" (see (a) above; *archēgos* as in 2:10). *Tēs pisteōs* has the article: "des Glaubens" can mean faith as such, or "our" faith (some add "unseres"). I did not add it.
   - *Anti tēs prokeimenēs autō charas* "um der Freude willen, die vor ihm lag / for the sake of the joy that lay before him / ter wille van die vreugde wat voor Hom gelê het". The alternative takes *anti* as "instead of": he gave up the joy that was available to him and chose the cross (cf. 11:25–26 Moses).
   - *Nephos martyrōn*: the witnesses of ch. 11 are people who were attested (*martyreō* 11:2, 39) and who testify, not only spectators. "Zeugen / witnesses / getuies" keeps both senses.
   - *Euperistatos*: rare; "leicht umklammernd / readily wraps itself around / maklik aan ons klou". The word may also mean "easily distracting" or "much admired"; there is no certain meaning.
   - 11:26 *apeblepen eis* (Moses "had his eyes fixed on the reward") is a different verb from *aphorōntes* (12:2). I let it echo the series title in all three languages. Tell me if that is too strong.
38. **12:17 "no place for repentance": whose repentance?** "Er fand keinen Raum für Umkehr, obwohl er unter Tränen danach suchte / he found no place for repentance, although he sought it with tears / hy het geen ruimte vir bekering gevind nie, hoewel hy met trane daarna gesoek het". Options:
   - (a) Esau could not undo what he had done: no opportunity to reverse the sale, whatever he felt
   - (b) Isaac's change of mind: Esau could not bring his father to revoke the blessing (Gen 27:33–38)
   - (c) Esau's own inner repentance was not granted

   *Autēn* "it" (feminine) can refer to the blessing (*eulogia*) or to repentance (*metanoia*). "Danach / it / daarna" keeps both open. This is a contested passage, close to 6:4–6 (part 2), so it is translated literally. Choose the reading for the devotional.
39. **12:3 *eis heautous* "against themselves".** SBLGNT reads the plural (well attested early). Many editions and translations read the singular "against himself/him". Literally: "such opposition from sinners against themselves" – sinners who harm themselves by opposing him. EN and AF ("teen hulleself") follow SBLGNT. DE "gegen sich selbst" can be singular or plural and keeps both open. If you want the familiar reading: EN "against himself", AF "teen Homself".
40. **12:18–24 Sinai and Zion.**
   - SBLGNT has no "mountain" in 12:18 ("etwas, das man berühren konnte / something that could be touched / iets wat aangeraak kon word"). Older Bibles add "Berg / mountain / berg", which is clearly implied by 12:20. *Psēlaphōmenō kai kekaumenō pyri* can also be read "to something touchable and burning with fire" (the mountain). I kept the two separate.
   - 12:22–23 punctuation follows SBLGNT: "zu Zehntausenden von Engeln, zur Festversammlung und Gemeinde der Erstgeborenen". The alternative is "zu Zehntausenden von Engeln in festlicher Versammlung, und zur Gemeinde …", which joins the festal gathering to the angels.
   - *Kritē theō pantōn*: "zu Gott, dem Richter aller / God, the judge of all / God, die regter van almal". The alternative is "to a judge who is God of all".
   - 12:24 "das besser spricht als Abel / that speaks better than Abel / wat beter praat as Abel": *para ton Abel* is literally "than Abel" (Abel himself, who "still speaks", 11:4), not "than Abel's blood". I left it as the Greek has it. Abel's blood cries for justice (Gen 4:10), and Jesus' blood speaks a better word.
   - 12:25 "den, der auf der Erde Weisung gab / the one who warned them on earth / hom wat op aarde gewaarsku het" can be God at Sinai or Moses. AF lower case "hom" keeps it open; "Hom" would decide for God.
41. **11:21 the staff.** "und er betete an, auf die Spitze seines Stabes gestützt / leaning on the top of his staff / terwyl hy op die punt van sy staf leun". Hebrews quotes Gen 47:31 LXX. The Hebrew consonants מטה can be read *miṭṭāh* "bed" (Masoretic text: "at the head of the bed") or *maṭṭeh* "staff" (Septuagint). Following DECISIONS.md, the translation keeps the Greek. "Gestützt / leaning" is added for the sense; literally he "worshipped upon the top of his staff". A devotional can note the difference from Genesis.
42. **11:40 and 12:23 "made perfect".** 11:40 "Sie sollten nicht ohne uns zur Vollendung gebracht werden / so that they would not be made perfect apart from us / sodat hulle nie sonder ons tot volmaaktheid gebring sou word nie" and 12:23 "die Geister der Gerechten, die zur Vollendung gebracht sind" use the *teleioō* rendering (2:10; 5:9; 7:19; 10:14). *Problepsamenou* is "vorgesehen / foreseen / voorsien", and it can mean "foreseen" or "provided". DE "vorgesehen" and AF "voorsien" cover both senses; EN "foreseen" covers only one ("provided" is the alternative).
43. **11:19 *en parabolē*.** "daher hat er ihn auch zurückerhalten – als ein Gleichnis / hence he did receive him back – as a symbol / vandaar het hy hom ook teruggekry – as 'n gelykenis". Options:
    - (a) figuratively: Isaac was as good as dead and was given back
    - (b) as a type, foreshadowing the resurrection (of Christ)

    *Hothen* can be "daher / hence / vandaar" (reason) or "von dort / from there" (from the dead). DE "daher" and AF "vandaar" keep both; EN "hence" takes the reason.
44. **11:26 *ton oneidismon tou Christou*.** "die Schmach des Christus / the reproach of the Christ / die smaad van die Christus". The article is kept. Options:
    - (a) the reproach that Christ bears, and Moses shares in it ahead of time (cf. 13:13 "seine Schmach tragen")
    - (b) "des Gesalbten / of the anointed one / van die Gesalfde", Israel as God's anointed (Ps 89:51–52 LXX)

    (a) is the usual reading.
45. **13:20–21 the closing blessing.**
    - SBLGNT: "mit allem Guten / with everything good / met alles wat goed is" (older Bibles: "in every good *work*"); "in uns / in us / in ons" (some manuscripts: "in you"); "in Ewigkeit / for ever / vir ewig" without "and ever" (see (a): the §1 table should be corrected for 13:21).
    - *En haimati diathēkēs aiōniou* "mit dem Blut eines ewigen Bundes / by the blood of an eternal covenant / deur die bloed van 'n ewige verbond" can belong to "heraufgeführt / brought up" (the resurrection as God's covenant act) or to "the great shepherd" (shepherd through his blood). The word order keeps both.
    - *Hō hē doxa* "Ihm sei die Herrlichkeit / to whom be the glory / Aan Hom die heerlikheid" can be God or Jesus Christ. All three keep it open.
    - The only resurrection statement in Hebrews is here (*anagagōn ek nekrōn*, "heraufgeführt / brought up / opgebring"), with an echo of Isa 63:11 LXX (Moses the shepherd brought up out of the sea).
46. **13:8 word order.** The Greek has no verb: "Jesus Christ yesterday and today the same, and for ever". DE "Jesus Christus ist gestern und heute derselbe – und in Ewigkeit / Jesus Christ is yesterday and today the same – and for ever / Jesus Christus is gister en vandag dieselfde – en vir ewig" follows that order, which avoids Elberfelder ("ist derselbe gestern und heute und in Ewigkeit") and AF 1933. The dash shows that "for ever" is added as a climax. If you prefer the familiar flow, I would write "derselbe gestern, heute und in Ewigkeit".
47. **12:28 *echōmen charin*.** "lasst uns dankbar sein / let us be thankful / laat ons dankbaar wees" (the idiom *charin echein* = to be thankful). The alternative is "lasst uns an der Gnade festhalten / let us have grace / laat ons die genade hê". That reading links to 12:15 and 13:9, where *charis* is "Gnade / grace / genade". *Latreuō* is "dienen / worship / dien" (part 2's row), and 13:10 has the same verb.
48. **12:7 *eis paideian hypomenete*.** SBLGNT reads *eis* ("for discipline"), not *ei* ("if you endure", the older Bibles). *Hypomenete* can be indicative ("Um der Erziehung willen harrt ihr aus / It is for discipline that you endure / Dit is ter wille van tug dat julle volhard", as translated) or imperative ("Harrt aus, es dient der Erziehung! / Endure it as discipline!").
49. **Smaller points.**
    - 11:4 *di' hēs … di' autēs* can refer to faith or to the sacrifice (both feminine). "Dadurch / through it / daardeur" keeps both.
    - 11:5 "er wurde nicht mehr gefunden …" is Gen 5:24 LXX, set in quotation marks. 11:18 (Gen 21:12), 12:20, 12:21 and 13:5 are prose quotations in quotation marks. 12:5–6 (Prov 3:11–12 LXX), 12:26 (Hag 2:6) and 13:6 (Ps 118:6) are set as `> ` lines.
    - 11:13 "Durchreisende / temporary residents / deurreisigers" deliberately avoids AF 1933 "bywoners" and ESV "exiles".
    - 11:31 *pornē* "die Prostituierte / the prostitute / die prostituut".
    - 11:35 *apolytrōsis* "Freilassung / release / vrylating" (part 2: 9:15 "Erlösung"; see (a)).
    - SBLGNT omits "they were tempted" in 11:37 (older Bibles have "zersägt, versucht").
    - 12:13 *ektrapē* "sich verrenkt / put out of joint / ontwrig" (medical, because "healed" follows). The alternative is "vom Weg abkommt / turn aside".
    - 13:4 is verbless: it can be a statement ("marriage is honourable among all") or an instruction (as translated).
    - 13:17 *peithesthe* "Folgt / Listen to / Luister na" (not *hypakouō* "gehorchen"). 13:18 *peithometha* "wir sind überzeugt" is the same verb.
    - 13:22 *anechesthe* "nehmt … geduldig an / bear with / aanvaar … geduldig".
    - 13:24 *hoi apo tēs Italias* "die aus Italien / those from Italy / die mense uit Italië" can mean Italians away from home (writing to Rome) or people in Italy. This is not decided.
    - 13:25 SBLGNT has no "Amen".

## 3. Style

- Modern, natural German, English and Afrikaans; nothing copied from Luther (1912/1984/2017), Elberfelder, ESV/NIV/KJV, or AF 1933/53 or 1983. Where a key word brings a phrase close to a traditional one, the key word wins (e.g. 5:6 "nach der Ordnung Melchisedeks").
- The `avoid_words` in series.yml do not appear (checked).
- Long periodic sentences: 1:1–4 is split into two sentences (1:1–2 and 1:3–4) at the relative clause *hos ōn*; the rhetoric (God spoke – he spoke; the Son's seven-fold description) is kept. 2:2–4 stays one question in the Greek; I ended the question after "Rettung / salvation / redding" in 2:3, and 2:3b–4 is a second sentence.
- Readers are *ihr / you / julle* (plural). God's address to the Son in the quotations: DE/EN *du / you*. AF follows the Jona and 1/2 Tim pattern:
  - *U* when a human (the psalmist) or Christ addresses God or the Son as God (1:8–12; 2:6–8, 12)
  - *jy/jou* when God addresses the Son (1:5, 13; 5:5, 6)
  - subject and object pronouns for God and Christ capitalised (*Hy, Hom, Homself, Ek, My*); possessives lower case (*sy, u, my*), as in 2 Timothy
- Quotations: poetic quotations as `> ` lines (verse numbers inside the lines where a verse begins within the poem); prose quotations in quotation marks („…“ DE, "…" EN/AF). Speech inside a poetic quotation (3:10, 11) follows a colon without quotation marks.
- *Laleō* = "sprechen / speak / praat, spreek" (1:1, 2; 2:2, 3, 5; 3:5; 4:8; 5:5). "Verkündigen / proclaim / verkondig" stays reserved for *kēryssō* (2 Timothy). In Hebrews it occurs only with *euangelizomai* ("die gute Botschaft verkünden"). *Apangellō* in 2:12 is "bekannt machen / declare / bekend maak".
- Book name and chapter/verse: Hebräer 1,1 (DE), Hebrews 1:1 (EN), Hebreërs 1:1 (AF).
