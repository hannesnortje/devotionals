## Digging Deeper: Context and Meaning

### Historical, Cultural and Literary Background

Having explained in the first half of chapter 5 how the church should care for its widows, Paul now turns to a second group in the church's household that needs both financial support and careful oversight: the elders (*presbyteroi*).

We need to remember that the crisis in Ephesus had arisen precisely among the elders. Years earlier, on the beach at Miletus, Paul had warned the Ephesian elders that men would arise "from among your own selves" speaking twisted things to draw away the disciples after them (Acts 20:30). By the time Paul writes this letter, that warning has come true (see 1 Timothy 1:19–20). Timothy therefore faced a sensitive and complex task: he had to honour and provide for faithful, hard-working elders; protect innocent leaders from malicious gossip; discipline sinning leaders without fear or favouritism; and prevent unsuitable men from being appointed too quickly to positions of leadership in the future.

### Insights from the Greek

The Greek text unfolds a masterpiece of spiritual oversight and pastoral care:

- **"Double honour" and "labour" (*diplēs timēs* / *kopiōntes* – διπλῆς τιμῆς / κοπιῶντες, v. 17):** Elders "who lead well" (*kalōs proestōtes* – those who go ahead with spiritual excellence and take care of others) are "worthy of double honour". Just as with the widows in verse 3, the word *timē* ("honour") includes both high esteem and tangible financial reward. "Double honour" does not mean they should be paid twice as much as a widow, but that they are owed both the respect of the church and a generous living. This applies especially to "those who labour in preaching and teaching" (*kopiaō* – to toil to the point of physical and spiritual exhaustion).
- **"For the Scripture says" (*legei gar hē graphē* – λέγει γὰρ ἡ γραφή, v. 18):** To support this financial provision, Paul quotes two texts under a single heading: *"For the Scripture says …"* The first quotation comes from the Old Testament (Deuteronomy 25:4 – the ox that may freely eat from the grain while doing the heavy work of threshing). The second (*"The worker deserves his wages"*), however, comes straight from the words of Jesus as recorded in Luke 10:7! This is a tremendous theological moment: already in the early sixties of the first century, Paul places the words of Jesus, as recorded in Luke's Gospel, on exactly the same level of divine authority ("the Scripture") as the books of Moses.
- **"Rebuke in the presence of all" (*enōpion pantōn elenche* – ἐνώπιον πάντων ἔλεγχε, vv. 19–20):** Because spiritual leaders are a constant target of the enemy, no accusation against an elder may be accepted on the basis of mere hearsay or one disgruntled person's word; it requires the biblical standard of two or three independent witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). But when an elder's sin is proven and he persists in it (*tous hamartanontas* – a present participle describing someone who keeps on sinning and refuses to repent), he must be rebuked "in the presence of all". Public leadership carries public accountability, so that a holy awe (*phobos*) may come over the whole church.
- **"Without prejudice … out of favouritism" (*chōris prokrimatos … kata prosklisin* – χωρὶς προκρίματος … κατὰ πρόσκλισιν, v. 21):** Paul calls on the highest witnesses imaginable – God, Christ Jesus and the chosen angels who watch over the church – to bind Timothy to complete fairness. He may do nothing out of *prokrima* (prejudice – judging someone guilty in advance because you don't like them) or out of *prosklisis* (favouritism – literally leaning towards someone's side because he is your friend or an influential member).
- **"Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands" (*cheiras tacheōs mēdeni epitithei* – χεῖρας ταχέως μηδενὶ ἐπιτίθει, v. 22):** This refers to the formal commissioning or ordination of someone to the office of elder (see 4:14). If Timothy hastily appoints an untested man to a position of spiritual authority, and that man later wreaks havoc through false teaching or scandal, Timothy has taken part in the sins of others (*koinōnei* – literally, become a partner in them).
- **"No longer drink only water" (*mēketi hydropotei* – μηκέτι ὑδροπότει, v. 23):** Right after telling Timothy, *"Keep yourself pure"*, Paul adds a deeply fatherly remark: *"No longer drink only water …"* The verb *hydropoteō* describes someone who has become a strict "water-drinker" on principle. Timothy had probably avoided all wine – in reaction to the drunkenness of some leaders (3:3, 8) and influenced by the ascetic climate in Ephesus – in order to keep his purity above suspicion. But the impure water of the ancient world had affected his stomach and made him chronically ill. Paul lovingly corrects him: true purity (*hagnos*) lies in moral and spiritual integrity, not in neglecting your physical health. In ancient medicine a little wine was used to purify water and relieve stomach complaints.
- **"Obvious … follow after" (*prodēloi … epakolouthousin* – πρόδηλοι … ἐπακολουθοῦσιν, vv. 24–25):** With these last two verses Paul returns to why the laying on of hands must never be hasty. Some people's sins (and good works) are *prodēlos* – clear as day from the start, walking ahead of them, as it were. Others are masters at hiding their true nature; their sins (or their quiet, hidden good works) "follow after them" (*epakoloutheō*). But if you wait patiently and let time do its work, both the hidden wickedness of the hypocrite and the quiet godliness of the humble servant will eventually come to the surface.

### Core Theological Truths

1. **Spiritual labour in the Word is noble but draining, and deserves generous support.** A church that financially neglects or emotionally belittles those who feed it with the gospel robs itself of spiritual blessing.
2. **Biblical justice balances protection with accountability.** The church must never be a breeding ground for unsubstantiated gossip about leaders – but neither may it become a hiding place where leaders' proven sins are swept under the carpet.
3. **Prejudice and cronyism are an abomination before the throne of heaven.** In God's house the same standard of holiness applies to everyone, regardless of position, wealth or friendships.
4. **Discernment requires patience.** Time and testing expose every false front and confirm every sincere character.

## Living It Out

This passage speaks directly to the health of our churches today and to our personal relationships.

In modern church culture, people often fall into one of two dangerous extremes in how they treat spiritual leaders. On the one hand there are churches that treat their pastors and elders with a consumer mentality: they expect leaders to be available around the clock, pick apart the sermon over Sunday lunch, and listen eagerly to every unsubstantiated rumour or complaint from a single disgruntled member. Paul says: **Stop the gossip in its tracks!** Don't even entertain an accusation unless it is backed by reliable witnesses – and show "double honour" to those who pour out their strength to feed your soul with the Word.

On the other hand, we see today the tragic results of a "celebrity culture" in some churches, where prominent leaders are considered untouchable. When such leaders fall into serious moral or financial sin, their inner circle closes ranks out of favouritism (*prosklisis*) to cover up the scandal "so that the ministry doesn't suffer". Paul's solemn charge before God and the angels shatters this practice: when sin is covered up, the church loses its holy fear of God.

On a very personal level, verses 22 to 25 give each of us wisdom for everyday choices:

- **Don't trust or promote people too quickly.** Whether it's appointing a leader in the church, choosing a business partner or entering a marriage – don't be dazzled by a shiny first impression. Give it time. What is hidden today will be revealed tomorrow under the pressures of life.
- **Encouragement for the quiet worker.** Perhaps you feel that nobody notices your good works and sacrifices, while others who are louder get all the credit. Be assured of the promise in verse 25: even good works that are not yet obvious "cannot remain hidden". God sees them, and in his time he will bring them to light.

### Questions for Personal Reflection

- Do I show sincere honour, gratitude and support to those who labour to minister the Word to me – and do I protect their reputation when others speak ill of them without evidence?
- Is my judgement of other people truly free of *prokrima* (prejudice against people I don't like) and *prosklisis* (overlooking the faults of my friends and favourites)?
- Do I practise patient discernment before giving people my trust, my vote or my support – and do I watch daily over both my spiritual purity and the healthy care of my body?

## Prayer

*Holy and righteous God, you sit on the throne surrounded by thousands of angels, and you judge without favouritism.*

*I bow today in deep awe before your majesty. Thank you for the faithful shepherds, pastors and elders you have given to your church – people who wear themselves out studying and proclaiming your Word to feed our souls. Teach me to treat them with double honour, to pray for them, and never to lend my ear to malicious, unsubstantiated gossip that seeks to destroy their ministry.*

*At the same time I pray for a spirit of holy fear and incorruptible fairness in our church. Keep our leaders from pride and secret sin, and keep us from ever sweeping sin under the carpet out of fear of people or favouritism. Let your church be a place of light, truth and purity.*

*Lord, give me personally the wisdom of patience. Keep me from hasty decisions and shallow judgements about people, for I know that your eyes see through every mask and that time reveals everything. Keep my heart, my thoughts and my hands pure in the midst of a soiled world, and give me the wisdom to care for my body too, as a serviceable temple for your glory. In the name of Jesus Christ, the righteous Judge and Head of the church. Amen.*
