# 1 Timothy – translation notes (for review)

Base text: **SBL Greek New Testament** (SBLGNT), © 2010 Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Textual-variant markers were removed; the text follows SBLGNT throughout.
Aim: close to the Greek, natural to read, and each key word rendered the same way every time it occurs, in all three languages. Divine names: "Herr / Lord / Here" in normal case, since there is no YHWH in the NT. Timothy is addressed as *du / you / jy*, and the closing greeting in 6:21 is plural (*euch / you all / julle*).
Poetry: 1:17, 2:5–6, 3:16b and 6:15–16 are set as hymn or doxology lines.

## 2. Choices you should check

1. **2:8–15: man/woman or husband/wife?** *anēr* and *gynē* mean both. The text says "the men" (2:8) and "women" (2:9), and in 2:11–12 the singular "a woman" and "a man". I translated *woman / man* throughout, which is the general reading. The alternative is *wife / husband* in 2:11–15, which restricts the passage to marriage. 2:13–14 (Adam and Eve) can support either reading.
2. **2:11–12 *hēsychia*.** I chose "Stille / quietness / rustigheid", not "Schweigen / silence / stilte". The same root is used in 2:2 for everyone ("a peaceful and quiet life"), and it usually means a calm, peaceable disposition rather than not speaking. AF "stilte" would be heard as silence, which is why I used "rustigheid". The alternative is "silence".
3. **2:12 *authentein* (only here in the NT).** I used the neutral "Autorität über einen Mann auszuüben / to exercise authority over a man / gesag oor 'n man uit te oefen". The main options are:
   - (a) exercise authority (neutral)
   - (b) assume or usurp authority, domineer (negative: self-assumed authority)
   - (c) "teach or domineer" read together as one idea, "teach in a domineering way"

   The word is rare and its sense is debated. Your Day 5 devotional should match whichever option you choose.
4. **2:15 *sōthēsetai dia tēs teknogonias*.** Translated literally: "she will be saved through childbearing, if *they* remain…" (the subject switches from singular to plural, as in the Greek). Readings include:
   - preserved *through* (the danger of) childbirth
   - saved through *the* childbearing, the birth of the Messiah (the article)
   - saved while fulfilling her calling
   - "they" = women in general, or the woman and her children

   I deliberately didn't smooth any of this out.
5. **3:2, 12 *mias gynaikos anēr*; 5:9 *henos andros gynē*.** I rendered these "Mann einer einzigen Frau / the husband of one wife / die man van een vrou" (and "Frau eines einzigen Mannes" etc. in 5:9). Readings:
   - faithful to one spouse ("a one-woman man")
   - married only once
   - not polygamous

   The alternative EN wording is "faithful to his wife", but that already interprets the phrase.
6. **3:11 *gynaikas*.** I translated "Die Frauen ebenso / Women likewise / Net so moet die vroue". The options are the wives of the deacons, or women deacons. The Greek has no possessive, which slightly favours women serving, but that is debated.
7. **3:16 *hos* ("who").** SBLGNT reads ὅς, the oldest manuscripts. Later manuscripts read θεός ("God was revealed in the flesh", as in Luther and 1933/53). I rendered the line "Er wurde offenbart / He was revealed / Hy is geopenbaar", where the subject is Christ, as the hymn implies. Readers who know the older Bibles may notice the difference, so it may deserve a sentence in Day 8. I translated *en pneumati* as "im Geist / by the Spirit / deur die Gees". "In the spirit" (in the spiritual realm) is also possible.
8. **1:10 *arsenokoitais*.** "Männer, die mit Männern schlafen / men who have sex with men / mans wat met mans slaap". This is a literal rendering of the compound (*arsēn* + *koitē*, cf. Lev 18:22; 20:13 LXX). I added no further interpretation.
9. **5:17 *diplēs timēs*.** "doppelter Ehre / double honour / dubbele eer". 5:18 shows the honour includes pay. I kept the literal phrase.
10. **5:23.** Translated literally: "no longer drink only water". The point is health, not an endorsement of drinking.
11. **6:10.** "*Eine* Wurzel aller Übel / *a* root of all kinds of evil / '*n* wortel van allerlei kwaad". The Greek has no article on *rhiza*, so not "*the* root of all evil". *Pantōn tōn kakōn* can mean "all evils" or "all kinds of evils".
12. **1:4 *oikonomia theou*.** "Gottes Heilsordnung / God's ordering of things / God se heilsorde". Alternatives are "God's plan of salvation", "stewardship from God" or "God's training". The sentence in 1:3–4 is grammatically unfinished in Greek (an anacoluthon), so it ends with a dash.
13. **1:15–16 *prōtos*.** "der erste / the first / die eerste" rather than "the worst" or "the chief", so that the link to 1:16 ("in me as the first") remains visible. The AF Day 2 title "Grootste van Sondaars" interprets the word and could stay as the title.
14. **eusebeia.** DE „Gottesfurcht“ (alternatives: „Frömmigkeit“, „Gottseligkeit“); AF "godsaligheid" (decided 30 Oct 2026, to match the AF devotionals; alternative "godsvrug"). All other choices in §2 approved as proposed.
15. **4:14 *presbyterion*.** "Ältestenschaft / council of elders / ouderlingeraad".
16. **6:20 *pseudōnymos gnōsis*.** "die fälschlich so genannte Erkenntnis / what is falsely called knowledge / die valslik sogenaamde kennis". This doesn't name a particular movement.

## 3. Style

- Modern, natural German, English and Afrikaans; nothing copied from Luther, Elberfelder, ESV/NIV, or AF 1933/53 or 1983.
- Paul's long sentences (1:3–4, 1:8–11, 6:13–16) are kept as one sentence where possible.
- OT quotations (5:18) are in quotation marks.
- Book name and chapter/verse: 1. Timotheus 1,1 (DE), 1 Timothy 1:1 (EN), 1 Timoteus 1:1 (AF).
